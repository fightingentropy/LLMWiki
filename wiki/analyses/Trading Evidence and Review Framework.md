# Trading Evidence and Review Framework

The common lesson in [[Risk Analysis]], [[Market Performance Measurement]] and [[Trading_Philosophy]] is to make a claim inspectable before judging its result. The corrected funding-trade bookmark is a useful example: keep the author's correction attached to the initial story.

## Before drawing a conclusion

Record the original claim, date, instrument, stated horizon and the evidence cited. Separate observations from forecasts. A hypothetical scenario in [[Markets - Articles]] remains a scenario even when its numbers are precise.

Write down the assumptions that matter: pricing basis, liquidity, fees, financing, collateral, off-venue positions and data coverage. Mark unknown values as unknown; do not quietly substitute zero.

## What would change the view

Use the [[Markets - Brief]] habit of observable triggers. State what observation would support the thesis, what would weaken it and when it will be reassessed. If those conditions cannot be stated, the item is still a research lead.

## Afterward

Preserve the original thesis and add the result separately. Distinguish whether the thesis was wrong, the execution differed, the measurement was incomplete or chance dominated the chosen period. Include drawdowns, costs and cash flows when comparing outcomes; headline PnL alone cannot resolve skill.

## Useful questions for a saved chart or leaderboard

Who produced it? What is included and excluded? Are account identities established? What period is measured? Are the returns comparable? Where is the underlying data? See [[Market Performance Measurement]] for the account-role questions and [[Trading_Psychology]] for the temptation to turn a persuasive story into conviction.

This is a research and journaling framework. It does not select a trade, position size or current market exposure.
