# Stress Management

Erlin's notes do not treat stress as one thing to minimise. They treat it as two opposite things to balance: acute stress is fuel, chronic stress is poison. The prescription is symmetric: eliminate chronic stressors and deliberately reintroduce acute ones. Modern life is framed as a pathological distribution, with too much chronic stress and too little acute. The idea is anchored in [[Body_Optimization|Body Optimization]] as a core distinction, and the notes surround it with antifragility, breathing techniques, cold showers and movement.

## Acute versus chronic stress

The framing comes from a [post by @NoahRyanCo](https://x.com/NoahRyanCo/status/1928925779699839308) saved in the body note (from [[Body]]):

> Acute stress is good. It makes you better, stronger, more adaptive. Chronic stress is bad. It makes you weak burnt out, and depressed
>
> Modern life is full of chronic stress (social isolation, bad sleep, soulless work, 24/7 news cycles) and void of acute stress (Competition, high intensity exercise, short conflict).
>
> Eliminate chronic stressors & replace with acute stressors.

A related line from the same author, on activity and recovery: "The [more](https://x.com/NoahRyanCo/status/1928840606974947344) you do, the more energy you have, the more you relax. World is full of people who do nothing, are constantly exhausted, and never recoup." (from [[Body]])

Bad sleep sits on the chronic list; [[Circadian Rhythm]] and [[ADHD]] cover the sleep-timing side. High-intensity exercise sits on the acute list; see [[Fitness]].

## Antifragility

A take saved in the quotes note extends the same idea (from [[Quotes]]):

> This idea cuts deep into how we think about systems, life, stress, and success:
> - Fragile systems: get destroyed by shocks.
> - Robust systems: survive shocks.
> - Antifragile systems: *need* shocks to improve.
>
> So if you are antifragile to a particular input, don’t seek comfort or stability—seek variability. For example:
> - Creativity thrives in chaotic environments.
> - Growth (personal, economic, biological) often accelerates under stress, not peace.
> - Relationships that survive tension tend to deepen.

"Antifragile" is [[Nassim Taleb]]'s term; the note's take does not name him.

The psychology note's [swimming rats story](https://www.shawnmaust.com/2016/12/swimming-rats-power-hope/#:~:text=A%20few%20weeks%20ago%2C%20I40%2D60%20hours%20on%20average.) sits close to this. In Curt Richter's 1957 experiments, wild rats dropped into water died within 15 minutes, while domesticated rats swam 40–60 hours. Wild rats that had been put in and pulled out a few times beforehand lasted about as long as the domesticated group. As the note retells it, the researchers postulated that the deaths were more psychological than physiological: "the real issue was one of hopelessness." Rats that had been pulled out before knew escape was possible, so they kept swimming. (from [[Psychology|Psychology & Behavior]])

Correction, 16 October 2026: in [Richter's paper](https://doi.org/10.1097/00006842-195705000-00004), the 15-minute and 40–60-hour figures are for rats whose whiskers had been trimmed. All 34 clipped wild rats died within 1–15 minutes, and 9 of 12 clipped domesticated rats swam 40–60 hours; intact domesticated rats swam 60 to 80 hours in 95 °F water. Wild rats conditioned by repeated brief holding and brief immersions swam as long as domestic rats or longer.

## Breathing and arousal

These are techniques from posts, not clinical protocols; stop any breathing practice that makes you dizzy or unwell.

- **Box breathing.** The body note saves a [post](https://x.com/StrongManGuide/status/1909559675818754406) under the label "Boxed breathing", listed beside the stress lines as a stress-regulation and focus tool. (from [[Body]])
- **Adrenaline on command.** A first-person [account by @sporadicalia](https://x.com/sporadicalia/status/1891306234147659905), saved with the question "how are you so happy?": "brother, when i was 17 i discovered that i could physiologically stimulate adrenaline release on command in just about any situation". The method: walk outside with high-tempo music, look at the sky, time "2-4 short rapid inhales through your nose then one long exhale" to the beat, open the eyes wide on the exhales, then "release it all with a big exhale and super wide eyes". The author says it takes practice, "like any sort of movement or skill". One person's account. (from [[Body]])

## Cold exposure

The body note keeps a post by @olivesagan and two replies (from [[Body]]):

> i always figured cold showers were a dumb grindset thing, but after seeing some research on how deliberate cold exposure facilitates fairly high levels of dopamine release, i decided to give it a rip and i'm a believer now.

> I've done five minute cold showers a few times now in response to feeling anxious and every time my day has done a 180 and i have felt great for the rest of the day, it's really drastic too, it almost feels like taking a pill or something.

> I'm a big fan of starting it hot, since I just feel I get cleaner that way- may be in my head, not sure- and then doing a cold rinse. Absolutely improves my day

These are anecdotes. The "taking a pill" comparison is a subjective description: the notes supply no controlled evidence that a five-minute cold shower is equivalent to medication. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]]) [[Fitness]] makes the same point.

A gentler variant is the [cold-shower "unbracing" exercise](https://x.com/jonnym1ller/status/1958345432410845515): notice the anticipatory tension before cold water and practise releasing it. It is an anecdotal somatic exercise and a mindfulness prompt, not treatment advice. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

## Movement, stillness and other therapies

- **Movement as medicine.** "Dance is therapy. Movement is therapy. Creating is therapy. Cuddling is therapy. Writing is therapy. Sun is therapy." The notes give the handle @Theholisticpsyc. (from [[Body]], [[Psychology|Psychology & Behavior]])
- **Stillness for clarity.** "if you need clarity all u have to do is put ALL devices away for 24 hours, exist in solitude, and simply let ur brain do its thing. go on walks. lie on bed and stare at ceiling. the harsh truths will [come](https://x.com/divya_venn/status/1928822020789145912)." (from [[Psychology|Psychology & Behavior]])

## Chronic stressors and mood

The psychology note holds a take whose list of causes overlaps the chronic-stress list above (from [[Psychology|Psychology & Behavior]]):

> So now that the chemical imbalance theory has been conclusively disproven what causes depression?
>
> -learned helplessness
>
> -Not leaving the house, not socializing, high dopamine baseline due to social media, lack of sunlight, poor diet, poor sleep.

The premise and the list are a take, not a clinical account; depression has many contributors and deserves proper assessment. See [[Mental_Health|Mental Health]].
