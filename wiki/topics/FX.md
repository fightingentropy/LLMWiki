# FX

Foreign exchange is where macro regimes often show up first: currency moves tend to come before moves in equities and bonds. The daily brief template in [[Markets - Brief]] asks for an FX read in every overview, covering "USD broad, key crosses, EM stress signals". This page covers those three pieces, the rate relationships behind them, carry trades and the yen, and a few saved framings on valuing currencies and reading them as policy.

## The dollar

### USD broad (trade-weighted)

- **What it is**: US dollar strength against a basket of trading-partner currencies.
- **Drivers**: interest rate differentials, growth expectations, risk sentiment.
- **Reading it**: a strong dollar usually means risk-off or high US rates.
- **Indices**: [[DXY]] and other broad indices track it. See [[DXY]] for the US dollar index itself.

The dollar also feeds into metals. Erlin's handbook note puts it as "rates low or dollar weaker = gold more attractive" (from [[Markets - Handbook]]). See [[Gold]].

## Key crosses

| Cross | What moves it |
| --- | --- |
| EUR/USD | ECB versus Fed rate differentials; Eurozone growth |
| GBP/USD | BoE versus Fed; UK growth and Brexit dynamics |
| USD/JPY | The yen is a safe haven and the carry-trade barometer; moves can be sharp |

## Emerging-market currencies

- **First to stress**: EM currencies weaken first in risk-off, as capital flees.
- **Leading signal**: EM currency weakness comes before EM equity weakness.
- **Contagion**: EM currencies breaking down signals systemic stress.
- **Flows**: EM inflows and outflows show up in currency moves.

## What drives currencies

### Risk-on and risk-off

- **Risk-on**: USD weak, as capital flows to higher-yielding EM; EM currencies strong.
- **Risk-off**: USD strong on the flight to safety; EM currencies weak.

### Interest rate differentials

- **Fed above ECB**: EUR/USD weaker, as carry trades unwind EUR longs.
- **Fed below ECB**: EUR/USD stronger, as carry trades build EUR longs.
- **Currencies move ahead of rates**: FX prices in rate expectations quickly. See [[Rates]].

### Real rate differentials

- **US real rates above global**: USD rallies on the better real return.
- **US real rates below global**: USD weakens, as the real return looks unattractive. See [[Real Yields]].

## Carry trades and the yen

- **Carry trade**: borrow a low-rate currency (the yen) to buy a high-rate one (AUD, NZD, EM).
- **Carry unwind**: in risk-off, carry trades reverse and currencies move sharply.
- **The yen as a gauge**: the yen rallies in stress; yen weakness signals complacency.
- **EM stress**: EM currency weakness is a red flag for stress ahead.

A saved post from early February 2026 shows how a carry unwind can be traced across assets (from [[X-Bookmarks]]). After a record $10.7B volume day in IBIT, @TheOtherParker_ guessed that one or more Hong Kong-based, non-crypto hedge funds had been running levered IBIT options with capital borrowed in yen. In his account, 10 October "could very well have blown a hole in their balance sheet", yen funding got dearer as "the JPY carry trade has been unwinding at an increasingly rapid pace", the funds "hopped on the Silver trade", and when that blew up a last push down in BTC finished them off. He says himself: "I have no hard evidence here, just some hunches and bread crumbs".

## Reading a currency as cheap or hated

[[goodalexander]]'s checklist for telling whether something is contrarian "without reading any tweets" has tests written for currencies (from [[X-Bookmarks]]):

- **Raw valuation**: "For currencies can consider PPP (it’s cheap to buy stuff in Japan and expensive to buy stuff in Switzerland)".
- **Growth against size**: "For commodities or currencies or crypto can consider demand growth vs market cap dominance".
- **Borrow cost**: in the item on whether people want to bet against something, "For fx if something has a high yield and a low credit default risk this is a reasonable proxy".
- **Macro consensus**: for currencies, look at economic-data expectations for balance-sheet expansion, interest rate cuts, and employment and inflation data.

His conclusion on all 11 factors: they are "a much more reliable guide to positioning than what people say publicly". See [[Positioning]].

## Currency as policy

A long passage in [[CT|Crypto Takes]], linked to a post by @SuitablePolitic, reads US policy through the currency. It says Bessent "was hired to strengthen the dollar by doing the opposite of all the warning signs he saw in the British Pound", where inflated housing and Treasury yields "pointed to the big short that the Soros fund pulled off". Pushing on home prices and bond yields is framed as strengthening "the leading indicators of a currency": "This is what I mean by "white hat hacking" the dollar." The post goes on to claim that tariff volatility deleveraged Wall Street, and that boxing in China could collapse the yuan and send money into US Treasuries. The full argument is on [[Macro Trading]].
