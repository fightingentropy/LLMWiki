# Trading Psychology

Trading psychology here means the behavioural side of trading: why people do it, how price and narrative act on the mind, and the habits that separate traders who keep money from traders who give it back. Most of the material is one-liners from practising crypto traders saved in Erlin's [[CT]] note (the [[Crypto Takes]] page) and the [[Psychology]] note ([[Psychology & Behavior]]), plus posts from [[Business & Finance]], the [[Markets - Trading]] note, the [[Quotes]] archive, two [[Youtube]] clips and process posts kept in [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]]. Everything on this page is attributed opinion from people who trade, not evidence that any of it is profitable. Every post linked below is listed with a description under Resources.

## Why people trade

@SplitCapital asked "Why do you trade? I want to hear real answers, truly." The replies Erlin kept (from [[Crypto Takes]]):

- "It's the greatest game with millions of variables, no single optimal way to play, you can never solve it but you can improve."
- "If you've ever had a fever dream where you're trying to solve something and it's always right out of you reach and you're obsessed, it's like that."
- "mixture of arrogance, issues with authority (didn't want a boss), work hour autonomy, fits my psychology and temperament, upside has no ceiling compared to my degree, downside floor is my degree."
- "Best computer game ever. Pure PvP where the leaderboard is your P&L. The 'level' changes every day. Always something interesting happening."
- "All my life I've had tendency to pick things up, get bored, then put I back down and go do something else. Can't do that w trading cause you can never really master it."
- "i come from a long line of traders. It is my destiny to out trade my father and his father before him"
- "i like trading as I like making music. It's performative, endless, introspective, disciplinary, humbling, and asymmetrically rewarding when done well."
- "cuz i lie to myself and cognitive dissonance tells me i have an edge. sometimes my bank account even reflects it"

[[Rewkang]] gives the structural version of the same answer ([post](https://twitter.com/Rewkang/status/1780351547181789353)): crypto will keep expanding because it beats both casinos and other trading markets on volatility, variety, complexity, social component and "higher odds of developing an edge". "It is simply the most engaging multi player money game." [[GiganticRebirth]] adds the opportunity-cost view: "You should be sacrificing your weekends to give everything you have to harvesting all possible yield out of this market... You will never have such easy opportunities for generational wealth even if you live another 80 years." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

The counter-view comes from [[goodalexander]] ([post](https://twitter.com/goodalexander/status/1771142731462480304)): "'I want to trade so I don't have a boss' — now you have a new boss that's the invisible preferences of a bunch of psychos. he controls your mood, thoughts, wakes you up in the middle of the night, prevents you from traveling or being truly present. congrats on your freedom." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

The ideal temperament, in a line the same note signs "Druckenmiller" (see [[Stanley Druckenmiller]]): "The greatest money making machine in history, a man with Jim Roger's analytical ability, George Soros' trading ability and the stomach of a riverboat gambler."

## News, narrative and price

**The truth of a headline matters less than the reaction to it.** [[GiganticRebirth]] (GCR), 21 May 2021: "When news impacts the price, market participants often fixate on whether it's true or not true. More often than not, the actual veracity of the headline is immaterial. It's how the market reacts to the news, and for how long, that turns out to be a lot more informative." (from [[Business & Finance]]). The [[Markets - Trading]] note says the same thing in its own words, with a timestamped clip attached: "Truth doesn't necessarily move [markets](https://youtu.be/rZ9rUh9WhwU?t=1876). Perception & Uncertainty/Certainty transition does."

**Markets are 6-8 months ahead of the news.** GCR again: "Price moves first, and the 'why' follows. Stocks peak when the best has already been discounted, with fundamentals often deteriorating later as expectations fail to match reality." He adds that "stage analysis" helps in riding trends. (from [[Business & Finance]]). The wiki's gloss: this is why correlation studies mislead; they read the past, not the future. [[HsakaTrades]] compresses it to "The orderflow frontruns the newsflow", attributed as a joke to Marcus Aurelius (from [[Crypto Takes]]).

**Narrative follows price.** [[CL207]], answering "whats the next narrative": "my trader in christ anytime price moves 10% we will literally hallucinate a story up for it." Compare @lightcrypto, who treats narrative as a pre-condition rather than an after-effect: "The market now lacks a viable schelling point narrative. Yet its desire for one is palpable. It wants to make back what it lost. We will suspend disbelief in an effort to do so once a vehicle emerges to carry this latent desire. In the wreckage are nascent roots." Both can be true in different regimes. @Stoiiic's post on the boom-bust sequence and narrative momentum versus price, especially narrative cycle tops, is the longer treatment ([post](https://twitter.com/Stoiiic/status/1749350221925040219)). (all from [[Crypto Takes]])

**The market's job is to force participation.** [[Pentosh1]]: "it moves up to force you to want to buy and down to force you to want to sell. it moves when people are offsides, forcing their participation. there is a lot of game/liquidity theory." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

**Everything is priced in.** The [[Business & Finance]] note keeps a post labelled as a shitpost: "Your very existence was priced in decades ago when the market was valuing Standard Oil's expected future earnings based on population growth that would lead to your birth, what age you would get a car, how many times you would drive your car every week... You have no original thoughts. Your consciousness is just an illusion, a product of the omniscent market. Free will is a myth." It is market theology, tongue-in-cheek, but it carries a truth about how forward-looking pricing works. A line kept in the CT note, unattributed: "It ain't what you don't know that gets you into trouble. It's what you know for sure that just ain't so."

## Reflexivity, flows and cash

**Reflexive pumps start with no sellers, not with buyers.** @diablofutur (from [[Business & Finance]]):

> "Reflexive pumps don't begin with a wave of buyers, they start with no one left willing to sell. A stubborn holder base that refuses to let go triggers a synthetic supply shock. Aggro-rage shorts pile in. A disbelief rally erupts, fuelled by sideline capital waiting on a nuke that never shows. FOMO kicks in, chasing price into thin air, with zero marginal sellers and infinite shorts left to cover. Parabola and attention games begin."

The CT note states the loop in shorthand: "Reflexivity Creates Virtuous and Vicious Cycles. Price → Perception → Fundamentals → Price → Perception → Fundamentals." [[HsakaTrades]] on sell walls, with a joke attribution to Karl Marx: "The sell walls are not there to suppress price. The sell walls are there to give us a chance to show how badly we want higher prices. Because the sell walls are there to stop the people who don't want it badly enough." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

**All-time-high corrections: acute flows versus passive flows.** [[Rewkang]] ([post](https://twitter.com/Rewkang/status/1765075321181929840)): "There is an element of reflexivity around ATH corrections. People sell because they see it's always rejected and then more people follow when it actually happens. Short term acute flows ($10B+ shizo day traders) overwhelm passive flows ($500m+ ETF buying). Eventually, passive flows win out. But there is some appeal of the optionality value of some cash to bid 40%+ wicks on alts." The line under it in the note: "Few more liquidations and then up only." [[LomahCrypto]] on the same fear ([post](https://twitter.com/LomahCrypto/status/1764783649004621961)): "You're scared of longing into Bitcoin ATHs, I understand. Never a great idea to long into a high timeframe resistance but.." (from [[Business & Finance]])

**Cash is an option.** Kris Abdelmessih (@KrisAbdelmessih), replying to @dailydirtnap: "Cash = option. Option value is maximized by vol. Inflation is option premium." Will Clemente ([post](https://twitter.com/WClementeIII/status/1765044203682136559)) argues that "the ability to think for oneself and lessons derived from the exposure to extreme volatility will make Bitcoin/crypto investors/traders some of the most well equipped capital allocators for generations to come." (from [[Business & Finance]])

**Rising volume on a rally is not bullish.** An unattributed note in [[Crypto Takes]]: "Means market is running into sellers, much ammo from buyers being spent. Wouldn't you rather see price floating up on no volume? Nobody willing to sell, buyers less quick to run out of cash."

**Where bottoms form.** @Husslin_ ([post](https://twitter.com/Husslin_/status/1781147687103893882)) lists why certain spots are ideal for bottoms: algos mindlessly closing longs and reducing leverage; liquidations resetting positioning; funds forced to open shorts to manage risk; market makers pulling bids and asks so books are thin; "Ppl afraid to catch knives here". The [second leg up](https://twitter.com/Husslin_/status/1781349288401846364) "is always the slowest grind. There's still supply here from last few days + ppl aren't fully convinced it's a bottom just yet." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

**Liquidations are a transfer, not a cause.** [[fewseethis]]: "cascades occur as a RESULT of prices going down within a levered environment. in other words a cascade is a secondary effect." GCR: "Liquidations are a forced transfer of wealth from impoverished traders who need leverage - to wealthy spot buyers." (from [[Crypto Takes]]; see [[Liquidations]])

## Growing capital versus protecting it

[[cobie]] says a tweet whose author he cannot remember changed his life: "you act too quickly when you are trying to grow capital, and act too slowly when you are trying to protect capital" (from [[Crypto Takes]] and [[Business & Finance]]). The asymmetry explains a large share of losses. Impulsive entries and hesitant exits, and their mirror image of panic selling and hesitant buying, are timing errors rather than analysis errors. [[Capital Allocation Timing]] has its own page.

- "If you spend your time obsessing over old losses you have a high probability of generating new losses." — @BigCheds (from [[Business & Finance]])
- "Do not watch your PnL or NW when you go to war. Just need to know levels where you need to act / add / reduce. Have a plan, dont get emotional." — [[HighStakesCap]] (from [[Crypto Takes]])
- Lord Ashdrake: "What Traders think they like: 'In and out every move, catch everything'. What Traders should be like: 'Sit on my ass, then a few times a year make massive bank then sit on my ass again'." [[cobie]]'s reply: "took me like 8 years to learn this toh" (from [[Crypto Takes]])
- [[0xaporia]] on why poker players start ahead ([post](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/1894332130093629551)): they are dealt a fixed hand in a controlled environment, so they accept variance and fold strategically. "That restraint—knowing you don't need to play every hand—is a massive advantage over people who start out with trading. Novice traders often conflate constant activity with progress. Poker players know this not to be true." (from [[Crypto Takes]])
- Tiny deltas compound. [@kelxyz_](https://x.com/kelxyz_) (from [[Psychology & Behavior]]): "Have a trading plan, but feeling like fomo blasting something from twitter? It's only one trade you say. But like, our life is just an accumulation of these tiny deltas. Most of the time we don't blow up with one catastrophic -100%." The basketball version: miss an open layup, get frustrated, give up an open three; the scoreboard says -3 but the expected-value swing is about -5, and a few of those is a blowout.

## Variance, losing streaks and strategy hopping

[[0xaporia]] ([post](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/1894297482500141122)): "Strategy hopping is one of the most under discussed issues on CT imo. It's mostly a flight from the grueling, nonlinear phase where most progress happens. All strategies, even brilliant ones, operate in a landscape of randomness and friction. When people encounter a losing streak, they interpret it as proof that their strategy is flawed, rather than recognizing that noise is part of the system." His numbers for the probability of a losing streak over 100 trades (from [[Crypto Takes]]):

| Streak length | 50% win rate | 40% win rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 or more | ~80% | ~98% |
| 6 or more | ~55% | ~87% |
| 7 or more | ~32% | ~69% |
| 8 or more | ~17% | ~49% |

"Losing streaks are not rare events. Yet, humans are terrible at internalizing this. We intuitively expect outcomes to balance out quickly, not grasping that streaks are inherent to randomness. A surgeon with a 95% success rate will eventually lose multiple patients in a row. A poker player should focus on whether they made mathematically optimal decisions, not whether they won the hand." His principle: **Decouple Process from Outcome.**

The same idea from other angles:

- **Decision quality is not outcome quality.** Randomness masks poor reasoning and luck masks bad strategy. Only the long run separates signal from noise. See [[Decision-Making]].
- **Every system pays a characteristic cost.** [[CryptoCred]] ([post](https://x.com/CryptoCred/status/2079138151528890777), from [[Markets - Trading]]): trend-following pays through false starts; momentum often enters late and can buy tops or sell bottoms; mean reversion periodically gets overwhelmed by a genuinely outsized move. The recurring failure is abandoning a valid system because its unavoidable cost is mistaken for evidence that the system is broken.
- **Crashes carry no signal.** Alex Krüger studied altcoin crashes greater than 25% in a day using ADA, "the ultimate liquid shitcoin": "crashes may come in the middle of a bull run, at the top, or at the bottom. That is right. There's no signal." (from [[Crypto Takes]])
- **Backtesting can remove clerical work without removing judgment.** [[0xaporia]] ([post](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/2092917529597321394)) separates repetitive implementation from the learning needed to spot look-ahead bias, overfitting and path dependence. Deleting losing weekdays after seeing results, or repeatedly asking an agent to improve the same historical sample, fits noise. Freeze the evaluation protocol, reserve unseen periods, and include execution costs and risk. The quoted profit screenshot is not evidence of a reproducible strategy. (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]])
- @tradebutwhy's poker coach taught game-theory-optimal moves, then played "weird unorthodox exploitative and seemingly crazy things" himself. "Do as I say, not as I do." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

[[Trading Evidence and Review Framework]] sets out how to judge any of these claims against records.

## Positions, mental capital and the reset

**The bag-holding test.** [[CryptoCred]] ([profile](https://x.com/CryptoCred), from [[Crypto Takes]]):

> "The price action of the last week leads to awkward bag-holding. You don't want to sell because it's already down a lot and/or you're tempted to wait for a bounce to reassess. Simple test: 'If I didn't have a position, would I want the exposure I have now?' Yes → Cool, you're fine. No → Do something about it. Active positions cost mental capital, which is a scarce resource. You're less likely to spot and execute the next good trade if the current shit trade is eating away at you."

The corollary: the sunk cost of an underwater bag consumes the bandwidth you need for the next good trade.

**Reset after a large move.** [[CryptoCred]] ([post](https://x.com/CryptoCred/status/2090201763987279967)) warns against revenge shorts, oversized late entries, exact-retest stories, binary exposure, paralysis and five-minute overtrading after missing or opposing a major move. The reset is to restate the signal, the trade type and horizon, the invalidation, the volatility and the size from scratch. Process guidance, not a recommendation to copy the example trade. (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]])

**Name the edge before the trade.** @frankdegods ([post](https://x.com/frankdegods/status/2103860364089708742)) gives four prompts: informational advantage, better analysis, behavioural discipline, and quantitative or execution advantage. For each trade, name the proposed edge and the result that would disprove it. A journal heuristic; the categories overlap and do not establish positive returns. **Do not manufacture trades to relieve boredom.** A post quoting [[jimtalbot]] ([post](https://x.com/lobcrunchwave/status/2103836826729623943)): judge a trade against the setup and the process, not the need for stimulation. If the setup has not changed and only your mood has, record that before adding risk. (both from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]]; the full selection is [[Evergreen Lessons from X - September 2026]])

**Leverage compresses the deadline for being right.** @xydotdot ([post](https://x.com/xydotdot/status/2091158742523863538)) frames leverage as an attempt to shorten the time to a financial target: raising it narrows the acceptable path and timing until being directionally right is no longer enough. Social feeds distort the perceived clock by overrepresenting fast outliers. Liquidation rules, volatility, financing, gaps, correlation, path dependence and total-loss capacity still set the real exposure. See [[Leverage]]. (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]])

**Execution beats public prediction.** A post by @blknoiz06 ([post](https://x.com/blknoiz06/status/2091151900729115029), building on [an 0xaporia post](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/2091034462561706287)): a correct turning-point call can still lose money through sizing, path, leverage or exit errors, while a mediocre entry can support an excellent trade. Calling an exact top or bottom in public is a status game distinct from running a strategy with defined exposure, invalidation, costs and exits. (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]])

**Preserve future rounds.** After a large loss, stabilisation and optionality matter more than repairing the past in one gamble. See [[Repeated Games]].

**Analysis complexity tracks the regime, not the edge.** [[CryptoCred]]: "Up only: *draws random line* looks good. Now: 3 momentum oscillators, 4 footprints, S&P renko chart, Bloomberg Terminal, aggregated spot exchange order book just to scalp 5 ticks on legacy open." And: "I miss simpler times. Before: price go down bad. Now: perp hedges getting unwound into spot sells but Bitfinex CVD is diverging and stablecoin smart money ratio is up while FTX TWAP absorbing." His list of the genre this page belongs to, worth keeping in mind while reading it: "Hindsight risk management post. Vacuous trading psychology post. Vague bullpost about surviving before real bull run. Hindsight did you buy the dip post on green day. Queue 'em up." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

## Cycle psychology

**First-cycle participants.** [[CryptoCred]]: "First cycle participants make money because they're open-minded, optimistic, and think 'What if I'm right?' instead of 'What if I'm wrong?' They lose most of their gains for the same reasons. Navigating cycles requires switching between childlike idealism and sagely cynicism." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

**Bear-market PTSD.** [[CryptoCred]]: "One argument for trading less actively when markets suck is that you don't learn habits that cost you money in bull markets. Bear market PTSD is real. Fading breakouts, rotating too quickly and being unable to hold a position, unbridled cynicism etc. are all hard to unlearn." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

**The back half of a bull run is fear, not greed.** [[Pentosh1]] ([post](https://twitter.com/Pentosh1/status/1779586050597249472)): "The largest gains come in the back half of the bull run as greed completely takes over. The only way to survive is fear at that point... Be fearful, when others are greedy. Greed is the parabola you sell into little by little... Once there are too many bulls, there's no-one left to buy. Then the market dies from dilution... Supply is infinite in this market as we dream up a million new coins a day. The end simply comes when the dilution exceeds the amount of capital inflows." (from [[Crypto Takes]]; see [[Market Cycles]])

**PvP versus PvE.** [[Rewkang]]: "There are certain heuristics that work in PvP market conditions that don't apply as much in PvE market conditions. The most obvious is fading high funding/up only price action. In bull markets funding can stay higher than shorts can remain solvent. The second is the need to rotate. Understand that new money does not schizo trade like crypto traders. They pick their favorite meme coins and just keep sending every paycheck in to continue buying every day/week. Traders that short memecoins because they are 'being rotated out of' eventually will get blown out just as those that faded AXS, ADA, LUNA last cycle. To outperform like retail you need to think like retail." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

**Low-conviction money arrives late and bids the newest thing.** [[cobie]] ([post](https://twitter.com/cobie/status/1483451870152638476)): late entrants offset their lack of belief by bidding only "new projects" so they can be "early", having lacked the conviction to bid at market for anything over the previous five years. "If you don't understand why BTC or why ETH did well, of course it makes sense to bid anything." He compares it to late 2017 and early 2018, when unattractive charts pushed new money into private ICOs. (from [[Crypto Takes]])

**Top signals.** @0xSisyphus: "Normies showing up isn't a top signal. When they start quitting their IRL jobs for crypto or starting funds that's when we need to worry." [[insiliconot]] on bull behaviour in a bear market: moths bumping themselves to death against lamps. Robert Rhea (1935), saved via @WifeyAlpha in [[Business & Finance]], divided bear markets into three phases (abandonment of the hopes the bull market was built on; the reflection of reduced earning power; distress liquidation to meet living expenses), "each of these phases seems to be divided by a secondary reaction which is often erroneously assumed to be the beginning of a bull market." The note adds: the secondary reaction is a bear-market rally. A post saved only under the label "Average bull market trading experience" ([post](https://twitter.com/CryptoUB/status/1773454483931971616)) has no further note. (from [[Crypto Takes]])

## Taking money off the table

[[Pentosh1]], after 2,500+ days of trading every day ([post](https://x.com/Pentosh1/status/1740042952108388405)): "If you make life changing money. Take it out of the market. Many of you will make life changing money, but few will keep it. You likely aren't a genius, but in favorable conditions... There are many experienced traders here that have traded multiple cycles. There are people with more information than you have, better systems, better execution, better everything, access to resources you don't. And over time, your money will eventually start ending up in those pockets... One day the music stops, and you won't have a chair. If you leave all your money on the table, it likely is only temporarily yours." His advice: "Secure it, then start over with just a bit less."

The same author after a first-dip wipeout ([post](https://twitter.com/Pentosh1/status/1779171728100339944)): "It was never yours to begin with... As long as you keep all your money on the table the odds of losing it eventually are around 100%. Like a gambling addict who always wants to play another hand. The only money that is yours is what you take out into the real world. Making money is easy. Keeping money isn't... Take the money from the casino, or someone else will take it from you. That's how the story ends for almost everyone. Greed will eventually cost you everything. Unless you learn to defeat it." (both from [[Crypto Takes]])

[[LomahCrypto]]'s rule, borrowed from @SalsaTekila: any money withdrawn from exchanges never goes back in. "I was annoyed with myself a bit for taking out a bulk of my portfolio in Feb. but hindsight being 20/20, it's probably the only reason I didn't give it all back." And: "leaving the casino is a crazy mindset, ever since the first time cat withdrew from exchange, cat never deposited ever again." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

[[GCRClassic]]'s consolation for losses is determinist: "whatever trading losses any of you have incurred, it never could have been any of other way. Find comfort in this." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

## Keeping perspective

[[cobie]] ([post](https://x.com/cobie/status/1450254997585858560)) on losing the meaning of money inside the bubble: "Sometimes you have a mediocre week & CT feed still curates you the other players' winners. It is easy to get caught up in the stress of outpacing everyone, or keeping up." Then an old coworker visits, always stuck between pay-checks and making choices about weekend trips, houses and kids, "and then I feel grateful and lucky, all of a sudden money has meaning again." @gainzy222 ([post](https://x.com/gainzy222/status/1475000397391712259)) went down the Facebook rabbit-hole of old schoolmates and felt detached from reality: "everyone is staring at an hourglass and the sand is moving too fast for them... and yet for many of us, after the last 2 years... the hourglass is fucking massive, the margin of error allowed to us is huge." His closing line: "i hate how much anxiety this thread creates. what happens if we lose it all". (both from [[Crypto Takes]])

A note in [[Psychology & Behavior]] on a story from [[Chris Williamson]]'s podcast ([clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7oJGvyVU65Y&t=804s)): "unless something is a simple material trade, grinding is prolly the worst thing you can do. better move is to completely detach yourself from the outcomes. it's paradoxical but it's the only thing that will maybe improve your odds of escaping the quicksand."

## Intuition, delusion and overthinking

**Intuitive traders need the information flow.** roon (@tszzl): "intuitive decision makers thrive when they're plugged in and on top of their game: they make preternaturally good calls after processing tons of information. after they grow old and become divorced from the info flow, they try to make confident inferences but will go awry." His follow-up: "imo this is why genius and madness ride so closely and why Nobel laureates, Howard Hughes, elon, etc go insane as time goes on. more careful measured thinkers may be safe from this." (from [[Psychology & Behavior]] and [[Business & Finance]]; see [[Genius and Madness]]). @MatriXBT, replying to [[trading_axe]]: "unironically, my BEST TRADES were PURE INSTINCT based trades. no TA, Orderflow or ANYTHING, NO TOOLS." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

**Overthinking plus underacting.** @TaylinSimmonds ([post](https://x.com/TaylinSimmonds/status/1878819866070679565)): "One pattern I've noticed in all miserable people: They overthink and underact." The note cuts off where his system begins; lines kept elsewhere in the same note say what it amounts to: "you are getting lapped by people 50% dumber than you because they don't overthink shit", "aim. fire. correct later", and "Most successful people I've met are not that smart. They just move fast, take risks and work a lot. Trust me." The system is: just move. Imperfect action beats perfect planning. (from [[Psychology & Behavior]])

**Delusion and soul in the game.** Two [[Youtube]] bookmarks sit next to each other in the trader mindset. The note gives only their titles; the wiki's reading of the pair:

- [Why delusion is important](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pR86kI5aDFA&t=3349s) — useful conviction that runs ahead of the evidence. Intuitive traders who commit before consensus catch moves; the disbelief is the edge. See [[Self-Improvement]] for the general form.
- [Soul in the game](https://youtu.be/a3YOMe4PnKE) — an extension of [[Nassim Taleb]]'s skin in the game: not just risk exposure, but caring about the output beyond personal stake. Edge comes from operators who care, not just operators who are paid.

**The contrarian reflex, taken to parody.** [[trading_axe]]: "When you've been trading for so long, eventually EVERYTHING IN YOUR LIFE becomes an INDICATOR." Too many bananas in other shoppers' carts and he counter-trades them with apples; too many people driving and he walks. (from [[Crypto Takes]])

## Never flex

## Seasonality of US markets

An unattributed excerpt in Erlin's [[Business & Finance]] note, written in the style of a trading-calendar book chapter, gives the annual rhythm:

| Period | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| After 1 January | Volume picks back up and stays seasonally strong |
| May to summer | "Sell in May and Go Away" comes into play as the lazy days of summer approach |
| Week after 4 July | The nadir of trading activity; desks "abandoned for lake cabins, golf courses, or seaside resorts" |
| Last week of August | Exceptionally low volume, often stretching past Labor Day until the September FOMC meeting |
| September | "The year really begins": end of summer, kids back in school, traders back at their desks |
| September FOMC day | One of eight scheduled meetings a year; "sure to see wild market gyrations", especially just after the statement |
| September to Christmas | Activity and volume remain robust, with a brief lull the day after Thanksgiving |
| Christmas to New Year | Relatively low market activity |

Why it matters: "low volume can often lead to unexpected market movements that don't follow typical patterns." See [[Exchange Hours]] for the daily clock.

## What a leaderboard proves

A public leaderboard and a convincing hindsight story can hide what was measured. [[Market Performance Measurement]] asks which accounts, flows, hedges, costs and time windows produce a displayed PnL. @alex_hunter20's quoted attempt to separate protocol contracts, market makers, token deployers, long-term holders and directional traders among the largest Hyperliquid accounts ([post](https://x.com/alex_hunter20/status/2102003574331130349)) is useful method, but identities, holdings, cost bases, hedges and calculated PnL need on-chain and counterparty verification. The Zcash hindsight post ([post](https://x.com/notcelon/status/2096555732543910320)), in which the author says the trade looked obvious in retrospect but was avoided because of its public associations, and the ex-Goldman trader clip relating trading behaviour to personal behaviour ([post](https://x.com/lyemcfly/status/2098904929599467823)) are anecdotes, not execution evidence. (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]])
