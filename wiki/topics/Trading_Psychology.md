# Trading Psychology

Trading psychology is why people trade, how price and narrative work on the mind, and the habits that separate traders who keep money from those who give it back. Most of it comes from crypto traders saved in [[Crypto Takes]]. The principles behind the process are on [[Trading Philosophy]].

## Why people trade

Asked why they trade, one trader answered "Best computer game ever. Pure PvP where the leaderboard is your P&L." Another, more honest: "cuz i lie to myself and cognitive dissonance tells me i have an edge". goodalexander on the freedom it promises:

> "I want to trade so I don't have a boss" — now you have a new boss that's the invisible preferences of a bunch of psychos ... congrats on your freedom

## Narrative follows price

- **The reaction is the news.** GCR: "More often than not, the actual veracity of the headline is immaterial." How the market reacts, and for how long, tells you more. (from [[Business & Finance]])
- **Stories come after.** CL207: "my trader in christ anytime price moves 10% we will literally hallucinate a story up for it" (from [[Crypto Takes]])
- **The market forces participation.** Pentosh1: "it moves up to force you to want to buy / and down to force you to want to sell" (from [[Crypto Takes]])

## Too fast to grow, too slow to protect

cobie credits a half-remembered tweet with changing his life: "you act too quickly when you are trying to grow capital, and you act too slowly when you are trying to protect capital". Impulsive entries and hesitant exits are timing errors, not analysis errors. Small lapses add up; kelxyz_: "our life is just an accumulation of these tiny deltas" (from [[Psychology & Behavior]]). HighStakesCap: "Do not watch your PnL or NW when you go to war."

## Variance and strategy hopping

0xaporia: "Strategy hopping is one of the most under discussed issues on CT imo. It's mostly a flight from the grueling, nonlinear phase where most progress happens." His odds of a losing streak somewhere in 100 trades:

| Streak of | 50% win rate | 40% win rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 or more | ~80% | ~98% |
| 6 or more | ~55% | ~87% |
| 7 or more | ~32% | ~69% |
| 8 or more | ~17% | ~49% |

"Losing streaks are not rare events. Yet, humans are terrible at internalizing this." His rule: **Decouple Process from Outcome.**

## Bag-holding and mental capital

CryptoCred's test for a losing position: "If I didn't have a position, would I want the exposure I have now?" If not, act. "Active positions cost mental capital, which is a scarce resource." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

After missing or fighting a big move, avoid revenge trades and oversized late entries; restate the signal, horizon, invalidation and size from scratch. Never add risk out of boredom.

## Cycle moods

- **First-cycle luck.** CryptoCred: "First cycle participants make money because they're open-minded, optimistic, and think "What if I'm right?" instead of "What if I'm wrong?"" They lose it for the same reasons. (from [[Crypto Takes]])
- **Bear-market scars.** CryptoCred: "Bear market PTSD is real." "Fading breakouts, rotating too quickly and being unable to hold a position, unbridled cynicism etc. are all hard to unlearn" (from [[Crypto Takes]])

## Take money off the table

Pentosh1: "The only money that is yours is what you take out into the real world. Making money is easy. Keeping money isn’t." His advice at life-changing money: "Secure it, then start over with just a bit less." (from [[Crypto Takes]]) LomahCrypto follows a rule from SalsaTekila: money withdrawn from an exchange never goes back in.

## Perspective and silence
