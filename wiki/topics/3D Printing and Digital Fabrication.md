# 3D Printing and Digital Fabrication

Digital fabrication turns a specific physical need into a reproducible object: measure the space, model the part, print it, check the fit. The saves cover each step, though no printer or storage system has been chosen yet. The posts are described in [[Bookmarks - AI & Agents]], [[Bookmarks - Developer Tools]] and [[Bookmarks - Personal & Life]].

## The leads

- **Photo to fitted part.** @dkundel photographed a space with a tape measure in frame, generated a custom model from the photo and printed a doorstop that fit. The tape gives the model its scale; the print is the test. See [[AI Agents]].
- **Printer hardware.** OpenTools Studio is an open printer project; its bill of materials, build difficulty and real-world results are still unknown.
- **Workshop storage.** VAULT is an expandable, printable storage system for woodworking and maker spaces, built on Gridfinity. The model files and licence are not yet identified.
- **Printable robot.** Kame is a compact, open-source quadruped robot with animal-like movement.

## Before printing

- What dimensions and tolerances define success, and how were they measured?
- Which source model, revision, licence, printer and material?
- Does a small prototype fit before committing to a whole storage system?
- What are the print time, material use, strength needs and failure modes?
- Is the editable model kept alongside the exported mesh and the printed result?
