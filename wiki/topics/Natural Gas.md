# Natural Gas

Natural gas is the most seasonal energy market in this wiki. Weather sets demand, storage sets the buffer, and policy and geopolitics shape long-term demand. Erlin's handbook note lists the drivers in a single line; this page sorts them into supply, demand, storage and policy, lays out the seasonal cycle, and draws the trading implications. For the asset class as a whole, see [[Commodities]].

## The handbook's list of drivers

The note files natural gas under commodities, which it sums up as "Boom & bust cycles, cyclical, depend on supply & demand". For gas it gives:

> factors: how much production from major gas fields, how much in storage, decisions by big producers, weather (colder months = more demand), liquified nat gas exports, energy policies, competition from renewables.

(from [[Markets - Handbook]])

## Supply

- **Major gas field production**: Russia (before sanctions), the Middle East, the US.
- **Producer decisions**: large producers manage output to manage price.
- **LNG exports**: liquefied natural gas export capacity and demand.
- **New project development**: Arctic, deepwater, offshore.

## Demand

- **Weather**: winter heating demand spikes with cold.
- **Industrial demand**: fertiliser, chemicals, power generation.
- **Renewable competition**: wind and solar reduce gas-fired power generation.

## Storage

- **Storage levels**: strategic and commercial inventory sit between supply and demand.
- Storage builds through summer and draws through winter, so the level going into winter is the number to watch.

## Policy and geopolitics

- **Energy policy**: support for natural gas versus renewables versus coal.
- **Geopolitical disruption**: Russian sanctions cut European supply.
- **LNG sanctions**: export restrictions or supply disruptions.
- **Climate policy**: long-term pressure from decarbonisation.

## Seasonality

| Season | Demand | Prices | Storage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winter (Oct–Mar) | Peak heating demand | Normally higher | Drawing |
| Summer (Apr–Sep) | Low demand | Normally lower | Building |

The shoulder seasons are the turn between storage building (summer) and storage drawing (winter).

## Trading implications

- **Highly seasonal**: don't short summer gas, and don't go long summer peaks.
- **Weather sensitive**: an early cold winter pulls demand forward and drains storage early, so it hits like a supply shock; an early warm winter is a demand shock.
- **Storage indicator**: monitor storage levels; low storage in winter is a risk.
- **Geopolitically sensitive**: supply disruptions can spike prices 50% or more.
- **Policy sensitive**: the renewable buildout is a structural long-term headwind.

## Where gas fits in the wider read

The daily brief template covers gas inside its commodities read, listed as "oil, gas, metals; macro implications" (from [[Markets - Brief]]). In the cross-asset frame, gas is the seasonal energy market, driven by weather, policy and geopolitics, while [[Oil]] is the cyclical one tied to growth.
