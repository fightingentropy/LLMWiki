# Design Thinking

This page is about how to think about design and taste. [[Design_and_Aesthetics|Design & Aesthetics]] holds the tools, libraries and galleries; this page holds the principles behind choosing among them. The main source is [[Thoughts|Thoughts & Observations]], with craft notes from the design pages. The through-line: taste reveals character, and good design needs tension.

## Taste as confession

> Your aesthetic taste is a map of your inner world projected outward. What you're drawn to reveals which parts of your spirit are awake. Aesthetic taste is an unconscious confession.

(from [[Thoughts]], also quoted in [[Design_and_Aesthetics]])

Taste is character revelation: what someone is drawn to, and what they build, shows who they are.

The same note applies the idea to a person, as a blind spot with strategic cost:

> Geniuses have blind spots, one of Elon's is beauty. He has bad taste and doesn't understand the power or political utility of image. It's why the women are mid, the logos are cringe, the jokes are bad, etc.

(from [[Thoughts]])

The reading: aesthetic failure is a strategic vulnerability, because image carries political utility.

## Beauty needs tension

Strong aesthetic lives on the edge of something uncomfortable. Safe beauty reads as stock photography; beauty with tension sticks. The note comes from the "Wicked Game" entry in [[Design_and_Aesthetics]]: a cover video whose creator describes a deliberate juxtaposition of beauty and toxicity.

## Taste before technique

Getting good at design takes aesthetic sensitivity, not just technical ability. The study list in [[Design_and_Aesthetics]]:

- Ralph Lauren, for brand identity.
- Tadao Ando, for architecture and minimalism.
- Motion design studios.
- Typography.

The design note also keeps two links under the bare label "taste" (listed under Resources).

## Systems, motion and story

- **Component thinking.** Break designs into the smallest reusable units. Scalable systems beat one-off designs. (from [[Design_and_Aesthetics]])
- **Intentional motion.** Movement, animation and micro-interactions should feel intentional, not decorative. Design-engineering craft is not the same as animation flourish; one saved post puts it as "design engineering is not animations". (from [[Design_and_Aesthetics]])
- **Narrative packaging.** Design is a vehicle for a story. A line in the note:

> storytelling is the ultimate compression algorithm for human attention. everything else, data, logic, tech feeds into it, but if you can't wrap it in a compelling narrative, nobody will give a shit. people think they make decisions based on facts, but they're mostly responding to the shape of a story, whether it's a personal arc, a company vision, or a product pitch.
> if you want to accomplish anything meaningful, the story is the interface between it & the world. get that wrong & nobody will give af. get it right & you bend steel with your bare hands.

(from [[Thoughts]]; see also [[Philosophy_and_Meaning]])

## Let the user manipulate the explanation

A camera-focus lab suggests a reusable interaction pattern for teaching: expose a parameter, show its consequence, and make the assumptions and readouts visible. A person learns the mechanism by changing it. The teaching lesson is a synthesis drawn from one example; the lab's physical accuracy and the post's claims about the model, cost and one-shot generation are unverified. (from [[Bookmarks - Design & Frontend]])

The wider set of lessons drawn from saved posts is in [[Evergreen Lessons from X - September 2026]].
