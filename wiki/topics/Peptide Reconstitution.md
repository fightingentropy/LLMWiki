# Peptide Reconstitution

Reconstitution means dissolving a freeze-dried (lyophilised) medicine in a specified liquid, the vehicle or diluent, before use. The right vehicle, concentration, storage conditions and use-by period depend on the actual formulation and its validated instructions. Erlin's notes hold one detailed reconstitution guide, written for the investigational drug [[Retatrutide]] and kept in full on [[Peptides]]. This page sets out what that guide can and cannot be trusted for.

None of this is medical advice; an injectable product's preparation belongs to its manufacturer, pharmacist or prescriber.

## What the saved guide covers

The guide on [[Peptides]] walks from powder to injection: supplies, dilution charts, a step-by-step mixing method, storage tables, an injection quick guide, a titration schedule and a list of common mistakes. It opens with its own disclaimer that retatrutide is an investigational compound in Phase 3 trials and not FDA-approved. (from [[Peptides]])

Its handling advice is mostly about not damaging the peptide or contaminating the vial: bring the vial to room temperature, swab the stoppers, run the water down the glass wall rather than onto the powder, roll or swirl instead of shaking, and discard a cloudy solution or one with floating particles. The guide's specific numbers are where its support runs out (see the correction below).

## The arithmetic

The guide's concentration formula is plain arithmetic (from [[Peptides]]):

- Concentration = peptide amount ÷ water added. For example, 10 mg in 2 mL gives 5 mg/mL.
- On a U-100 insulin syringe, 100 units = 1 mL.

A numerical concentration calculation fixes only the concentration. It says nothing about the product's identity, purity, sterility or stability.

## What the bacteriostatic water label supports

The [Bacteriostatic Water prescribing label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display) describes 0.9% benzyl alcohol and directs users to the drug manufacturer's instructions. It notes that compatibility varies. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

- Benzyl alcohol is a preservative. It is not proof of stability and not a universal solvent choice.
- A preservative does not validate a universal 28-day shelf life for every reconstituted drug.
- The guide itself flags benzyl alcohol sensitivity in a small percentage of people. (from [[Peptides]])

See [[Bacteriostatic Water]].

## Retatrutide's regulatory status

The [FDA states](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss) that retatrutide is not a component of an FDA-approved drug and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. That statement is about US regulatory status, not every country's approvals. Research-vendor products and a saved injection guide do not establish clinical suitability, identity, purity or formulation stability. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

## Corrections to the guide

**Correction, 22 September 2026:** the saved [[Retatrutide]] guide does not generalise to injectable research peptides in general. Its exact potency-loss percentages (for shaking, freezing and room-temperature storage), its blanket 28-day shelf life and its titration schedule have no formulation-specific evidence. No supporting stability study is attached to those figures, and they should not be treated as established facts. The original text remains on [[Peptides]]. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

Correction, 16 October 2026, on the guide's 28-day (BAC water) and 24-hour (sterile water) storage figures: the [Bacteriostatic Water label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display) says to use a reconstituted drug promptly and not to store it unless the drug's own manufacturer directs otherwise. The 28-day figure is the USP <797> in-use limit for an opened manufactured multiple-dose vial, [as described by the CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/injection-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/index.html), not a shelf life for a peptide mixed in it. The stability studies and approved tirzepatide label cited on [[Bacteriostatic Water]] do not support the guide's shaking, freezing and room-temperature percentages.

## Evidence required before trusting a protocol

1. Identify the regulated product and its formulation.
2. Use its manufacturer or pharmacy instructions for the diluent, preparation, route, storage and expiry.
3. Clinical-trial material has its own controlled protocol; follow that, not a general guide.

A research seller's label and a numerical concentration calculation do not supply these missing assurances. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]]) [[Health Evidence]] records the standard used when adding clinical claims.
