# Peptide Reconstitution

Reconstitution means dissolving a freeze-dried (lyophilised) drug in a liquid before injection. The one guide saved here, "Retatrutide Reconstitution Guide: From Powder to Injection", is for retatrutide, an unapproved weekly injection. Its arithmetic is sound; its storage, potency and dosing figures are not backed. An injectable's preparation, dose and storage should come from its manufacturer, pharmacist or prescriber.

[Read the original note](/source/0ff64277cff7323551e6)

## Retatrutide and its status

Retatrutide (LY3437943) is Eli Lilly's triple-agonist obesity drug, injected under the skin once a week.

- The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss) says it is not a component of any FDA-approved drug and has not been found safe and effective. It cannot be used in compounding under US federal law, and research-labelled GLP-1 products are of unknown quality.
- [Lilly](https://lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide) says no regulator has approved it. In the phase 3 [TRIUMPH-1](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-powerful-weight-loss-in-pivotal-phase-3-obesity-trial) trial, 2,339 adults lost 28.3% of body weight on average at the top dose over 80 weeks, against 2.2% on placebo. TRIUMPH-2 and -3 met their goals.
- Lilly plans to file with the FDA in early 2027 and says the drug [may not legally be sold](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-substantial-weight-loss-and-a1c-reduction-underscoring-its-potential-promise-for-people-with-obesity-and-type-2-diabetes-302891798.html) for human use. No filing or approval had been reported by 16 October 2026.
- A vendor's vial and a correct dilution say nothing about identity, purity, sterility or a safe dose.

## The dilution maths

```text
Concentration (mg/mL) = peptide in vial (mg) ÷ water added (mL)
Units to inject       = dose (mg) ÷ concentration (mg/mL) × 100
```

On a U-100 insulin syringe, 100 units = 1 mL, as [insulin labels](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b519bd83-038c-4ec5-a231-a51ec5cc291f) confirm. The guide's "Most Popular Setup" is a 10 mg vial plus 2 mL of bacteriostatic water: 5 mg/mL, so a 4 mg dose is 80 units.

| Vial | Water added | Concentration | Units per 1 mg |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mg | 1 mL | 5 mg/mL | 20 |
| 5 mg | 2 mL | 2.5 mg/mL | 40 |
| 10 mg | 1 mL | 10 mg/mL | 10 |
| 10 mg | 2 mL | 5 mg/mL | 20 |
| 30 mg | 3 mL | 10 mg/mL | 10 |
| 30 mg | 6 mL | 5 mg/mL | 20 |

Doses over 100 units need splitting or a bigger syringe. A vial lasts its size divided by the weekly dose: 10 mg covers five weeks at 2 mg.

- Correction (16 October 2026): the guide lists a 12 mg dose drawn from a 10 mg vial, which holds only 10 mg.
- Correction (16 October 2026): its doses-per-vial table runs up to 30 weeks, past its own 28-day use-by date for a mixed vial. Its tip is to reconstitute only what you will use in 3–4 weeks.

## Mixing and injecting, per the guide

Warm the vial and water for 15–20 minutes. Swab both stoppers. Run the water down the glass wall over 10–20 seconds, never onto the powder. Roll or swirl, never shake. Discard anything cloudy or with particles. Label the concentration and date, and refrigerate. Inject under the skin once a week, rotating abdomen, thigh and upper arm.

The guide's titration runs 1 mg weekly for weeks 1–4, then 2, 4, 6 and 9 mg in four-week steps, reaching 12 mg from week 21. Correction (16 October 2026): Lilly's phase 3 trials started at 2 mg, not 1 mg ([Lilly](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-successful-in-two-additional-phase-3-obesity-trials-delivering-significant-improvements-in-weight-and-a1c)).

## Bacteriostatic water and storage

Bacteriostatic Water for Injection is sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol as a preservative. Its [label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display) says to follow the drug maker's instructions, warns of incompatibilities and of benzyl-alcohol toxicity in newborns, and says to use a reconstituted drug promptly.

The guide claims BAC water is "NOT sterile water"; a mixed vial keeps "Up to 28 days" refrigerated, or 24 hours with plain sterile water; shaking "reduces potency 30–50%"; room temperature "Loses 10–20% potency per day"; and in a frozen vial "Ice crystals destroy peptide bonds". It adds that some people react to benzyl alcohol and should mix with sterile water for each dose.

- Correction (16 October 2026): BAC water is itself sterile; "NOT sterile water" only separates it from preservative-free Sterile Water for Injection.
- Correction (16 October 2026): 28 days is the USP <797> limit for an opened multiple-dose vial, [per the CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/injection-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/index.html), not a shelf life for a peptide mixed from one.
- Correction (16 October 2026): [shaking](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35290820/) and [freezing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17963151/) can damage protein drugs, but no source gives the guide's percentages or says ice breaks peptide bonds. Approved peptides set their own limits: [Zepbound](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=487cd7e7-434c-4925-99fa-aa80b1cc776b) forbids freezing but allows 21 days unrefrigerated.
- Correction (22 September 2026): the guide covers the one product it describes; nothing supports applying it to other research peptides.
