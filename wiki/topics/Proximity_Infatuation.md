# Proximity Infatuation

Proximity infatuation is the observation that sustained physical closeness manufactures perceived attraction, independent of actual compatibility. The notes name it explicitly, under a saved meme about coworkers. It is the coworker-crush effect generalised: feelings that seem to belong to a person may belong to a schedule and a shared space.

## The coworker effect

> ur coworker is not Hot they’re just within 10 ft of u 40 hrs/week

The note labels this "proximity infatuation" ([post](https://x.com/Nia_mp4/status/1869907388557340785) by @Nia_mp4; from [[Psychology|Psychology & Behavior]], also in [[X Takes]] and echoed in [[Women]]).

- **Context creates perceived attraction.** Sustained proximity produces what feels like chemistry but is repeated exposure (from [[Psychology|Psychology & Behavior]]).
- **Remove the proximity.** [[Dating_and_Relationships|Dating & Relationships]] draws the practical rule: remove the proximity and the attraction often evaporates, so recognise the illusion and don't build futures on it.

## Proximity as kryptonite

A take saved in [[Women]] applies the effect to women specifically:

> You can have the most profoundly intricate connection for six months straight, disappear for two weeks, and she's already met a guy whose made her feel everything she felt with you in the span of a day. Women are very fickle, very in the moment.
>
> Proximity is their kryptonite..

See [[Gender_Dynamics|Gender Dynamics]] for the wider set of takes on how men and women differ in attraction.

## Implications

- **For dating.** Being in the room matters more than being the right person. Geographic and venue choice is upstream of romantic outcomes.
- **For self-knowledge.** Feelings attributed to a specific person may be feelings attributed to a specific schedule and space.
- **For where you live.** The same mechanism powers the claim, in [[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]] and [[Culture_and_Environment|Culture and Environment]], that you are shaped by places more than people. A [post](https://x.com/tishray/status/2031783721397809397) by @tishray saved in [[X Takes]] puts it this way: "You're not just the average of the 5 people closest to you. You're the average of the 5 places, the 5 routines, and the 5 inputs you're exposed to most." It describes "New values absorbed through proximity instead of effort" and concludes that choosing where you live "is one of the most important decisions you'll ever make."
