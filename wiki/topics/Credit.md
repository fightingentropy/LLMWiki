# Credit

Credit is debt that carries default risk: corporate bonds, loans, mortgages and private credit. It is often where tail risk emerges first. Credit markets are less liquid than equities and can dislocate sharply in stress. The daily market-overview template gives credit its own slot in the cross-asset read: IG/HY spreads, refinancing stress and defaults (from [[Markets - Brief]]). This page covers the metrics to watch, the credit cycle, the credit-equity warning signal, mortgage prepayment, and saved case studies.

## Credit spreads

A spread is the extra yield a borrower pays over Treasuries. It is the market's price for default risk.

- **IG spreads**: investment-grade corporate bonds vs. Treasuries; 100–200bps is normal.
- **HY spreads**: high-yield (junk) bonds; 400–600bps is normal.
- **Spread widening**: risk-off. Spreads widen on flight to safety and default fears.
- **Spread tightening**: risk-on. Spreads tighten on complacency and risk appetite.

Rising real yields typically widen spreads as default risk is priced in; falling real yields tighten them (see [[Real Yields]] and [[Rates]]).

## Stress indicators

- **High-yield spreads > 600bps**: stress signal; some defaults likely.
- **IG spreads > 200bps**: early warning; not extreme, but elevated.
- **Credit default swap spreads**: derivative pricing of default risk.
- **Lending standards**: banks tightening credit means the economy is weakening.

## Credit events

- **Refinancing stress**: maturing debt rolling over at higher rates.
- **Covenant breaches**: companies failing to meet the terms of their credit agreements.
- **Rating downgrade**: signals deteriorating credit quality.
- **Default**: the company cannot pay; the holder loses value.

## The credit cycle

| Phase | What happens | Risk or opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boom | Low spreads, strong earnings, easy refinancing. New issues priced easily. Leveraged buyouts and acquisitions. | Leverage builds without recognition. |
| Peak | Spreads compress to lows. Earnings growth slowing but valuations highest. Refinancing at "best" rates. | Vulnerable if anything breaks. |
| Bust | Earnings miss expectations. Default forecasts rising. Refinancing becomes difficult and expensive. | Spreads widen; a credit crunch develops. |
| Trough | Spreads wide, values attractive. Defaults occurring. Fear at extremes. | Opportunity: credit recovers. |

Where the credit cycle sits is one input to the wider regime call in [[Market Regime]] and [[Macro Trading]].

## Credit-equity divergence

When equities hold up but spreads widen, that is a warning sign:

- The market is repricing default risk.
- Equities have not caught up to the credit repricing.
- An equity correction is likely ahead.

See [[Cross-Asset Analysis]] for credit-equity-rate relationships, [[Equities]] for the equity side, and [[Risk Analysis]] for credit tail-risk scenarios.

## Mortgage prepayment and refinancing

The contractual right to repay a mortgage early can give the borrower a refinancing opportunity if rates fall, while creating reinvestment or prepayment risk for the lender. Fees, penalties and the borrower's ability to qualify for a new loan constrain its value. The cited [Federal Reserve staff paper](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2012/201222/201222pap.pdf) concerns US fixed-rate conforming mortgages; the [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-prepayment-penalty-en-1957/) confirms that some loans have prepayment penalties. This is a contractual concept, not a universal mortgage-buying rule (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]], via a [post by @liensofnewyork](https://x.com/liensofnewyork/status/2104717103508967657)).

Refinancing pain is also the subject of a [misleading Bank of England mortgage explanation](https://x.com/TJiMTS/status/2095425688698290214). The post argues that a bank borrowed the mortgage principal from the Bank of England in 2021 and simply charges a higher rate in 2027. The mechanics are wrong: commercial-bank lending creates a deposit, and mortgage pricing also reflects Bank Rate expectations, deposit and wholesale funding, hedging, capital, credit, term, competition and margin (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]]).

## Collateral and private credit

- **Inventory as collateral**: Italian lenders use maturing PDO Parmigiano Reggiano wheels as collateral, matching credit to an inventory asset that needs a long ageing period before sale. The bank can monitor, maintain, value and sell standardized stock if a borrower defaults. It is a concrete asset-backed-lending case.
- **Private-credit defaults**: the MFS and Atlas case involves warehouse financing, alleged double pledging, defaults and litigation.

Both come from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]]; the links are below.
