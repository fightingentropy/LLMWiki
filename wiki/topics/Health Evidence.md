# Health Evidence

Saved health posts often claim more than their research shows. This page is a method for weighing such claims, the traps that recur, and every correction made to the health notes. The posts and studies are in [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]]. Medication, procedures and screening decisions belong with a clinician.

[Read the original note](/source/810486fe40bd01a69b82) (medical), and the evidence notes of [22 September 2026](/source/e5b8d4a6eb9efdbcaa02) and [16 October 2026](/source/9bb26978b70193fd749b).

## The evidence ladder

1. A clip or personal report is a lead, not proof that something works.
2. A lab, ex-vivo or animal result says nothing yet about people.
3. A pilot or single patient shows feasibility; safety, dose and comparison stay open.
4. A randomized trial covers only its population, intervention, comparator, duration and outcomes.
5. Choosing a product still turns on contraindications, formulation, dose, monitoring and the person.

Examples: a bleach sinus-rinse anecdote (1); gum that clumped HPV in saliva samples (2); a one-infant gene-editing case (3); water before meals adding modest weight loss to a diet (4).

## Traps that recur

- **Association read as cause.** The 96% push-up figure, cannabis and DNA methylation, body-odour disgust and xenophobia, and mind-wandering and unhappiness are all associations.
- **A narrow truth stretched wide.** Low mood can overlap with low testosterone, but diagnosis needs symptoms plus consistently low morning levels ([Endocrine Society](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/testosterone-therapy)). Depression can blunt memory ([NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression)) without meaning permanent brain damage.
- **Anecdote against the trial.** Intralymphatic allergy immunotherapy barely beat placebo in a double-blind trial, and a small pilot had two anaphylaxis cases. Red light has evidence for some skin ageing and hair loss, by device ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/safety/red-light-therapy)), not for mood or energy.
- **One harm swapped for another.** Smoking does not prevent long COVID: the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/long-covid/about/index.html) says anyone infected can develop it. "Drink less, smoke more" trades one carcinogen for another.
- **Protocols not to copy.** Household bleach in a sinus rinse is not the device-made hypochlorous acid studied in sinusitis; [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/rinsing-your-sinuses-neti-pots-safe) uses distilled, sterile or boiled water.
- **A ranking where there is a choice.** Tazarotene beat tretinoin on some photoageing measures, with more irritation; it is not simply "stronger".

## Still open

- No methods or outcomes: a three-day "dopamine reset", antihistamines for "ADHD brains", imagined contractions building strength, resisting peak cravings to "rewire".
- Repetitive negative thinking and Alzheimer's markers: an association; Marchant et al. (2020) is the likely study.
- A post selling purpose as the cure for depression is motivation, not a clinical answer.
- The Medical note's takes on testosterone, cocaine users' looks and overprescribed SSRIs carry no evidence.

## Corrections

Each also sits on its page.

- **Fluoride** (this page, 22 September 2026): "Fluoride competes with iodine at thyroid receptor sites" has no clinical support; the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/how-to-keep-your-teeth-clean/) advises 1,350 ppm fluoride toothpaste twice daily.
- **Sunlight and dopamine** ([[Circadian Rhythm]], 22 September 2026): the tyrosine-hydroxylase step needs tyrosine, oxygen and tetrahydrobiopterin, not sunlight.
- **ADHD** ([[ADHD]], 22 September 2026): the circadian paper describes a subgroup and rejects calling ADHD circadian.
- **Push-ups** ([[Fitness]], 22 September 2026): the 96% figure is an association in 1,104 male firefighters.
- **High reps** ([[Fitness]], 16 October 2026): light loads taken to failure build muscle like heavy ones.
- **Testosterone** ([[Fitness]], 16 October 2026): sleep drives most release; leg days raise it briefly; the sun link is observational.
- **Creatine** ([[Body Optimization]], 16 October 2026): AIS Group A for sport, not a research lead.
- **Magnesium** ([[Body Optimization]], 16 October 2026): probably effective for migraine; weak for sleep; few trials for hearing.
- **Vitamin C** ([[Body Optimization]], 16 October 2026): the figures are treadmill fat burning in a tiny study, not fat loss.
- **Blueberries** ([[Body Optimization]], 16 October 2026): better blood-vessel function, no effect on executive function or mood.
- **Cold exposure** ([[Body Optimization]], 16 October 2026): the dopamine rise took an hour at 14 °C; no mood effect in trials.
- **Tongkat ali** ([[Body Optimization]], 16 October 2026): raises testosterone, mainly in low-T men; the pomegranate fix is unsupported.
- **Swimming rats** ([[Mental Health]], 16 October 2026): Richter's quick deaths and 40–60-hour swims were whisker-trimmed rats; intact rats swam 60–80 hours.
- **Mind-wandering** (this page, 16 October 2026): pleasant topics did not lower happiness; neutral and unpleasant ones did ([Science, 2010](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1192439)).
- **RO pricing** ([[Water Filtration]], 16 October 2026): "almost the same price" excludes installation.
- **BAC water** ([[Peptide Reconstitution]], 16 October 2026): it is sterile; 28 days dates an opened multi-dose vial, not a mixed peptide.
- **Peptide guide** ([[Peptide Reconstitution]], 22 September and 16 October 2026): no source gives its shaking, heat or freezing losses, or extends it to other peptides.
- **Dosing tables** ([[Peptide Reconstitution]], 16 October 2026): a 10 mg vial cannot give 12 mg, some vials outlast 28 days, and trials started at 2 mg.
