# Health Evidence

The latest health bookmarks are most useful as an evidence-discipline page. They repeatedly contrast a vivid post or before-and-after image with the narrower question the underlying research can actually answer.

## Evidence ladder

1. A clip or personal report is a lead, not efficacy evidence.
2. An ex-vivo or animal result does not establish a clinical outcome in people.
3. A small pilot can establish feasibility while leaving safety, dose, and comparative effectiveness unresolved.
4. A randomized trial supports only its population, intervention, comparator, duration, and measured outcomes.
5. Product choice still depends on contraindications, formulation, dose, monitoring, and individual context.

## Examples in the current batch

- Tazarotene may outperform tretinoin on selected photoageing measures in some formulations, with more early irritation; this is not a universal ranking across every indication or patient.
- Red-light therapy has indication- and device-specific evidence, not proof of universal benefits for mood, recovery, energy, and health.
- A bleach-in-sinus-rinse anecdote is not a protocol. Device-generated hypochlorous acid research does not validate improvised sodium-hypochlorite mixtures.
- Smoking does not prevent long COVID, and a population-risk analogy does not justify substituting cigarettes for alcohol.
- Intralymphatic immunotherapy has mixed controlled evidence and reported systemic reactions; an anecdotal improvement is a specialist discussion lead, not a cure.
- Bioengineered gum aggregating HPV or selected bacteria ex vivo does not show that ordinary chewing clears infection or prevents cancer.
- UK prostate-cancer incidence is not, by itself, an argument for universal PSA screening; overdiagnosis and downstream harm remain part of the decision.

## Psychology and behavior

Limerence is a descriptive concept, not an official diagnosis. ADHD-related hyperfocus may be one vulnerability, but reciprocal knowledge of a real person should be separated from a checking-and-rumination loop built around an imagined relationship. See [[ADHD]] and [[Mental_Health]].

A randomized mobile-internet blocking study reported improvements in subjective well-being and sustained attention, but compliance was difficult and the intervention preserved calls, texts, and internet access on other devices. The result supports testing attention boundaries, not claims of literal rejuvenation.

## September 2026 contradictions and unresolved claims

- The circadian ADHD source is a perspective about a subgroup; see the corrected [[ADHD]] and [[Circadian_Rhythm]] pages.
- Positive and negative stimulant accounts are preserved together as individual experiences, not a universal verdict on medication.
- The three-day cure and antihistamine-for-ADHD posts remain unverified claims. A product or vendor reference is not clinical evidence.
- Repetitive negative thinking and Alzheimer's biomarkers is an association claim pending the underlying study. It does not establish that worrying causes dementia.
- Imagined muscle contractions, wearable-guided physical therapy, and craving “rewiring” need specific methods and outcomes before practical conclusions.
- The September source rejects purpose as a universal depression cure, contradicting earlier absolute wording in [[Mental_Health]]. Retention of the motivational post does not settle that clinical question.
