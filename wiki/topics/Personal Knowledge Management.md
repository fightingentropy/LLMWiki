# Personal Knowledge Management

Two stores work together. Topical bookmark notes hold selected references, from AI & Agents to Personal & Life; this wiki turns them into pages where claims, tools and contradictions are easy to find again. These are the rules for what to keep, how to distil it and when to let it go.

## Keep what lasts

- **Evergreen only.** Keep things still worth knowing in a few years. Leave out release news, hype and disposable posts. What survives from X is on [[Evergreen Lessons from X]].
- **Keeping is not endorsing.** A saved post is a lead, not a finding. Label claims as claims.
- **Old sources stay intact.** A new rule changes what gets added, not what earlier notes kept.

## From source to reusable knowledge

- Keep the source untouched and save new versions beside it.
- Separate the date something was saved from the date its claims were checked.
- Keep a correction next to the claim it corrects rather than erasing the trail.
- Give a tool or idea its own page once there is enough to say; until then, leave an unidentified screenshot or bare link marked as unresolved.
- Fold each insight into the topic it belongs to instead of a dated pile.

## Read deeply, and expect decay

Seneca's second letter to Lucilius warns that moving constantly among many books keeps ideas from taking root; a modernised screenshot of it is saved in [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]]. A short post by @eigenrobot adds that adult life shows how fast knowledge loses currency. Together: revisit the notes that matter, and re-check anything that depends on current products, prices, research or events. A third thesis holds that sustained reading may become a visible marker of agency as feeds erode attention.

## Collecting is not using

[@signulll](https://x.com/signulll) on the productivity-tool economy:

> we've built an entire shadow economy around the simulation of work rather than work itself.

> the more you consume these meta-tools, the more you train yourself to exist in a preparatory state instead of an executory one.

He calls it "a kind of paralysis disguised as momentum" and "an intelligence trap—spending more time honing the system than actually using it." The test for any store of notes is whether its contents get revisited, corrected and used. Passive input fails it fastest: "There's almost zero alpha in listening to podcasts. You're not getting smarter doing this, it just feels like you are." _(@growing_daniel)_

## How long a link should live

Different links have different lifetimes. A saved account portal does not mean setup is done, and a watch-later link does not mean it was watched.

| Kind of link | Keep it until | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account portal | The account or agreement closes | Argos Pay, V12 Finance |
| Setup or activation link | Installation is complete | Hyperoptic Getting Connected |
| Product shortlist | A purchase decision is made | UniFi Express 7, Hommix Sparo |
| Reading or watch queue | It is read and any lasting point distilled | an FT long read, a YouTube recommendation |
| One-off how-to | Its conclusion has been acted on | Stremio on AltStore |
| Idea collection | It becomes another endless queue | Billion Dollar Tweets |
| Durable principle | Never; re-read it | Seneca on scattered reading |

For consoles and utilities, keep the stable entry point and drop brittle deep links: the Twilio Console rather than regional API-key pages, the SearXNG instance directory rather than one instance. The links themselves live in [[Bookmarks - Personal & Life]] and [[Bookmarks - Reference & Utilities]].
