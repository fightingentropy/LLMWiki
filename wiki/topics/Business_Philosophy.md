# Business Philosophy

Business philosophy here means the principles about how businesses create and preserve value, as distinct from the tactics on [[Business_Strategy]]. Erlin's notes draw them from Jeff Bezos's last shareholder letter as Amazon CEO, the Chinese "catfish effect", a few one-line investing takes, and the entropy/extropy frame from political philosophy. Most business and market detail lives in [[Business_Strategy]] and [[Markets]]; this page holds the overlays that cut across them: distinctiveness, managed competition, and stagnation as the bigger risk. [[Culture|Culture & Observations]] points here for the catfish framing.

## Managed competition, not disruption

- China's [[Catfish_Effect]]: introduce curated outside pressure, such as Tesla in China, to shake domestic players out of complacency (from [[Culture|Culture & Observations]], [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]]).
- It differs from Western disruptive-innovation frameworks. The aim is deliberate agitation, not destruction, because stagnation is the bigger risk.
- The [catfish post](https://x.com/ruima/status/1937720853829157027) saved in the note calls it "managed agitation" and puts the rationale plainly: "let’s introduce a little stress on purpose, because stagnation is the bigger risk here." _(@ruima)_
- In China, the post says, competition "can be curated, introduced, even welcomed", to get domestic players moving again rather than to destroy them.

## Distinctiveness as active work

Bezos's last annual shareholder letter as Amazon CEO is saved in [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]] under its heading "Differentiation is Survival and the Universe Wants You to be Typical". Its argument, condensed: the world wants you typical; democracies aren't normal and tyranny is the historical norm; it takes continuous work to maintain distinctiveness; the world pulls at you in a thousand ways (from [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]], [[Business_Strategy]]). See [[Jeff Bezos]].

- "Democracies are not normal. Tyranny is the historical norm."
- "The world wants you to be typical - in a thousand ways, it pulls at you. Don't let it happen."
- The letter's illustration is a happily married couple's running joke: the husband asks the wife, "Can't you just be normal?" Her distinctiveness is what he loves, even though being more normal would take less energy.
- Its warning against the fairy-tale version of "be yourself": distinctiveness is worth it, but it is never easy or free, and the energy has to be paid continuously.

## Entropy and the pull toward equilibrium

- All companies face pressure to merge into equilibrium with their environment. The letter takes the image from a passage in Richard Dawkins's *The Blind Watchmaker*: a living body keeps its temperature, water content and chemistry different from its surroundings only by constant work, and when the work stops it settles into equilibrium, which is death.
- Amazon's challenge, in the letter, is to stay distinct despite that pull. Bezos extends it to all companies, all institutions and each individual life.
- The frame mirrors [[Curtis_Yarvin]]'s extropy: organised complexity needs energy input or it decays. Yarvin's version, kept in [[Culture|Culture & Observations]]: "Fundamentally it's entropy versus extropy." See [[Entropy_vs_Extropy]].

## Quality before entry price

> "it's far better to buy a bussin company at a sus price than a sus company at a bussin price, fr fr"

(from [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]])

Fundamentals beat entry price, but entry price sets the margin of safety.

## Overearning and value extraction

- A thread in the note, replying to @buccocapital, starts from AirBnB's degrading brand perception. The diagnosis: a "classic situation where fundamentals deteriorate with improving financials", because the company is overearning and consuming the entire consumer surplus (from [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]]).
- The thread adds that this is acute in tech, where venture capital usually subsidises consumer surplus for a decade.
- A further reply adds regulatory risk: the strategy is to exploit legal loopholes to scale, then try to change policy once big enough.
- The note's one-liner on where value comes from: "All profit comes from margins." See [[Economics]].

## Speed: growth versus protection

- The lifecycle reading kept on [[Business_Strategy]]: act quickly to grow, slowly to protect, and most people reverse these.
- Contradiction: the tweet behind this idea names acting too quickly while growing and too slowly while protecting as the mistake, which points the other way. [[Capital_Allocation_Timing]] follows the tweet's literal reading.
