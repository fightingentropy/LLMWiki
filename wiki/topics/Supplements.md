# Supplements & Biohacking

Erlin's [[Body]] and [[Medical|Medical & Health]] notes mix public-health guidance, supplement leads and mechanisms that no attached source supports. This page sorts them into three groups: guidance with an official source, claims still awaiting evidence, and claims that were checked and found wrong. Judge each claimed outcome on its own. Not medical advice: check suitability, dose and interactions with a clinician or pharmacist before starting anything.

## Supported guidance and its scope

- **Vitamin D:** the [NHS vitamin D guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/) advises adults to consider 10 micrograms daily in autumn and winter. Some groups are advised to take it year-round. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])
- This is a specific public-health recommendation. It does not support the rest of the stack it sits beside in the note.

## The stack in the notes

The core stack, as the [[Body]] note states it:

> "Vitamin D3 supplementation: largely accepted nowadays. Creatine: starting to break through. Next: Magnesium (Threonate or Bisglycinate). Why? Well for starters, Mg can protect against hearing loss, solid data on reducing migraines & it can improve sleep."
>
> "30-60min before bed."

Other supplement lines in the same note:

- **L-theanine with caffeine:** the note links a [Reddit nootropics thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/Nootropics/comments/emu26p/ltheaninecaffeine_is_fcking_amazing/) saying the pair "allows coffee to work its brain-boosting charm without letting it raise your blood pressure or induce anxiety & jitterness."
- **Tongkat ali and pomegranate:** "Pomegranate juice to offset oestrogen block from tongkat Ali." And: "Also don’t take tongkat Ali too late in the afternoon because it can disrupt sleep."
- **Blueberries:** a [post on brain fog](https://x.com/DejaRu22/status/1884529318564708759) says: "Near-instant cure for brain fog: Eat 500g+ of blueberries in one sitting". It claims you will "tangibly *feel* your brain getting a rush" because "the flavonoids specifically and powerfully lower inflammation in the brain."
- **Vitamin C:** a line the note attributes to Dr Dynamis: "People with marginal vitamin C status burned 25% less fat during exercise. After vitamin C supplementation? Fat burning increased 4-fold." No study is attached.

## Claims awaiting evidence

These are leads, not findings. The saved notes supply no controlled clinical evidence for them. That is a statement about the source record, not proof that every possible effect is absent. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

- **Creatine:** a training-supplement research lead. No formulation, indication or individual suitability has been assessed.
- **Magnesium:** the note groups hearing, migraine and sleep claims together. Each needs its own evidence and population check. "30–60 minutes before bed" is a timing, not a dose.
- **L-theanine with caffeine:** the promise of no blood-pressure rise or anxiety is too absolute. Nothing in the source establishes that outcome for every person.
- **Tongkat ali and pomegranate:** the hormone-balancing mechanism and the combined protocol are unverified.
- **Blueberries:** a subjective report does not establish an instant brain-fog cure or the claimed brain-inflammation mechanism.
- **Vitamin C and fat burning:** the 25% and fourfold figures come with no cited study.

## Corrections

Correction, 22 September 2026 (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]]):

- **Fluoride and the thyroid.** The [[Medical|Medical & Health]] note says fluoride "competes with iodine at thyroid receptor sites" and recommends hydroxyapatite toothpaste instead. This is unsupported for normal toothpaste use and conflicts with NHS dental advice. The [NHS guide to keeping teeth clean](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/how-to-keep-your-teeth-clean/) recommends fluoride toothpaste twice a day, at least 1,350 ppm fluoride for adults.
- **Sunlight and dopamine.** The note says sunlight "is needed" for the tyrosine hydroxylase step of dopamine synthesis. It is not. That reaction uses tyrosine, oxygen and tetrahydrobiopterin; sunlight is not a reagent ([PubChem pathway](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pathway/BioCyc%3AHUMAN_PWY66-301); [enzyme kinetics study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6117320/)). Light does affect circadian timing, which is a separate question; see [[Circadian Rhythm]].
- **Cold showers.** The note quotes @olivesagan: "after seeing some research on how deliberate cold exposure facilitates fairly high levels of dopamine release, i decided to give it a rip and i'm a believer now." A reply says five-minute cold showers "almost feels like taking a pill or something." Anecdotes like these do not establish medication-equivalent effects.
- **Neck blood flow.** The note says "Better blood flow to the neck makes you more intelligent" and links a [YouTube clip](https://youtu.be/2vap5UzphfI?t=442). The attached notes do not support it.

## Peptides and investigational drugs

[[Retatrutide]] is an investigational drug, not a nutritional supplement. The [FDA's statement on unapproved GLP-1 drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss) says retatrutide is not a component of any FDA-approved drug and has not been found safe and effective for any condition; that is a US regulatory statement. Research-vendor products and a saved injection guide do not establish identity, purity, stability or clinical suitability. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

The [[Peptides]] guide does not establish a validated preparation protocol. [[Peptide Reconstitution]] explains the missing formulation evidence. The [DailyMed label for Bacteriostatic Water](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display) describes 0.9% benzyl alcohol and directs use according to the drug manufacturer's instructions; a preservative does not give every reconstituted drug a 28-day shelf life. See [[Bacteriostatic Water]].

The [[Body]] note also carries a take on peptides, attributed to @goth600:

> "Tired: losing all your neuroplasticity at 21
> Wired: using peptides & micro dosing psilocybin to reinvigorate neural growth"
