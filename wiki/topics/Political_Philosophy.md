# Political Philosophy

This page holds the foundational frames behind the wiki's political material, not policy positions. The dominant frame is [[Curtis_Yarvin|Curtis Yarvin]]'s entropy-versus-extropy reset of the left/right axis, which treats politics as thermodynamic rather than policy-granular. Around it sit three smaller frames: judge systems by function rather than label, treat sovereignty as an asset that can become distressed, and notice how environment shapes political values. [[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]] extends the frames; [[Political_Commentary|Political Commentary]] applies them to current events.

## Entropy versus extropy

The line saved in [[Culture|Culture & Observations]]:

> "the left is entropic the right is extropic"

The note identifies it as a direct or very close quote from Yarvin (Mencius Moldbug) in his February 2026 interview on Peter McCormack's podcast, and gives the fuller version as going "something like":

> "Fundamentally it's entropy versus extropy. The left is entropic, so things can just decay. But if you're building something, you need to build."

Yarvin borrows the thermodynamic terms and turns them into a political metaphor:

- **Entropy (the left).** In physics, the tendency of a closed system toward disorder and decay unless energy is added. Politically, the left "deconstructs, levels, and dissolves existing structures": hierarchies, traditions, standards, borders, merit-based systems. The result is more "equality" on paper and, in his view, breakdown, inefficiency and eventual collapse in practice.
- **Extropy (the right).** The made-up opposite of entropy, popularised in futurism and transhumanism: actively increasing order, complexity, intelligence, vitality and capability. Life itself is extropic; it builds organised structures (cells → organisms → societies). The true right, for Yarvin, builds and maintains higher-order systems: strong hierarchies, sovereign decision-making ("one person or small group in charge who can actually act"), innovation that creates new capability, and patchwork-style governance or corporate-sovereign models that get things done.
- **"Cthulhu always swims left."** His famous line, per the note, for the drift of modern progressivism, democracy and bureaucracy: everything slowly slides leftward and gets messier unless deliberate energy pushes back.

The note's plain-English summary:

> Left = lets the system rot (entropy wins).
>
> Right = fights rot by actively building stronger, smarter structures (extropy wins).

On this reading politics "isn't really about policy details anymore" but is "a cosmic battle between decay and deliberate creation". The poster who surfaced the line called it the best line that no one was mentioning. The full treatment is in [[Entropy_vs_Extropy|Entropy vs Extropy]].

## Why conservative versus liberal misses the point

For Yarvin, "conservative vs. liberal" or "Republican vs. Democrat" misses the point. Most "conservatives" are still slower versions of the entropic trend: they "conserve yesterday's leftism". Real right-wing energy "has to be creative and order-building", not passive conservatism that just slows the left down. The note adds his claim that interesting, high-openness people used to flock left and are now starting to shift right as the entropy becomes obvious.

## Follow function, not labels

A post saved in [[Politics]] compares UK wage compression with the Soviet Union. The figures are the post's own.

| Comparison | Figure |
|---|---|
| UK take-home on full-time (40-hour) minimum wage | £22,555 |
| UK take-home on a £100k salary | £68,558 |
| UK net income ratio, minimum wage to top-5% salary | 3.04:1 |
| USSR, same comparison | never below 5:1 |

The post adds that reality is worse, because the £100k earner often has student loans, and ends:

> Britain is nominally capitalist but functionally communist. China is nominally communist but functionally capitalist.
> Funny how that works.

The lesson: system labels are not system function. Follow incentives and outcomes, not rhetoric.

A long [post on the Iranian uprising](https://x.com/DeTahmineh/status/2009680255091405074) _(@DeTahmineh)_, also in [[Politics]], applies the same test to central planning. It describes Iran as a state-dominated economy of price controls, subsidies, nationalisation and bureaucratic micromanagement, and says of those policies: "They have been tried. They have failed. Catastrophically." It argues that authoritarianism "is not a Western invention imposed from outside, but something many societies are actively trying to escape." Its media argument is covered in [[Political_Commentary|Political Commentary]].

## Sovereignty as a distressed asset

[[Politics]] saves the phrase "Sovereignty is a distressed asset" as a heading, with a [post](https://x.com/howdymerry/status/2010407057313202406) _(@howdymerry)_, and files the UK wage-compression figures under it. Read together: when a state cannot maintain basic property rights, wage justice or economic coherence, sovereignty itself becomes distressed. That invites capital flight, brain drain and institutional capture. [[Entropy_vs_Extropy|Entropy vs Extropy]] reads this as entropy showing up in institutions.

## Environment and political values

A take in [[Politics]] argues that environment shapes political values:

> u dont see ppl who spend lots of time in nature supporting mass immigration, its mostly an urbanite idea bc theyve already gotten used to living like rats and see no problem debasing themselves further & stuffing as many ppl into their nation as possible. theyre spiritual rodents

The claim is that nature dwellers hold to boundaries while urban density normalises radical openness. It is a take, not a finding. A gentler version of the same idea, that place shapes the person, runs through [[Culture|Culture & Observations]]: "Every space you occupy is voting on who you become" ([post](https://x.com/tishray/status/2031783721397809397) _(@tishray)_). See [[Culture_and_Environment|Culture and Environment]].
