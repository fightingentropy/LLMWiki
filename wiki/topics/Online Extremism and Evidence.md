# Online Extremism and Evidence

Several new sources preserve hostile racial, religious, and gender rhetoric. Their value to the wiki is diagnostic: how a real event, partial statistic, screenshot, or anecdote is converted into an identity-level claim. The claims are catalogued as attributed rhetoric, not endorsed or treated as established evidence.

## Common transformations

- **Real event → broader conspiracy.** The Las Vegas suspected-biolab case is extended into claims not supported by the cited charge history.
- **Association → secret coordination.** A released email or family/company connection is used to imply a hidden movement without evidence of the larger causal claim.
- **Anecdote → universal group rule.** One parent, interview, cooking clip, relationship post, or business closure becomes a claim about an entire sex, race, religion, or nation.
- **Unidentified biology → racial diagnosis.** A supposed allele, IQ figure, or crime statistic is asserted without a named study, dataset, population definition, effect size, confounder analysis, or replication.
- **Scenario document → covert plan.** A public demographic model is redescribed as proof of a secret replacement programme.
- **Shock language → borrowed certainty.** Slurs and provocation make a clip memorable while contributing no evidence.

## Review checklist

1. Identify the narrow claim actually supported by the primary material.
2. Separate the event date, publication date, legal status, and later commentary.
3. Ask whether the post supplies the named study, dataset, methods, denominator, and comparison group it implies.
4. Test whether identity or association is being substituted for causation.
5. Preserve contradictions and uncertainty instead of silently resolving them.
6. Avoid repeating dehumanizing wording when a factual description of the rhetoric is sufficient.

This page also covers the raw `Funny.md` addition that presents racist pseudo-scientific claims about food access and intelligence. It is retained only as an example of dehumanizing propaganda; the source supplies no credible evidence for its group claims.
