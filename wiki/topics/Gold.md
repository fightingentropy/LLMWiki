# Gold

Gold is a macro asset and a safe haven that works as insurance in a portfolio. Its price is driven mainly by real rates, the US dollar and risk sentiment, with central-bank buying, jewellery demand and mine supply underneath. Erlin's handbook note lists the drivers in one line. This page expands them, sets out how gold trades against rates, the dollar and equities, walks through four macro scenarios, and keeps a few saved posts that question gold's safe-haven label.

## The handbook's list

The note's line on gold (from [[Markets - Handbook]]):

> "factors: investor sentiment, safe haven in economic uncertainty, interest rates, DXY (rates low or dollar weaker = gold more attractive), central banks buying, jewellery demand, mining supply."

The same note sets gold against silver, whose drivers include "relative value vs gold (gold–silver ratio)". See [[Silver]] for the industrial and precious metals hybrid, and [[Commodities]] for the broader framework.

## Drivers

### Macro

- **Real yields**: higher real rates mean lower gold, because they raise the opportunity cost of holding an asset that yields nothing. See [[Real Yields]] and [[Rates]].
- **US dollar**: a weak dollar means higher gold, because gold gets cheaper in other currencies. See [[DXY]] and [[FX]].
- **Risk sentiment**: gold gains in risk-off periods on safe-haven flows.
- **Economic uncertainty**: flight to quality and gold's insurance value.

### Physical and investment demand

- **Central-bank buying**: official-sector demand for reserves.
- **Jewellery demand**: consumption and adornment.
- **Mine supply**: primary production and recycling.
- **Investment flows**: ETF and fund positioning.

### Technical

- **Positioning**: commodity futures positioning can drive sharp moves. See [[Positioning]].
- **Technical levels**: support and resistance drive mechanical flows.
- **Options**: gamma exposure and volatility spikes.

## Trading relationships

- **Inverse to real rates**: rising real rates, especially unexpected rises, push gold down.
- **Inverse to the dollar**: a strong dollar, especially a risk-off dollar, pushes gold down.
- **With risk-off**: when equities fall, gold typically rallies.

The last two can pull against each other. In a risk-off move that also lifts the dollar, the safe-haven bid and the dollar headwind compete.

## Scenarios

| Scenario | Real rates | Gold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recession | Fall (terminal rate lower) | Rallies |
| Stagflation | Under pressure | Rallies despite inflation |
| Deflation | Spike | Vulnerable (but competing with nominal yields) |
| Growth acceleration | Rise | Under pressure |

See [[Market Regime]] and [[Macro Trading]] for how to call which regime is in play.

## Value and positioning checks

[[goodalexander]]'s checklist for spotting contrarian setups has commodity tests that apply to gold (from [[X-Bookmarks]]):

- **Raw valuation**: "For commodities compare to production cost".
- **Storage cost**: "For commodities if it is insanely expensive to store something chances are ppl don’t want to be long".
- **Consensus**: for commodities, look at consensus supply and demand data; "if consensus is that oil inventories are low being long oil is consensus".

## Safe haven or casino

Two posts from 30 January 2026, a day of heavy selling in silver, question the safe-haven label (from [[X-Bookmarks]]):

- "Safe haven asset owners are beginning to realize everything is a casino" _(@0xRacist)_
- "Silver and Gold may collapse in speculative mania, but it’s giving you the opportunity to acquire rare earth minerals and uranium. Both of which have strong fundamental drivers, and are being unfairly dragged around by the metals frenzy." _(@AviFelman)_

A speculative post by MONK imagines gold on crypto rails. The imagined trading day in 2032 opens: "I just opened a long gold, short nasdaq trade on Hyperliquid, using my spot HYPE as collateral." For how metals already trade on [[Hyperliquid]], see [[HIP-3 Silver Microstructure]].
