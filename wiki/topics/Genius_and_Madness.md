# Genius and Madness

The observation that intuitive decision-makers who thrive on saturated information flow tend to slide into unreliable judgment as that flow thins. That is why the line between genius and madness can run through the same person over time. The wiki uses it inside [[Trading_Psychology]] and [[Psychology]], usually to explain why preternaturally good callers later become preternaturally bad ones. It is not a theory the wiki develops rigorously; it works as a warning label on intuitive strategies.

## The observation

The idea comes from a pair of posts by roon (@tszzl), saved in both [[Psychology]] and [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]]:

> "intuitive decision makers thrive when they're plugged in and on top of their game: they make preternaturally good calls after processing tons of information. after they grow old and become divorced from the info flow, they try to make confident inferences but will go awry"

> "imo this is why genius and madness ride so closely and why Nobel laureates, Howard Hughes, elon, etc go insane as time goes on. more careful measured thinkers may be safe from this"

## Key themes

- **Intuition is information-bound.** Intuitive traders thrive when plugged in and processing a lot of information constantly. That is when they make preternaturally good calls. (from [[Psychology]], [[Trading_Psychology]])
- **Aging thins the flow.** As intuitive operators age and lose their information flow, confident inferences go awry. The confidence persists; the input doesn't.
- **Examples.** Nobel laureates, Howard Hughes and Elon are named in [[Psychology]] as patterns of late-career drift into madness.
- **Measured thinkers are safer.** More careful, process-driven thinkers are less spectacular but less prone to this trap. (from [[Trading_Psychology]])
- **Implication for traders.** Build processes that don't depend on one operator's intuition aging gracefully. Decouple edge from identity. See [[Decision-Making]].

Other saved lines on brilliance, blind spots and instinct, from the same notes:

- **Intelligence misapplied.** "Charlton (2009a, 2009b) argued that the reason why individuals with high IQ tend to fail in understanding and coping with basic human situations is that they try to apply complex and abstract thinking to problems for which instinctive strategies are more adaptive." (from [[Thoughts]])
- **Love is not logical.** "Why do intelligent people struggle with love? Because it's not [logical](https://x.com/lokymann/status/1886166317381669170)." (from [[Thoughts]])
- **Ability is a bundle, not a scalar.** Analytical intelligence does not automatically supply competitive judgment, persistence, composure, social perception or the willingness to act under pressure, though unusually effective people may combine them. (from [[Thoughts]])
