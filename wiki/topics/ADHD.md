# ADHD

## Circadian phenotype and correction

**Correction, 2026-09-22:** the earlier page described ADHD as fundamentally a circadian disorder. The cited [Luu and Fabiano perspective](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1697900/full) instead identifies a substantial subgroup with circadian dysfunction and explicitly rejects exclusive reclassification. It proposes sleep/circadian assessment and adjunctive interventions, while noting that evidence for ADHD remission is lacking. The paper was published in December 2025; September 2026 is the bookmark date. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

**Key resource:** [NIH - ADHD Medications Stimulate Alertness and Motivation](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/adhd-medications-stimulate-alertness-motivation)

## Social Characteristics
The personal notes describe friendships that resume easily after long gaps, using the phrase “no friendship degradation mechanics.” Preserve this as a social observation from [[Psychology]], not a universal characteristic or diagnostic criterion.

## Cognitive Profile
The source admires independent reasoning and agency in neurodivergent people. That is an attributed personal framing, not evidence that ADHD or autism confers universal objectivity, integrity, or freedom from social influence. See [[Neurodiversity]].

## Hyperfocus and limerence

The latest source notes a possible connection between ADHD-related hyperfocus and vulnerability to limerence. It is not inevitable, and limerence is not an official diagnosis. Distinguish reciprocal knowledge of a real person from a relationship constructed through checking, projection, and rumination; see [[Mental_Health]] and [[Health Evidence]].

## Contrasting September treatment accounts

The source retains both a negative account of prescribed Vyvanse and a positive account of Adderall. They concern different people and drugs; neither resolves what another person will experience. The three-day cure, antihistamine, and dopamine-reset posts remain unverified claims rather than alternative treatment protocols. Medication concerns belong with the prescriber. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])
