# ADHD

ADHD appears in Erlin's notes from four angles: a 2025 paper arguing that a large subgroup of people with ADHD have a delayed body clock, an NIH summary of how stimulant medication works, two opposing first-person accounts of stimulant treatment alongside a cluster of unverified "fix" posts, and a few social and cognitive observations about how people with ADHD think and keep friends. The circadian material is the strongest, and it carries a dated correction. Nothing on this page is a diagnosis or a treatment plan.

## Circadian timing: the Luu and Fabiano paper

Erlin's medical note records the line "We recently reconceptualized ADHD as a circadian rhythm disorder." beside a post by Nicholas Fabiano, co-author of the paper. (from [[Medical & Health]])

What the paper and Brandon Luu's thread about it say (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]]):

- People with ADHD tend to have later biological sleep/wake timing. The paper cites delayed sleep/wake timing in up to 78%, sleep disturbances in up to 80% of adults and 82% of children, and an evening melatonin rise about 45 minutes later in children and 90 minutes later in adults.
- The thread's graphic depicts later melatonin timing and a later, flatter morning cortisol rise.
- Early evidence suggests that shifting the body clock earlier may ease symptoms for some people.
- The proposed approach: screen for sleep and circadian problems; keep a consistent wake-up time; get morning bright light; cut evening light and screen exposure; keep regular routines; use selective low-dose melatonin when delayed timing is identified.
- The authors describe these measures as additions to standard ADHD care, with treatment evidence still developing.

**Correction, 22 September 2026:** the earlier version of this page described ADHD as fundamentally a circadian disorder. The cited [Luu and Fabiano perspective](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1697900/full) instead identifies a substantial subgroup with circadian dysfunction and explicitly rejects exclusive reclassification. It proposes sleep/circadian assessment and adjunctive interventions, while noting that evidence for ADHD remission is lacking. The paper was published in December 2025, not September 2026 as the earlier version implied. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

The same medical note adds: "Dopamine is synthesized from the amino acid Tyrosine, The first and crucial step is made by the enzyme Tyrosine Hydroxylase, Sunlight is needed for this step." The note gives no source for the sunlight clause. **Correction:** [[Medical & Health]] records that the tyrosine-hydroxylase reaction uses oxygen and tetrahydrobiopterin and does not require sunlight as a reagent; light's role in circadian timing is a separate matter, and [[Circadian Rhythm]] keeps the line as a viewpoint to investigate, not a mechanism. (from [[Medical & Health]])

## Medication: how stimulants work, and two opposing accounts

**Key resource:** [NIH - ADHD Medications Stimulate Alertness and Motivation](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/adhd-medications-stimulate-alertness-motivation), an NIH Research Matters summary of research on the mechanism behind ADHD medications. Saved in Erlin's medical note without comment. (from [[Medical & Health]])

The notes hold two first-person accounts of prescribed stimulants that point in opposite directions (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]]):

- **Negative, Vyvanse.** @LawrenceElliots attributes relationship problems, sensory irritation, isolation, and personality changes to 70 mg lisdexamfetamine, and quotes a broader anti-medication claim from @AntiDoc. One person's experience, not evidence that prescribed stimulants cause autism or affect everyone the same way.
- **Positive, Adderall.** @Gena_I_Gorlin says medication helped her feel more consistently herself, and links a longer essay about moving from ADHD scepticism to treatment.

They concern different people and different drugs. Neither resolves what another person will experience. Medication concerns belong with the prescriber.

## Unverified claims circulating as fixes

These posts are saved as examples of what circulates, not as treatment options. None is evidence, and none should change medication without a clinician or pharmacist. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

- **Three-day cure.** @oxidativestate claims a "dopamine reset" rapidly cured depression and ADHD. Unsupported; kept as a misinformation example.
- **Antihistamines for "ADHD brains".** @be_like_legend1 compares Allegra, Zyrtec, and Pepcid for people with ADHD. Unsupported comparison.
- **Histamine, circadian rhythm, and sleep.** @ArsAlendi's anecdotal thread treats ADHD as partly a histamine and sleep issue after one reported response to an antihistamine. It does not establish a general mechanism or a safe treatment.
- **Procrastination tips.** @apaji_x's introductory post promises ADHD-friendly ways to start tasks, but the saved record does not contain the actual list.

## Hyperfocus and limerence

Limerence is an intense, involuntary fixation on another person. It is not an official diagnosis. Cleveland Clinic's overview discusses insecure attachment and a possible vulnerability related to ADHD hyperfocus; it is not an inevitable outcome of ADHD or a universal biochemical explanation. The post that prompted the bookmark overstates this as a severe dopamine addiction. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

A useful distinction: reciprocal knowledge of a real person versus a relationship constructed through checking, projection, and rumination. Persistent distress or impairment is a reason to seek professional support. See [[Mental Health]] and [[Health Evidence]].

## Social and cognitive observations from Erlin's notes

- **Friendships that resume after long gaps.** Saved from @ADHDForReal: "one thing i really love about ADHD people is how y'all simply don't have friendship degradation mechanics. i can text an ADHD person after months or even years of radio silence and just pick right back up where we left off". A social observation, not a universal characteristic or a diagnostic criterion. (from [[Psychology]])
- **Independent reasoning.** The notes admire independent reasoning and agency in neurodivergent people: "Only high T alpha males and aneurotypical people (hey autists!) are actually free to parse new information with an objective 'is this true?' filter." That is an attributed personal framing, not evidence that ADHD or autism confers universal objectivity, integrity, or freedom from social influence. See [[Neurodiversity]]. (from [[Psychology]])
