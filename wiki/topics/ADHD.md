# ADHD

ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition affecting attention, activity and impulse control. It appears here through a 2025 paper on a delayed body clock, research on how stimulants work, two opposite treatment stories, and takes on how neurodivergent people think and keep friends. Diagnosis and medication belong with a clinician.

## A delayed body clock in a subgroup

Nicholas Fabiano's post opens: "We recently reconceptualized ADHD as a circadian rhythm disorder." The paper behind it, Luu and Fabiano's [perspective in Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1697900/full) (December 2025), presents no new data. It cites:

- delayed sleep/wake timing in up to 78% of people with ADHD;
- sleep problems in up to 80% of adults and 82% of children;
- an evening melatonin rise about 45 minutes late in children and 90 minutes late in adults.

It proposes adding to standard care: screen for sleep and circadian problems, keep a fixed wake time, get morning bright light, cut evening light and screens, keep regular routines, and use low-dose melatonin where a delay is found. Brandon Luu's thread walks through the figures; [[Circadian Rhythm]] covers the clock itself.

Correction (22 September 2026): the paper describes a circadian subgroup, links an earlier clock to fewer symptoms only by correlation, says evidence of remission is lacking, and rejects reclassifying ADHD as solely circadian.

## How stimulants work

[NIH](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/adhd-medications-stimulate-alertness-motivation) and [Washington University](https://source.washu.edu/2025/12/stimulant-adhd-medications-work-differently-than-thought/) summaries of Kay et al. (Cell, 2025) report that in 5,795 children's brain scans, stimulants acted on arousal and reward networks rather than attention networks. The [Vyvanse label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=704e4378-ca83-445c-8b45-3cfa51c1ecad) calls lisdexamfetamine a dextroamphetamine prodrug whose exact action in ADHD is unknown.

Two first-person accounts point opposite ways. @LawrenceElliots blames 70 mg Vyvanse for relationship problems, sensory irritation and personality change. @Gena_I_Gorlin says Adderall helped her feel more consistently herself. Different people, different drugs; neither predicts anyone else's response.

## Fixes that circulate

A three-day "dopamine reset" cure, antihistamines (Allegra, Zyrtec, Pepcid) for "ADHD brains", and a thread recasting ADHD as a histamine and sleep problem after one antihistamine response. None shows methods or outcomes. A post promising ADHD-friendly ways to start tasks never gave the list.

## Hyperfocus and limerence

Limerence is an intense, involuntary fixation on another person. [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/limerence) says it is not a diagnosis, links it to insecure attachment and possibly ADHD hyperfocus, and compares it with addiction. The post that prompted the bookmark called it a severe dopamine addiction, which goes further. The useful line runs between knowing a real person and a relationship built from checking, projection and rumination.

## Neurodivergent minds

The notes treat autism and ADHD as a cognitive style as much as a diagnosis: less filtered by consensus, more by whether a thing is true. A take in [[Psychology & Behavior]] says people who cannot defend themselves physically run a consensus filter:

> They literally do not ask "is this true", they ask "will others be OK with me thinking this is true".

> Only high T alpha males and aneurotypical people (hey autists!) are actually free to parse new information with an objective "is this true?" filter.

It calls consensus thinkers "very malleable to brute force manufactured consensus" and concludes: "This is why a Republic of high status males is best for decision making." See [[Decision-Making]].

On autism, from [[Thoughts & Observations]]:

> As I get older, I realize more and more that the best thing to do in life is to find autistic people and surround yourself with them

> the correlation between autism and traits like honor and integrity is astronomically high relative to the general population.

On ADHD friendship, from @ADHDForReal:

> one thing i really love about ADHD people is how y'all simply don't have friendship degradation mechanics. i can text an ADHD person after months or even years of radio silence and just pick right back up where we left off

A post by @alexeixbt casts ADHD as a hunter-gatherer phenotype. NIMH describes a developmental disorder, and the story does not remove the need for treatment.
