# Decision-Making

Choosing under uncertainty, in trading, careers, relationships and arguments. The core idea: good process looks like bad process in short samples, so do not let outcomes retrain it. Most of it comes from [[Thoughts & Observations]] and [[Crypto Takes]].

## Judge the decision, not the outcome

Luck hides bad strategy and noise hides good strategy; only the long run tells them apart. [[0xaporia]]'s rule is "Decouple Process from Outcome." Over 100 trades at a 50% win rate, a run of 5 or more losses is about 80% likely and 8 or more about 17%; at 40%, about 98% and 49%.

> "A surgeon with a 95% success rate will eventually lose multiple patients in a row. A poker player should focus on whether they made mathematically optimal decisions, not whether they won the hand." (from [[Crypto Takes]])

- **Every system has a cost.** Trend-following pays in false starts, momentum in late entries, mean reversion in outsized moves. Traders quit valid systems mistaking that cost for failure ([[CryptoCred]]).
- **Two checks on an open position.** [[CryptoCred]]: "If I didn't have a position, would I want the exposure I have now?" [[cobie]]: "you act too quickly when you are trying to grow capital, and act too slowly when you are trying to protect capital".

## Intuition and its limits

roon (@tszzl):

> "intuitive decision makers thrive when they're plugged in and on top of their game: they make preternaturally good calls after processing tons of information. after they grow old and become divorced from the info flow, they try to make confident inferences but will go awry"

He adds that this is "why genius and madness ride so closely", and that "more careful measured thinkers may be safe from this". The confidence stays after the input is gone, so a trading process should not depend on one person's intuition ageing well.

Contradiction: other saved lines trust instinct outright. @MatriXBT: "unironically, my BEST TRADES were PURE INSTINCT based trades. no TA, Orderflow or ANYTHING, NO TOOLS." The two fit only while the information flow is live.

- **Ability is a bundle.** Reasoning, persistence, composure, social perception and the will to act are separate traits. @iamgingertrash says intelligence does not bring the rest; @lefttailguy answers that the most effective people combine them.
- **Reality is the constraint.** Clifford Sosin argues many hard problems are limited by contact with reality, not by reasoning. Ideas still have to be built, tested and corrected.

## Play the repeated game

A repeated game is any interaction you will play again: the same person, market or bankroll. What counts is the strategy that keeps future rounds good.

- **Tit for Tat as a boundary model.** Start cooperative, answer exploitation promptly and in proportion, and restore cooperation when the other side does. The aim is to "make cooperation profitable and defection predictably costly while avoiding permanent revenge loops." The @alexeixbt essay behind this overstates Tit for Tat; in Axelrod's tournaments its results depend on noise and the other players. It applies to [[Dating & Relationships]] as much as to trading.
- **Preserve later rounds.** After a big loss, one gamble to erase it can end the game. Stabilise, cap losses and recover over later rounds (@incentivising). Wealth comes from surviving drawdowns, not rare crash bets (@Evan_ss6).
- **Insure only unaffordable variance.** Positive expected value is not survivable risk. Insure a ruinous low-odds loss; take a small good bet whose loss you can afford (Alec Torelli).
- **Read the payoff matrix.** A broken-looking system may be producing exactly what its incentives reward, and more effort inside it changes nothing. Change the game or move to one that rewards your ability (@incentivising).
- **Leaving a safe job.** "the payoff of staying is known and comfortable. the payoff of leaving is uncertain and scary." _(@moneyfetishist)_
- [[Nassim Taleb]]: "Collect as many free nonlottery tickets (those with open-ended payoffs) as you can, and, once they start paying off, do not discard them."

## Reason from evidence

- **Groups are not individuals.** A population average does not decide one case, and one exception does not disprove a tendency. Ask how large the effect is and how much the groups overlap ([[Nassim Taleb]] on within-group variance).
- **Persuasive is not true.** Fluent arguments can make opposite positions sound right. Name the observation that would tell them apart (@ArtemisConsort).
