# Decision-Making

How the wiki thinks about choice under uncertainty: separating the quality of a decision from the quality of its outcome, and the conditions under which intuitive judgment works or breaks down. The theme runs through [[Trading_Psychology]], [[Psychology]] and [[Thoughts]]. The recurring frame: good process in a noisy environment looks like bad process in short samples, and the discipline is not letting outcomes retrain the process.

## Judge the decision, not the outcome

- **Decouple decision from outcome.** A market outcome is not the quality of the decision. Randomness masks poor reasoning; luck masks bad strategy. Only the long run separates signal from noise. (from [[Trading_Psychology]])
- **Variance tolerance.** Most traders misread a losing streak as strategy failure rather than noise. Strategy hopping is mostly a flight from "the grueling, nonlinear phase where most progress happens." [[0xaporia]]'s answer: "Decouple Process from Outcome." ([Strategy](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/1894297482500141122), from [[CT]])
- **Know what the system costs.** Trend-following pays through false starts; momentum enters late and can buy tops or sell bottoms; mean reversion periodically gets overwhelmed by an outsized move. A recurring failure is abandoning a valid system because its unavoidable cost is mistaken for evidence that it is broken. ([[CryptoCred]], from [[Markets - Trading]])
- **Visible success is selected, not just earned.** Networks, sponsorship, access to capital, institutional incentives, narrative appeal and usefulness to powerful allocators can lift one person while equally capable people stay unseen. A correction to pure meritocratic storytelling, not proof that every success is manufactured. (from [[Thoughts]])

How often losing streaks happen over 100 trades, per 0xaporia's post (from [[CT]]):

| Streak length | 50% win rate | 40% win rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 or more | approx. 80% | approx. 98% |
| 6 or more | approx. 55% | approx. 87% |
| 7 or more | approx. 32% | approx. 69% |
| 8 or more | approx. 17% | approx. 49% |

"A surgeon with a 95% success rate will eventually lose multiple patients in a row. A poker player should focus on whether they made mathematically optimal decisions, not whether they won the hand." Poker players also know they don't need to play every hand: "Novice traders often conflate constant activity with progress." ([Poker](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/1894332130093629551))

Two quick checks for a position already on:

- "If I didn't have a position, would I want the exposure I have now?" Yes: fine. No: do something about it. "Active positions cost mental capital, which is a scarce resource." ([@CryptoCred](https://x.com/CryptoCred), from [[CT]])
- "you act too quickly when you are trying to grow capital, and act too slowly when you are trying to protect capital" ([[cobie]], from [[CT]])

## Intuition and its limits

- **Intuitive vs measured thinkers.** "intuitive decision makers thrive when they're plugged in and on top of their game: they make preternaturally good calls after processing tons of information. after they grow old and become divorced from the info flow, they try to make confident inferences but will go awry" His follow-up: "more careful measured thinkers may be safe from this" — less spectacular, but safer. _(roon, @tszzl, from [[Psychology]])_ See [[Genius_and_Madness]].
- **Ability is a bundle, not a scalar.** Analytical reasoning, persistence, composure, social perception, competitive judgment and willingness to act under pressure are separable. They are not exclusive types either: unusually effective people may combine several. The posts' three-trait chart is an illustration, not population evidence. (from [[Thoughts]])
- **Contact with reality.** Clifford Sosin's thesis: abundant high-quality reasoning can transform work with smooth, verifiable solution spaces, but many important problems stay constrained by contact with reality. Ideas still have to be built, tested, measured and corrected; reality supplies facts reasoning cannot derive in advance. (from [[Thoughts]])

## Act instead of overthinking

- **Overthinking = underacting.** "One pattern I've noticed in all miserable people: They overthink and underact." ([pattern](https://x.com/TaylinSimmonds/status/1878819866070679565), from [[Psychology]]) The working rule: just act; imperfect action beats perfect planning. The note puts it as "aim. fire. correct later" and "you are getting lapped by people 50% dumber than you because they don't overthink shit."
- **High agency as a trait.** "The older i get, the more I'm convinced the single most important trait that matters in your friends, coworkers, life partner, and kids is high agency." (from [[X Takes]])
- **Tiny deltas compound.** A passage in the Psychology note: "our life is just an accumulation of these tiny deltas. Most of the time we don't blow up with one catastrophic -100%." Skipping the gym for one day, or one FOMO trade against the plan, feels insignificant in the moment. (from [[Psychology]])
- **Decisions reshape the actor.** A saved post claims: "my physiognomy has changed with the decisions I make in my life, when I started to make more aggressive masculine decisions, my features got sharper, my frame got bigger and I put on muscle easier as I was in more precarious environments". A personal anecdote; the author says he can't explain the mechanism. ([can't](https://x.com/BlondB00/status/1882121878757847356), from [[Thoughts]])

## Read the game you are in

- **Read the payoff matrix.** A system that appears broken may be producing exactly the outcomes its incentives reward. More effort inside the same payoff matrix raises output without changing the matrix. Ask whose interests the structure serves, then change the game or play the one that actually exists. (from [[Thoughts]])
- **Change environment before endlessly polishing ability.** Getting more skilled or disciplined does not help if the environment cannot reward it. Ask which setting would convert what you already have into a return, what moving costs, and whether changing structure now beats another decade of self-polishing in the wrong place. (from [[Thoughts]])
- **Optimise the repeated game.** In repeated interactions, start cooperative, answer exploitation promptly and proportionally, and restore cooperation once the other side does. Make cooperation profitable and defection predictably costly; avoid permanent revenge loops. The source essay overstates Tit for Tat as universally dominant; its performance depends on the population, noise and game structure. See [[Repeated Games]]. (from [[Thoughts]])
- **Preserve later rounds.** After a major loss, one decisive gamble to erase it can destroy the only thing that makes recovery possible: staying in the game. Stabilise, cap further losses, keep optionality, and plan recovery across later rounds. (from [[Thoughts]])
- **Leaving a safe career is a repeated-game decision.** A saved framework on diminishing learning, golden handcuffs, status-quo bias and asymmetric regret: "the payoff of staying is known and comfortable. the payoff of leaving is uncertain and scary." (from [[Bookmarks - Personal & Life]] and [[X-Bookmarks]])
- **Collect open-ended payoffs.** "Collect as many free nonlottery tickets (those with open-ended payoffs) as you can, and, once they start paying off, do not discard them." Attributed in the note to [[Nassim_Taleb|Taleb]]. (from [[Thoughts]])

## Reason from evidence

- **Separate group and individual inference.** An exception does not by itself disprove a population tendency, but a population average does not decide an individual case. Ask whether the claim is really statistical, how large and well-supported the effect is, how much the groups overlap, and whether the decision concerns a population or the person in front of you. Taleb's version: within-group variance can dominate a selection decision; group evidence updates a prior but does not replace direct evidence, measurement quality, selection effects and the cost of errors. (from [[Thoughts]] and [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]])
- **Test the argument against observations.** Persuasive language can make incompatible positions sound compelling. State an observation that distinguishes the positions, an explicit falsifier, and the contrary evidence that would change the conclusion. A practical check on argument quality, not a diagnosis of the speaker. (from [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]])
