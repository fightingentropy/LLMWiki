# Market Regime

Market regime is the backdrop against which every trading decision is made: the growth and inflation mix, liquidity conditions and the volatility structure. The daily brief in [[Markets - Brief]], written in a [[Stanley Druckenmiller]] style, asks for an explicit regime call with a confidence level, and this page follows its structure; the brief holds the full daily methodology. The edge is spotting a regime change before consensus does.

## Regime components

### Growth and inflation

Three primary states:

- **Disinflationary slowdown:** growth declining, inflation falling.
- **Reacceleration:** growth and/or inflation picking up.
- **Stagflation risk:** growth stalling, inflation persistent or rising.

### Liquidity backdrop

- **Fed policy:** rate path, QE/QT stance.
- **Treasury issuance:** government financing needs.
- **QT/QE proxies:** balance-sheet flows and derivatives positioning.
- **Dollar liquidity:** global funding conditions. See [[DXY]].

### Volatility

Three primary states:

- **Suppressed:** low realized and implied volatility; complacency risk.
- **Transitioning:** volatility stepping up, models adapting.
- **Unstable:** high and unpredictable volatility, momentum reversals.

### Confidence

Every regime call carries a confidence level: High, Medium or Low.

## Regime shifts

The key to [[Macro Trading]] is identifying when the regime changes before consensus does. The brief asks for 5–10 specific shifts, each with what is changing, why it matters, and an observable confirmation signal.

Signals that can confirm a shift:

- Curve inversion, flattening or steepening
- DXY breaks (currency trends)
- Credit spread widening or tightening
- Equity breadth deterioration or improvement
- Commodity spike or collapse
- VIX/GARCH regime changes
- Yield curve reconditioning

## Why regime matters

Regime drives expected returns, risks and correlations:

- **Growth regime:** cyclicals outperform; credit spreads widen in a slowdown.
- **Inflation regime:** real assets outperform; duration risk matters. See [[Real Yields]].
- **Liquidity regime:** the vol regime and crowded positioning create squeeze risk. See [[Positioning]] and [[Flow Analysis]].
- **Volatility regime:** option value, gamma exposure and CTA flow mechanics change.

The active drivers change with it: momentum and flow lead in trends, positioning extremes in transitions, catalysts around macro events ([[Market Drivers]]). The longer boom-bust frame is in [[Market Cycles]], and the economic background in [[Economics]].

## Reading the regime across assets

The brief's cross-asset read, two to five bullets each ([[Cross-Asset Analysis]]):

- [[Rates]]: front-end, long-end, curve, real yields, implied policy path.
- [[FX]]: USD broad, key crosses, EM stress signals.
- [[Equities]]: index internals, breadth, leadership, cyclicals vs defensives.
- [[Credit]]: IG/HY spreads, refinancing stress, defaults.
- [[Commodities]]: oil, gas, metals; macro implications.
- Crypto, if macro-relevant, especially BTC as a liquidity and risk proxy ([[Crypto Trading]]).

The brief then covers positioning and flow (consensus and crowded trades; dealer, gamma, CTA and systematic flows; sentiment extremes and squeeze risk), a risk radar of the top five upside and top five downside risks with explicit trigger levels or events, and a Polymarket signal check on the highest-volume markets. Its style rules: "No fluff", "Prioritize signal over narrative", and "Separate facts, interpretation, and positioning implications".
