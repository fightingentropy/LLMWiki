# Economics

Economics here is mostly price theory and incentives: what happens when things become free, who does the spending, how labels differ from outcomes, and how markets get built for assets that lack one. The common thread is to follow incentives and outcomes, not rhetoric. Most of it comes from [[Business & Finance]]; macro and trading economics are on [[Macro Trading]] and [[Rates and Credit]].

## Weird things happen at zero

> "You can make things cheap and it will fix a lot of problems that will arise by making things free. Weird things happen at 0.00."

At a price of zero the scarcity signal disappears, demand has no brake, and the system buckles. Two cases:

- **Free healthcare.** Asked why Singapore does not offer free medical care, Ong Ye Kung: "when healthcare is free, and it's all paid for already by taxes, people have no discipline. The demand just booms." His example is the NHS: nine months to a year for a simple X-ray, seven million people waiting, and "The NHS is crumbling under the demand."
- **Free college.** San Francisco made its community college free, "And now apparently the slots get filled with scammers".

The corollary: "All profit comes from margins."

## Who does the spending

> "The economy is propped up by women, they spend money like nobody else. I've been around billionaires who watch their dollars closer than the average 20 year old in a big city." _(@bubblebabyboi)_

The figures gathered beside it, without named sources: women control or influence up to 85% of US consumer spending, over $31.8 trillion of spending globally by some estimates, and most household purchases, especially groceries, with strong influence over homes and cars. See [[Gender Dynamics]].

## Labels versus outcomes

A passage from the politics notes argues the UK has a flatter net income spread than the Soviet Union had.

| Measure | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| UK take-home pay, full-time minimum wage | £22,555 |
| UK take-home pay on a £100k salary | £68,558 |
| UK net ratio | 3.04:1 |
| USSR, same comparison | Never below 5:1 |

It adds that the gap is smaller still because the £100k earner often has student loans, and concludes:

> "Britain is nominally capitalist but functionally communist. China is nominally communist but functionally capitalist. Funny how that works."

The figures are the passage's own. The argument continues on [[Political Commentary]].

## Markets for illiquid assets

**Perpetual futures.** @sershokunin calls perps "the best pmf in all of crypto". The idea predates crypto: in 1992 Robert Shiller proposed perpetual futures, with hedonic-regression price indices, to create derivatives markets for illiquid, rarely priced assets such as single-family homes, and for untraded indices such as labour costs or the consumer price index.

**Tokenization.** Securities today sit in siloed databases at banks, brokers and law firms, or idle on cap-table software. A thread calls the result "value extraction, mistake, and inefficiency across all stages of an asset's lifecycle". Tokens offer:

- 24/7 global trading instead of exchange hours.
- Borrowing against assets and posting them as collateral in other contracts.
- A wider pool of buyers, including on-chain users and DAOs.
- Sending assets anywhere, instantly.

"The assets that benefit the most from tokenization will be those that don't currently have a robust market structure": real estate, IP and start-up shares rather than liquid ETFs.

The thread argues both sides. A reply on brokers: "You don't own that. Your broker owns that on your behalf and you hold an account at your brokerage". The sceptic: "No one cares. We WANT to outsource all this bs, esp for no fee and it's regulated by SEC so we sleep at noight". The comeback: "Until you get debanked or account frozen due to your political speech online. Think Canadian truckers".
