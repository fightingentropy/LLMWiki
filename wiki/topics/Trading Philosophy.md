# Trading Philosophy

The principles behind these trading notes: price follows perception, regime and observable signals come before stories, and a sound process matters more than any single result. This page also holds the daily brief's risk radar and the case for patience and cash. The psychology of sticking to it is on [[Trading Psychology]]; how to test a claim is on [[Trading Evidence and Review Framework]].

## Perception over truth

"Truth doesn't necessarily move markets. Perception & Uncertainty/Certainty transition does." (from [[Markets - Trading]]) Markets pay those who understand what others believe, and the biggest moves come when uncertainty turns to certainty, or back. The reflexive loop runs price, then perception, then fundamentals, then price again.

## What the commitment costs

> "He who has entered the charmed circle of the Exchange is in eternal agitation and sits in a prison, the key of which lies in the ocean and the bars of which are never opened." (from [[Markets - Articles]])

Trading breaks people. A line saved as "the story of Toto Wolff" gives the hope: "The world breaks everyone, and afterward, many are stronger at the broken places."

## Working from the daily brief

The [[Markets - Brief]] prompt, written in a [[Stanley Druckenmiller]] style, builds in four habits:

- **Regime first.** Call growth, inflation, liquidity and volatility, with a confidence level, before taking positions. Let the regime set the size, and expect a shift to break old convictions. See [[Macro Trading]].
- **Signal over narrative.** "Prioritize signal over narrative" and "Separate facts, interpretation, and positioning implications." Watch what is happening, not why.
- **Positioning extremes.** Find crowded trades and squeeze risk. Fade extremes; ride momentum when the regime is clear. See [[Positioning and Sentiment]].
- **Observable triggers.** For each regime shift, state what is changing, why it matters and the signal that would confirm it. Use levels and events as decision points, not indefinite holds.

## The risk radar

The brief asks for the top five upside and top five downside risks, each with a trigger level or event, and checks Polymarket's highest-volume markets as a read on event risk. Risks fall into three groups:

- **Macro:** growth or inflation shocks, policy error, sovereign debt stress, war.
- **Market structure:** liquidity drying up, positioning unwinds, leverage cascades, ETF or stablecoin outflows. See [[Leverage and Liquidations]].
- **Credit:** refinancing stress, defaults, opaque private credit, duration blow-ups. See [[Rates and Credit]].

Useful triggers: curve inversion depth, high-yield spread breaks, breadth divergence, DXY and emerging-market currency breaks, oil spikes and VIX regime shifts. Citrini's June 2028 crash scenario is a worked downside case, kept as a scenario rather than a forecast.

## Process over outcome

- **Every system pays a characteristic cost.** CryptoCred: trend-following pays through false starts; momentum enters late and can buy tops or sell bottoms; mean reversion gets run over by an outsized move. The common failure is quitting a valid system because its normal cost looks like breakage.
- **Losing streaks are normal.** 0xaporia's streak table and "Decouple Process from Outcome" are on [[Trading Psychology]].
- **Restraint is an edge.** 0xaporia: poker players "accept variance as part of the game" and avoid "forcing action when odds or context are unfavorable". (from [[Crypto Takes]])
- **Execution beats prediction.** A correct top call can still lose through sizing, path, leverage or exits, while a mediocre entry can make a great trade. Public calls are a status game; a strategy has defined exposure, invalidation, costs and exits.
- **Backtests fit noise.** AI makes it cheap to re-optimise one history until noise looks like edge. Reserve unseen periods, test parameter sensitivity, and include fees, slippage and latency.

## Patience and cash

Most account damage comes from one asymmetry: acting too fast when growing capital and too slow when protecting it (see [[Trading Psychology]]). The answer is patience and dry powder.

- **Cash is an option.** "Cash = option / Option value is maximized by vol. / Inflation is option premium." Rewkang sees "some appeal of the optionality value of some cash to bid 40%+ wicks on alts". (from [[Business & Finance]])
- **Sit still.** Lord Ashdrake: "Sit on my ass, then a few times a year make massive bank then sit on my ass again". cobie replied: "took me like 8 years to learn this toh". (from [[Crypto Takes]])
- **Or sacrifice your weekends.** GiganticRebirth: "You should be sacrificing your weekends to give everything you have to harvesting all possible yield out of this market". Which advice fits depends on how rich the regime is.
- **A counterpoint.** "Capital Allocation Is Dead", listed in [[Markets - Articles]], challenges the premise by its title alone.
