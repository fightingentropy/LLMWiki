# Trading Philosophy

The principles that run through the trading notes: price follows perception rather than truth, regime and observable signal come before story, and process matters more than any single outcome. The wiki's own synthesis (regime first, signal over narrative, positioning extremes, observable triggers) sits next to quotes from [[CT|Crypto Takes]], the articles in [[Markets - Articles]] and the trading note in [[Markets - Trading]]. Where the notes disagree, both sides are kept: [[cobie]] on sitting still against [[GiganticRebirth]] on sacrificing weekends.

## Perception over truth

> Truth doesn't necessarily move [markets](https://youtu.be/rZ9rUh9WhwU?t=1876). Perception & Uncertainty/Certainty transition does.

(from [[Markets - Trading]])

Markets reward those who understand what other participants believe, not what is objectively true. The biggest moves come at transitions from uncertainty to certainty, or the reverse.

A related line in the Crypto Takes note sits under the heading "Reflexivity Creates Virtuous and Vicious Cycles" and gives the loop as a chain: `Price-Perception->Fundamentals-Price-Perception-Fundamentals` (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]).

## The commitment

### The prison

From [The Prison Of Financial Mediocrity](https://x.com/systematicls/status/2004900241745883205):

> "It is foolish to think that you can withdraw from the Exchange after you have tasted the sweetness of the honey. He who has entered the charmed circle of the Exchange is in eternal agitation and sits in a prison, the key of which lies in the ocean and the bars of which are never opened."

Once you enter trading, it consumes you. This is both warning and reality: understand the commitment required. (from [[Markets - Articles]])

### Stronger at the broken places

From the [story of Toto Wolff](https://www.bizofspeed.com/p/toto-wolff-turning-trauma-into-a?utm_campaign=post&utm_medium=twitter) (see [[Toto Wolff]]):

> "The world breaks everyone, and afterward, many are stronger at the broken places."

Trading breaks traders. The question is whether you emerge stronger at the broken places. This applies to both individual trades and psychological resilience. (from [[Markets - Articles]])

## Working practices

The daily-brief prompt in [[Markets - Brief]] is written in the style of [[Stanley Druckenmiller]]: "direct, high-conviction, PM-ready". The four practices below are the wiki's synthesis, and the brief's structure builds each one in. See [[Market Regime]] and [[Cross-Asset Analysis]] for the methods.

### Regime first

- Determine market regime before position-taking.
- Let regime assessment drive position sizing.
- Expect regime shifts to invalidate prior convictions.

The brief puts the regime call straight after the executive snapshot: a growth and inflation call, the liquidity backdrop, the volatility regime (suppressed, transitioning or unstable), and a confidence level of High, Medium or Low.

### Signal over narrative

- Focus on what is happening, not why it's happening.
- Observable confirmations matter more than predictions.
- Narratives are useful for crowd coordination, not price discovery.

The brief's own rules: "Prioritize signal over narrative" and "Separate facts, interpretation, and positioning implications."

[[HsakaTrades]]: "The orderflow frontruns the newsflow." In the note the line is followed by "- Marcus Aurelius". Price moves first; the narrative comes to justify it. (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]; see also [[Trading_Psychology]] on post-hoc narratives.)

The Trading note lists what to look for when reading the crypto market (from [[Markets - Trading]]):

- CT sentiment
- Crypto is momentum driven (meaning moving averages play a role and market structure is important)
- Macro
- Mechanical movements
- Liquidations
- Top traders' positioning
- Catalysts
- Stablecoin dominance
- Stablecoin yields
- Funding rates
- Options positioning

### Positioning for extremes

- Identify positioning extremes (consensus trades, squeeze risk).
- Trade mean reversion when positioning is extreme.
- Trade momentum when the regime is clear and consensus uncertain.

The brief asks for consensus positioning and crowded trades, dealer, gamma, CTA and systematic flow where available, and sentiment extremes and squeeze risk. See [[Positioning]].

### Specific, observable triggers

- Establish what would confirm or invalidate your view.
- Set specific levels and events as decision points.
- Avoid narrative-driven indefinite holding.

For each regime shift the brief wants what is changing, why it matters and the observable confirmation signal. Its risk radar asks for explicit trigger levels or events where possible.

## Process, variance and restraint

### Decouple process from outcome

[[0xaporia]] on variance: [Strategy](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/1894297482500141122) hopping is "one of the most under discussed issues on CT imo. It's mostly a flight from the grueling, nonlinear phase where most progress happens." People who hit a losing streak read it as proof the strategy is flawed instead of recognising that noise is part of the system. Traders who flee the nonlinear grind for new strategies are interpreting noise as signal. (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]])

His numbers for the chance of a losing streak of a given length at some point in 100 trades:

| Losing streak of | 50% win rate | 40% win rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 or more | approx. 80% | approx. 98% |
| 6 or more | approx. 55% | approx. 87% |
| 7 or more | approx. 32% | approx. 69% |
| 8 or more | approx. 17% | approx. 49% |

So streaks of 7 or more turn up 32 to 69 percent of the time in strategies with a 50 to 40 percent win rate. "Losing streaks are not rare events. Yet, humans are terrible at internalizing this." His analogies: "A surgeon with a 95% success rate will eventually lose multiple patients in a row. A poker player should focus on whether they made mathematically optimal decisions, not whether they won the hand." The post closes: "Decouple Process from Outcome."

### Poker discipline

[[0xaporia]]: [Poker](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/1894332130093629551) players "accept variance as part of the game", so they can fold strategically and avoid "forcing action when odds or context are unfavorable". Novice traders "often conflate constant activity with progress. Poker players know this not to be true." Restraint ("knowing you don't need to play every hand") is a structural edge. (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]])

### Every system pays a characteristic cost

A [post by @CryptoCred](https://x.com/CryptoCred/status/2079138151528890777) is the source the Trading note cites for this list (see [[CryptoCred]]):

- Trend-following pays through false starts.
- Momentum often enters late and can buy tops or sell bottoms.
- Mean reversion periodically gets overwhelmed by a genuinely outsized move.
- A recurring failure mode is abandoning a valid system because its unavoidable cost is mistaken for evidence that the system is broken.

Naming a system's expected pain does not validate it: a strategy still needs out-of-sample evidence and realistic costs; naming its expected pain does not validate it. (from [[Markets - Trading]])

## Speed, patience and capital

### Fast to grow, slow to protect

> you act too quickly when you are trying to grow capital, and act too slowly when you are trying to protect capital

The Crypto Takes note carries this under @cobie's handle with the lead-in "Someone once told Cobie". In a separate reply in the same note, cobie writes: "lak who said it but I remember a long time ago a tweet that said something like "you act too quickly when you are trying to grow capital, and you act too slowly when you are trying to protect capital" and it rly changed my life tbh". The note does not say who first wrote it.

The wiki's reading: most account destruction comes from this asymmetry, eager to size up and hesitant to cut. (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]; see [[Capital Allocation Timing]])

### Sit on your ass, or sacrifice your weekends

Lord Ashdrake, with a reply from [[cobie]]:

> What Traders think they like: "In and out every move, catch everything"
>
> What Traders should be like: "Sit on my ass, then a few times a year make massive bank then sit on my ass again"

cobie's reply: "took me like 8 years to learn this toh". (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]])

This directly contradicts [[GiganticRebirth]]:

> You should be sacrificing your weekends to give everything you have to harvesting all possible yield out of this market
>
> Oh, you're 22 and want to have fun with your friends?
>
> You will never have such easy opportunities for generational wealth even if you live another 80 years.

Both are in [[CT|Crypto Takes]]. The wiki's reading: which one applies depends on how rich the regime is.

## Evidence and execution

### Backtesting without hindsight

AI can remove clerical friction, but it also makes it cheap to keep optimizing the same history until noise looks intentional. Keep research and evaluation separate, reserve unseen periods, test parameter sensitivity, and include fees, slippage, latency, and risk. See [[0xaporia]] and [[Trading Evidence and Review Framework]].

- [0xaporia's post on AI backtesting](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/2092917529597321394) separates repetitive implementation work from the learning needed to spot look-ahead bias, overfitting and path dependence. Removing losing weekdays after seeing the results, or repeatedly asking an agent to improve the same historical sample, can fit noise rather than find a robust edge. The profit screenshot it quotes is not evidence of a reproducible strategy.
- The [Stanford pairs-trading study](https://web.stanford.edu/class/msande448/2020/Final_reports/gr1.pdf) is a negative-result example: changing one rolling window can reverse the result on the same stock and day. The source post, from [@RuujSs](https://x.com/RuujSs/status/2081397093919236257), framed it as a "live trading playbook"; read it as an overfitting lesson. Parameter sensitivity is something to test out of sample, not a guarantee of an edge.

(from [[X-Bookmarks]] and [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]])

### Execution over predictions

An exact top or bottom call can still lose money through sizing, leverage, path, or exit mistakes. A mediocre entry can support a good trade when exposure, invalidation, costs, and exits are coherent. Public predictions and profitable execution are separate scoreboards: a top-and-bottom call is a status game, a strategy has defined exposure, invalidation, costs and exits. The [@blknoiz06 post on execution](https://x.com/blknoiz06/status/2091151900729115029) makes the point, and its [original post](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/2091034462561706287) is by @0xaporia. (from [[X-Bookmarks]])
