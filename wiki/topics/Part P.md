# Part P

This page concerns **England**. Part P of the Building Regulations is a statutory electrical-safety requirement; **Approved Document P** is guidance on satisfying it. The page sets out what Part P covers, which work is notifiable, and how certification, notification and competent person schemes differ.

## Law and guidance

- Part P itself is law. Approved Document P is guidance on how to meet it.
- Correction, 22 September 2026: Part P itself is statutory, not guidance, and its scope is not uniform across the UK.
- England's notification categories do not automatically apply to the other UK jurisdictions. Check their own rules.
- [[Building Regulations]] separates legislation from technical guidance. [[BS 7671]] supplies the technical requirements for the installation itself.

## Scope

- [GOV.UK's Approved Document P](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-approved-document-p), sections 2–3 of the 2013 document, covers dwellings and specified associated installations. That includes some business premises sharing a dwelling's electricity meter. (from [[Electrical Evidence Review - September 2026]])
- The Resources note's shorthand is that Part P "applies where people permanently reside (dwellings)." (from [[Electrics - Resources]]) The official scope is wider than that line: it also reaches the specified associated installations.

## Notification

- Notifiable work includes new circuits, consumer-unit replacement, and additions or alterations within defined special locations.
- Being in a kitchen alone does not make all work notifiable.
- Non-notifiable work still has to comply.
- Special-location boundaries are defined in the official document; check them for the installation in question.

## Certificates, notification and registration

- Choose the applicable certification and notification route before work begins.
- An installation certificate and a Building Control notification serve different purposes.
- Holding qualifications alone does not establish registration in a competent person scheme, which [GOV.UK guidance of 1 July 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/self-certification-schemes-mandatory-technical-competence-requirements) calls a self-certification scheme.
- [[EIC and MEIWC]] covers installation certificates. [[EICR]] covers condition reporting on an existing installation.
