# Neurodiversity

Neurodiversity is the umbrella for cognitive variation: ADHD, autism, and adjacent neurotypes. Erlin's notes treat it less as a diagnostic category and more as a cognitive style with social consequences. The recurring claim is that neurodivergent minds are less filtered by consensus and more oriented to whether a thing is true. The clinical side of ADHD lives on [[ADHD]]; this page holds the cognitive-style takes and the links behind them.

These are takes and saved posts about cognitive style and social experience, not universal traits, diagnostic criteria, or measured population comparisons.

## Truth filter versus consensus filter

A take saved in [[Psychology|Psychology & Behavior]] splits people by how they process new information. It says people who cannot defend themselves physically ("women and low T men") parse information through a consensus filter as a safety mechanism:

> They literally do not ask "is this true", they ask "will others be OK with me thinking this is true".

Its counterpart is the free-thinking advantage:

> Only high T alpha males and aneurotypical people (hey autists!) are actually free to parse new information with an objective "is this true?" filter.

(from [[Psychology|Psychology & Behavior]])

- **Manufactured consensus.** The take says consensus-filtered thinkers are "very malleable to brute force manufactured consensus": if every screen they look at says the same thing, their brain reads it as everyone in the tribe believing it.
- **Republic of free thinkers.** Its political conclusion: "This is why a Republic of high status males is best for decision making. Democratic, but a democracy only for those who are free to think." See [[Decision-Making]].
- **Cognitive-style asymmetry.** Read together with the autism lines below, the wiki's synthesis is that neurodivergent thinkers act as anomaly detectors where consensus-filtered cognition fails: socially costly, epistemically valuable.

## Autism and integrity

Two lines from [[Thoughts|Thoughts & Observations]]:

> As I get older, I realize more and more that the best thing to do in life is to find autistic people and surround yourself with them

> the correlation between autism and traits like honor and integrity is astronomically high relative to the general population.

The wiki's shorthand for the pair, not a line from the note: autists don't do social theater; they do truth. The same lines are summarised on [[Thoughts and Observations]].

## ADHD and friendship

- **No friendship degradation.** A saved [post](https://x.com/ADHDForReal/status/1871828303029342363) says: "one thing i really love about ADHD people is how y'all simply don't have friendship degradation mechanics. i can text an ADHD person after months or even years of radio silence and just pick right back up where we left off" (from [[Psychology|Psychology & Behavior]]; see also [[ADHD]]).
- It is a positive social trait that cuts against the usual stereotypes of ADHD.

## ADHD: disorder, phenotype, or body clock

Health claims in this section are framings and early evidence; diagnosis and treatment belong with a clinician.

- **The circadian reframing.** [[Medical|Medical & Health]] saves a [post](https://x.com/NTFabiano/status/2020162108919357483) that opens: "We recently reconceptualized ADHD as a circadian rhythm disorder."
- **The subgroup version.** Brandon Luu's [thread](https://x.com/BrandonLuuMD/status/2102338491942347175) links ADHD to later biological sleep/wake timing. The explanation it quotes and the Luu and Fabiano [2025 perspective](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1697900/full) cite delayed sleep/wake timing in up to 78%, sleep disturbances in up to 80% of adults and 82% of children, and an evening melatonin rise about 45 minutes later in children and 90 minutes later in adults. Their proposed measures (screening for sleep and circadian problems, a consistent wake time, morning bright light, less evening light and screen exposure, regular routines, selective low-dose melatonin when delayed timing is found) are additions to standard ADHD care, with treatment evidence still developing (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]]).
- **Correction, 22 September 2026:** the earlier universal redefinition is qualified. The perspective identifies a substantial subgroup with circadian dysfunction and does not reclassify ADHD as exclusively circadian. See [[ADHD]] and [[Circadian_Rhythm|Circadian Rhythm]] for the subgroup distinction, and [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]] for the source trail.
- **The evolutionary story.** A saved [post](https://x.com/alexeixbt/status/2102822521510789146) frames ADHD as a hunter-gatherer phenotype. Read it as a story, not medical fact: NIMH describes ADHD as a developmental disorder, and the evolutionary story does not establish absence of impairment or remove treatment needs (from [[X Bookmark Review - 30 September 2026]]).
