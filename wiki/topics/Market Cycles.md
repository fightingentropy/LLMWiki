# Market Cycles

Market cycles are the recurring boom-bust and seasonal patterns in prices and participation. The material here leans heavily on crypto and comes mostly from the trader commentary in [[CT|Crypto Takes]]; [[Crypto Trading]] draws on it. It explains cycles through three lenses: supply dilution against capital inflows, who is in the market (traders against traders, or new money buying every paycheck), and the crowd's swing between idealism and cynicism. The frames overlap and are not reconciled: Rhea's 1935 bear phases, cobie's supercycle and GCR's inverse supercycle sit side by side.

## How bull markets end

- **Dilution kills bulls.** [[Pentosh1]]: "The end simply comes when the dilution exceeds the amount of capital inflows." In the fuller passage the market "needs some astronomical inflows daily just to support the current prices bc of daily unlocks", and "Dilution is the bull market killer. Supply is infinite in this market as we dream up a million new coins a day" ([post](https://twitter.com/Pentosh1/status/1779586050597249472); from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]).
- **Greed in the back half.** The same post: "The largest gains come in the back half of the bull run as greed completely takes over." "Greed is the parabola you sell into little by little." "Once there are too many bulls, there's no-one left to buy."
- **Take life-changing money out.** [[Pentosh1]] warns that experienced traders with better systems and information collect the money of newer ones "towards the end of the cycle": "One day the music stops, and you won't have a chair." His advice: "If you make life changing money. Take it out of the market." and "Secure it, then start over with just a bit less." He says it "is not a bear post" ([post](https://x.com/Pentosh1/status/1740042952108388405); from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]).

## Supercycle or inverse supercycle

- [[cobie]]: "A decade long supercycle bubble of specualtive cryptocoins as a siren for the market top of the multi-decade Everything Bubble" (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]).
- [[GCRClassic]] answers a post asking whether permabears "recognize this is the same as saying supercycle as they say it's going to zero?": "been making this point in chats inverse supercycle is crypto going to 0". His reasons: gambling is one of the largest human use cases; gen z prefers memes to growth stocks; crypto is the most addictive financial vice and "addictions parabolic"; more UBI, pandemics and joblessness (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]).
- **Disputed reading.** [[Crypto Trading]] and [[GCRClassic]] read this as a call that crypto goes to zero as gen z chases memes over growth stocks. The reasons GCR lists are demand drivers, so the post can also be read as saying the zero camp is making the supercycle case in reverse. The note does not settle it.

## Who is in the market: PvP and PvE

[[Rewkang]] separates PvP conditions, where traders mostly trade against each other, from PvE conditions, where retail money arrives and keeps buying. "There are certain heuristics that work in PvP market conditions that don't apply as much in PvE market conditions" (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]):

- "The most obvious is fading high funding/up only price action. In bull markets funding can stay higher than shorts can remain solvent"
- New money "does not schizo trade like crypto traders. They pick their favorite meme coins and just keep sending every paycheck in to continue buying every day/week." Traders who short memecoins because they are "being rotated out of" eventually get blown out, as those who faded AXS, ADA and LUNA did the cycle before.
- "To outperform like retail you need to think like retail"

A line beside [[thiccythot]]'s handle in the note adds that price discovery moves "from blunt proxies to more precise expressions with each wave", and "you see it with crypto too with each cycle becoming more selective with asset quality" (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]).

## Cycle psychology

- **First-cycle participants.** [[CryptoCred]]: "First cycle participants make money because they're open-minded, optimistic, and think "What if I'm right?" instead of "What if I'm wrong?"" They "lose most of their gains for the same reasons". "Navigating cycles requires switching between childlike idealism and sagely cynicism" (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]).
- **Bear-market habits.** Lines under [[CryptoCred]]'s handle in the note: "One argument for trading less actively when markets suck is that you don't learn habits that cost you money in bull markets." "Bear market PTSD is real." "Fading breakouts, rotating too quickly and being unable to hold a position, unbridled cynicism etc. are all hard to unlearn" (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]).
- The wider psychology lives in [[Trading_Psychology|Trading Psychology]] and [[Behavioral_Finance|Behavioral Finance]].

## Bear-market structure

Robert Rhea, 1935, quoted in [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]] and carried in [[Markets]]:

> "Bear markets seem to be divided into three phases: the first being the abandonment of hopes upon which the uprush of the preceding bull market was predicated; the second being the reflection of the decreased earning power and reduction of dividends; the third representing distress liquidation of securities which must be sold to meet living expenses. Each of these phases seems to be divided by a secondary reaction which is often erroneously assumed to be the beginning of a bull market."

The note adds: "secondary reaction is a bear market rally".

## Turning points and reflexivity

- **Reflexive pumps.** "Reflexive pumps don't begin with a wave of buyers, they start with no one left willing to sell. A stubborn holder base that refuses to let go triggers a synthetic supply shock. Aggro-rage shorts pile in. A disbelief rally erupts, fuelled by sideline capital waiting on a nuke that never shows. FOMO kicks in, chasing price into thin air, with zero marginal sellers and infinite shorts left to cover. Parabola and attention games begin." (from [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]])
- **The reflexive loop.** A heading in the crypto note: "Reflexivity Creates Virtuous and Vicious Cycles", with the loop "Price-Perception->Fundamentals-Price-Perception-Fundamentals" (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]).
- **Reflexivity at all-time highs.** A passage linked to a [[Rewkang]] post: "There is an element of reflexivity around ATH corrections. People sell because they see it's always rejected and then more people follow when it actually happens." Short-term flows ("$10B+ shizo day traders") overwhelm passive flows ("$500m+ ETF buying"). "Eventually, passive flows win out." (from [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]])
- **A bubble needs a misconception.** Citrini of [[Citrini Research]] separates an expensive asset from a bubble in the Soros sense: a real trend and a misconception about it reinforce each other until perception and fundamentals cannot stay aligned. A top call has to name the misconception and the catalyst that breaks it; this is a thesis test, not proof that prices are justified (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]]; see [[AI Infrastructure Economics]]).
- **Crashes carry no timing signal.** Alex Krüger studied altcoin crashes of more than 25% in a day using ADA: "crashes may come in the middle of a bull run, at the top, or at the bottom. That is right. There's no signal." (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]])
- **Bottoms and second legs.** Husslin's reasons a flush makes a good bottom: algos closing longs and cutting leverage, liquidations resetting positioning, funds forced to open shorts to manage risk, market makers pulling bids and asks so books are thin, and people afraid to catch knives. Then: "Second leg up is always the slowest grind. There's still supply here from last few days + ppl aren't fully convinced it's a bottom just yet" (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]; see [[Liquidations]]).

## Seasonality

[[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]] keeps a calendar of U.S. trading activity and volume:

- "For the U.S. markets the year really begins in September. It's the end of summer, the kids are back in school, and traders are back at their desks."
- The last week of August has exceptionally low volume. The lull often stretches past Labor Day to the September FOMC meeting, a day "sure to see wild market gyrations".
- Activity stays robust until Christmas, with a brief lull the day after Thanksgiving. Christmas to New Year is quiet.
- Volume picks up after January 1 and stays seasonally strong until "Sell in May and Go Away" as summer approaches.
- "Trading activity reaches its nadir the week after July 4".
- Why it matters: "low volume can often lead to unexpected market movements that don't follow typical patterns."

Correction, 15 October 2026: this page earlier gave the strong stretch as January to October. The note puts it at January to "Sell in May" and from the September FOMC to Christmas, and it describes trading volume, not returns.

## Equities ahead of the physical cycle

Jukan asks whether memory shares can peak three or four quarters before memory prices, as announced fab capacity, including a 2028 supply response, enters the horizon. SK hynix's Yongin investment and its June 2026 supply explanation support tracking the capacity response, not the post's market-timing conclusion. Treat it as a falsifiable scenario, not a fixed lead-time rule. The evidence to watch: capex, clean-room and wafer-start timing, product mix, utilization, contract-price revisions, and whether HBM's larger wafer requirements absorb nominal capacity. The operating detail lives in [[Equities]] (from [[X-Bookmarks]] and [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]]).
