# AI Infrastructure Economics

The current sources contain both the infrastructure bull case and the valuation bear case. They are scenarios to test, not a resolved forecast.

## Demand thesis

AI compute demand can grow through two separate variables: more users and more tokens per user. Coding-agent infrastructure may also spread beyond programmers into ordinary knowledge work. The relevant evidence is paid workflow adoption, verified task completion, integration cost, and whether usage becomes durable rather than experimental. (from [[X-Bookmarks]])

## Cost and valuation thesis

Frontier labs may face repeated training and serving investment while cheaper models constrain pricing and application discovery lags. Large compute commitments are visible before the revenue and margins that justify them. Open models may redistribute profit without reducing aggregate compute demand. These are valuation sensitivities, not proof that the sector succeeds or fails.

## Physical constraints

Data-centre economics depend on electricity generation, transmission, interconnection queues, congestion, contracts, regional pricing, and delivery timing. Practitioner primers should be checked against grid operators, regulators, utilities, project filings, and actual power contracts.

## Questions to track

- Which workflows generate recurring paid demand?
- Can cash generation keep pace with training, serving, and capacity commitments?
- Where do integration, power, data, and verification bottlenecks remain?
- Who captures value when model costs fall: labs, infrastructure, applications, or users?
- When does capacity become overbuild and margin pressure?

## September 2026 scenarios

Two new speculative branches extend the existing questions: an efficient-model shift away from hyperscaling that might redirect capital toward Treasuries, and a private-frontier/wet-lab thesis about where model-enabled discoveries could be monetised. Neither is established by the retained posts. Track the technical, commercial, and policy premises separately before drawing a valuation conclusion. (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]] and [[Bookmarks - AI & Agents]])
