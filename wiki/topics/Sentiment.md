# Sentiment

Market sentiment is the aggregate emotion and conviction of participants. Extremes in sentiment often coincide with market inflections. Erlin's trading note puts "CT Sentiment" first on its list of things to look for, and this wiki holds a large set of crypto-trader takes on how narratives form, peak and mislead.

## Perception over truth

The trading note opens with this line (from [[Markets - Trading]]):

> "Truth doesn't necessarily move [markets](https://youtu.be/rZ9rUh9WhwU?t=1876). Perception & Uncertainty/Certainty transition does."

[[Trading Philosophy]] builds on the same idea: perception matters more than truth. An unattributed line in the crypto note puts it as a loop (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]):

> "Reflexivity Creates Virtuous and Vicious Cycles. Price-Perception->Fundamentals-Price-Perception-Fundamentals"

See [[Market Drivers]] for sentiment as one driver among several.

## Sentiment indicators

### Crypto sentiment (CT)

- **Crypto Twitter sentiment**: the collective view on direction and catalysts.
- **Narrative dominance**: which story is winning, the bull case or the bear case.
- **Influencer positioning**: what popular traders are saying. [[Traders Directory]] lists the key sentiment makers and influencers.
- **Retail positioning**: follower count and engagement as a gauge of retail conviction.

See [[Crypto Trading]] for how this fits a crypto process.

### Positioning extremes

When many participants are on the same side:

- **Consensus long**: everyone owns the asset, so there are no buyers left and it is vulnerable to pullbacks.
- **Consensus short**: everyone is short, so there is squeeze risk if they are wrong.
- **No consensus**: an inflection period; markets are uncertain.

[[Pentosh1]] on the same mechanic at a cycle top ([post](https://twitter.com/Pentosh1/status/1779586050597249472)):

> "The largest gains come in the back half of the bull run as greed completely takes over. The only way to survive is fear at that point … Be fearful, when others are greedy. Greed is the parabola you sell into little by little … Once there are too many bulls, there's no-one left to buy."

See [[Positioning]] for the data side.

### Sentiment reversals

Market reversals often occur when:

- **Bullish extreme**: headlines are at their most optimistic; a final push up, then a reversal down.
- **Bearish extreme**: headlines are at their most pessimistic; capitulation, then a reversal up.
- **Narrative shift**: the story changes (rate cut to rate hike) and sentiment whipsaws.

@Husslin_ on why washed-out spots make bottoms ([post](https://twitter.com/Husslin_/status/1781147687103893882)):

> "Algos mindlessly closing longs and reducing leverage in mkt. Liqs reset positioning. Funds forced to open shorts to manage risk. Market makers pulling bids/asks so books are piss thin rn. Ppl afraid to catch knives here"

And on the disbelief that follows ([post](https://twitter.com/Husslin_/status/1781349288401846364)):

> "Second leg up is always the slowest grind. There’s still supply here from last few days + ppl aren’t fully convinced it’s a bottom just yet. Next leg up and onwards should be smooth sailing given most longs from higher have been liquidated, yet shorts haven’t capitulated"

[[Liquidations]] covers the forced-selling mechanics behind these resets.

### Cycle psychology

[[CryptoCred]] (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]):

> "First cycle participants make money because they're open-minded, optimistic, and think 'What if I'm right?' instead of 'What if I'm wrong?' They lose most of their gains for the same reasons. Navigating cycles requires switching between childlike idealism and sagely cynicism"

See [[Market Cycles]].

## Measurement

Sentiment is subjective and hard to quantify, but proxies include:

- **Put/call ratios**: options sentiment.
- **Surveys**: Investors Intelligence, AAII.
- **Social media**: Twitter sentiment, Reddit positioning.
- **Positioning data**: Commitment of Traders (COT) reports.
- **Extremes**: when everyone agrees, look for a reversal.

The trading note's checklist sets CT sentiment beside top traders' positioning, [[Funding Rates]], options positioning, liquidations, stablecoin dominance and catalysts (from [[Markets - Trading]]).

## Cautions

- **Self-fulfilling**: sentiment-driven moves do not need fundamentals to justify them.
- **Tricky timing**: sentiment can stay extreme for longer than expected. [[Rewkang]]: "In bull markets funding can stay higher than shorts can remain solvent" (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]).
- **Narrative trap**: a hot narrative can keep you on the wrong side.
- **Reversal vs. continuation**: a sentiment extreme does not guarantee a reversal; confirm with technicals or regime.

## Narrative dynamics

### Post-hoc hallucination

[[CL207]] (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]):

> "'whats the next narrative' — my trader in christ anytime price moves 10% we will literally hallucinate a story up for it"

Narratives are backfit to price.

### Schelling-point narratives

@lightcrypto (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]):

> "The market now lacks a viable schelling point narrative. Yet its desire for one is palpable. It wants to make back what it lost. We will suspend disbelief in an effort to do so once a vehicle emerges to carry this latent desire. In the wreckage are nascent roots."

Narratives can also be a required coordination mechanism for a rally.

**Contradiction:** CL207 says narratives are invented after the move; @lightcrypto says a rally needs one before it can run. Both can be true: markets hallucinate short-term stories after the fact *and* need a dominant narrative to sustain a cycle.

### Narrative cycle tops

The crypto note saves @Stoiiic's [Boom-Bust Sequence](https://twitter.com/Stoiiic/status/1749350221925040219), which compares narrative momentum with price, for its reading of narrative cycle tops in particular.

### Normies as a top signal, inverted

@0xSisyphus (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]):

> "Normies showing up isn't a top signal. When they start quitting their IRL jobs for crypto or starting funds that's when we need to worry"

### When the tide goes out

[[GCRClassic]] (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]):

> "celsius, terraform, etc are just inevitable blowups of a macro unwinding, long prophesied by a handful of augurs. there used to be a boomer meme along the lines of: when the tide goes out, you see who is swimming naked"

When macro unwinds, latent frauds reveal themselves. See [[Liquidations]].

### Everything is an indicator

[[trading_axe]] parodies contrarian sentiment reading, abridged (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]):

> "When you've been trading for so long, eventually EVERYTHING IN YOUR LIFE becomes an INDICATOR. I'm shopping and see TOO MANY bananas in random people's carts? I'm COUNTER TRADING them and buying apples instead … I'm fucking WALKING. COUNTER-TRADE that shit. The list goes on and on. EVERYTHING IS AN INDICATOR."

### Flex culture as a contra-indicator

The note's FTX passage on "the EVIL EYE/NAZAR effect" sits under [[CryptoCred]]'s handle. It says the loudest traders who "ROUTINELY flexed their PnL's all over Twitter" were mainly on FTX, and that envy from onlookers fed the collapse: "The combined EVIL EYE of all these onlookers contributed to the GRAND COLLAPSE of FTX." Public PnL flexing attracts jealousy that corrodes.

[[trading_axe]]: "NEVER FLEX. EVER. Hide what you have."

[@coinfessions](https://twitter.com/coinfessions): "The worst thing you can do in the crypto space is telling your irl “friends” your achievements. 99% of the people I thought were real ones were jealous fucks. Trust no one and learn to keep your mouth shut."

See [[Trading_Psychology|Trading Psychology]].
