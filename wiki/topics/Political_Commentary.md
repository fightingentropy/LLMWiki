# Political Commentary

Erlin's notes on politics are takes, not policy analysis: who controls the news, why some stories go uncovered, what a country's system really is once the labels come off, and how a civilisation decays. Most of the material comes from the [[Politics]] note, with a clip from [[Top Pods]] and essays saved in [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]]. The recurring move is to judge a system by its incentives and outcomes, not its rhetoric. The deeper frames sit in [[Political Philosophy]] and [[Entropy vs Extropy]]; the hostile racial and religious material is assessed in [[Online Extremism and Evidence]].

## Media capture by elites

> Certain ultra wealthy rich have slowly politicised the news over the past decades in the way of brainwashing the population into believing their agenda.

(from [[Politics]])

The argument, as the note lays it out:

- Wealthy owners possibly run multiple competing channels, left- and right-leaning, so viewers who leave one land in another inside a controlled spectrum.
- This creates an illusion of choice while editorial control stays in the same hands.
- Entertainment replaces the news and educational parts of the news.
- The end goal is brainwashing the population into elite agendas.

All channels funnel toward the same conclusions, and the audience thinks it chose the truth. A [YouTube video](https://youtu.be/BDREZmpkIz8) sits at the head of the note, above this take. See [[Media Critique]].

## Conspiracy and narrative control

> That the government creates or at least promotes crazy conspiracy theories to make the true ones seem crazy by association.

(from [[Politics]])

This is **discrediting by association**: mix true and false to poison both.

Two saved items sit on that line between real events and conspiracy narrative:

- A [post saved in the note](https://x.com/Villgecrazylady/status/1933512614740419061) contrasts Israel's years of covert preparation (a hidden drone base inside Iran, the pager attack on Hezbollah) with being caught off guard by Hamas on 7 October. It claims the warning systems all failed at once, the response took six hours and destroyed homes and cars with Israeli citizens inside, and ends in sarcastic disbelief: "Sure thing". It insinuates rather than evidences. _(@Villgecrazylady)_
- A [bookmark on the Las Vegas suspected-biolab case](https://x.com/catgirlprostate/status/2092948078055579817) frames two alleged Israeli-run US labs with COVID samples as a suppressed conspiracy and claims all federal pathogen charges against Ori Solomon were dropped. Local reporting ([FOX5 Las Vegas](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/05/26/federal-gun-complaint-dropped-against-man-tied-las-vegas-bio-lab-case/), [KTNV](https://www.ktnv.com/news/crime/federal-charges-for-property-manager-connected-to-las-vegas-illegal-biolab-dropped)) says the dismissed federal complaint was a firearms case, dismissed without prejudice, while a Nevada hazardous-waste matter remained pending. It shows a real investigation turned into an ethnic conspiracy narrative; check the live court status before citing it. (from [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]])

## Iran uprising and Western media

A long passage in the note, linked to a [post by @DeTahmineh](https://x.com/DeTahmineh/status/2009680255091405074), argues that Western liberal media ignores the Iranian uprising because "the Iranian people are rebelling against Islam itself, and that fact shatters the moral framework through which these institutions understand the world." (from [[Politics]])

- **Root problem.** Western media has "racialized" Islam, treating it as ethnicity, race or identity rather than a belief system or ideology, "as if Islam were a skin color rather than a doctrine."
- **Consequences.**
  - Criticising Islam is framed as attacking "brown people".
  - Iranians are erased: Persian, not Arab, with a distinct history.
  - Millions are stripped of the ability to reject the ideology.
  - Iranian protesters become unintelligible to a Western audience.
- **Economic dimension.** Iran's state-controlled economy (price controls, subsidies, nationalisation) shows such systems fail catastrophically. Admitting this undermines Western institutions that advocate softer versions of the same policies.
- **The real threat.** Acknowledging Iran means abandoning lazy moral categories: oppressor and colonizer versus oppressed and colonized. Iran shows that authoritarianism is not a Western invention; it is what societies actively try to escape.

The passage concludes that this terrifies Western institutions, hence the deliberate erasure of the uprising. Its last line: "So the silence continues."

## UK: nominally capitalist, functionally communist

The note opens the comparison with a claim: "The UK has a far flatter income distribution than the Communist Soviet Union." It then compares net income at two points on the pay scale (from [[Politics]]):

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| UK (2025) minimum wage, full time at 40 hours a week, take-home | £22,555 |
| UK (2025) £100k salary, take-home | £68,558 |
| UK net income ratio | **3.04:1** |
| USSR, same comparison | **never fell below 5:1** |

> We are now at the point where the wage compression and taxes in the UK means that the difference between minimum wage and a top 5% salary is a net income difference of only-3x.

> It's actually even worse in reality because the person earning £100k in the UK often has student loans.

> Britain is nominally capitalist but functionally communist. China is nominally communist but functionally capitalist. Funny how that works.

The reading: Britain has private ownership and markets but extreme wage compression and redistribution. China has a communist state ideology but creates wealth through capital markets. System labels are not actual function. Follow the incentives and outcomes, not the rhetoric. The ratio rests on two take-home figures, not a full distribution measure.

For how class and power form under communist regimes in practice, the bookmarks keep [The New Class by Milovan Djilas](https://x.com/The_Davos_Man/status/2032142165262970992).

## Sovereignty, wealth and state power

The note heads its UK material "Sovereignty is a distressed asset" and links a [post by @howdymerry](https://x.com/howdymerry/status/2010407057313202406) under that heading. When governments cannot maintain basic property rights, wage justice or economic coherence, sovereignty itself becomes distressed. The potential consequences are capital flight, brain drain and institutional capture.

Saved essays that extend the theme (from [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]]):

- **Asset inflation and populism.** Currency debasement plus unequal asset ownership drives populist demand for redistribution. Kyla Scanlon's essay makes a related case: owning appreciating assets now pays more than labour, which widens the generational divide and pushes people toward speculation.
- **State leverage over companies.** Derek Thompson on what government leverage over AI companies means for private-property rights and institutions.
- **Dismantling a criminal economy.** Nayib Bukele says advisers warned that rapid suppression of gangs could hurt consumption that illicit income supported, but El Salvador accepted the risk and the economy still grew. This is his own account, not a causal evaluation, and his security model involves mass detention and suspended civil liberties.
- **Government by scale.** A heuristic attributed to [[Nassim Taleb]]: communal sharing within the family, mutual provision among friends and community, stronger formal government at state level, and more liberty at federal level. The attribution is not yet traced to Taleb's own wording.

## Decline and the fall of empire

[[Top Pods]] saves an [[Andrew Tate]] clip labelled "Tate about Fall of Empire (Second Half)". The title names a fall-of-empire theme, read here as applying to the contemporary West; the note gives no summary of the clip.

It pairs with [[Curtis_Yarvin|Curtis Yarvin]]'s line "the left is entropic the right is extropic". [[Culture & Observations]] places it in his February 2026 interview on Peter McCormack's podcast and gives the fuller version as "something like":

> Fundamentally it's entropy versus extropy. The left is entropic, so things can just decay. But if you're building something, you need to build.

Read together: the institutions dissolve first, the roles they held in place dissolve second, and the men who filled those roles get blamed for the dissolution. See [[Entropy vs Extropy]] and [[Masculinity_and_Men|Masculinity & Men]].

## COVID-19 origins: positions versus findings

The White House presents a laboratory or research-related accident in Wuhan as the most likely origin of COVID-19, drawing on the House Select Subcommittee's 2024 report. That is the administration's official position, not a settled scientific conclusion. (from [[Politics]])

| Who | Position |
|---|---|
| White House | A lab or research-related accident is the most likely origin. Official position. |
| WHO scientific advisory group (report of 27 June 2025) | The weight of available evidence suggests zoonotic spillover. Every hypothesis stays open because important early data remain unavailable. |
| US intelligence community | Natural exposure and a laboratory-associated incident are both plausible. Agencies are divided, most at low confidence. |

## Separatism and demographic rhetoric

A [reply by @itispsychomath](https://x.com/itispsychomath/status/2082206097029595410) argues that racial solidarity and territorial balkanization are the only path to political victory, naming the Pacific Northwest, Great Lakes and New England as logistically viable areas. It is kept as an example of explicit online racial-separatist advocacy: not neutral demographic analysis, not a demonstrated political strategy, and not endorsed here. (from [[Politics]])

Related demographic, replacement, genetics and ethnic-bloc claims are reviewed in [[Online Extremism and Evidence]]. Two bookmarks give the evidence side (from [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]]):

- **Ethnic blocs.** A post argues that falling trust in heterogeneous societies pushes in-group political coordination and top-down state management. A [meta-analysis of 87 studies](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-polisci-052918-020708) found a modest negative diversity–trust link, strongest locally; the slide to hardened ethnic blocs and authoritarian technocracy is contested and unproven.
- **"Replacement Migration".** The UN's 2000 report is a scenario study of how migration levels would affect population decline and ageing. It is not evidence of a coordinated replacement plan.
