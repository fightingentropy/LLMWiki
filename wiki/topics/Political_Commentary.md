# Political Commentary

Takes on politics rather than policy analysis: who shapes the news, what a system does once the labels come off, and how a society decays. The recurring move is to judge a system by its incentives and outcomes, not its rhetoric. [Read the original note](/source/1e920f952b4bc3be85e6).

Most of the material is opinion from X posts. The posts about race, religion and ethnicity are hostile rhetoric: they show how such claims are built and carry no weight as evidence about any group.

## Follow function, not labels

A saved post compares take-home pay at two points on the UK scale:

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Full-time minimum wage, take-home | £22,555 |
| £100k salary, take-home | £68,558 |
| UK net ratio | 3.04:1 |
| USSR, same comparison | never below 5:1 |

> Britain is nominally capitalist but functionally communist. China is nominally communist but functionally capitalist.
> Funny how that works.

The ratio rests on two take-home figures, not a full distribution. It sits under the heading "Sovereignty is a distressed asset", with a [post by @howdymerry](https://x.com/howdymerry/status/2010407057313202406): a state that cannot keep property rights and economic coherence invites capital flight, brain drain and capture. Milovan Djilas's *The New Class* is saved for how class forms under communist rule.

Related essays in [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]] extend the theme: currency debasement plus unequal asset ownership feeds populist demand for redistribution; Kyla Scanlon on an economy that pays owners of appreciating assets more than workers; Derek Thompson on state leverage over AI companies; Nayib Bukele's own account of crushing El Salvador's gang economy; and a scale rule attributed to [[Nassim Taleb]]: sharing in the family, mutual help in the community, government at state level, liberty at federal level.

## Who shapes the news

> Certain ultra wealthy rich have slowly politicised the news over the past decades in the way of brainwashing the population into believing their agenda.

The theory: owners run left- and right-leaning channels so viewers who leave one land in another inside a chosen spectrum, and entertainment crowds out news and education. A [YouTube video](https://youtu.be/BDREZmpkIz8) sits above the take. Its companion:

> That the government creates or at least promotes crazy conspiracy theories to make the true ones seem crazy by association.

Mixing true and false poisons both. The same move runs in reverse online, where a real document lends credibility to a false caption (below). Saved posts show narrative winning elsewhere too: Roman Storm on prosecutors turning a defendant's search history into a story, and an essay on language models as consensus amplifiers, where convenience makes repetition feel like verification. The lesson is to trace a claim back to its evidence. Why story beats fact is on [[Philosophy & Meaning]].

## Iran and Western media

A long [post by @DeTahmineh](https://x.com/DeTahmineh/status/2009680255091405074) argues that Western liberal media ignores the Iranian uprising because "the Iranian people are rebelling against Islam itself, and that fact shatters the moral framework through which these institutions understand the world."

- **Islam racialised.** It is treated "as if Islam were a skin color rather than a doctrine", so criticising it reads as attacking "brown people".
- **Iranians erased.** They are Persian, not Arab, and treating Islam as identity strips them of the right to reject it.
- **Central planning failed.** Price controls, subsidies and nationalisation "have been tried. They have failed. Catastrophically."
- **Categories break.** Iran shows that authoritarianism "is not a Western invention imposed from outside, but something many societies are actively trying to escape."

It is an argument about motive, not a study of coverage.

## How hostile group claims are built

The saved posts repeat a few moves. Each borrows the credibility of something real for a claim it does not support.

- **Real event to conspiracy.** A Las Vegas suspected-biolab case is recast as an Israeli-run COVID plot with all pathogen charges dropped; local reporting says the dismissed federal complaint was a firearms case.
- **Association to secret coordination.** A real Robert Trivers email to Jeffrey Epstein becomes proof that Epstein funded a movement; a historical link between Chiliad and the FBI becomes decades of mass data-mining; mesirah is posed as a ban on reporting crimes, though rabbinic bodies say it does not cover abuse.
- **Anecdote to group rule.** One edited street interview, subway clip, cooking video or parent becomes a verdict on a religion, race or sex.
- **Unnamed biology to racial diagnosis.** An allele, IQ figure or crime rate with no study, dataset or definition. A real 2026 *Nature* ancient-DNA paper gets an intelligence gloss its authors warn against.
- **Scenario to plan.** The UN's 2000 "Replacement Migration" report is a scenario study, not a plan. A meta-analysis of 87 studies finds a modest negative link between diversity and trust, strongest locally; the leap to hardened ethnic blocs is unproven.
- **Open advocacy.** A [reply by @itispsychomath](https://x.com/itispsychomath/status/2082206097029595410) calls racial solidarity and balkanization of the Pacific Northwest, Great Lakes and New England the only path to victory.
- **Shock language.** Slur clips and racial jokes are memorable and carry no information.

Before using any of them: find the narrow fact the primary item supports, ask whether the post names its study, data and comparison group, and check whether association is standing in for cause. Taleb's point that group averages do not settle individual cases is on [[Social Commentary]]. The posts themselves are in [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]].
