# Entropy vs Extropy

[[Curtis_Yarvin|Curtis Yarvin]]'s thermodynamic reframe of the left/right axis. The left is entropy: decay, dissolution, levelling. The right is extropy: the active construction of order, complexity and vitality. The frame turns politics into a contest between decay and deliberate creation rather than an argument over policy detail. The explanation below comes from [[Culture|Culture & Observations]]. The frame is used in [[Political_Philosophy|Political Philosophy]], [[Political_Commentary|Political Commentary]] and [[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]], and applied loosely in [[Business_Philosophy|Business Philosophy]] and [[Masculinity_and_Men|Masculinity & Men]]: anywhere deliberate structure resists decay.

## The line

"the left is entropic the right is extropic" is a direct or near-direct quote from Yarvin's February 2026 interview on Peter McCormack's podcast. The fuller version from the episode, as the note gives it:

> "Fundamentally it's entropy versus extropy. The left is entropic, so things can just decay. But if you're building something, you need to build."

A post by [@minordissent](https://x.com/minordissent/status/2030516741307289976), quoting [Peter McCormack's post of the interview](https://x.com/PeterMcCormack/status/2026019007443157320), called it "the best line from this interview that no one is mentioning" (from [[X-Bookmarks]]).

Yarvin borrows two terms from thermodynamics and flips them into a political metaphor.

## Entropy: the left

- **In physics.** Entropy is the natural tendency of any closed system to move toward disorder, randomness and decay. Things fall apart unless energy is constantly added.
- **In politics.** The left is "entropic" because it deconstructs, levels and dissolves existing structures:
  - hierarchies
  - traditions
  - standards
  - borders
  - merit-based systems
- **The result.** More "equality" on paper, but societal breakdown, inefficiency and eventual collapse: "things can just decay."
- **The drift.** Modern progressivism, democracy and bureaucratic drift are entropy in action. Everything slowly slides leftward and gets messier, Yarvin's "Cthulhu always swims left" ([[Curtis_Yarvin|Curtis Yarvin]]).

## Extropy: the right

- **The term.** Extropy is the made-up opposite of entropy, popularised in futurism and transhumanism. It means actively increasing order, complexity, intelligence, vitality and capability.
- **Life is extropic.** It builds organised structures, cells to organisms to societies, that fight entropy.
- **In politics.** The true right is "extropic" because it builds and maintains higher-order systems:
  - strong hierarchies
  - sovereign decision-making: one person or a small group in charge who can actually act
  - innovation that creates new complexity and capability
  - patchwork-style governance or corporate-sovereign models that get things done instead of decaying
- **Not preservation.** It is not passive conservatism ("just slow down the left a bit"). It is energetic construction.

## Why it replaces left and right

- **Conservatism is slow-motion leftism.** "Conservative vs. liberal" or "Republican vs. Democrat" misses the point. Most "conservatives" are still slower versions of the entropic trend: they conserve yesterday's leftism. Real right-wing energy has to be creative and order-building ([[Curtis_Yarvin|Curtis Yarvin]]).
- **Who is moving.** Interesting, high-openness people used to flock left but are now starting to shift right as the entropy becomes obvious, in Yarvin's view.
- **The reframe.** Politics is no longer about policy details. It is a cosmic battle between decay and deliberate creation ([[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]]).

The note's summary:

> Left = lets the system rot (entropy wins).
>
> Right = fights rot by actively building stronger, smarter structures (extropy wins).

## Distinctiveness as extropy

The same idea, without the politics, is the subject of [[Jeff_Bezos|Jeff Bezos]]'s last shareholder letter as Amazon CEO, under the heading "Differentiation is Survival and the Universe Wants You to be Typical" (from [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]]). He quotes Richard Dawkins' *The Blind Watchmaker*: "Staving off death is a thing that you have to work at. Left to itself - and that is what it is when it dies - the body tends to revert to a state of equilibrium with its environment." Then he applies it:

> This phenomenon happens at all scale levels. Democracies are not normal. Tyranny is the historical norm. If we stopped doing all of the continuous hard work that is needed to maintain our distinctiveness in that regard, we would quickly come into equilibrium with tyranny.

> We all know that distinctiveness - originality - is valuable. We are all taught to "be yourself." What I'm really asking you to do is to embrace and be realistic about how much energy it takes to maintain that distinctiveness. The world wants you to be typical - in a thousand ways, it pulls at you. Don't let it happen.

Companies, like organisms, have to keep working against equilibrium or they merge into their surroundings ([[Business_Philosophy|Business Philosophy]]).

## Decay in institutions and daily life

- **Sovereignty decay.** The [[Politics]] note heads its UK material "Sovereignty is a distressed asset", with a [post by @howdymerry](https://x.com/howdymerry/status/2010407057313202406). When governments cannot maintain property rights or economic coherence, sovereignty itself becomes distressed: entropy showing up in institutions ([[Political_Commentary|Political Commentary]]).
- **Collapse without a happening.** A passage in [[Culture|Culture & Observations]] describes decay the entropic way, as a slow slide rather than an event: "There will be no 'happening.' It's far more insidious than that." It points to T. S. Eliot's "The Hollow Men" and lists the signs: "every day simple things will become a little more expensive. Everyone's homes and apartments will start to get smaller. Your work hours will get longer, but your pay will decrease." Its ending: "That is the collapse. The reduction of the American man into a feudal serf, incapable of feeling love or hate".
