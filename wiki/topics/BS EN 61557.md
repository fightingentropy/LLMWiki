# BS EN 61557

BS EN 61557 is "Electrical safety in low voltage distribution systems up to 1000 V AC and 1500 V DC — Equipment for testing, measuring or monitoring of protective measures". It is the performance and accuracy standard for the instruments used to verify that a fixed electrical installation complies with [[BS 7671]].

Where [[BS EN 61010]] guarantees the instrument is **safe**, 61557 guarantees it is **accurate enough** for the verification readings to mean something. Check part numbers and limits against the current BSI edition before relying on them.

## What the standard covers

From the source notes: *"BS EN 61557 (IEC 61557) — Covers: accuracy specifications, measurement reliability, test result validity, performance standards. Requirement: test equipment should provide accurate measurements per this standard."*

The standard is published in numbered parts, each covering one type of installation test. The key parts for a UK electrician:

| Part | Covers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 61557-1 | General requirements | Common to all parts |
| 61557-2 | Insulation resistance | Used at 250 V / 500 V / 1000 V DC |
| 61557-3 | Loop impedance (line-earth, line-line) | Covers Ze, Zs, Zdb readings |
| 61557-4 | Resistance of earth connection and equipotential bonding | The low-resistance ohmmeter / continuity test |
| 61557-5 | Resistance to earth | Earth electrode resistance, typically for TT systems |
| 61557-6 | Residual current devices (RCDs) | Tripping time and tripping current tests |
| 61557-7 | Phase sequence | Phase sequence indicators |
| 61557-8 | Insulation monitoring devices | For IT systems |
| 61557-9 | Insulation fault location | In IT systems |
| 61557-10 | Combined (multi-function) test equipment | The typical MFT found in the field |
| 61557-12 | Performance measuring and monitoring devices | PMD |
| 61557-13 | Clamp and hand-held current sensors | For leakage current measurement |

Correction, 16 October 2026: Part 12 is titled Power metering and monitoring devices (PMD), not performance measuring; its current edition is IEC 61557-12:2018 with Amendment 1:2021 ([IEC webstore](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/30065)).

## Why it matters in practice

[[BS 7671]] requires specific numerical outcomes: a measured Zs below the tabulated maximum, insulation resistance above a stated minimum, continuity within a calculated value. None of those decisions are defensible if the instrument's accuracy spec is unknown.

61557 sets out:

- The **operating range** over which the instrument must meet its accuracy spec (for example, for insulation resistance, from MΩ up into GΩ).
- The permitted **overall operating uncertainty** at the extremes of that range.
- The **influence quantities** (temperature, battery voltage, supply variations) the instrument must tolerate without drifting out of spec.
- The **functional safety** of the test sequence itself (for example, an insulation resistance tester must indicate a live circuit rather than apply 500 V blindly).
  Correction, 16 October 2026: Part 1 states that it does not cover functional safety or cybersecurity ([IEC 61557-1:2019+AMD1:2024](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/102414)).

This is why instruments stamped "IEC 61557" are the only ones suitable for completing the schedule of test results on an [[EIC and MEIWC|EIC]] or [[EICR]].

## Calibration versus accuracy checks

61557 defines how accurate the instrument must be **when in spec**. It does not prove the instrument is **still** in spec on any given day. Two practices cover that gap:

- **Annual calibration** by a UKAS-accredited lab, giving a certificate of accuracy on the calibration date.
- **Regular on-site accuracy checks** against a known reference: a calibration box, a short link at 0 Ω, a known-value resistor, or mains voltage.

From the source notes: *"Calibration is a point-in-time verification... Best practice: perform accuracy checks between calibrations."* The [[PAT Testing]] source recommends recording these checks (for example on Form V6).

## How it fits with other standards

- [[BS EN 61010]] covers the instrument's **safety** construction and CAT rating.
- [[GS38]] covers the **leads and probes** attached to the instrument.
- 61557 covers what the instrument **measures and how well**.

All three must be satisfied for compliant installation verification.
