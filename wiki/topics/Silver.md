# Silver

Silver is a hybrid asset: it has industrial demand like copper, but safe-haven characteristics like [[Gold]]. It is more volatile than gold because its market is smaller. The [[Markets - Handbook]] lists its drivers in one line:

> "industrial demand (electronics + solar), investor/ETF flows (safe-haven + speculation), interest rates/real yields + DXY, relative value vs gold (gold–silver ratio), mining + recycling supply (often by-product of base metals), futures positioning/volatility."

See [[Commodities]] for the broader framework.

## Key drivers

### Industrial demand

- **Electronics**: conductivity and reliability; shrinking but structural.
- **Solar panels**: grid-scale solar requires significant silver; growing.
- **Photography and other**: legacy and niche uses.

### Precious metal and safe haven

- **Investor flows**: ETFs, funds, safe-haven positioning.
- **Safe-haven value**: insurance demand in risk-off periods.
- **Speculation**: leverage and positioning can amplify moves.

### Supply

- **Primary mining**: dedicated silver mining is niche; most silver is a by-product.
- **By-product supply**: silver extracted from copper, zinc and lead mining.
- **Recycling**: electronics and industrial recycling add supply.
- **Mine economics**: by-product status means supply does not respond quickly to price.

### Macro factors

- **Real yields**: like gold, silver moves inversely to real rates. See [[Real Yields]].
- **USD strength**: a weak dollar means higher silver, because it is cheaper in foreign currencies. See [[DXY]].
- **Gold-silver ratio**: relative value between the two precious metals.

## Trading characteristics

- **More volatile than gold**: a smaller market means bigger moves on the same flows.
- **Industrial cycle**: coupled to industrial production and solar demand.
- **Positioning leverage**: futures positioning can drive sharp reversals.
- **Mean reversion**: high gold-silver ratios tend to compress over time.

## The gold-silver ratio

- **What it is**: the price of gold divided by the price of silver (a ratio of ounces).
- **High ratio**: silver is cheap relative to gold; history suggests the ratio compresses.
- **Low ratio**: silver is expensive relative to gold; it could be overvalued.
- **Mean-reversion play**: extremes often reverse.

## Scenarios

| Scenario | How silver behaves |
|---|---|
| Risk-off | Both metals rally, but gold rallies more (safe-haven demand dominates industrial) |
| Growth surge | Silver outperforms gold (industrial demand kicks in) |
| Real-rate spike | Both fall, but silver falls harder (a leveraged inverse play on real yields) |
| Tech boom | Silver rallies (electronics and solar demand) |
