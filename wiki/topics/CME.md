# CME

The Chicago Mercantile Exchange is the world's largest centralised derivatives exchange and the primary venue for commodity, equity-index and currency futures. In this wiki it is mainly the centralised benchmark that on-chain venues such as [[Hyperliquid]] are measured against. The product and structure notes below are general background; Erlin's notes reach CME through the Hyperliquid silver comparison and the rise of crypto perps.

## Key products

- **Commodities:** oil, natural gas, gold, silver and agricultural contracts. See [[Commodities]], [[Oil]], [[Natural Gas]], [[Gold]], [[Silver]].
- **Equities:** S&P 500, Nasdaq and Russell 2000 futures. See [[Equities]].
- **Currencies:** Dollar index and FX futures. See [[FX]], [[DXY]].
- **Rates:** Treasury futures and Eurodollar. See [[Rates]].
- Correction, 15 October 2026: the benchmark US Dollar Index (DXY) futures contract is listed on ICE Futures U.S., not CME.
- Correction, 15 October 2026: CME's Eurodollar futures were replaced by SOFR futures in 2023.

## Significance for markets

- **Price discovery:** CME is the primary venue for commodity and equity-index futures.
- **Leverage:** it gives institutional and retail traders access to leverage. See [[Leverage]].
- **Global access:** available to traders worldwide.
- **Regulation:** regulated by the CFTC, with transparent position reporting; the CFTC's Commitments of Traders reports are the usual positioning read. See [[Positioning]].

## Microstructure

- **Centralised matching:** a central order book.
- **Pit-trading legacy:** trading is now electronic.
- **Liquidity:** generally deep, especially in the major contracts.
- **Hours:** extended, 24/5 for most products, with a short daily break. See [[Exchange Hours]].

The wider framework is on [[Exchange Microstructure]].

## Competitive landscape

CME increasingly competes with:

- [[Hyperliquid]] in crypto and decentralised trading.
- Crypto perpetual futures, which are easier to access. A line in the note calls [perps](https://x.com/sershokunin/status/1945857878771818753) "the best pmf in all of crypto" and adds that Robert Shiller proposed perpetual futures in 1992, as a way to build derivatives markets on illiquid, rarely priced assets such as single-family homes (from [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]]).
- Offshore exchanges, through regulatory arbitrage.

### Silver on Hyperliquid versus CME

- [[HIP-3 Silver Microstructure]] compares CME with [[Hyperliquid]] for silver futures, from an [X article by @shaundadevens](https://x.com/shaundadevens/status/2019782009267085656) saved in [[Markets - Articles]].
- A post from 30 January 2026 claims silver did about $3B of volume on Hyperliquid that day while CME traded 275k contracts (about $130B notional), so Hyperliquid took roughly 2% of the world's primary silver market for an asset it had listed 30 days earlier. _(@0xCryptoSam)_
