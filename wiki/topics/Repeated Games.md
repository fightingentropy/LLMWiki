# Repeated Games

A repeated game is any interaction you will play again: with the same person, the same market, or the same bankroll. The practical unit is not the isolated win but the strategy that preserves good future rounds. Erlin's notes apply this to setting boundaries, to recovering after a loss, and to deciding which risks to insure.

## Tit for Tat as a boundary model

Begin cooperatively, respond promptly and proportionally to exploitation, and restore cooperation when the other side does. The point is not permanent revenge; it is to make cooperation profitable and defection predictably costly. In the note's words:

> The durable lesson from Tit for Tat is not “dominate every encounter,” but make cooperation profitable and defection predictably costly while avoiding permanent revenge loops.

(from [[Thoughts|Thoughts & Observations]])

- **Limits.** Axelrod's tournaments do not prove universal dominance. Performance changes with noise, error correction, population, and game structure. The note says the source essay "dramatizes Robert Axelrod's tournaments and overstates Tit for Tat as universally dominant", and treats it as a practical boundary-setting analogy rather than a mathematical law for relationships.
- **Sources.** The [original essay](https://x.com/alexeixbt/status/2083512257808417082) and a [commentary with Prisoner's Dilemma framing](https://x.com/alexeixbt/status/2085402227489800240), both by @alexeixbt.
- **Where it applies.** Relationships and personal boundaries ([[Dating_and_Relationships|Dating & Relationships]], [[Self_Improvement|Self-Improvement]]) as much as trading and business ([[Decision-Making]]).

## Recovery after loss

A demand to erase a large loss in one decisive gamble can destroy the resource that makes recovery possible: continued participation. Stabilize first, cap further damage, preserve optionality, and distribute recovery across later rounds instead of demanding that the present round repair the past. This links directly to [[Trading_Psychology|Trading Psychology]] and [[Leverage]]. (from [[Thoughts|Thoughts & Observations]], based on a [post](https://x.com/incentivising/status/2083985433973702656) by @incentivising)

- **Survive the drawdown.** A saved post, [The wrong lesson from The Big Short](https://x.com/Evan_ss6/status/2067033343216984194), argues that durable wealth usually comes from surviving drawdowns and participating in recoveries, not from romanticizing rare crash bets (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]]).

## Insurance and affordable variance

Positive expected value is not the same as survivable risk. Rejecting a small favorable bet can be irrational when the loss is affordable; buying insurance can be rational when a low-probability loss would be ruinous. Alec Torelli's [post](https://x.com/AlecTorelli/status/2089548439101362213) frames insurance as chiefly protection against unaffordable variance (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]]).

The framing is useful but incomplete: risk aversion, legal requirements, correlated loss, liquidity, exclusions, insurer solvency, and peace of mind still matter. See [[Risk Analysis]] and [[Behavioral_Finance|Behavioral Finance]].
