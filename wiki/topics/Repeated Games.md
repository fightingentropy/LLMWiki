# Repeated Games

The practical unit is not the isolated win but the strategy that preserves good future rounds.

## Tit for Tat as a boundary model

Begin cooperatively, respond promptly and proportionally to exploitation, and restore cooperation when the other side does. The point is not permanent revenge; it is to make cooperation profitable and defection predictably costly. Axelrod's tournaments do not prove universal dominance—performance changes with noise, error correction, population, and game structure. (from [[Thoughts]])

## Recovery after loss

A demand to erase a large loss in one decisive gamble can destroy the resource that makes recovery possible: continued participation. Stabilize first, cap further damage, preserve optionality, and distribute recovery across later rounds. This links directly to [[Trading_Psychology]] and [[Leverage]].

## Insurance and affordable variance

Positive expected value is not the same as survivable risk. Rejecting a small favorable bet can be irrational when the loss is affordable; buying insurance can be rational when a low-probability loss would be ruinous. Legal requirements, correlated loss, liquidity, exclusions, and insurer solvency still matter. (from [[X-Bookmarks]])
