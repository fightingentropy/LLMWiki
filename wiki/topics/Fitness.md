# Fitness

Fitness in Erlin's notes is mostly bodyweight training: a plan that covers the body with push-ups, pull-ups, squats and a few accessory moves, plus saved clips on core work, posture and lower-leg mobility. The NHS weekly activity baseline and the push-up study behind the widely shared "96 percent" heart-disease line have primary sources behind them, and the rep-range and testosterone claims carry corrections from trials below. The rest are ideas, takes and anecdotes from [[Body]] and the health bookmarks.

## Activity baseline

For adults aged 19–64, [NHS guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/) combines aerobic activity with strength work:

- 150 minutes of moderate activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity each week.
- Strengthening the major muscle groups on at least two days a week.
- Adjust activity to health, ability and experience.

(from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

## Push-ups and heart disease

The body note saves this under the handle @robkhenderson (from [[Body]]):

> Men who can do 40 push-ups or more have a 96 percent reduced risk of heart disease compared with men who could do less than 10 push-ups.
>
> Push-ups are a better predictor of whether a man will get heart disease than an aerobic treadmill test.

What the study actually found: [Yang et al. (2019)](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2724778) followed 1,104 active male firefighters. Higher baseline push-up capacity was associated with fewer cardiovascular events. The widely quoted 96% figure compares groups in that observational cohort. It does not mean doing forty push-ups causes a 96% risk reduction, and it does not validate push-ups as a universal replacement for clinical assessment or treadmill testing. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

## A bodyweight plan from the notes

Fit any of this to your own health, ability and injury history. The body note's plan "to cover most of body" (from [[Body]]):

| Area | Exercises |
|---|---|
| Most of the body | Push-ups, pull-ups, squats |
| Abdominals | Leg raises |
| Lower back | Supermans and plank |
| Hamstrings | Side squats/lunges |
| Calves | Calf jumps/calf string skipping |
| Shoulders (and abs) | Pull up bar loops, dips, L sits |
| Neck | Neck bridges and neck raises |

- **Legs without bulk.** "The key to strengthening the legs without bulking up is to keep the reps high (at least 15 reps per set). Perform three rounds of each exercise with minimal rest between each movement."
- Correction, 16 October 2026: a [meta-analysis of 21 trials](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/) found that light-load, high-rep training and heavy-load training built similar muscle size when sets were taken to failure, while heavy loads gave more maximal strength. High reps on their own do not prevent muscle growth.

- **Muscle-building mistakes.** A [thread saved with the hook](https://twitter.com/TheJackBly/status/1564953697465016322) "If you want to build more muscle than 93% of men, avoid these 13 mistakes." _(@TheJackBly)_
- **The pump.** "Lifting weights but never looking at the pump as it is impermanent."

## Posture, neck and lower legs

These clips are demonstrations to evaluate, not a diagnosis or a rehabilitation programme; pain or an injury warrants qualified assessment.

- **Spinal alignment.** "[F]requently and efficiently noticing and correcting your spinal alignment," saved with a [post by @YeshuaGod22](https://x.com/YeshuaGod22/status/1923766069015048680). (from [[Body]])
- **Neck blood flow.** After the neck exercises, the note saves a [video at 7:22](https://youtu.be/2vap5UzphfI?t=442) with the aside "(Better blood flow to the neck makes you more intelligent, use this secret information well.)" (from [[Body]])
- **Foot, ankle and rotation.** A short [Guy Macchia clip](https://x.com/wirallop/status/2092319105508356490) demonstrates lower-leg work, standing rotation, supported movement and bouncing. Its claims, such as doing 75% of training below the knee, rotation being the master of decompression, and broad fascia explanations, are not substantiated by the clip. Suitability depends on the person, load, balance and injury history. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])
- **Wearable-guided physical therapy.** A [prototype demo](https://x.com/thefountaindead/status/2097089544357327268) connects a wearable to a biomechanical model to suggest personalised back-pain exercises. It does not validate the model, identify the cause of pain, or show the suggested exercises are safe. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

## Energy, the brain and the mind

- **Activity breeds energy.** "The [more](https://x.com/NoahRyanCo/status/1928840606974947344) you do, the more energy you have, the more you relax. World is full of people who do nothing, are constantly exhausted, and never recoup." (from [[Body]])
- **Exercise and the brain.** A [post saved under the line "Exercise rewires the brain"](https://x.com/NTFabiano/status/1926295974428766537). (from [[Body]])
- **Acute stress.** High-intensity exercise is one of the acute stressors the notes want more of; see [[Stress Management]] and the [@NoahRyanCo post](https://x.com/NoahRyanCo/status/1928925779699839308) on acute versus chronic stress. (from [[Body]])
- **Imagined contractions.** A [reaction post](https://x.com/JIMMYEDGAR/status/2101074736600011033) to a quoted claim that mentally rehearsing muscle contractions increased strength without exercise. The study, muscle group, protocol, comparison and effect size still need finding before this counts as training guidance. _(@JIMMYEDGAR quoting @NTFabiano)_ (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

## Physique and hormone claims

These are attributed lines from notes and posts; none comes with evidence attached.

- [Keep a small waist](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/yF7LXLeK-js) — a YouTube Short saved under that line. (from [[Body]])
- "One of the best parts of losing weight is getting your face structure [back](https://twitter.com/hxdleyo/status/1764968381898260587)." (from [[Body]])
- "Testosterone is secreted when you sleep, you train legs, and spend time in the sun." (from [[Body]])
- Correction, 16 October 2026: most of men's daily testosterone release happens during sleep, and a week of five-hour nights lowered young men's daytime levels by 10–15% ([Leproult & Van Cauter, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21632481/)). A high-volume leg session raised testosterone only briefly after exercise and added no arm muscle or strength over 15 weeks ([West et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19910330/)). Sun exposure has only an observational link with testosterone in humans ([Parikh et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34433056/)).
- Under the handle "@Dr Dynamis" (see [[DrDynamis]]): "People with marginal vitamin C status burned 25% less fat during exercise. After vitamin C supplementation? Fat burning increased 4-fold." See [[Supplements|Supplements & Biohacking]]. (from [[Body]])

## Cold showers

The body note keeps a post by @olivesagan on cold exposure and dopamine, followed by replies reporting that five-minute cold showers turned anxious days around; one says "it almost feels like taking a pill or something." The quotes and the stress framing are on [[Stress Management]]. (from [[Body]], [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

## What the notes do not establish

**Correction, 22 September 2026:** an earlier version of this page turned the body note's bodyweight ideas, posture clips, high-repetition leg work and cold-shower anecdotes into a general routine, and promised particular physique and hormone outcomes. That confidence was not justified by the supplied evidence.

- "15+ reps prevents bulk" is not established by the source; choose progression and recovery to suit the goal.
- The neck-bridge and neck-blood-flow advice has not been validated.
- Hair appearance is not a substitute for a health assessment.
- Cold-shower mood reports are personal experiences, not evidence of medication equivalence.

[[Health Evidence]] sets the standard for adding clinical claims.
