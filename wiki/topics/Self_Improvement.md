# Self-Improvement

This page collects the principles Erlin's notes keep returning to on getting better at life: small decisions compound, action beats planning, agency is the trait that matters, capacity is finite, and ability only pays off in an environment that rewards it. Most of it comes from posts saved in [[Psychology & Behavior]], [[Thoughts & Observations]], [[Body]] and [[Youtube]], plus a thematic cut of the [[Quotes]] archive. The posture is practical rather than aspirational: do the thing, accept the limit, keep moving. The critique of self-improvement as a consumer product lives on [[Self-Improvement Culture Critique]].

## Small decisions compound

The note that anchors the page is a reflection on "tiny deltas", saved in both [[Psychology & Behavior]] and [[Body]] and credited to [@kelxyz_](https://x.com/kelxyz_):

> "An obvious thing that still blows my mind: the compounding effects of replacing tiny bad decisions (negative deltas) with tiny good ones (positive deltas) In the moment it feels pretty insignificant. Supposed to go the gym, but wanna stay in and order pizza? It's only one day you say. Have a trading plan, but feeling like fomo blasting something from twitter? It's only one trade you say. But like, our life is just an accumulation of these tiny deltas. Most of the time we don't blow up with one catastrophic -100%. People typically don't go from fit to obese overnight No, it's the days that we want to be +1s or +2s towards our goals. But we allow to be -1s or -3s instead" (from [[Psychology & Behavior]])

The basketball example from the same post: miss a wide-open layup, get frustrated, give up a wide-open 3. "The score only changed -3 against you, but in reality your expected value is at a high probability +2, so it's a -5 point swing. Give up a few of those in a game and all of the sudden you're down -40. Looking like the Suns in a Luka game 7."

- Life is an accumulation of micro-choices, not catastrophic blowups.
- Most failure is slow erosion, not a single blow. Most success is a steady accumulation of +1s.
- The same logic runs through [[Trading Psychology]]: one FOMO trade is "only one trade" in exactly the way one skipped gym day is "only one day".

The [[Nietzsche|Friedrich Nietzsche]] line in the quotes below ("the history of every day") is the same idea put as a question about habits.

## Act before you are ready

### Imperfect action beats perfect planning

> "One pattern I've noticed in all miserable people: They overthink and underact." (from [[Psychology & Behavior]], linked to [a post by @TaylinSimmonds](https://x.com/TaylinSimmonds/status/1878819866070679565) that promises "the system I use to escape the cold, dark prison of overthinking")

A one-line system from the same note: **aim. fire. correct later.** Imperfect action beats perfect planning. The preparatory state (research, optimization, system design) is comfortable procrastination; execution is where reality hits.

Flagged for study in [[Go Do]]: [[Alex Hormozi]] on [[Chris Williamson]]'s podcast, at the segment on ["Imperfect action"](https://youtu.be/Gk8EGWoGnEQ?t=3590). The lesson drawn from it: perfect plans are procrastination, and the plan corrects against contact with reality, not against more planning. The same episode produced three bookmarks in [[Youtube]]: ["My Goal in life is not necessarily to be happy. My goal in life is to do epic shit."](https://youtu.be/Gk8EGWoGnEQ?t=8918), ["The only way is through"](https://youtu.be/Gk8EGWoGnEQ?t=9136) and ["Views on happiness"](https://youtu.be/Gk8EGWoGnEQ?t=9084).

This compounds with **kaizen**, continuous improvement, which [[Go Do]] lists among the things "Japan gets" (see [[Japanese_Philosophy|Japanese Philosophy & Cultural Concepts]]): small corrections in flight, not a single perfect launch.

### The enthusiasm gap

> "you are getting lapped by people 50% dumber than you because they don't overthink shit." (from [[Psychology & Behavior]]; also in [[Quotes]])

> "Most successful people I've met are not that smart. They just move fast, take risks and work a lot. Trust me." (from [[Psychology & Behavior]])

Smart people get paralysed by the possibility space; average people just move. They aim, fire, and correct later.

### Embarrassment is the cost of entry

> "Embarrassment is the cost of entry. If you aren't willing to look like a foolish beginner, you'll never become a graceful master." (from [[Body]])

> "If babies understood embarrassment, most of them would never learn to walk or speak because of the amount of failure required to improve. This was one of our first lessons in life but somehow, we forgot." (from [[Quotes]])

Mastery requires public incompetence first. A saved practice in the same spirit, from [[Bookmarks - Personal & Life]]: [rue yi's daily low-stakes social exposure](https://x.com/ruebytwo/status/1924272358623228396), trying to make one stranger laugh each day at age 20 and reporting less anxiety and better conversational reading over time. A personal exposure-style exercise, not a clinical protocol.

### Delusion as a tool

Bookmarked in [[Youtube]]: ["Why delusion is important"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pR86kI5aDFA&t=3349s). Functional self-belief that runs ahead of the evidence is a feature, not a bug: it gets you to act before the data would justify it, and the action itself produces the data. Related to [[Trading Psychology]], where the intuitive operators who commit before consensus are the ones who catch the move.

Delusion is not the opposite of realism here. It is realism about what belief does, not just what belief claims. A passage saved in [[Thoughts & Observations]] makes the same case from the other direction:

> "We don't go out as much because when we were younger we had this hopefully naivete that we can get ahead and maybe it was a delusion and not rational but it was a useful fiction that actually propelled us forward and motivated us . As we got older and some of us got smacked in the face with reality we started losing some that momentum and optimism and in its place took this cynicism and bitterness ." (from [[Thoughts & Observations]])

### Motivation follows action

["How can you lack motivation?"](https://youtu.be/ONs2tlD1YgE?t=2074), bookmarked in [[Youtube]], treats absent motivation as a category error rather than a deficiency to be fixed. The same timestamp sits in [[Funny]] under the line: "How can an antelope, in the jaws of the tiger, say 'I Lack Motivation'?" The reframe: motivation follows action, not the reverse. See [[Mental Health]] on overthinking paralysis.

## Agency

### High agency is the core attribute

> "The older i get, the more I'm convinced the single most important trait that matters in your friends, coworkers, life partner, and kids is high agency." (from [[X Takes]])

Agency is the ability to make things happen, solve problems, and move forward despite obstacles. Everything else is subordinate. Two lines from [[Quotes]] sharpen it:

> "intelligence without agency is [depression](https://x.com/gaxrav/status/1912750985828790533)." (from [[Quotes]])

> "i hate how well asking myself 'if i had 10x the agency i have what would i do' works." (from [[Quotes]])

A saved post in [[Bookmarks - Personal & Life]], [urgency as respect for time](https://x.com/josbjohnson/status/2077012864380919989) by jo johnson, reframes high-agency speed as reverence for finite time: answer while context is warm, finish small tasks before they accumulate, move on ideas before hesitation turns into drift.

### Autonomy and self-direction

> "The state can substitute for what women want from men, but it can't substitute for what men want from women." — @naval (from [[Thoughts & Observations]]; see [[Naval Ravikant]])

Humans need genuine autonomy, purpose and agency. Systems can provide comfort, not meaning.

### Serendipity and positioning

From [[Nassim Taleb]], copied into [[Thoughts & Observations]]:

> "If a big publisher (or a big art dealer or a movie executive or a hotshot banker or a big thinker) suggests an appointment, cancel anything you have planned: you may never see such a window open up again. I am sometimes shocked at how little people realize that these opportunities do not grow on trees. Collect as many free nonlottery tickets (those with open-ended payoffs) as you can, and, once they start paying off, do not discard them. Work hard, not in grunt work, but in chasing such opportunities and maximizing exposure to them. This makes living in big cities invaluable because you increase the odds of serendipitous encounters-you gain exposure to the envelope of serendipity." (from [[Thoughts & Observations]])

The optionality value of high-trust encounters with important people is immense. The envelope of possibility contains ten times more for those positioned inside it.

## Capacity and pace

### One optional thing per day

> "Compared to my 20s, I'm much more at peace with the fact that I only have the capacity to consistently do one optional thing really well each day. This is after all of my non-optional tasks every day (dog, relationship(s), work, chores, etc.). Acceptance is good because it helps me not revenge procrastinate, stress out about whether I'm doing enough, etc. I've got one thing, that's okay, and it's better to just do one thing each day than burn myself out indefinitely trying to do 3 or 4 things." ([a post by @skooookum](https://x.com/skooookum/status/1932184434784010379), from [[Thoughts & Observations]])

Acceptance prevents revenge procrastination, stress and self-judgment.

### Balance across seasons, not every day

Ambitious work, fitness, travel, relationships and family may require deliberate periods of over-allocation rather than keeping every metric green daily. Make the season explicit, keep hard health and ethical limits, set a review date, and rebalance across years. Intensity is not permission to normalise burnout or neglect. ([Aporia's post](https://x.com/aporia9n/status/2086213665573704091), from [[Bookmarks - Personal & Life]])

### Preserve chosen friction

Outsourcing drudgery saves time, but removing every inconvenience also removes small, frequent practice in navigating, cooking, improvising, asking for help and tolerating disrupted plans. Remove waste; keep the difficulty you chose. Hardship is not automatically meaningful, and paid adventure does not substitute for respecting people who face involuntary constraints. ([Aporia's post](https://x.com/aporia9n/status/2090604149595357399), quoting [a reflection by Sahil Bloom](https://x.com/SahilBloom/status/2090478737573711950); from [[Bookmarks - Personal & Life]])

## Environment and reality

- **Ability needs a converting environment.** More discipline is not always the answer when the surrounding system cannot reward current capacity. Ask which environment would convert what you already have into a return, what moving there costs, and whether changing the structure now beats another decade of polishing yourself inside the wrong one. Change the payoff matrix or move to a setting where the ability has a return. ([incentivising on environment](https://x.com/incentivising/status/2082173497166446786) and [on systems producing incentives](https://x.com/incentivising/status/2081434700711596312), from [[Thoughts & Observations]])
- **Intelligence is not always limiting.** Clifford Sosin's thesis: abundant reasoning transforms work with smooth, verifiable solution spaces, but important problems still require building, testing, measuring and correction from reality. ([Clifford Sosin's post](https://x.com/CliffordSosin/status/2078594661359194500), from [[Thoughts & Observations]])
- **Win later rounds.** After a loss, stabilise, cap further losses and preserve participation rather than demanding that the present round repair the past. ([incentivising on recovery](https://x.com/incentivising/status/2083985433973702656); see [[Repeated Games]].) The same framing applies to [leaving a safe career](https://x.com/moneyfetishist/status/2039528758936207757): diminishing learning, golden handcuffs, status-quo bias and asymmetric regret, saved in [[Bookmarks - Personal & Life]].
- **[[David Cifaldi]].** The Granite Peak story in [[Stories]] (a trekking pole through the torso near the summit on 20 July 2026, then a ten-mile descent with two companions under their own power) is a striking account of calm and endurance, not a medical protocol or an instruction to self-rescue. ([Original post](https://x.com/SMB_Attorney/status/2081926110174957611); [Guardian report](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/27/montana-man-hikes-10-miles-fall-trekking-pole))

## Inner work

### Shame as fuel versus parasite

Saved in [[Psychology & Behavior]] as "Note to myself today, maybe some of you need to read it too", linked to [a post by Jason Koon](https://x.com/JasonKoon/status/1870104809107230763):

> "The darkness you carry inside is lying. It's tricked you into believing it's a superpower. When you mess up, the shame and anger you feel towards yourself burn a memory into your mind so that you will never repeat the same mistake. For this reason, your self-loathing creates a mirage that its existence is a part of who you are, and you can't be you without it. It's your secret weapon, your edge! The truth of it all is it's nothing more than a parasite. Sucking you dry, stealing your presence, and forcing you to burn energy that could be spent on building a flourishing existence for you and your loved ones. Fight the darkness, be kind to yourself, and keep moving forward." (from [[Psychology & Behavior]])

Shame burns painful memories in (preventing repeat mistakes), but it is parasitic, not symbiotic. It steals the presence and energy that could build a flourishing existence.

### Self-labelling and reality

> "Also words are like spells, be very careful while labelling yourself as 'traumatized' or 'depressed', they start to become real." (from [[Thoughts & Observations]]; also in [[Psychology & Behavior]])

Language rewires neurology; self-conception becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy. The line sits directly under [a post](https://x.com/FiercelyVirgo/status/1892433169657233763) claiming a study shows mental health improves by training the suppression of unwanted thoughts ("you can just get over things"). The [[Bruce Lee]] quote below makes the same point about self-talk.

### Reading as emotional systematisation

["Reading is systematizing the way you feel"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xwx0mf8aGC4&t=4154s) is a saved YouTube timestamp in [[Psychology & Behavior]]. Converting emotional experience into language structure creates understanding and perspective.

### Optimisation as avoidance

Hyper-optimisation culture (morning routines, productivity systems, biohacking) can mask avoidance. The sharpest version is a passage saved in [[Thoughts & Observations]] under [@signulll](https://x.com/signulll):

> "we've built an entire shadow economy around the simulation of work rather than work itself. the productivity-industrial complex feeds on the desire to feel like you're making progress rather than actually making it." (from [[Thoughts & Observations]])

> "the more you consume these meta-tools, the more you train yourself to exist in a preparatory state instead of an executory one. [...] it's a kind of paralysis disguised as momentum." (from [[Thoughts & Observations]])

True optimisation is doing the thing, not optimising how you will do the thing. The passage calls it "an intelligence trap—spending more time honing the system than actually using it." It closes on a linked [post about AI exposing how much "expertise" is just memorised heuristics](https://x.com/signulll/status/1891192852711190634). See [[Self-Improvement Culture Critique]] for the wider argument.

### Action, courage, agency

> "Man has it all in his hands and it slips away out of sheer cowardice." — [[Dostoevsky|Fyodor Dostoevsky]] (from [[Quotes]]; the archive also holds the wording "Man has it all in his hands, and it all slips through his fingers from sheer cowardice.")

> "Anxiety is what happens when your brains are bigger than your balls... The only way to demolish anxiety is to choke it out with ruthless action." (from [[Quotes]])

> "Brilliant thinking is rare, but courage is in even shorter supply than genius." — [[Peter Thiel]] ([video](https://youtu.be/E_vDZqQgmeQ); from [[Quotes]])

> "say it with me now. experts are fake, smart generalists rule the world, everything is designed by people no smarter than you, and courage is in shorter supply than genius." — @tszzl (from [[Quotes]])

> "You are getting lapped by people 50% dumber than you because they don't overthink shit." (from [[Quotes]])

> "Action produces information. If you're unsure of what to do, just do anything, even if it's the wrong thing. This will give you information about what you should actually be doing." (from [[Quotes]])

### Habit as character

> "The history of every day... are they the product of innumerable little cowardices and lazinesses or of your courage and inventive reason?" — [[Nietzsche|Friedrich Nietzsche]] (from [[Quotes]])

> "He who cannot obey himself will be commanded." — [[Nietzsche|Friedrich Nietzsche]] (from [[Quotes]])

### Self-betrayal and self-talk

> "Your worst sin is that you betrayed yourself for nothing." — [[Dostoevsky|Fyodor Dostoevsky]] (from [[Quotes]])

> "Don't speak negatively about yourself, even as a joke. Your body doesn't know the difference. Words are energy and cast spells, that's why it's called spelling." — [[Bruce Lee]] (from [[Quotes]])

> "The only real measure of intelligence is if you get what you want out of life." — [[Naval Ravikant]] (from [[Quotes]])

### Creating, not consuming

> "Die empty. Get every idea out of your head and into reality... Make finishing your top priority." — Derek Sivers, *Here's How To Live: Create*, 23 December 2021 (from [[Quotes]])

> "To practice any art, no matter how well or badly, is a way to make your soul grow. So do it." — Kurt Vonnegut (from [[Quotes]])

> "Man's greatest burden is unfulfilled potential." (from [[Quotes]])

### Discomfort and growth

> "You are in danger of living a life so comfortable and soft, that you will die without even realizing your true potential." — [[David Goggins]] (from [[Quotes]])

> "Comfort is a drug. It's addictive... The comfort zone is where dreams go to die." (from [[Quotes]])

> "A man who has not passed through the inferno of his passions has never overcome them. They then dwell in the house next door, and at any moment a flame may dart out and set fire to his own house." — [[Carl Jung]] (from [[Quotes]])
