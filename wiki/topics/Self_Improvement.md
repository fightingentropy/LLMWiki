# Self-Improvement

The principles the notes keep returning to on getting better at life: small choices compound, action beats planning, agency matters most, and capacity is finite. Alongside it sits a sharp critique of self-improvement sold as a product. More quotes are in [[Quotes]].

## Small choices compound

@kelxyz_ on "tiny deltas":

> "our life is just an accumulation of these tiny deltas. Most of the time we don't blow up with one catastrophic -100%. People typically don't go from fit to obese overnight"

Skipping the gym is "only one day"; a FOMO trade is "only one trade". His basketball example: miss an open layup, sulk, give up an open three, and a -3 on the scoreboard is really a -5 swing. Most failure is slow erosion and most success a run of +1s. Nietzsche asks the same of habits: "are they the product of innumerable little cowardices and lazinesses or of your courage and inventive reason?"

## Act before you are ready

- "One pattern I've noticed in all miserable people: They overthink and underact." _(@TaylinSimmonds)_ The one-line system: "aim. fire. correct later."
- "you are getting lapped by people 50% dumber than you because they don't overthink shit."
- "Action produces information. If you’re unsure of what to do, just do anything, even if it’s the wrong thing."
- "Embarrassment is the cost of entry. If you aren't willing to look like a foolish beginner, you'll never become a graceful master." rue yi practised by trying to make one stranger laugh every day.
- Belief ahead of evidence can be useful. One saved passage misses the youthful "delusion" that "was a useful fiction that actually propelled us forward".
- Motivation follows action: "How can an antelope, in the jaws of the tiger, say “I Lack Motivation”?"

Alex Hormozi's segment on imperfect action, with Chris Williamson, is saved in [[Youtube]] and flagged in [[Go Do]].

## Agency

> "The older i get, the more I'm convinced the single most important trait that matters in your friends, coworkers, life partner, and kids is high agency."

- "intelligence without agency is depression."
- "i hate how well asking myself "if i had 10x the agency i have what would i do" works."
- jo johnson treats urgency as respect for finite time: reply while context is warm and finish small tasks before they pile up.
- In [[Stories]], David Cifaldi hiked ten miles out after falling onto a trekking pole near the Granite Peak summit.

## Capacity and pace

- @skooookum: "I only have the capacity to consistently do one optional thing really well each day." Accepting that ends revenge procrastination and burnout.
- **Balance across seasons.** Aporia: some seasons need deliberate over-allocation to work, travel or family. Name the season, keep health limits, set a review date.
- **Keep chosen friction.** Outsourcing every inconvenience also removes practice at cooking, navigating and improvising. Cut waste; keep the difficulty you chose (Aporia, quoting Sahil Bloom).
- When effort is not paying, the problem may be the environment rather than you; see [[Decision-Making]].

## Inner work

Jason Koon on self-loathing:

> "The darkness you carry inside is lying. It's tricked you into believing it's a superpower. [...] The truth of it all is it's nothing more than a parasite."

- Bruce Lee: "Don't speak negatively about yourself, even as a joke. Your body doesn't know the difference."
- Dostoevsky: "Your worst sin is that you betrayed yourself for nothing."

## The case against optimisation culture

@signulll on productivity content:

> "we’ve built an entire shadow economy around the simulation of work rather than work itself. the productivity-industrial complex feeds on the desire to feel like you’re making progress rather than actually making it."

Consuming guides and frameworks trains you to live in a preparatory state, "a kind of paralysis disguised as momentum". The fix is doing the thing, not refining how you will do it.

@xirtman goes further, calling Tim Ferriss, Andrew Huberman and Lex Fridman a "secular priesthood" of men without families:

> "a priesthood of hyper-individualist eunuchs preaching salvation through narcissistic self-improvement, leading us away from real, rooted life into the infinite loop of personal branding, quantified selfhood, and “high performance” — but not legacy."

He calls what they sell "Surrogate masculinity". The critique does not say the protocols fail; the wiki still uses Huberman for brain, sleep and supplement protocols ([[Podcast Hosts]]). Its charge is that optimisation became the goal instead of a tool for building something. [[Gender Dynamics]] carries the masculinity side.

Contradiction: these notes prize relentless small optimisations and also keep a critique that optimisation is a sterile substitute for a life. They fit only when the discipline serves something outside yourself.
