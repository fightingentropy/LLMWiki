# Market Drivers

Market drivers are the forces that move prices. Not every driver matters in every regime, so the working skill is knowing which ones are active now. The core of this page is the checklist in Erlin's trading note ([[Markets - Trading]]), grouped by kind, together with the positioning-and-flow section of the daily brief ([[Markets - Brief]]).

## Perception over truth

- The trading note opens: "Truth doesn't necessarily move markets. Perception & Uncertainty/Certainty transition does." It links the line to a [timestamped video](https://youtu.be/rZ9rUh9WhwU?t=1876) (from [[Markets - Trading]]).
- A passage in [[Bizz_and_Finance|Business & Finance]] makes the same point about headlines: "More often than not, the actual veracity of the headline is immaterial. It's how the market reacts to the news, and for how long, that turns out to be a lot more informative."

## The checklist

The trading note's "Things to look for", as written:

- CT Sentiment
- Crypto is momentum driven (meaning moving averages play a role & market structure is important)
- Macro
- mechanical movements
- liquidations
- Top traders positioning
- Catalysts
- Stablecoin dominance
- Stablecoin yields
- Funding rates
- options positioning

## Drivers by kind

### Sentiment and flow

- **CT sentiment:** community sentiment and positioning in crypto. See [[Sentiment]].
- **Stablecoin dominance:** USDT and USDC flows show commitment to or away from risk.
- **Stablecoin yields:** borrow and lend rates reveal leverage and funding pressure.
- **Funding rates:** extremes in perpetual-futures funding show crowding. See [[Funding Rates]]. Fading high funding is a trader heuristic that can fail in a retail-driven bull market, where funding "can stay higher than shorts can remain solvent" (see [[Market Cycles]]).

### Technical and mechanical

- **Moving averages:** momentum confirmation and trend structure. The note's reason: crypto is momentum driven.
- **Liquidations:** cascading liquidations drive volatility spikes. See [[Liquidations]].
- **Options positioning:** gamma exposure and vega dynamics.
- **Market structure:** bid-ask spread and order-flow imbalances. See [[Exchange Microstructure]].

[[fewseethis]] pushes back on treating liquidations as a cause: "the simple truth is that cascades occur as a RESULT of prices going down within a levered environment. in other words a cascade is a secondary effect." Then, "outside of a mechanical bounce", "prices can resume wherever they were going in the first place: up or down." (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]). Both views are kept: cascades amplify volatility, but they may not set the direction.

### Fundamental and macro

- **Macro regime:** the growth, inflation and liquidity backdrop. See [[Market Regime]] and [[Macro Trading]].
- **Catalysts:** events with binary outcomes, such as earnings, policy and geopolitics.
- **Top trader positioning:** the conviction of notable players. The trading note keeps a list of good traders to watch, among them [[fejau_inc]], [[HsakaTrades]], [[TheFlowHorse]], [[cobie]] and [[HighStakesCap]]; the full list is in [[Markets - Trading]] and [[Traders Directory]].

### Mechanical flows

- **CTA and systematic flow:** trend-following and risk-parity rebalances.
- **Dealer gamma:** hedging flows from option sales.
- **Redemptions:** ETF and fund outflows that force selling.

The daily brief asks for the same picture under "Positioning & Flow": consensus positioning and crowded trades; dealer, gamma, CTA and systematic flow context where available; sentiment extremes and squeeze risk (from [[Markets - Brief]]). See [[Positioning]] and [[Flow Analysis]].

## Which drivers matter when

Not all drivers matter equally in all regimes:

- **Trending markets:** momentum and flow dominate.
- **Choppy markets:** technicals and gamma whipsaw.
- **Regime transitions:** positioning extremes matter most.
- **Macro events:** fundamental catalysts dominate.

Every way of trading these drivers has a built-in cost. The trading note, summarising a [[CryptoCred]] post:

- Trend-following pays through false starts.
- Momentum often enters late and can buy tops or sell bottoms.
- Mean reversion periodically gets overwhelmed by a genuinely outsized move.
- "A recurring failure mode is abandoning a valid system because its unavoidable cost is mistaken for evidence that the system is broken."
