# Circadian Rhythm

The circadian rhythm is the body's roughly 24-hour clock. It times sleep, alertness and hormones such as melatonin and cortisol, and light is its strongest cue ([NIGMS](https://www.nigms.nih.gov/education/fact-sheets/Pages/circadian-rhythms)). Here it links [[ADHD]], sleep and the body note; persistent sleep problems belong with a clinician.

## ADHD and a late clock

Luu and Fabiano's 2025 perspective reports delayed sleep timing in up to 78% of people with ADHD, and an evening melatonin rise about 45 minutes late in children and 90 in adults. Brandon Luu's thread adds a graphic of later melatonin and a later, flatter morning cortisol rise; the curves illustrate the idea rather than measure anyone.

The suggested routine: a fixed wake time, morning bright light, less evening light and screen time, regular routines, and low-dose melatonin only where a delay is found. The paper applies this to a subgroup, as an addition to standard care. Full figures are on [[ADHD]].

A separate thread recasts ADHD as partly a histamine and sleep problem after one person's response to an antihistamine. One anecdote, no mechanism.

## Sleep timing and length

- From the body note: "sleeping late and waking up late brain fog."
- Tim Urban asks why people give up two hours of sleep they would gladly buy in pill form.
- [NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/how-much-sleep) puts adult need at seven to nine hours; [NICHD](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/sleep/conditioninfo/how-much) says it varies with age and the person.
- A video saved under "Reason why you can't sleep at night" ([at 53:22](https://youtu.be/hQ78Hpwrt80?t=3202)).

## Light and dopamine

Light sets the clock, which is why the routine above uses morning light and avoids evening light. The Medical note makes a different, biochemical claim: "Dopamine is synthesized from the amino acid Tyrosine, The first and crucial step is made by the enzyme Tyrosine Hydroxylase, Sunlight is needed for this step."

Correction (22 September 2026): the tyrosine-hydroxylase reaction uses tyrosine, oxygen and tetrahydrobiopterin; sunlight is not a reagent ([PubChem](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pathway/BioCyc%3AHUMAN_PWY66-301)). Light's effect on the clock is a separate mechanism.

## Bedtime habits from the body note

- Magnesium "30-60min before bed", from a stack attributed to @hubermanlab. Trials rate the sleep evidence low to very low certainty; see [[Body Optimization]].
- "Also don't take tongkat Ali too late in the afternoon because it can disrupt sleep."
- Cold showers appear as mood anecdotes, not as a way to reset the clock.
