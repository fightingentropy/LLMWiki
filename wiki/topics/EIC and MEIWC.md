# Electrical Certificates

The Electrical Installation Certificate (EIC) and Minor Electrical Installation Works Certificate (MEIWC) certify new work; the Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) reports on an existing installation, like an MOT. Details follow the IET's A4:2026 model forms.

|Document|Use|Scope|
|---|---|---|
|EIC|New circuits, consumer unit replacement, rewires, new installations|One or many circuits|
|MEIWC|Additions and alterations to an existing circuit, no new circuit|One per circuit|
|EICR|Condition of an existing installation|Whole installation or agreed extent|

Correction, 16 October 2026: work on several existing circuits needs no EIC per circuit; it takes one MEIWC each or a single EIC. An EIC is needed once a new circuit is added.

## Before testing

Record the supply: maximum demand, live conductors, earthing (TN-C-S, TN-S, TT), nominal voltage (U/U0), frequency, prospective fault current, Ze, and the origin device, usually the DNO fuse.

## The EIC

The EIC is complete only with its schedules: the 14-item schedule of inspections, circuit details and test results. It has three signatories: designer, constructor/installer, and inspector (inspection and testing). The client does not sign. A4 adds a second designer signature for shared design, and a single-signature version for one person in all three roles.

Correction, 16 October 2026: the A4 EIC has no qualifications field, and its declarations sit on the first page.

Exam tip: for signatories and layout, open the GN3 Chapter 5 model forms, not Chapter 2.

## The MEIWC

For adding sockets or lighting points to an existing circuit, moving a switch, or replacing accessories or luminaires. Replacements are not notifiable, but [Approved Document P](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a802da7ed915d74e622ceed/BR_PDF_AD_P_2013.pdf) para 3.13 says they should still be inspected, tested and certificated, and the A4 MEIWC says inspection and testing should always be done.

Correction, 16 October 2026: like-for-like replacements were described as not always needing certification.

## Add to good, not to bad

Regulation 132.16: no addition or alteration unless the existing installation is adequate and safe. The instructor's phrase: "We add to good, we don't add to bad."

Example: an old lighting circuit with no effective CPC cannot take a Class 1 luminaire. A CPC visible at one point does not prove the circuit; old conduit may serve if its continuity is reliable. Options: fit a Class 2 fitting, advise the client, or rewire the circuit on an EIC.

Correction, 22 September 2026: this example is too brief to decide compliance; it illustrates the judgement, and BS 7671 sets the conditions.

## The EICR

An EICR answers one question: is the installation safe for continued use? It records supply details, agreed extent and limitations, inspections, test results, coded observations, the overall result and the next inspection date.

|Code|Meaning|Effect|
|---|---|---|
|C1|Danger present; act immediately|Unsatisfactory|
|C2|Potentially dangerous; urgent action|Unsatisfactory|
|C3|Improvement recommended|Advisory|
|FI|"Further investigation is advised"|Advisory|

Each inspection item is recorded as a tick, C1 or C2, C3, FI, N/V, LIM or N/A, with "X" only for inadequate intake equipment. The declaration, by the inspector and the person authorising issue, asks for name, signature, organisation, position, address and date, with no qualifications field.

Correction, 16 October 2026: FI was listed as "required without delay" and making a report Unsatisfactory; that was the superseded A2:2022 form, and the [IET explains the change](https://electrical.theiet.org/wiring-matters/years/2026/110-july-2026/further-investigation/).

### Landlords

- **England** ([SI 2020/312, reg 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/312/regulation/3)): inspect and test at least every five years, or sooner if the report says. Give the report to new tenants before they move in, existing tenants within 28 days, and the council within 7 days of a request. Finish remedial work within 28 days, then send tenants and the council a qualified person's written confirmation within 28 days.
- [S.I. 2025/1043](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/1043/contents) extended the rules to social landlords; HMOs are covered.
- Contradiction: [GOV.UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance) (November 2025) still treats FI as 28-day work; the A4 form treats it as advisory.
- **Scotland** ([Repairing Standard](https://www.gov.scot/publications/repairing-standard-statutory-guidance-private-landlords/pages/16/)) and **Wales** ([2022 regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2022/6/regulation/6)) each require inspection at least every five years.

Correction, 16 October 2026: HMOs and social landlords fall within the England regulations.

## Signing and notifying

With the qualifications and genuine competence, one person can sign an EIC, MEIWC or EICR. Hold public liability and professional indemnity insurance, even for family and friends. Keep a copy of every certificate.

Signing a certificate is not notifying Building Control. Notifiable work ([[Building Regulations]]) can be self-notified only through a competent person (self-certification) scheme such as NICEIC or NAPIT. Otherwise use a third-party notification service, or notify building control, whose inspector checks the work. Registration involves assessment, inspection of work and ongoing CPD.
