# Self-Improvement Culture Critique

The notes carry a sharp critique of the self-optimisation and biohacking podcast axis: [[Tim_Ferriss|Tim Ferriss]], [[Andrew_Huberman|Andrew Huberman]] and [[Lex_Fridman|Lex Fridman]] as a "secular priesthood" of high-performance content cut off from family, lineage and real-world consequence. The core text is one X post by @xirtman, saved in full in both [[X Takes]] and [[Body]]. [[Masculinity_and_Men|Masculinity & Men]] develops it as one of two masculine archetypes the algorithm rewards. It sits in tension with [[Self_Improvement|Self-Improvement]], which the wiki treats as genuinely useful when it is pointed at building something.

## The secular priesthood

The [post](https://x.com/xirtman/status/1902893343014592631) _(@xirtman)_ opens:

> What we have here is a secular priesthood preaching gospel to a generation of men hungry for guidance, yet incapable of fulfilling the most basic biological or civilisational imperative: the creation of a family.

It then names the three priests:

> Tim Ferris: the productivity hacker turned self-experimenting monk. Andrew Huberman: the neuroscience demigod whose dopamine maps rarely intersect with intimacy or fatherhood. Lex Friedman: the emotionless black-clad pod-priest who flirts with sentimentality but recoils at generativity. These aren’t men with families — they’re cult leaders of sterile optimisation. Their mantra: hack your life — not live your life.

Its verdict on them:

> Ferris, Huberman, and Friedman are not countercultural sages. They are the ideal products of a dying civilisation: libidinally spent, spiritually castrated, generationally irrelevant.
>
> And what does it say about us — that we’ve enthroned them as our guides?

And its summary of the whole movement:

> I would call this the ultimate psyop: a priesthood of hyper-individualist eunuchs preaching salvation through narcissistic self-improvement, leading us away from real, rooted life into the infinite loop of personal branding, quantified selfhood, and “high performance” — but not legacy.

(from [[X Takes]]; also saved in [[Body]])

## Surrogate masculinity

The post's central charge is that the product on sale is a stand-in for masculinity:

> You want to know what they’re really selling? Surrogate masculinity. Masculinity unmoored from duty, generational continuity, or even real-world consequences. What they offer is a hollow, anaesthetized masculinity: dopamine schedules, journaling routines, five-hour morning rituals — but no sons, no daughters, no wives, no lineage. This is not wisdom. It’s a simulation.
>
> And millions of young men, starved of fathers, swallow it whole.

The Huberman line carries the sharpest version: his "dopamine maps rarely intersect with intimacy or fatherhood". His content is expert on managing your state and silent on why you would want a well-managed state ([[Andrew_Huberman|Andrew Huberman]]).

## What the algorithm selects

> Where are the podcasts about building families, holding down difficult relationships, sacrificing for children, standing up for tradition? The algorithm doesn’t reward those. It rewards slick, postmodern celibacy dressed as enlightenment.

Optimisation content is legible and drives engagement, so the feed rewards it. What it selects against is the generative thing the critique points toward ([[Masculinity_and_Men|Masculinity & Men]]).

The other algorithm-rewarded pole is the [[Andrew_Tate|Andrew Tate]] axis of overt display: wealth, aggression, anti-therapeutic rhetoric. It is the mirror image, performance in a different register. Both are signal-emission without load-bearing. [[Top Pods]] saves related clips: "The performative Male epidemic", "The war on Men", and two Tate clips labelled "about Fame" and "about Fall of Empire".

## The closing question

> Let that sink in.
>
> Do you want to follow men who optimise their testosterone — or men who use it to build a future?

Correction, 15 October 2026: this page earlier quoted the line as "men who optimize their lives — or men who use optimized lives to build futures"; the saved post says "testosterone" and "a future".

## What the critique concedes

The critique does not deny that the protocols work. It argues that the framing pulls men away from relational and generational responsibility as the path to meaning. The wiki still uses Huberman for brain, sleep and supplement protocols, and [[Top Pods]] saves his episode on reclaiming your brain.

[[X Takes]] also carries the joke version of the counter-argument. After reports that Huberman was seeing five women at once, two posts treat it as proof the protocols work:

> Andrew Huberman's ability to keep five women happy and sexually satisfied simultaneously, without their awareness of one another, at the age of 48, is the ultimate testament to the effectiveness of a bio-optimised lifestyle that you dorks should ever need.

> Andrew Huberman has been “outed” for having relationships with 5 different women at the same time. He’s a 48 years old man with the energy to date 5 different women. If that’s not a ringing endorsement of his methods, I don’t what is.

The second links to a [post by @francisjfoster](https://twitter.com/francisjfoster/status/1772300104193482788). The reports are claims in the posts.

## Optimisation as a loop

Two posts in [[Thoughts|Thoughts & Observations]] make the same critique from the side of work rather than masculinity.

- **The productivity-industrial complex.** From [@signulll](https://x.com/signulll): "we’ve built an entire shadow economy around the simulation of work rather than work itself. the productivity-industrial complex feeds on the desire to feel like you’re making progress rather than actually making it." Consuming guides, planners and frameworks trains you "to exist in a preparatory state instead of an executory one" — "a kind of paralysis disguised as momentum." See [[Technology_Critique|Technology Critique]].
- **Environment must convert ability into returns.** Self-improvement can become an endless optimisation loop when the surrounding structure stays untouched. More skill or discipline does not guarantee a better outcome if the environment cannot reward it. Ask which environment would turn what you already have into a return, and whether changing the structure would beat another decade of polishing yourself inside the wrong one ([post](https://x.com/incentivising/status/2082173497166446786) _(@incentivising)_).
