# Equities

Equities are the highest-risk, highest-return asset class and the most sensitive to growth expectations. In the daily market brief, the equity read covers index internals, breadth, leadership, and cyclicals versus defensives (from [[Markets - Brief]]). This page sets out what to check on each of those, the flows specific to stocks, the signals that point to recession, and the equity theses and trade setups saved from X, each kept as a hypothesis rather than a finding.

## Index level and breadth

- **Index moves**: the level of the SPX, Nasdaq and Russell 2000.
- **Advance/decline ratio**: the share of stocks advancing versus declining. Deteriorating breadth means weakness ahead.
- **Breadth indicators**: if a few stocks drive the whole index, that is concentration risk.

## Leadership and sector rotation

- **Mega-cap tech dominance**: gains concentrated in 7–10 names mean concentration risk.
- **Cyclicals vs. defensives**: rotation between them shows growth expectations.
- **Cyclical outperformance**: growth expected (rates up, earnings growth expected).
- **Defensive outperformance**: growth concerns (rates down, recession expectations).

See [[Sector Rotation]] for rotation patterns.

## Valuation

- **P/E ratios**: high valuations leave less margin of safety and more downside in a correction.
- **Earnings yield**: the inverse of P/E. Compare it with bond yields to judge relative attractiveness (see [[Rates]]).
- **PEG ratio**: P/E relative to the growth rate, which adjusts valuation for expected growth.
- **Forward guidance**: company earnings guidance reflects management's expectations.

## Growth and rate sensitivity

- **Earnings sensitivity**: tech and growth stocks are more sensitive to rates than value stocks.
- **Duration analogy**: high-growth stocks act like long-duration bonds, so they are rate-sensitive.
- **Multiple compression**: when rates rise, growth multiples compress even if earnings hold.

## Equity-specific flows

- **Buyback season**: Q1 has the most buybacks, which gives price support late in the quarter.
- **Index rebalancing**: on rebalancing dates, predetermined flows hit the market.
- **Options expiry**: monthly and weekly expiries can create gamma-hedging flows.

## Recession indicators

- **Earnings revisions**: falling forward estimates signal recession risk.
- **Credit spreads widening**: companies struggling to refinance means trouble ahead (see [[Credit]]).
- **Unemployment rising**: the first serious sign of recession.
- **Valuations contracting**: multiple compression means the market is forecasting lower earnings.

## Memory equities and the capacity response

[Jukan asks](https://x.com/jukan05/status/2078462124486283755) whether memory shares can peak three or four quarters before memory prices themselves, as announced fab capacity enters the horizon. Treat it as a **watchlist scenario**, not a timing rule: the post raises a plausible market question, not evidence that the cycle has already turned. SK hynix's own disclosures support tracking the capacity response, not the post's timing conclusion.

What to track:

- Announced capex against clean-room completion and wafer-start timing.
- Product mix and utilization.
- Contract-price revisions.
- Whether HBM's larger wafer requirements absorb nominal capacity.

This is the equity-market expression of the supply-response problem in [[Market Cycles]]. (from [[X-Bookmarks]] and [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]])

## AI and equity valuations

- **AI infrastructure**: adoption and tokens per user can drive demand, while frontier-lab pricing, training capex, future revenue, power, and eventual overbuild decide who captures value. Fin's essay makes the two-driver case and expects overbuilding and margin pressure; its company figures and its optimistic 2027–2028 conclusion are the author's claims. See [[AI Infrastructure Economics]].
- **Slower model progress**: a compact downside scenario holds that if frontier capability gains flatten sharply, current AI equity valuations may need a severe reset.
- **Concentrated gains**: a contrarian thesis argues the visible gains remain concentrated in hyperscalers and semiconductors without broad productivity gains. A repricing scenario, not settled evidence.
- **Agent-driven usage**: an out-of-consensus basket of public infrastructure businesses that could benefit from agent-driven internet usage.

## Research hypotheses

Each of these is a question to test, not a conclusion.

- **GLP-1 and apparel**: changing body size and shopping behavior could affect apparel demand. Geiger Capital attributes Victoria's Secret's claimed one-year share-price rise to GLP-1 use, but a single share-price move cannot isolate that effect from company execution, valuation, marketing, or the market.
- **Lab-grown diamonds**: falling production costs support a commoditization bear case (Saul Sadka's argument). Natural provenance premiums, branding, resale liquidity, supply discipline, and changing taste keep the outcome open.
- **Situational Awareness liquidation**: Axios reported that the AI-focused fund sold its public-equity portfolio to Citadel after heavy losses. That transfer is distinct from Flood's unverified scenario that a liquidity provider engineered or anticipated the fund's risk-limit failure.
- **"Orchestrated market" claims**: one post links Nasdaq weakness, the Situational Awareness liquidation, geopolitical headlines, oil reversals, Nvidia commentary and later rallies into a theory of coordinated manipulation. A sequence of moves and headlines is not evidence of coordination without order-level data, causal identification, communications, or independent corroboration.

## Single-stock setups and market mechanics

- **SNDK extension and fade**: one trader's setup used ATR extension from the 50-day average plus an earnings gap and an opening-range-breakout entry; another's postmortem tied the gap's fade to extreme trend extension, profit-taking liquidity, and call-hedge unwinds.
- **IPO opening**: a Nasdaq IPO may not trade at the opening bell, because the exchange runs a display-only period and a pre-launch phase before the IPO Cross.
- **Thematic ETFs**: one framework moves crypto traders' narrative and sector-rotation instincts onto thematic ETFs rather than single stocks.
- **Tokenized stocks**: venue-issued wrappers such as SPYx and QQQx are tokenized exposure, not shares.
