# Culture and Environment

The thesis here is that place is a first-order cause of personality. Country, city, routine and physical surroundings shape a person more than a job title or a life plan, whether or not they notice. The idea comes from a post saved in [[Culture|Culture & Observations]] and [[X Takes]], and is extended in [[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]] under "Identity & Geographic Destiny". It sits underneath many of the wiki's dating, politics and self-improvement observations. The implicit lever: change your location, change your defaults.

## Every country has an energy

The core text is a post by [@tishray](https://x.com/tishray/status/2031783721397809397), saved in both [[Culture|Culture & Observations]] and [[X-Bookmarks]]:

> Every country has an energy. And that energy rewires you whether you notice it or not. People move to Japan and become minimal. People move to Mexico and their entire relationship with time softens. People move to New York and suddenly they can't sit still. Your personality is far more malleable than you think. We treat it like something fixed, but new surroundings give you new defaults. New pace. New habits. New values absorbed through proximity instead of effort. You're not just the average of the 5 people closest to you. You're the average of the 5 places, the 5 routines, and the 5 inputs you're exposed to most. Your commute shapes you. The weather shapes you. Every space you occupy is voting on who you become. That's why I believe choosing where you live is one of the most important decisions you'll ever make. More important than your job title. Maybe more important than your five-year plan. Because the place shapes the plan. The place shapes your energy, your habits, your relationships, your default state. Get the place right and half of the other decisions start making themselves. Get it wrong and you'll fight yourself every day.

The claims, broken out:

- **Personality is malleable.** Far more so than assumed. New surroundings give new defaults, pace, habits and values, absorbed through proximity rather than effort (from [[X-Bookmarks]], [[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]]).
- **The average of 5 places, not 5 people.** The places, routines and inputs you are exposed to most set who you become. Commute and weather count.
- **Place over job title.** Choosing where you live is one of the most important decisions, more than career choices, because "the place shapes the plan" ([[Culture|Culture & Observations]], [[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]]).

The same mechanism, proximity doing the work that effort would otherwise do, drives attraction in [[Proximity_Infatuation|Proximity Infatuation]].

## Countries and cities as characters

Japan is the recurring example of a place that sets a person's defaults. A post saved in [[X Takes]] pushes back on the usual story about it:

> Before visiting Japan, I'd read all the usual books: loneliness, isolation, hikikomori. the whole narrative. But when I got there, I saw the opposite. subways full of kids, teens in big groups, laughing, going places together. I live in NYC. You rarely see that here. It made me think maybe we've been telling the wrong story

It quotes @DeLicious_Tacos: "Japan is doing fine. People are happy. Turns out chasing economic growth is horseshit." More on Japanese ideas in [[Japanese_Philosophy|Japanese Philosophy & Cultural Concepts]].

Cities get the same treatment. [Jon Wu's San Francisco versus New York post](https://x.com/jonwu_/status/2093463094156828880) satirically contrasts New York's commitment to desire and desirability with a San Francisco tech culture that suppresses sex appeal to focus on hard problems. A [counterpost by @_johnsonator](https://x.com/_johnsonator/status/2093745991182647766) calls the "tech hot girl renaissance" a sad retrospective rewrite of high-school status. Both are caricatures, not sociology (from [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]]).

## Spaces shape behaviour

"Every space you occupy is voting on who you become" also works at room scale. A post by @signulll, saved in [[Culture|Culture & Observations]]:

> just went to a [cocktail](https://x.com/signulll/status/1926453874753175612) bar in austin that confiscates your phone at the door.
> first you feel phantom limb syndrome but then you remember what it's like when conversations actually finish instead of reaching for your phone anytime you have a gap or get a notification.
> i want more spaces like this.

The [[Catfish_Effect|Catfish Effect]] applies the same idea to organisations: a post by @ruima describes Chinese business culture deliberately adding a little hostility to an environment because stagnation is the bigger risk, and ends: "is that tank you're in the best environment for you? Are you swimming because you want to, or because you finally have to?" (from [[Culture|Culture & Observations]])

## Climate and air conditioning

Climate is treated as a precondition for civilisation. [[Lee_Kuan_Yew|Lee Kuan Yew]], founding father of Singapore, quoted in [[Body]]:

> "Air conditioning was a most important invention for us, perhaps one of the signal inventions of history.
>
> It changed the nature of civilization by making development possible in the tropics.
>
> Without air conditioning you can work only in the cool early-morning hours or at dusk.
>
> The first thing I did upon becoming prime minister was to install air conditioners in buildings where the civil service worked. This was key to public efficiency."

The jokes and takes around it (the figures and the intelligence claim are the posters' own, unsourced):

- "Thinking about how lack of air conditioning kills 4x more people in Europe than guns in America is my Roman Empire" ([post by @gammichan](https://x.com/gammichan/status/2019970324411080873), from [[Culture|Culture & Observations]]).
- "My [lasting](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1940877091601522768) legacy will be getting AC installed in every last building in Europe reviving their eco brain rotted minds back to high IQ and ambition" (@levelsio, from [[X Takes]]).
- "[Temperature is DIRECTLY related to intelligence](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1919475601574183307)" (@levelsio, from [[Body]]).

## Nature, cities and politics

Environment is also read as a source of political values. From [[Politics]], summarised in [[Political_Commentary|Political Commentary]]:

> u dont see ppl who spend lots of time in nature supporting mass immigration, its mostly an urbanite idea bc theyve already gotten used to living like rats and see no problem debasing themselves further & stuffing as many ppl into their nation as possible. theyre spiritual rodents

The claim is a take, with no data behind it: time in nature builds a sense of boundaries, while dense city life normalises crowding.

## When a place changes

If place shapes people, a changing place is felt as personal loss. Posts in this vein:

- [@CF_Farrow on central London](https://x.com/CF_Farrow/status/1933053157971525731): "I walked through central London last night and realised something that made me genuinely sad: The city I once loved—vibrant, safe, recognisably English—is gone." (from [[X Takes]])
- [The hollowed-out UK high street](https://x.com/TheCriticalDri2/status/2085371797763998127): The Critical Drinker describes high streets of vape shops, nail salons, phone shops and barbers as an atmosphere of commercial decline. An observation to test against vacancy, footfall and rent data; the hint that quiet businesses are illegitimate is unsupported (from [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]]).
- [Returning through a British airport](https://x.com/stefanroberts/status/2098694726945816958): a short note on feeling both disgust and comfort on coming home (from [[Bookmarks - Culture & Ideas]]).
