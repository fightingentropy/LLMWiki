# Mental Health

Saved takes on depression, therapy, stress, hope, attention and psychedelics, with a study beside the ones that overreach. They are viewpoints, not treatment advice; persistent symptoms need a clinician. The links live in [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]] and [[Psychology & Behavior]]; [[Health Evidence]] sets out how strong each kind of evidence is.

## Depression

One saved post gives a single list of causes:

> So now that the chemical imbalance theory has been conclusively disproven what causes depression?
>
> -learned helplessness
>
> -Not leaving the house, not socializing, high dopamine baseline due to social media, lack of sunlight, poor diet, poor sleep.

Other posts name one cause each: lost purpose, low testosterone, or a dopamine problem fixed by a three-day "reset". Contradiction: [NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression) describes genetic, biological, environmental and psychological contributors, treated with therapy, medication or both. The lifestyle list helps; it is not the whole cause.

## Therapy, movement and medication

One saved line prefers walking to therapy:

> I've met many more people who turned their lives around by walking around aimlessly for a couple hours a day than I’ve met people who have had their lives improved at all by professional therapy

@Theholisticpsyc: "Dance is therapy. Movement is therapy. Creating is therapy. Cuddling is therapy. Writing is therapy. Sun is therapy." A saved list of takes adds "ssris are overprescribed". Yet a therapist's line below is kept as the closure that worked, and nothing saved shows movement beats medication.

On ADHD stimulants, two first-person accounts disagree: one user blames Vyvanse for irritability, isolation and personality change (@LawrenceElliots); Adderall made one woman feel more like herself (@Gena_I_Gorlin). See [[ADHD]].

## Stress: acute is fuel, chronic is poison

> Acute stress is good. It makes you better, stronger, more adaptive. Chronic stress is bad. It makes you weak burnt out, and depressed
>
> Modern life is full of chronic stress (social isolation, bad sleep, soulless work, 24/7 news cycles) and void of acute stress (Competition, high intensity exercise, short conflict).
>
> Eliminate chronic stressors & replace with acute stressors.

_(@NoahRyanCo)_ A saved take on [[Nassim Taleb]]'s antifragility goes further: "Antifragile systems: *need* shocks to improve."

Practices saved beside it, all personal accounts:

- **Box breathing** for stress and focus.
- **Adrenaline on command.** @sporadicalia walks outside to fast music and times "2-4 short rapid inhales through your nose then one long exhale" to the beat. Stop any breathing drill that makes you dizzy.
- **Cold showers.** "it almost feels like taking a pill or something" _(reply to @olivesagan)_. A gentler version: notice the bracing before cold water and let it go (@jonnym1ller).
- **Stillness.** "put ALL devices away for 24 hours, exist in solitude, and simply let ur brain do its thing." _(@divya_venn)_

## Hope

In Curt Richter's 1957 experiments, whisker-trimmed wild rats dropped in water died within 15 minutes, while trimmed domesticated rats swam 40 to 60 hours. Wild rats that had been held and dipped a few times first swam as long as the domestic ones. Richter read the quick deaths as hopelessness: rats that knew escape was possible kept swimming.

Correction (16 October 2026): the 15-minute and 40–60-hour figures apply to whisker-trimmed rats, not to all rats.

## Attention and rumination

- **Mind-wandering.** Killingsworth and Gilbert (2010) found people were less happy while their minds wandered, even toward pleasant topics.
- **Blocking mobile internet.** In a randomised trial, two weeks without phone internet improved well-being, mental health and sustained attention. Only 119 of 467 participants fully complied.
- **Isolation loops.** Staying home, worry and falling motivation feed each other; the saved advice is one small first action.

Overthinking versus acting is covered in [[Self-Improvement]].

## Psychedelics and false insight

After four cups of ayahuasca, one poster wrote:

> If psychedelics have given me any durable insight, it's that our consciousness is extremely fallible and often misleading, particularly with respect to valence.

The poster argues the same false-insight mechanism gives psychedelics therapeutic potential, if aimed at a real problem. The replies disagree: "Taking psychedelics is the smartest you’ll ever think you are, combined with the dumbest you’ll ever be." Another calls them harmful for most people.
