# Mental Health

Erlin's notes on mental health are mostly saved X posts and personal one-liners: explanations of depression, scepticism about therapy, lines on honesty and love, a stress framework, psychedelic insight, hope, and attention. Where a saved claim overreaches, NIMH guidance, a clinical guideline or a published study sits beside it. Quotations are the notes' viewpoints, not diagnoses or treatment advice. [[Health Evidence]] holds the evidence ladder applied to these claims.

## Depression: the single-explanation claim

A post in the Psychology note answers "what causes depression?" with one list (from [[Psychology & Behavior]]):

> So now that the chemical imbalance theory has been conclusively disproven what causes depression?
>
> -learned helplessness
>
> -Not leaving the house, not socializing, high dopamine baseline due to social media, lack of sunlight, poor diet, poor sleep.

The older notes treated reversing those factors as a complete solution. The post's premise that the chemical-imbalance theory is "conclusively disproven" is its own claim; the notes do not check it. The purpose-as-cure bookmark repeats the same single-explanation pattern: [@minordissent's post](https://x.com/minordissent/status/2094327547672805434) frames depression as the psyche refusing an unaligned life, with purpose as the cure. Neither source establishes that.

**Correction, 22 September 2026:** [NIMH's depression overview](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression) describes genetic, biological, environmental and psychological contributors, and treatments that may include psychotherapy, medication or both. Meaning, routines and relationships can be topics for care without being a universal cure. The invitation to examine meaning, relationships, work and environment is worth keeping; purpose does not replace assessment, therapy, medication, safety planning or other appropriate care. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

Other depression-related posts in the notes:

- **Testosterone and mood.** [A post](https://x.com/wanted4mogging/status/2094567596490162625) argues that prescribing antidepressants without testosterone screening proves systemic failure. The narrower point is useful: low mood, energy, libido, concentration and sexual symptoms can overlap with hypogonadism and may justify clinical evaluation. Diagnosis needs compatible symptoms plus consistently low, accurately measured morning testosterone, not universal screening or an assumption that testosterone is the cause. The [Endocrine Society guideline](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/testosterone-therapy) is the reference. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])
- **A three-day cure.** [A post](https://x.com/oxidativestate/status/2097515710172025053) claims a dopamine reset rapidly cured depression and ADHD. Unsupported; it is a misinformation example, not evidence, diagnosis or treatment guidance.
- **Gut microbiota in rats.** A [2016 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2016.07.019) transferred pooled fecal microbiota from three severely depressed male donors, or three matched controls, into microbiota-depleted rats and reported anhedonia- and anxiety-like behaviour plus altered tryptophan metabolism. Small preclinical animal evidence; not proof that human depression is transmissible, and not a basis for self-treatment.

### Depression, memory and concentration

Difficulty concentrating, remembering or making decisions is a recognised symptom of depression, and severe cases can involve prominent memory and thinking problems (NIMH, above). Three saved posts sit here:

- [Laiiyylaa's post](https://x.com/Laiiyylaa/status/2085925048335126831): a lived-experience account the note ties to depression's effect on concentration and memory.
- [An anhedonia-to-skill-loss model](https://x.com/thehowlchemist/status/2094249098312384915): reduced pleasure and practice can plausibly erode fluency.
- [A memory-loss observation](https://x.com/DEUILSTEARS444/status/2096602733654552989): depression and anxiety can be associated with substantial memory problems. A prompt for clinical reading, not a self-diagnosis or causal conclusion.

None of them establishes inevitable permanent "brain damage", neurotoxicity or irreversible pruning. Persistent cognitive symptoms deserve clinical assessment rather than self-diagnosis from a post. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

## Therapy scepticism in the notes

The personal notes criticise therapy and favour movement, connection and making things (from [[Thoughts & Observations]]):

> I've met many more people who turned their lives around by walking around aimlessly for a couple hours a day than I’ve met people who have had their lives improved at all by professional therapy

A list of takes in the Medical note says "ssris are overprescribed" and dismisses therapy in derogatory terms (from [[Medical & Health]]). A saved line in the Psychology note, next to the handle @Theholisticpsyc, lists the alternatives: "Dance is therapy. Movement is therapy. Creating is therapy. Cuddling is therapy. Writing is therapy. Sun is therapy."

The notes are not uniform. They also keep a therapist's line as the closure that worked (see Relationships below). The preference for walking and making things is a viewpoint. It does not establish that therapy is ineffective or that lifestyle practices replace indicated care.

### ADHD and medication

Two first-person accounts of prescribed stimulants point in opposite directions (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]]):

- **Negative, Vyvanse.** [@LawrenceElliots](https://x.com/LawrenceElliots/status/2102046696142238206) attributes relationship problems, sensory irritation, isolation and personality changes to 70 mg lisdexamfetamine, and quotes a broader anti-medication claim from @AntiDoc. One person's experience, not evidence that prescribed stimulants cause autism or affect everyone similarly.
- **Positive, Adderall.** [@Gena_I_Gorlin](https://x.com/Gena_I_Gorlin/status/2101802695325528433) says medication helped her feel more consistently herself, and links a longer essay about moving from ADHD scepticism to treatment. A contrasting individual experience, not a guarantee of benefit or a dosing recommendation.

Neither resolves what another person will experience; medication concerns belong with the prescriber. [[ADHD]] covers the circadian-timing paper and the stimulant mechanism summary in detail.

## Words as reality builders

> Also words are like spells, be very careful while labelling yourself as "traumatized" or "depressed", they start to become real.

The line closes [a post](https://x.com/FiercelyVirgo/status/1892433169657233763) that opens with "Study shows you can improve your mental health by training the suppression of unwanted thoughts aka 'you can just get over things'"; the note does not name the study. The quotation is a metaphor about self-description. The notes do not establish that naming a condition creates it, or that avoiding a label removes a clinical problem.

Related lines in the Psychology note:

- "every super smart person i know is obsessed with how manipulating the meaning of words allows a person to warp the world" (next to the handle @mollyfmielke).
- "Your [subconscious](https://x.com/manini_m/status/1907296417661513758) mind only knows you. Every time you think and speak ill of someone else, it assumes you’re talking about yourself."

The note gives the handle @muchnerve beside it. Sometimes truth serves ego, not healing. Context and audience matter. (from [[Psychology & Behavior]])

## Self-criticism and regret

Two motivational posts in the Psychology note push against self-punishment. [One](https://x.com/JasonKoon/status/1870104809107230763) argues that self-loathing passes itself off as a useful edge:

> The darkness you carry inside is lying. It’s tricked you into believing it’s a superpower. [...] The truth of it all is it’s nothing more than a parasite. [...] Fight the darkness, be kind to yourself, and keep moving forward.

The other, with no link, argues against regret:

> Never entertain the thought that you might have ruined your life. Everyone screws up at some point and feels the pain. But the regret only festers when you falsely believe that the alternative would've been a perfect reality.

It continues: "It's not on you to predict or control. Take it one day at a time and keep moving forward." The bookmarks add a related frame. [Legitimate suffering and neurotic avoidance](https://x.com/aristomarinetti/status/2018034523141804336) is a Jungian idea: attempts to evade unavoidable human pain can manufacture larger, unnecessary suffering ([[Carl Jung]]). All three are motivational framing, not treatment.

## Relationships as mental health

The notes put intimate relationships near the centre of how a life feels, and offer no comparative evidence that they are the strongest predictor of mental health.

> My psychology professor's words today: "You fall in love with the people who make you love the person you are when you're around them"

> My therapist once said "People don't abandon the people they love, they abandon the people they're using" and that was all the closure I needed

> Real closure is being okay with unfinished endings, messy conclusions, and words left [unsaid](https://x.com/onlystresstoday/status/1891428063193292829).

Two entries separate attraction from attachment:

- **Proximity infatuation.** A saved post says "ur coworker is not Hot they’re just within 10 ft of u 40 hrs/week", [called](https://x.com/Nia_mp4/status/1869907388557340785) "proximity infatuation". See [[Proximity Infatuation]].
- **Limerence.** [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/limerence) describes limerence as intense, involuntary fixation on another person. It is a descriptive concept, not an official medical diagnosis. The clinic discusses insecure attachment and a possible vulnerability tied to ADHD hyperfocus, not an inevitable outcome of ADHD or a universal biochemical explanation. The [original post](https://x.com/apaji_x/status/2091560421555614093) overstates it as a severe dopamine addiction. A useful distinction is reciprocal knowledge of a real person versus an imagined relationship reinforced by checking and rumination. Persistent distress or impairment is a reason to seek professional support, not a social-media dopamine label. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

## Overthinking, rumination and isolation

The notes favour action over rumination:

> aim. fire. correct later

> you are getting lapped by people 50% dumber than you because they don’t overthink shit.

> Most successful people I've met are not that smart. They just move fast, take risks and work a lot. Trust me.

Another post says [one pattern in all miserable people](https://x.com/TaylinSimmonds/status/1878819866070679565) is that "They overthink and underact." A podcast-clip comment in the same note argues that grinding is usually the worst move and that detaching from outcomes is "paradoxical but it's the only thing that will maybe improve your odds of escaping the quicksand" ([clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7oJGvyVU65Y&t=804s)). Keep these as motivational framing, not an explanation of everyone's distress.

Bookmarked posts add more careful versions:

- **Isolation loops.** [A post](https://x.com/sunnkssdseraph/status/2086173566710743236) describes staying home, repetitive thought, worry and declining motivation reinforcing one another, then recommends a small first action. A useful recognition prompt. "Nervous system stuck in survival mode" is not a diagnosis, and similar changes can come from depression, anxiety, physical illness, disability, exhaustion, grief, unsafe circumstances or ordinary preference. Small re-entry steps are an experiment, not a diagnosis; persistent impairment warrants professional assessment rather than forced exposure. The reference is [NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression).
- **Repetitive negative thinking.** [A humorous reaction](https://x.com/dhh/status/2101644926094373128) to a quoted claim that repetitive negative thinking is associated with Alzheimer's-related protein accumulation. Association does not establish causation or show that optimism prevents disease; the underlying study, population, measures and effect size are not in the note. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])
- **Mind-wandering.** Killingsworth and Gilbert's 2010 [experience-sampling study](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1192439) found lower momentary happiness while minds wandered, including toward neutral or pleasant topics, across many daily activities. It is observational self-report about attention and concurrent mood. It does not prove that consuming instead of creating causes clinical depression, or that a "creative quest" is a diagnosis or cure. The note came via [@NTFabiano](https://x.com/NTFabiano/status/2086444739805532641).

A saved question, "You’ve Been Tricked Into Believing Your [Thoughts](https://youtu.be/sYdfgkC3ivs)", points to a video; the note adds no summary.

## Stress architecture

From the Body note, with the post [here](https://x.com/NoahRyanCo/status/1928925779699839308):

> Acute stress is good. It makes you better, stronger, more adaptive. Chronic stress is bad. It makes you weak burnt out, and depressed
>
> Modern life is full of chronic stress (social isolation, bad sleep, soulless work, 24/7 news cycles) and void of acute stress (Competition, high intensity exercise, short conflict).
>
> Eliminate chronic stressors & replace with acute stressors.

- **Acute stress** (competition, intense exercise, conflict) = adaptive, beneficial.
- **Chronic stress** (constant low-level threat, isolation, drudgery) = toxic.

This is a framing about stress, not a prescription to seek conflict or intense exposure. The same note carries a bare "Boxed [breathing](https://x.com/StrongManGuide/status/1909559675818754406)" link. See [[Stress Management]] and [[Body Optimization]].

## Psychedelics and false insight

A post about four cups of ayahuasca (from [[Psychology & Behavior]]):

> At some point, the sense of epiphany continued without any meaningful referent, as if I was leaning on the epiphany key in my brain.
>
> That then triggered a meta-epiphany that psychedelics only make you feel like you're learning something new or deep, sorta like how déjà vu is probably just your brain's familiarity circuit misfiring in a context that isn't actually familiar.
>
> If psychedelics have given me any durable insight, it's that our consciousness is extremely fallible and often misleading, particularly with respect to valence.

Psychedelics make consciousness feel exceptionally clear and generate a sense of deep insight. **But consciousness is fallible, especially about valence.**

In the follow-up the poster says the same mechanism behind "false insight" gives psychedelics their therapeutic potential, which is why intentionality matters: aim the epiphanies at a real psychological blocker. One person in the group said ayahuasca helped him forgive his father, something he already wanted to do. The poster compares it to LLM hallucinations: whether an insight is "false" depends on whether it [attaches to some ground truth](https://x.com/hamandcheese/status/1866744014432514340). The poster also says psychedelics are not for everyone and that ayahuasca is not a good first trip.

The notes disagree on this:

- A reply: "Your “meta-epiphany” is the typical takeaway if you have a firm sense of self."
- Another: "Taking psychedelics is the smartest you’ll ever think you are, combined with the dumbest you’ll ever be." It adds that this is "a great recipe for a specific type of person to lose touch with [reality](https://x.com/Liu_eroteme/status/1866885071887651093)."
- A third, "unpopular opinion": [psychedelics are harmful and counterproductive](https://twitter.com/thecaptain_nemo/status/1764387877381894520) to the vast majority of people. "But *very* rarely you meet someone who has clearly gone to the other side, seen the void, crossed the river styx and returned with skulls."

The therapeutic-potential claim is speculation. It does not establish safety, effectiveness or a self-treatment protocol.

## Hope and hopelessness

The Psychology note saves a post on [Swimming Rats and the Power of Hope](https://www.shawnmaust.com/2016/12/swimming-rats-power-hope/#:~:text=A%20few%20weeks%20ago%2C%20I40%2D60%20hours%20on%20average.). Its account: in a 1957 study, Dr. Curt Richter's team found that domesticated Norwegian rats swam around 40-60 hours in tolerable water, while wild Norwegian rats died within 15 minutes. Wild rats that were put in the water and pulled out a few times beforehand lasted about as long as the domesticated group. The team postulated that the deaths were more psychological than physiological, a matter of hopelessness. The post concludes that giving someone hope may be one of the most valuable gifts.

The notes use the story as a metaphor for hope and endurance. The wiki holds no primary experimental record, so the timings and the exclusively psychological reading are the post's retelling. Do not infer a clinical mechanism from it.

## Exercise, movement and neuroplasticity

Movement is a recurring preference in the personal notes: "Exercise rewires the [brain](https://x.com/NTFabiano/status/1926295974428766537)" (Body note), the walking line and the "Dance is therapy" list above. Saved posts add:

- **Neuroplasticity and deliberate repetition.** [Alexei's motivational essay](https://x.com/alexeixbt/status/2086051211271196886) centres the continuing capacity of adult brains to change through experience, and the importance of repeated practice, attention, recovery and sleep. It is an accessible behavioural frame, not a neuroscience protocol: acetylcholine is one of several neuromodulators rather than a universal "save switch", and the essay does not establish that brief intense movement guarantees learning, that willpower is a single trainable muscle, or that wealth, confidence and personality reduce to repeated neural wiring. A [human acetylcholine and motor-cortex plasticity study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6673455/) is linked as background.
- **Cravings.** [A post](https://x.com/alexeixbt/status/2100716907108151736) claims that resisting a craving at peak intensity creates a larger "rewiring" effect. Motivational framing, not an established dose-response rule; exposure and addiction interventions can need professional support and should not be improvised from a post.
- **Cold-shower "unbracing".** [An anecdotal somatic exercise](https://x.com/jonnym1ller/status/1958345432410845515): notice the anticipatory tension before cold water and practise releasing it. A mindfulness prompt, not medical or psychological treatment advice.

**Correction, September 2026:** an earlier version of this page said walking, dancing, cold exposure and sun were all proven more effective than most pharmaceuticals for mood. The cited material does not support that, and it is not carried forward as guidance.

## Reclaiming attention

[[Top Pods]] saves [[Andrew Huberman]]'s "[How to reclaim your brain in 2026](https://youtu.be/YvWU4Zd-IMc)". This page treats attention as upstream of the overthinking and screen habits above. That is a framing, not an established mechanism.

- **Two weeks without mobile internet.** A preregistered month-long randomized trial ([PNAS Nexus](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/2/pgaf017/8016017)) had participants block internet access on their smartphones for two weeks, keeping calls, texts and internet on other devices. Intention-to-treat analyses found better subjective well-being, mental health and objectively measured sustained attention. Compliance was difficult: only 119 of 467 committed participants met the preregistered compliance threshold. It was not total smartphone or internet abstinence, and the "ten years younger" comparison is an effect-size analogy, not literal rejuvenation. Found via [@NTFabiano](https://x.com/NTFabiano/status/2084270984022905286), with [additional discussion](https://x.com/sentientist/status/2054591492162040312). (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])
- **Devices away, solitude.** A post says: "if you need clarity all u have to do is put ALL devices away for 24 hours, exist in solitude, and simply let ur brain do its thing. go on walks. lie on bed and stare at ceiling. the harsh truths will [come](https://x.com/divya_venn/status/1928822020789145912)."
- **Why stillness is avoided.** "Do people hate being still/unstimulated/without distractions because the body will automatically and wisely serve you whichever thing is higher on the stack of things you need to resolve? Is that why they’re so [avoidant](https://x.com/nosilverv/status/1928018733739409816) of it?"
- **Phone and memory.** A saved moment on [the phone's impact on memory and insights](https://youtu.be/JHjhw8Ek3Zk?t=1594).
