# HIP-3 Silver Microstructure

"HIP-3 Silver Microstructure: Hyperliquid vs. CME" is a post by @shaundadevens, saved in [[Markets - Articles]], comparing how silver trades on [[Hyperliquid]] (decentralised) and on [[CME]] (centralised). The note keeps only the title and link. This page sets out the two venues, the volume claims from posts saved around Hyperliquid's silver launch, and the questions a venue comparison like this answers.

## The two venues

- **Hyperliquid**: decentralised exchange with on-chain settlement. HIP-3 markets are permissionless, builder-deployed perpetual markets on its chain; [[TradeXYZ]] is one such builder DEX. See [[Hyperliquid]].
- **CME**: centralised, traditional derivatives exchange.

## Silver volume on Hyperliquid

Posts from early 2026 claim Hyperliquid's silver market drew real volume, and a measurable share against CME, within weeks of listing (from [[X-Bookmarks]]). The figures are the posters' own.

- @0xCryptoSam, 30 January 2026: "Silver did ~$3B in volume on Hyperliquid today. Meanwhile, the CME traded 275k contracts (~$130B notional). Hyperliquid just captured ~2% of the world's primary silver market for an asset it listed 30 days ago."
- That was a violent day for silver. @TheShortBear: "You are witnessing the second biggest down day in Silver in 60y." @DonAlt: "The silver chart is hilarious / Just straight up rug pull crypto style".
- @MomentumKevin, March 2026: "There are new eyes being set on Hyperliquid as the optimal place to trade due to the volatility and volume density in HIP-3 commodities. It's a noteworthy shift in the available tools for retail trading".

## Questions to bring to the comparison

The note saves only the title, so these are the dimensions a Hyperliquid-versus-CME comparison turns on, not a summary of the post's findings. See [[Exchange Microstructure]] for each concept.

- **Order flow**: how orders are matched and executed.
- **Spreads**: bid-ask spreads and slippage.
- **Price discovery**: which venue leads.
- **Liquidity provision**: how market makers are incentivised.
- **Latency**: geographic advantages and speed.
- **Regulatory structure**: how rules affect trading. One saved post imagines regulated or KYC venues coexisting with unregulated HIP-3 markets, linked by arbitrage; that is speculation, not an announced feature (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]]).

What the answers tell a trader:

- **Trading edge**: which venue offers better execution.
- **Price discovery**: which market moves first.
- **Arbitrage**: dislocations between venues. The [[CT|Crypto Takes]] note saves a post with the reminder to "check Dislocation betweetn Different exchanges."
- **Structural stability**: how each venue holds up in stress periods.

## Why it matters

As crypto derivatives mature and attract institutional volume, knowing how on-chain venues compare with traditional centralised exchanges becomes critical for traders judging execution quality and price discovery. Silver is a clean test case: the same commodity now trades on a long-established futures exchange and on an on-chain perpetual market. For silver's own drivers, see [[Silver]] and [[Commodities]].
