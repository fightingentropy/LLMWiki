# Building Regulations

Electrical work in a building meets several separate requirements: building law, the technical standard, safe-working duties and certification. This page covers the law and guidance that apply in England, Part P notification, concealed cable routes, and limits for chases and notches. For real work, use the current [[BS 7671 and GN3|BS 7671]] text and the official guidance.

## Law and guidance

|Statutory|Non-statutory guidance|
|---|---|
|Health and Safety at Work Act; Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations; Electricity at Work Regulations; PUWER; Building Regulations|Approved Documents (P, M, A, L); BS 7671; GN3; On-Site Guide|

Approved Documents show one way to comply; they are not the law itself. A qualification, a test certificate and a Building Control notification are separate records, and one never stands in for another.

## Part P (England)

Part P is the legal requirement. [Approved Document P](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-approved-document-p) (2013, still the current edition) is guidance on meeting it.

- **Scope** (AD P para 2.2): dwellings, plus installations outside the dwelling and in outbuildings, common areas of blocks of flats, and business premises on the same meter as a dwelling.
- **Notifiable** ([regulation 12(6A)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2010/2214/regulation/12)): every new circuit and every consumer unit replacement, wherever it is; additions or alterations to existing circuits only inside a special location (the space around a bath or shower, or a room with a swimming pool or sauna heater). A kitchen alone is not a trigger.
- **Non-notifiable work** still has to comply, and AD P para 3.13 says it should still be inspected, tested and certificated to BS 7671.
- **Routes:** self-notify through a competent person scheme, use a third-party notification service, or notify building control. See [[Electrical Certificates]].
- England's categories do not carry over to the other UK nations; each sets its own rules.

Correction, 22 September 2026: Part P itself is statutory and Approved Document P is guidance; the rules are not uniform across the UK.

Correction, 16 October 2026: "applies where people permanently reside (dwellings)" is too narrow; AD P para 2.2 also covers outbuildings, common areas and shared-meter business premises.

## Concealed cables

Cables in walls are protected by where they run, by additional protection, and by the construction around them. A screw or drill can hit a cable outside the zones, so every condition in the current standard applies.

**Safe zones:** vertically above or below a switch or socket, horizontally to an accessory, within 150 mm of the ceiling, and within 150 mm of a corner.

The course summarises the rules as:

- Cables less than 50 mm deep in safe zones need 30 mA RCD protection (Reg 522.6.202).
- Cables in earthed metallic covering, conduit or trunking can avoid the RCD (given as "Reg 522.5.204").
- A circuit in a partition with metallic parts needs a 30 mA RCD, irrespective of depth, unless Reg 522.6.204 is followed (Reg 522.6.203).

Plain plastic conduit is not equivalent to an earthed metallic or other specifically permitted method. The [IET's 2015 cables-in-walls article](https://electrical.theiet.org/media/1445/cables-in-walls.pdf) explains how zones, RCDs and alternative construction combine; it was written for the seventeenth edition.

Correction, 22 September 2026: this page once said a 30 mA RCD alone makes a route outside the zones acceptable. It does not; an RCD does not stop a drill hitting a cable.

Correction, 22 September 2026: "522.5.204" is a source misnumbering; the rules sit in Regulation Group 522.6, and the metallic-covering exception has conditions.

Correction, 16 October 2026: the 522.6.203 rule covers any partition with metallic parts other than fixings such as nails or screws, not only a stud wall "made mainly of metal".

## Chases and notches

|Item|Study-note limit|Approved Document A, para 2C30|
|---|---|---|
|Vertical chase|1/3 of wall thickness|1/3 of the wall, or of the leaf in a cavity wall|
|Horizontal chase|1/6 of wall thickness|1/6 of the leaf|
|Joist notch|0.125 × joist depth|No rule given|

Chases must not impair the wall's stability. Course rules of thumb: if the wall thickness is unknown, assume one brick (about 100 mm), and exclude plaster, which is not structural. One brick is a guess, not a design basis; use the actual construction and [Approved Document A](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/structure-approved-document-a). Further reading: an "Electrics guide to Building Regs", pages 38 and 168, edition not recorded.

Correction, 16 October 2026: horizontal chases are limited to 1/6 of the leaf, not of the whole wall, and Approved Document A gives no joist-notch rule.
