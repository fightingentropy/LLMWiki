# Positioning and Sentiment

Positioning is where the money already is. Sentiment is what the crowd believes. Flows are the money moving now. Together they show crowded trades, squeeze risk and who will be forced out. The daily brief asks for consensus positioning, crowded trades, dealer, gamma and CTA flows, and sentiment extremes ([[Markets - Brief]]). [[Markets - Trading]] puts "CT Sentiment" first on its checklist, beside top traders' positioning, options positioning, stablecoin dominance and funding.

## Crowding and extremes

When everyone is long there are no buyers left; when everyone is short there are no sellers. The more concentrated the crowd, the more violent the unwind.

| State | Signs | Setup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long extreme | Leverage high, funding rich, stablecoin dominance low | Any bad news cascades long liquidations |
| Short extreme | Borrow and puts expensive | Any good news forces a short squeeze |
| Balanced | Low leverage, no consensus | No asymmetry; wait |

[[Pentosh1]]: "Greed is the parabola you sell into little by little … Once there are too many bulls, there's no-one left to buy."

Extremes can last. [[Rewkang]]: "In bull markets funding can stay higher than shorts can remain solvent". An extreme is a setup, not a signal; confirm with price or regime. Leverage, funding and cascades are on [[Leverage and Liquidations]]. fewseethis adds that a cascade is "a secondary effect" of prices falling in a levered market; after the mechanical bounce, prices resume their direction.

## Reflexive pumps

From @diablofutur:

> "Reflexive pumps don't begin with a wave of buyers, they start with no one left willing to sell. A stubborn holder base that refuses to let go triggers a synthetic supply shock. Aggro-rage shorts pile in. A disbelief rally erupts, fuelled by sideline capital waiting on a nuke that never shows."

Then FOMO, zero marginal sellers, shorts forced to cover, and a parabola. Rewkang on all-time highs: people sell because highs have always been rejected, and "Short term acute flows ($10B+ shizo day traders) overwhelm passive flows ($500m+ ETF buying). Eventually, passive flows win out."

## Bottoms

@Husslin_ on why washed-out moments make bottoms: "Algos mindlessly closing longs and reducing leverage in mkt. Liqs reset positioning. Funds forced to open shorts to manage risk." Then on the disbelief after: "Second leg up is always the slowest grind."

## Reading flows

| | Normal | Flow-driven | Squeeze |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flow | Balanced | One-way | Forced by extreme positioning |
| Price | Moves on news | Moves more than the news justifies | Gaps through levels, then reverses |

- Sellers hit the bid and buyers lift the offer, so prints at the bid show selling and prints at the ask show buying.
- A trend on rising volume has strong flow; buying into weakness that fails to move price signals exhaustion.
- Crypto flows: stablecoins moving onto exchanges, coins moving off them to self-custody, stablecoin borrow yields, ETF flows.
- Dealers who are short gamma buy rallies and sell dips, amplifying moves; long-gamma dealers dampen them.

## Narratives

Price usually comes first and the story follows. CL207: "anytime price moves 10% we will literally hallucinate a story up for it". Yet @lightcrypto argues a rally needs a story to coordinate around: "The market now lacks a viable schelling point narrative. Yet its desire for one is palpable." Both hold: short-term stories are backfit, and a cycle needs a dominant narrative to run.

Watch the reaction, not the fact-check. GCR (GiganticRebirth), May 2021: "More often than not, the actual veracity of the headline is immaterial. It's how the market reacts to the news, and for how long, that turns out to be a lot more informative."

[[CryptoCred]] on cycle psychology: first-cycle traders win because they ask "'What if I'm right?' instead of 'What if I'm wrong?'" and lose it the same way. "Navigating cycles requires switching between childlike idealism and sagely cynicism".

[[trading_axe]] parodies the habit: "When you've been trading for so long, eventually EVERYTHING IN YOUR LIFE becomes an INDICATOR."

## Positioning from data, not tweets

[[goodalexander]]'s checklist reads positioning without reading anyone's posts: raw and relative valuation, sell-side ratings, borrow cost and perp funding, earnings revisions against price, macro consensus, and options and CDS pricing. He calls it "a much more reliable guide to positioning than what people say publicly". Other proxies: put/call ratios, AAII and Investors Intelligence surveys, and COT reports for futures.
