# Retatrutide

Retatrutide (LY3437943) is the investigational compound discussed in the saved [[Peptides]] guide. That guide is an archive of claims, not an approved product label. This page records the compound's regulatory status and what the guide leaves unproven.

## Key ideas

- **Regulatory status.** The FDA says retatrutide is not a component of an FDA-approved drug and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. This is a US regulatory statement; no UK approval conclusion is inferred from it, and it is not a finding about every country's approvals. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])
- **What the guide says about itself.** Its own disclaimer calls retatrutide "an investigational compound still in Phase 3 clinical trials", says it is "not FDA-approved", and describes the content as "for educational and research reference purposes only". (from [[Peptides]])
- **What the guide covers.** A reconstitution guide, "From Powder to Injection": supplies, dilution charts, reconstitution steps, storage, injection technique, a titration schedule and common mistakes. It describes subcutaneous, once-weekly injection.
- **Products and guides are not clinical evidence.** Research-vendor products and a saved injection guide do not establish clinical suitability, identity, purity or formulation stability. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

## What this wiki has not established

- The archive does not authenticate any vendor's vial.
- It does not establish a safe personal dose.
- It does not validate a storage period. The guide's 28-day refrigerated limit for a reconstituted vial is its own claim. The Bacteriostatic Water for Injection label describes 0.9% benzyl alcohol and directs use according to the drug manufacturer's instructions; compatibility varies, and a preservative does not validate a universal 28-day shelf life for every reconstituted drug.
- It does not provide a formulation-specific stability study. The guide's precise potency-loss percentages for shaking, freezing and room-temperature storage have no supporting stability study attached.

The guide's dose-escalation and dilution tables remain accessible in the original source but are not carried forward as clinical instructions.
