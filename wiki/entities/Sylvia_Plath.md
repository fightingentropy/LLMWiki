# Sylvia Plath

American poet and novelist (1932–1963), author of *The Bell Jar* and *Ariel*. She is in this wiki as a voice for restlessness and the fear of wasted potential: two of her lines are kept in Erlin's [[Quotes]] note.

## Key ideas

- **Fear of being useless.** The line names the dread of promise that never turns into anything.

  > "What horrifies me most is the idea of being useless: well educated, brilliantly promising, and fading out into an indifferent middle age." (from [[Quotes]])

- **Restlessness.** A short line about wanting more and being unable to rest.

  > "Something in me wants more. I can't rest." (from [[Quotes]])

- **Where they fit.** Both lines feed the "unfulfilled potential" thread in [[Self_Improvement|Self-Improvement]] and the mortality and time cluster in [[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]].
- **Attribution.** In the quotes note, "Something in me wants more" carries her name. "What horrifies me most" stands without a name; the [[Quotes]] page credits it to her.
