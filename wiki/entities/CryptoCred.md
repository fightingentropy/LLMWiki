# CryptoCred

Cred ([@CryptoCred](https://x.com/CryptoCred)) is a crypto trader and educator on X. Erlin's notes cite him for trading psychology, the bag-holder test, how traders behave across cycles, and self-aware jokes about Crypto Twitter (CT). Most of the quotes come from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]; the trading-system and reset notes come from [[Markets - Trading]] and [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]].

## Key ideas

### The bag-holder test

On holding a losing position after a bad week (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]], cited in [[Trading Psychology]]):

> "The price action of the last week leads to awkward bag-holding. You don't want to sell because it's already down a lot and/or you're tempted to wait for a bounce to reassess."
>
> "'If I didn't have a position, would I want the exposure I have now?' Yes → Cool, you're fine. No → Do something about it. Active positions cost mental capital, which is a scarce resource."
>
> "You're less likely to spot and execute the next good trade if the current shit trade is eating away at you."

### First-cycle participants

Open-minded optimism makes first-cyclers money, and loses it for them at the top (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]]; see [[Market Cycles]]):

> "First cycle participants make money because they're open-minded, optimistic, and think "What if I'm right?" instead of "What if I'm wrong?" They lose most of their gains for the same reasons."
>
> "Navigating cycles requires switching between childlike idealism and sagely cynicism"

### Bear market PTSD

> "One argument for trading less actively when markets suck is that you don't learn habits that cost you money in bull markets"
>
> "Bear market PTSD is real. Fading breakouts, rotating too quickly and being unable to hold a position, unbridled cynicism etc. are all hard to unlearn"

(from [[CT|Crypto Takes]])

### Every system pays a cost

[Every trading system pays a characteristic cost](https://x.com/CryptoCred/status/2079138151528890777) (from [[Markets - Trading]]):

- Trend-following pays through false starts.
- Momentum often enters late and can buy tops or sell bottoms.
- Mean reversion periodically gets overwhelmed by a genuinely outsized move.

Do not confuse a strategy's expected pain profile with proof that the system stopped working. Abandoning a valid system because its unavoidable cost looks like failure is a recurring mistake. See [[Trading Evidence and Review Framework]].

### Reset after a major move

After missing or opposing a [large move](https://x.com/CryptoCred/status/2090201763987279967), avoid revenge shorts, oversized late entries, exact-retest stories, binary exposure, paralysis and five-minute overtrading. Rebuild the trade from signal, trade type, horizon, invalidation, volatility and size, with fresh eyes. This is process guidance; the post's example trade is not a recommendation. (from [[X-Bookmarks]] and [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]])

### Structural sources of retail edge

A [post on small-account advantages](https://x.com/CryptoCred/status/1913485279706677328) lists five: low market impact, a flexible time horizon, tolerance for marked volatility, freedom to concentrate, and the option to stay in cash. These freedoms only create opportunity. They do not rescue weak selection, excessive leverage, concentration error or poor risk management. (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]])

### Speculation moved from crypto to AI

An [essay on why speculation left crypto](https://x.com/CryptoCred/status/2021553644764619217) covers institutionalisation, extraction, lost inefficiency and the opportunity cost of AI. Its core claim: "AI is the frontier for speculation, and has many more tangible use cases and impact than crypto in a much shorter time frame." (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]] and [[X-Bookmarks]])

### FTX, flexing and the "evil eye"

A passage filed under CryptoCred in the note frames FTX's collapse as an "EVIL EYE/NAZAR effect": the biggest loudmouths who publicly showed large trades were mainly on FTX, "ROUTINELY flexed their PnL's all over Twitter", and the envy they attracted "contributed to the GRAND COLLAPSE of FTX." The next entry in the note, from [[trading_axe]], draws the lesson: "NEVER FLEX. EVER. Hide what you have." (from [[CT|Crypto Takes]])
