# goodalexander

Alexander (@goodalexander) is a crypto and macro commentator on X. Erlin's notes keep him for political-economy framings of crypto (elite debt default, tokenized sovereign debt), a long valuation checklist for spotting contrarian setups, and blunt one-liners about what trading does to the trader.

## Key ideas

### Crypto as a silent debt default

> "At some point you'll need to sleep at night knowing the elites just used cryptocurrency as a silent debt default mechanism. And you won a zero sum game vs the uneducated and willfully ignorant masses. So at least do your best to build something interesting that helps people" (from [[CT]])

Also summarised on [[Macro Trading]].

### Trading gives you a new boss

The note saves this under a link to [his post](https://twitter.com/goodalexander/status/1771142731462480304):

> "'I want to trade so I don't have a boss' — now you have a new boss that's the invisible preferences of a bunch of psychos. he controls your mood, thoughts, wakes you up in the middle of the night, prevents you from traveling or being truly present. congrats on your freedom" (from [[CT]])

See [[Trading Psychology]].

### How to tell if something is contrarian

His [checklist](https://x.com/goodalexander/status/2032143213344276528) for judging whether an asset is hated "without reading any tweets" (from [[X-Bookmarks]]):

1. **Raw valuation.** You get your money back in 5–10 years from cash flow or earnings. For currencies, use PPP; for commodities, compare price to production cost.
2. **Relative valuation.** Cheap against peers (10x earnings where a typical competitor trades at 30x).
3. **Relative GARP.** Growth at a reasonable price: gross and FCF margins and their expansion, revenue growth and its acceleration. Cheap with high-quality growth while expensive peers do not grow is cheap on GARP. For commodities, currencies or crypto, compare demand growth with market-cap dominance.
4. **History of 1–3.** The cheapest it has ever been on raw valuation, against peers and on GARP means it is hated and contrarian.
5. **Crypto.** Protocol earnings or gas, supplemented by cost of attack (hash power, amount staked). For small caps, value against the lifetime value of the user base and recent acquisition prices in the sector.
6. **Sell-side ratings.** An average of hold or sell means people hate it.
7. **Borrow cost.** Expensive to borrow, deeply negative perp funding, a high FX yield with low default risk, or very expensive storage for a commodity all signal that people do not want to be long.
8. **Earnings revisions against price.** Next-year EPS expectations revised up while the stock falls.
9. **Expected deceleration.** Consensus revenue and EPS growth expected to slow against history.
10. **Macro consensus.** For currencies, expectations for balance-sheet expansion, rate cuts, employment and inflation; for commodities, consensus supply and demand.
11. **Options and derivatives.** Puts dearer than calls, implied vol rich to realized, far out-of-the-money puts dearer than at-the-money, credit default swaps blown out against history.

His conclusion: considering all 11 "is a much more reliable guide to positioning than what people say publicly." See [[Positioning]].

### AI disappointment and on-chain finance scenario

An [essay](https://x.com/goodalexander/status/2089906200817066108) argues that AI spending may produce slower economic and corporate returns than valuations imply while accelerating hacking, addictive media, labor displacement and political conflict. He then expects fiscal stress, capital flight, stablecoins, tokenized debt, FX and leveraged on-chain markets to benefit crypto infrastructure. It is a scenario map, not a forecast; test each premise against AI productivity, capex and margins, rates, regulation, security, tokenized-asset adoption and actual on-chain fee growth. (from [[X-Bookmarks]]) See [[AI Infrastructure Economics]] and [[Crypto Trading]].

### Tokenization, stablecoins and sovereign debt

A later [essay](https://x.com/goodalexander/status/2100700327003467985) links tokenization, stablecoins, CBDCs, privacy, ZK systems, institutional settlement and agentic trading to demand for sovereign debt. It extends the on-chain-finance scenario above without resolving its regulatory, technical or economic premises. Treat it as an attributed scenario; adoption is not the same as value capture for token holders. (from [[Bookmarks - Markets & Crypto]])

### Druckenmiller in chatbot prose

A [joke post](https://x.com/goodalexander/status/2092060880271728715) imagines a Bank of England / George Soros conversation written in familiar LLM phrasing about being half right and finding the load-bearing point. His [clarification](https://x.com/goodalexander/status/2092065011766013982) says it alludes to an article attributed to Stan Druckenmiller that sounded AI-written. A style parody, not a real quotation (from [[Funny]]).

## Corrections

Correction, 15 October 2026: this page used to credit goodalexander with the Bessent "white hat hacking" thread. In [[CT]] that thread sits under a link to a post by @SuitablePolitic, not under goodalexander's handle, so the note does not support the attribution. The thread argues that Bessent's tariff volatility was a controlled detonation: it deleveraged hedge funds, boxed in China, and could force a Yuan collapse, a flight into Treasuries and lower rates to refinance $9T of expiring debt. It is kept in full on [[CT]] and summarised on [[Macro Trading]].
