### Hype cycles and framing

- Citrini's own hype-cycle framework: "Investors are always either discounting the promise of the future or the reality of the present. And they are never equally weighting them." He calls it "basic, but that’s the point (frameworks shouldn’t be overly complex, I think)." (from [[X-Bookmarks]])
- Early in a cycle investors discount the future and focus on the present; his example is Nvidia at the end of 2022, when the crypto GPU glut, weak gaming PC demand and rising rates dominated. Later the imagined future takes over. At the peak "the present is not considered at all". Mid-2021 SaaS valuations are his example of the future overvalued against the present.
- A negative catalyst reverses the mechanism, and the fall is asymmetric ("stairs up, elevator down"). If the technology keeps advancing, he says another chance to get long appears (the internet is his example); if it stops advancing, the cycle restarts with a new technology.
- Geopolitical risk: re-posting a financial history article Citrini first published in January 2023, he says its point is that "markets have a habit of treating geopolitical risk as background noise right up until the moment it’s not". (from [[X-Bookmarks]])

### Near-term bearish call

- In March 2026: "I can’t really see the scenario where stocks don’t go lower in the near term." He argues the Fed’s cutting cycle "is on its way to being completely priced out". A strong NFP would wreck rates; a weak one would not help equities either.
- On AI and jobs: AI is "maybe not good enough to convince companies to replace workers with machines", but good enough for companies to try it in roles they cut under economic pressure "and potentially find out they don’t need to hire that role back". (from [[X-Bookmarks]])
