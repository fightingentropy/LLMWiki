# Bacteriostatic Water

Bacteriostatic Water for Injection is sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol as a preservative, used as a vehicle for compatible drugs. It is in this wiki because the saved [[Peptides]] guide uses it to reconstitute [[Retatrutide]]. This page is a source reference; it does not select a diluent for any individual or vendor product.

## What the label says

- The referenced [prescribing label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display) describes sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol, intended as a vehicle for compatible drugs under their manufacturer's instructions.
- Compatibility varies from drug to drug.
- The label identifies incompatibilities and important restrictions, including benzyl-alcohol toxicity in neonates. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

## What the peptide guide claims

The Retatrutide reconstitution guide in the [[Peptides]] note makes these claims. They are the guide's, not established facts:

- BAC water "Contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol as preservative — **NOT** sterile water, saline, or tap water".
- The preservative "inhibits bacterial growth", which the guide says allows a reconstituted vial to be stored for up to 28 days and drawn from several times.
- Reconstituted with BAC water and refrigerated at 2–8°C: "Up to 28 days". Reconstituted with sterile water only: "Use within 24 hours".
- "A small percentage of people are sensitive to benzyl alcohol." For them the guide says to use sterile water and reconstitute fresh for each dose.
- Shaking the vial "reduces potency 30–50%", freezing a reconstituted solution lets "Ice crystals destroy peptide bonds", and room-temperature storage "Loses 10–20% potency per day".

## Correction

Correction, 22 September 2026: a preservative does not make every peptide compatible or establish a universal 28-day refrigerated life after mixing. The original [[Peptides]] note supplied no formulation-specific evidence for its 28-day versus 24-hour comparison, and its precise shaking, freezing and room-temperature potency-loss percentages have no supporting stability study attached. Storage and expiry must come from the actual product's validated instructions. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

Correction, 16 October 2026, on the guide's "**NOT** sterile water": the [DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display) describes Bacteriostatic Water for Injection as itself a sterile, nonpyrogenic water for injection. The guide's phrase separates it from preservative-free Sterile Water for Injection, not from sterility.

Correction, 16 October 2026, on the "Up to 28 days" and "Use within 24 hours" storage figures: the [DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display) says to use a reconstituted drug promptly and not to store it unless the drug's own manufacturer directs otherwise. The 28-day limit in USP <797>, [as described by the CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/injection-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/index.html), dates an opened manufactured multiple-dose vial, such as the BAC water vial itself, unless its manufacturer states another date; it is not a shelf life for a drug reconstituted from that vial.

Correction, 16 October 2026, on the shaking, freezing and room-temperature figures: agitation can aggregate peptide drugs such as insulin at air–liquid interfaces, at a rate that depends on how strong the agitation is and how much interface the solution meets ([Chouchane et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35290820/)); freezing can cost a protein drug activity through low temperature, freeze-concentration and ice formation ([Bhatnagar et al., 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17963151/)); and the approved [Zepbound (tirzepatide) label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=487cd7e7-434c-4925-99fa-aa80b1cc776b) says not to freeze it but allows its single-dose pens and vials up to 21 days unrefrigerated at up to 30°C. None of these gives the guide's fixed 30–50% or 10–20%-per-day losses or describes ice crystals destroying peptide bonds, and each limit belongs to its own formulation, not to a research vial.
