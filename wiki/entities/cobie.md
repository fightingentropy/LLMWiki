# cobie

Twitter: @cobie. Prominent crypto and macro trader known for thoughtful analysis and positioning calls, a long-form essayist on CT, and has hosted the *UpOnly* podcast. [[Markets - Trading]] lists him under "Good traders" to watch, and [[CT|Crypto Takes]] keeps an extensive archive of his posts.

## Key ideas

Quotes are verbatim from [[CT|Crypto Takes]].

### Grow fast, protect slow

The note heads this one "Someone once told Cobie", and he repeats it (also cited in [[Trading_Psychology|Trading Psychology]]):

> "you act too quickly when you are trying to grow capital, and act too slowly when you are trying to protect capital"

Replying to @CanteringClark, he credits an unnamed tweet: "lak who said it but I remember a long time ago a tweet that said something like "you act too quickly when you are trying to grow capital, and you act too slowly when you are trying to protect capital" and it rly changed my life tbh".

### "Sit on my ass"

Lord Ashdrake (@Lord_Ashdrake) contrasts what traders think they like, "In and out every move, catch everything", with what they should be like: "Sit on my ass, then a few times a year make massive bank then sit on my ass again". cobie replies: "took me like 8 years to learn this toh".

### Supercycle and the Everything Bubble

> "A decade long supercycle bubble of speculative cryptocoins as a siren for the market top of the multi-decade Everything Bubble"

[[GCRClassic]]'s "inverse supercycle" is the flipside of this take.

### Elites and inevitability

> "The billionaires multiplied wealth selling sugar to children, selling addiction to teenagers, selling adverts to people that can't afford to consume. They mixed entertainment into the news, removed the truth & decayed reality."
>
> "They tried to prevent crypto … But they are faced with the inevitability of us now."
>
> "We got money too now. Are you scared?"

### Low-conviction money bids new projects

The note saves [a long cobie comment](https://twitter.com/cobie/status/1483451870152638476) under a line on "infinite private money entering the ecosystem thru funds and new projects" while price action is atrocious. His argument: late, low-conviction money and their fomo-capitulating boomer LPs bid only new projects so they can feel early, and their first entry to crypto is funding "an outerspace metaverse hotel in 2022".

- "Low conviction money enters extremely late and offsets their lack of belief by bidding only "new projects" so they can be "early" as they perceive lower risk in buying at the lowest possible entry."
- "They're fomo-capitulating into bidding things they don't understand. They're sick of us winning. The simple truth is: they recursively programmed themselves to lose by living an uninspired life."
- He compares it to late 2017 and early 2018, when new entrants poured money into private ICOs.
- He closes by casting the "seed-funding fomo boomers" as a human centipede, with the retail buyers who bought their projects' bags and "turned that buy into your identity" as the tail.

A separate post makes a related warning: "Next time I will remember when a gigantic, notorious non-native VC fund starts saying "if you help direct us to the good seed projects then we'll bid them up 3-5x in private markets for you" to the crypto VCs then it is probably a bad sign for the health of the global economy"

### Jubilee pushback

Replying to @twobitidiot's "If the investment banks die, they die … Let's have a jubilee and get on with it already":

> "It's likely they have carefully woven themselves nicely into the lives of regular people, probably via pensions or something, such that letting them die is also letting normal people die. They will say "don't save us for us, but please save the innocent people!""

### Grateful gulf

In [a post on feeling lost in crypto](https://x.com/cobie/status/1450254997585858560), he sets friends with "deca-billions" against old co-workers "always stuck between pay-checks with goals that seem unreachable":

> "They are always making choices. Can I go on that weekend trip? How many months does that put me back on buying a house? Should I really be eating at this restaurant? Can I afford to have another kid? And then I feel grateful and lucky, all of a sudden money has meaning again."

### Decentralized machines

Replying to @Chubbicorn219, who notes that crypto's largest crash left the decentralized systems holding up with "an orderly deleveraging and mass death":

> "Everyone died, but the machines did their jobs perfectly. A beautiful world."
