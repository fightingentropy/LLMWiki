# Friedrich Nietzsche

German philosopher (1844–1900). He is the most-quoted author in [[Quotes]], Erlin's quote collection, and his lines turn up around the edges of nearly every theme there: morality, habit, aesthetics, power and self-overcoming. Here his lines are grouped by idea, with the pages that build on each.

## Key ideas

### Habit, fatigue and self-command

- **On habit:** "The history of every day. — What is the history of every day in your case? Look at your habits that constitute it: are they the product of innumerable little cowardices and lazinesses or of your courage and inventive reason?"
- **On self-command:** "He who cannot obey himself will be commanded." The note leaves this line unattributed.
- **On fatigue:** "When a man is tired, he's attacked by ideas he conquered long ago."
- **On melody and teleology:** "The end of a melody is not its goal." The note spells the attribution "Nietche".

### Interpretation, power and morals

- **On interpretation:** "All things are subject to interpretation. Whichever interpretation prevails at a given time is a function of power and not truth."
- **On aesthetic morality:** "If you crush a cockroach, you're a hero. If you crush a beautiful butterfly, you're a villain. Morals have aesthetic criteria."
- **On herd morality** (*Beyond Good and Evil*): a long passage on a society grown so soft and tender that it sides even with those who harm it, and shrinks from the idea of punishment. It ends: "herd morality, the morality of timidity, draws its ultimate consequence."

### Outsiders

- **On outsider perception:** "And those who were seen dancing were thought to be insane by those who could not hear the music."

### Potential and optionality

A line in [[Quotes]] attributes the starting point to him and then applies option pricing to people:

> "Nietzsche says humans have no intrinsic value. But they do have the optionality/potential of becoming something. So if you kill a baby you are killing an options that has a big premium but if you kill an old bum the option premium has diminished and his potential is far less so people tend to care less."

The wiki reads this as an extension toward Taleb's antifragility; see [[Nassim Taleb]].

## Where the wiki uses him

- **Habit as character:** "History of every day" maps onto the compounding-delta principle in [[Self-Improvement]]: small daily choices, not catastrophic blowups.
- **Self-command:** "Cannot obey himself will be commanded" is the ground under the agency and discipline threads in [[Self-Improvement]] and [[Masculinity_and_Men|Masculinity & Men]].
- **Option-value of potential:** the optionality note links him to [[Nassim Taleb]].
- **Interpretation as power:** echoes [[Political Commentary]] and [[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]], where storytelling is treated as a compression algorithm.
- **Fatigue attacks resolved ideas:** a line about mental states under tiredness; see [[Mental Health]].
