# Charles Bukowski

American poet and novelist (1920–1994). Known for a terse, working-class voice and advice that sounds crude or reductive but is often operationally sound. He is in this wiki through one saved YouTube clip and three quotes in [[Quotes]].

## Key ideas

### The saved clip

A YouTube clip in [[Youtube]] is titled "Bukowskis advice is funny but might work for a lot of people". The title says the advice sounds like a joke but may hold a workable rule of thumb for many. The note does not say what the advice is.

### Connection to other themes

- **Action over overthinking**: fits the [[Self_Improvement|Self-Improvement]] frame of imperfect action over perfect planning.
- **Find what you love and let it kill you**: the commonly attributed Bukowski posture lines up with the [[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]] "do epic shit" framing: life organized around doing the thing, not optimizing around it. The saved line behind that framing is "My goal in life is not necessarily to be happy. My goal in life is to do epic shit."

### Quotes cited (from [[Quotes]])

The note lists these three lines together with no name beside them. "We're all going to die" and "Find what you love" are commonly attributed to Bukowski; "I wanted the whole world or nothing" sits between them in the note.

- "We're all going to die, all of us, what a circus! That alone should make us love each other but it doesn't. We are terrorized and flattened by trivialities, we are eaten up by nothing."
- "Find what you love and let it kill you."
- "I wanted the whole world or nothing."

All three fit the same register: mortality pressure as the argument for caring, and caring as the argument for going all-in on the thing. See [[Philosophy_and_Meaning|Philosophy & Meaning]] "Do Epic Shit" for the downstream framing.
