# RCD Protection

An RCD detects residual current and disconnects under specified conditions. It does not protect against every type of electric shock. Device selection, coordination and verification depend on the installation and applicable standard.

## Protection has limits

[HSE guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/electricity/faq.htm) explains that an RCD rated no more than 30 mA can reduce harm from certain shocks, but secure isolation is still required before work. The device's test button is a functional check, not proof that the entire installation is safe.

## Corrections to earlier notes

- A 30 mA RCD does not, by itself, permit concealed wiring outside prescribed zones. See [[Building Regulations]].
- Rated residual current is not a promise to trip at exactly that current in every condition.
- Trip-time requirements depend on device type, test conditions and the applicable rules; the earlier blanket “100–300 ms” statement was incomplete.
- A higher-rated device is not automatically selective. The earlier claim that a 300 mA device lets upstream 30 mA devices operate first was misleading; coordination needs an actual design.
- The note's generic “metal covering means no RCD” exception omitted conditions and other reasons an RCD may be required.

Use [[BS 7671]] for current requirements and [[GN3 Guidance Note 3]] for verification. [[Electrical Evidence Review - September 2026]] records what was checked; the original notes remain available below.
