## The Iran uprising and Western media

A long [X post](https://x.com/DeTahmineh/status/2009680255091405074) _(@DeTahmineh)_ argues that Western liberal media ignores the Iranian uprising because covering it honestly would break its moral framework. Its argument, in order:

- **The core claim.** "The Iranian people are rebelling against Islam itself", and admitting that "shatters the moral framework" through which these institutions see the world.
- **Why people risk death.** The Islamic Republic has spent decades suffocating speech, work, family, art, women and economic survival under a clerical system that "treats liberty as a crime". The story cannot be told without confronting the regime.
- **Islam racialised.** Western progressive discourse treats Islam as a stand-in for race or ethnicity, not a belief system or political ideology. Criticising Islam is framed as an attack on "brown people", Arabs or "the Middle East".
- **Iranians erased.** The post blames historical illiteracy: all Middle Easterners are taken for Arabs, all Arabs for Muslims, all Muslims for one oppressed identity group. Iranians are Persian, not Arab, with an ancient and distinct culture.
- **No right to reject it.** Treating Islam as racial identity strips millions of the ability to reject it. Iranian protesters become unintelligible, so the media speaks over them or ignores them.
- **The economic dimension.** Iran is also a state-dominated economy: price controls, subsidies, nationalisation and bureaucratic micromanagement destroyed the middle class and entrenched corruption. Covering Iran honestly means admitting these policies failed "catastrophically", which undermines Western institutions that promote softer versions of the same ideas.
- **The silence.** Acknowledging Iran would mean dropping the categories of oppressor and oppressed, colonizer and colonized, white and non-white. Iranians show that authoritarianism is not a Western invention but something many societies are trying to escape. "That is what terrifies Western liberal media."

## COVID-19 origins: divided evidence

The [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/lab-leak-true-origins-of-covid-19/) presents a laboratory or research-related accident in Wuhan as the most likely origin of COVID-19, drawing on the House Select Subcommittee's 2024 report. This is the administration's official position, not a settled scientific conclusion.

The evidence remains divided:

- The World Health Organization's 2025 expert review says the weight of available evidence suggests zoonotic spillover, but keeps every hypothesis open because important early data remain unavailable.
- The US intelligence community considers both natural exposure and a laboratory-associated incident plausible. Agencies are divided, and most judgments are held at low confidence.
