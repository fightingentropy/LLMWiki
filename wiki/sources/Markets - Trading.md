# Markets - Trading

A working checklist for crypto trading: one principle on what moves price, the drivers to check before taking a view, the cost every system pays, and the traders worth following. The links are the dashboards and tools kept for flow, liquidations, on-chain data, Hyperliquid and research. The wider frame is on [[Crypto Trading]] and [[Trading Philosophy]].

## What moves markets

> Truth doesn't necessarily move markets. Perception & Uncertainty/Certainty transition does.

Price reacts to what people believe, and to moments when an uncertain outcome becomes certain (or the reverse), more than to the facts themselves.

- [Video on the principle](https://youtu.be/rZ9rUh9WhwU?t=1876) — The YouTube video linked from this line, opening at 31:16.

## Drivers to check

- **CT sentiment** — Crypto Twitter mood and positioning.
- **Momentum** — "Crypto is momentum driven (meaning moving averages play a role & market structure is important)."
- **Macro** — regime and policy drivers; see [[Macro Trading]].
- **Mechanical moves** — technical levels and liquidation cascades.
- **Liquidations** — live liquidation pressure and squeeze risk; see [[Leverage and Liquidations]].
- **Top traders' positioning** — what notable traders hold and where flows go; see [[Positioning and Sentiment]].
- **Catalysts** — events and announcements that move price.
- **Stablecoin dominance** — USDT/USDC flows and shifts in dominance.
- **Stablecoin yields** — borrow and lend rates on stables, a proxy for funding demand.
- **Funding rates** — perpetual futures funding, a gauge of leverage.
- **Options positioning** — implied volatility and gamma exposure.

## Every system pays a cost

Trend-following pays through false starts. Momentum enters late and can buy tops or sell bottoms. Mean reversion gets overwhelmed now and then by an outsized move that does not revert before risk limits are hit. The common mistake is abandoning a valid system because its unavoidable cost looks like proof that it is broken. The better question is not "does this strategy lose?" but "what loss pattern is the price of its edge, and can I survive it?" See [[Trading Psychology]].
