# Markets - Trading

Erlin's working checklist for crypto trading. It opens with one principle about what actually moves price, lists the drivers to check before taking a view, sets out what every trading system costs, and names the traders worth following. The links are the dashboards the note keeps for flow, liquidations, on-chain data, sector performance and screening, plus one wallet to track. For the wider frame see [[Crypto Trading]], [[Market Drivers]] and [[Trading Philosophy]].

## Core principle

> Truth doesn't necessarily move markets. Perception & Uncertainty/Certainty transition does.

Price reacts to what people believe and to moments when an uncertain outcome becomes certain (or the reverse), not necessarily to the facts themselves.

- [Video reference for the principle](https://youtu.be/rZ9rUh9WhwU?t=1876) — YouTube video the note links from this line, starting at 31:16.

## Things to look for

The note's checklist of market drivers, each expanded in [[Market Drivers]]:

- **CT sentiment** — Crypto Twitter mood and positioning. See [[Sentiment]].
- **Momentum** — "Crypto is momentum driven (meaning moving averages play a role & market structure is important)."
- **Macro** — the macro regime and policy drivers. See [[Macro Trading]].
- **Mechanical movements** — technical levels and liquidation cascades.
- **Liquidations** — real-time liquidation pressure and squeeze risk. See [[Liquidations]].
- **Top traders' positioning** — what notable traders hold and where flows go. See [[Positioning]] and [[Flow Analysis]].
- **Catalysts** — events and announcements with market impact.
- **Stablecoin dominance** — USDT/USDC flows and shifts in dominance.
- **Stablecoin yields** — borrow and lend rates on stables, a proxy for funding demand.
- **Funding rates** — perpetual futures funding, a gauge of leverage. See [[Funding Rates]].
- **Options positioning** — implied volatility and gamma exposure.

## Every trading system pays a characteristic cost

- Trend-following pays through false starts.
- Momentum often enters late and can buy tops or sell bottoms.
- Mean reversion periodically gets overwhelmed by a genuinely outsized move that does not revert before risk limits are hit.
- A recurring failure mode is abandoning a valid system because its unavoidable cost is mistaken for evidence that the system is broken.

This extends [[Trading_Psychology|Trading Psychology]] and [[Trading Philosophy]] with a more useful question than "does this strategy lose?": **what loss pattern is the price of its edge, and is that price survivable?**

- [Every trading system pays a characteristic cost](https://x.com/CryptoCred/status/2079138151528890777) — The [[CryptoCred]] post the section above is drawn from. _(@CryptoCred)_
