# Health Evidence Review - September 2026

Research notes dated 22 September 2026 that test recurring claims in [[Body]], [[Medical|Medical & Health]] and [[Peptides]] against NHS, FDA, NCBI and journal sources. Each section gives the claim, what the source says, and how far the evidence goes. The notes paraphrase the sources for specific claims; they are not a review of every health bookmark. All sources were accessed on 22 September 2026. For the running picture of what is and is not supported, see [[Health Evidence]].

## Dental health

The [[Medical|Medical & Health]] note recommends avoiding ordinary fluoride toothpaste on a thyroid-suppression theory: "Fluoride competes with iodine at thyroid receptor sites." NHS guidance says the opposite.

- Correction, 22 September 2026: [NHS guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/how-to-keep-your-teeth-clean/) recommends fluoride toothpaste twice daily, with at least 1,350 ppm fluoride for adults. Ordinary fluoride toothpaste remains part of NHS dental advice.
- The saved note supplies no clinical evidence establishing the thyroid-suppression theory for normal toothpaste use.

## Dopamine synthesis

The [[Medical|Medical & Health]] note says dopamine synthesis starts with tyrosine hydroxylase and that "Sunlight is needed for this step."

- Correction, 22 September 2026: the tyrosine-hydroxylase reaction uses tyrosine, oxygen and tetrahydrobiopterin. Sunlight is not a reagent in that reaction ([NCBI PubChem catecholamine biosynthesis pathway](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pathway/BioCyc%3AHUMAN_PWY66-301); [original enzyme kinetics study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6117320/)).
- Effects of light exposure on circadian timing are a separate question. See [[Circadian Rhythm]].

## Exercise

Activity should fit the person's health and ability.

- [NHS guidance for adults aged 19–64](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/): 150 minutes of moderate activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity per week, with strength work on at least two days.
- The [[Body]] note carries the viral push-up claim: "Men who can do 40 push-ups or more have a 96 percent reduced risk of heart disease compared with men who could do less than 10 push-ups."
- Correction, 22 September 2026: the [2019 push-up study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2724778) (Yang et al., JAMA Network Open, doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.8341) followed 1,104 active male firefighters. Greater baseline push-up capacity was associated with fewer cardiovascular events.
- The 96% figure describes a comparison between groups in that observational cohort. It is not a reduction caused by doing forty push-ups, not an exercise prescription that lowers anyone's risk by 96%, and not a universal cardiac screening test. [[Fitness]] keeps the study's scope.

## Vitamin D

- [NHS vitamin D guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/) says adults should consider 10 micrograms of vitamin D daily in autumn and winter. Some groups need year-round supplementation.
- This is one specific public-health recommendation. It does not support the note's entire supplement stack. See [[Supplements|Supplements & Biohacking]].

## Retatrutide and peptide preparation

Retatrutide is unapproved in the US, and nothing here validates dosing.

- The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss) states retatrutide is not a component of an FDA-approved drug and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. The statement covers US regulatory status, not every country's approvals. See [[Retatrutide]].
- Research-vendor products and a saved injection guide do not establish clinical suitability, identity, purity or formulation stability.
- The [Bacteriostatic Water for Injection label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display) describes 0.9% benzyl alcohol and directs use according to the drug manufacturer's instructions. Compatibility varies. See [[Bacteriostatic Water]].
- Correction, 22 September 2026: the [[Peptides]] guide gives a 28-day refrigerated life for a reconstituted vial, says shaking "can reduce potency 30–50%", says reconstituted peptide left at room temperature "Loses 10–20% potency per day", and warns that freezing destroys peptide bonds. A preservative does not validate a universal 28-day shelf life for every reconstituted drug, and none of the guide's shaking, freezing or room-temperature potency-loss claims has a supporting stability study attached.
- A preservative and a dilution calculation do not validate research-vial dosing or stability. See [[Peptide Reconstitution]].

## Unresolved claims

The saved notes supply no controlled clinical evidence for these claims from [[Body]]:

- A near-instant brain-fog cure from eating "500g+ of blueberries in one sitting".
- A universal magnesium stack for sleep, hearing and migraine.
- A tongkat-ali and pomegranate hormone-balancing protocol.
- A five-minute cold shower being equivalent to medication; the note says it "almost feels like taking a pill or something."

They remain attributed claims and leads for investigation, not established treatment guidance. This judges the support in the saved notes; it does not prove that every possible effect is absent. See [[Supplements|Supplements & Biohacking]], [[Body_Optimization|Body Optimization]] and [[Health Evidence]].
