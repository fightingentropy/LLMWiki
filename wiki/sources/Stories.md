## Dignity and endurance

### Taleb's father

From *Antifragile* (p. 312), [[Nassim Taleb]] on his greatest lesson in courage. He admired his father's erudition but says erudition alone does not make a man. His father had a large ego and immense dignity. During the Lebanese war a militiaman insulted him at a road check; he refused to comply and got angry at the disrespect, and as he drove away the gunman shot him in the back. The bullet stayed in his chest for life, and he had to carry an X-ray through airports. Taleb's conclusion: "dignity is worth nothing unless you earn it", unless you are willing to pay a price for it.

### David Cifaldi's Granite Peak self-rescue

An account of calm and endurance, not a model for treating impalement or for turning down emergency extraction.

On 20 July 2026, emergency-room nurse [[David Cifaldi]] slipped near the summit of Granite Peak in Montana and fell onto a trekking pole, which passed through his torso and narrowly missed his vital organs. He checked that he could breathe, had full feeling and was not bleeding heavily. He and his two companions alerted authorities by satellite communicator, then spent about six and a half hours descending ten miles on their own. One friend steadied him while the other warned approaching families about the injury. Surgeons later removed the pole, and the group plans to try the summit again once he recovers. ([X post](https://x.com/SMB_Attorney/status/2081926110174957611); [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/27/montana-man-hikes-10-miles-fall-trekking-pole))
