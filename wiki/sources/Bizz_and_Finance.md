# Business & Finance

A running note on money, markets and business, mostly pasted from X threads, a trading book and a Bezos shareholder letter, with a few asides of its own, such as a reminder to look into coffee and cocoa futures. It is a collection of takes, not a model. The crypto-specific archive is [[Crypto Takes]].

## Reading the news

Whether a headline is true matters less than how price reacts to it.

> "More often than not, the actual veracity of the headline is immaterial. It's how the market reacts to the news, and for how long, that turns out to be a lot more informative." — GCR (@GiganticRebirth), 2021

> "Markets are typically 6-8 months ahead of the news. Price moves first, and the 'why' follows."

The "Everything is priced in" shitpost for WallStreetBets takes it to the end: "the market has already priced in the heat death of the universe".

## Valuation and bear markets

An Arbor Research chart, via @MacroAlf, splits equity value into three layers: what's real (book value), what's likely (three years of discounted earnings), and "Hopes & Dreams (everything else)". "Way to go before Hopes & Dreams turn into Despair & Nightmares."

Robert Rhea's three bear phases (1935), via @WifeyAlpha:

> "the first being the abandonment of hopes upon which the uprush of the preceding bull market was predicated; the second being the reflection of the decreased earning power and reduction of dividends; the third representing distress liquidation of securities which must be sold to meet living expenses."

Each phase ends in a bear-market rally "often erroneously assumed to be the beginning of a bull market."

## The US trading calendar

From a trading book on seasonality: low volume brings moves that break the usual patterns.

- "For the U.S. markets the year really begins in September."
- FOMC days bring wild swings, especially just after the statement.
- Volume stays strong from September to Christmas and from January to May; then "Sell in May and Go Away".
- The low point is the week after July 4, when trading desks are "abandoned for lake cabins, golf courses, or seaside resorts."

## Flows and reflexivity

Crypto-flavoured lines on volatility, cash and who is left to sell. Perps, which one post calls "the best pmf in all of crypto", began as Robert Shiller's 1992 proposal for markets in illiquid assets such as houses.

> "Cash = option. Option value is maximized by vol. Inflation is option premium."

> "Reflexive pumps don't begin with a wave of buyers, they start with no one left willing to sell."

> "Short term acute flows ($10B+ shizo day traders) overwhelm passive flows ($500m+ ETF buying). Eventually, passive flows win out." — [[Rewkang]]

> "If you spend your time obsessing over old losses you have a high probability of generating new losses." — Kris Abdelmessih

## Strategy and distinctiveness

The catfish effect, a Chinese business metaphor: one catfish in a sardine tank keeps the fish moving and alive. @ruima's example is Tesla, let into China to shake up domestic EV makers. Bezos, in his last letter as Amazon CEO, borrows Dawkins: living things work to stay unlike their surroundings. More on [[Business Strategy]].

> "It's not 'disruption' as a natural market phenomenon, it's more like managed agitation. Like, let's introduce a little stress on purpose, because stagnation is the bigger risk here." — @ruima

> "The world wants you to be typical - in a thousand ways, it pulls at you. Don't let it happen." — Jeff Bezos

> "Democracies are not normal. Tyranny is the historical norm." — Jeff Bezos

> "it's far better to buy a bussin company at a sus price than a sus company at a bussin price, fr fr"

Two practical rules sit beside these. A company can be "overearning and consuming the entirety of consumer surplus" while its brand decays. And ask both people privately before making an introduction, the double opt-in rule from @reidhoffman.

## Tokenization

A reply thread on putting assets onchain. For: 24/7 trading, borrowing against assets, and liquidity for markets that have none. Against: people want to outsource custody, and regulation lets them sleep.

> "The assets that benefit the most from tokenization will be those that don't currently have a robust market structure."

> "You don't own that. Your broker owns that on your behalf and you hold an account at your brokerage"

> "Next, try taking out a loan against your newly bought 'stock' on an open competitive market. Or send the stock to your friend in Yemen."

## Incentives and price zero

Free changes behaviour more than cheap does.

> "You can make things cheap and it will fix a lot of problems that will arise by making things free. Weird things happen at 0.00."

> "The problem is that when healthcare is free, and it's all paid for already by taxes, people have no discipline. The demand just booms." — Ong Ye Kung, on why Singapore does not offer free care

Two jokes follow the same logic. The best money-laundering front has no inputs: "A golf driving range is perfect." And a banker's parody calls cyclists a disaster for the economy, ending "PS: walking is even worse. Pedestrians don't even buy a bicycle!"
