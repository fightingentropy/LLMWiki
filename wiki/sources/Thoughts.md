## Work and attention

Capacity is limited, and most productivity culture simulates work instead of doing it.

- "it's better to just do one thing each day than burn myself out indefinitely trying to do 3 or 4 things." _(@skooookum)_
- "we’ve built an entire shadow economy around the simulation of work rather than work itself." It trains you "to exist in a preparatory state instead of an executory one" — "a kind of paralysis disguised as momentum." _(@signulll)_
- "I've had to try and learn myself that death lies in banality." "We crash cars just because we couldn't wait to read a notification." _(@rao_zam)_

## Opportunity and selection

Luck has to be recognised and chased. Visible success is partly selection: networks, sponsorship and capital amplify some, while equally capable people stay unseen.

- "If a big publisher (or a big art dealer or a movie executive or a hotshot banker or a big thinker) suggests an appointment, cancel anything you have planned: you may never see such a window open up again." _(Nassim Taleb)_
- "Collect as many free nonlottery tickets (those with open-ended payoffs) as you can, and, once they start paying off, do not discard them." Big cities give "exposure to the envelope of serendipity."

## Intelligence and AI

Intelligence is a bundle, not one number, and it is not always the limiting factor. An exception does not refute an average, but an average does not decide the person in front of you. Clifford Sosin's point: ideas still have to be built, tested and corrected against reality.

- "the correlation between autism and traits like honor and integrity is astronomically high relative to the general population."
- High-IQ people fail at basic human situations because they "apply complex and abstract thinking to problems for which instinctive strategies are more adaptive." (Charlton) "-Why do intelligent people struggle with love? Because it’s not logical." _(@lokymann)_
- "ai exposes how much of what we call “creativity” is remixing, how much “insight” is regurgitation, & how much “expertise” is just memorised heuristics." _(@signulll)_

## Taste and story

What you like reveals who you are, and story decides whether an idea lands.

- "Your aesthetic taste is a map of your inner world projected outward. What you're drawn to reveals which parts of your spirit are awake. Aesthetic taste is an unconscious confession."
- "Geniuses have blind spots, one of Elon's is beauty. He has bad taste and doesn't understand the power or political utility of image. It's why the women are mid, the logos are cringe, the jokes are bad, etc."
- "storytelling is the ultimate compression algorithm for human attention." "the story is the interface between it & the world. get that wrong & nobody will give af. get it right & you bend steel with your bare hands."
- "martinis are not meant to taste good they are meant to harden your spirit. Very masculine, but also, really gay, because of the glass. Interesting drink"

## Identity and tribe

People crave labels and belonging, and grown-ups are improvising.

- "a high-level rizz move that requires a fair bit of intellectual dishonesty is to basically be a real-life Sorting Hat. People want identity and a sense of purpose and tribe SO badly".
- "Really wild how we grow up thinking there's so much structure in the world of adults, and then one day it just dawns on you that nearly everyone is just completely fucking winging it"
- "The state can substitute for what women want from men, but it can't substitute for what men want from women." _(Naval Ravikant)_

## Mood and meaning

Optimism is a useful fiction; losing it narrows life.

- "I've met many more people who turned their lives around by walking around aimlessly for a couple hours a day than I’ve met people who have had their lives improved at all by professional therapy"
- "when we were younger we had this hopefully naivete that we can get ahead and maybe it was a delusion and not rational but it was a useful fiction that actually propelled us forward". "depression is a progressive narrowing of the things that give you pleasure."

## Incentives and repeated games

- A system that looks broken may be producing exactly what its incentives reward. Change the game, or play the one that exists. _(@incentivising)_
- Self-improvement stalls when the environment cannot reward it. Ask which environment would turn what you already have into a return. _(@incentivising)_
- In repeated games, cooperate first, punish exploitation promptly and proportionally, then forgive. Tit for Tat is an analogy for boundaries, not a law. _(@alexeixbt)_ See [[Decision-Making]].
- After a big loss, do not gamble to erase it. Stabilise, cap losses and keep playing later rounds. _(@incentivising)_
