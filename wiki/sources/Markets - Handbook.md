# Markets - Handbook

A markets reference card: when the major stock exchanges are open, in local and UK time, and what moves the main commodity futures. Session handoffs and how venues work are on [[Exchange Microstructure]]; commodity detail is on [[Commodities]].

## Stock exchange hours

| Exchange | Local hours | UK hours (GMT) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NYSE | 9:30 AM – 4:00 PM ET | 2:30 PM – 9:00 PM |
| NASDAQ | 9:30 AM – 4:00 PM ET | 2:30 PM – 9:00 PM |
| Shanghai Stock Exchange | 9:30 AM – 3:00 PM | 1:30 AM – 7:00 AM |
| Tokyo Stock Exchange | 9:00 AM – 3:30 PM | Midnight – 6:30 AM |
| Euronext | 9:00 AM – 5:30 PM CET | 8:00 AM – 4:30 PM |

Correction (15 October 2026): Tokyo has closed at 3:30 PM local since 5 November 2024; the original 3:00 PM close was the earlier schedule.

- China and Japan do not use daylight saving, so Shanghai and Tokyo trade an hour later in UK time during British Summer Time.
- The US and UK change clocks on different dates, so for a few weeks each spring and autumn the US exchanges open at 1:30 PM UK.
- In UK time the day runs Asia overnight, Europe from 8:00 AM, then the US from 2:30 PM. Euronext and the US overlap from 2:30 PM to 4:30 PM.

## Commodity drivers

Commodities are "Boom & bust cycles, cyclical, depend on supply & demand".

| Commodity | Main drivers |
| --- | --- |
| Oil | OPEC decisions and production; geopolitics and supply disruption; GDP growth and activity; policy such as renewables adoption |
| Natural gas | Output from major fields; storage levels; big producers' decisions; weather (cold months raise demand); LNG exports; energy policy; competition from renewables |
| Gold | Sentiment and risk appetite; safe-haven demand; interest rates (higher rates make gold less attractive); the US Dollar Index (DXY); central bank buying; jewellery demand; mining supply |
| Silver | Industrial demand (electronics, solar panels); investor and ETF flows, safe-haven and speculative; rates, real yields and DXY; the gold–silver ratio; mining and recycling supply (often a by-product of base-metals mining); futures positioning and volatility |
| Orange juice | Weather and forecasts; crop disease and other supply shocks; supply and demand shifts; trade policy and tariffs |

On gold: "rates low or dollar weaker = gold more attractive". The dollar is covered on [[FX]], rates and real yields on [[Rates and Credit]].
