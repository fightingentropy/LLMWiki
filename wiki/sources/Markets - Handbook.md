# Markets - Handbook

Erlin's handbook note is a short markets reference card. It covers two things: when the major stock exchanges are open, in local and UK time, and what moves the main commodity futures. The fuller treatment of each subject lives on [[Exchange Hours]] and [[Commodities]].

## Stock exchange opening hours

| Exchange | Local hours | UK hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NYSE (New York Stock Exchange) | 9:30 AM – 4:00 PM ET | 2:30 PM – 9:00 PM |
| NASDAQ | 9:30 AM – 4:00 PM ET | 2:30 PM – 9:00 PM |
| Shanghai Stock Exchange | 9:30 AM – 3:00 PM | 1:30 AM – 7:00 AM |
| Tokyo Stock Exchange | 9:00 AM – 3:00 PM | Midnight – 6:00 AM |
| Euronext | 9:00 AM – 5:30 PM CET | 8:00 AM – 4:30 PM |

Correction, 15 October 2026: the Tokyo Stock Exchange extended its afternoon session to 3:30 PM local from 5 November 2024, so it now closes at 6:30 AM UK (GMT). The 3:00 PM close in the note is the earlier schedule.

The UK times for Shanghai and Tokyo are winter (GMT) figures; neither country uses daylight saving, so both trade an hour later in UK time during British Summer Time. The US and UK change clocks on different dates, so for a few weeks each spring and autumn the US exchanges open at 1:30 PM UK.

In UK time the day runs Asia overnight, Europe from 8:00 AM, then the US from 2:30 PM, with Euronext and the US overlapping from 2:30 PM to 4:30 PM. How these session handoffs drive opening gaps and overnight positioning is covered on [[Exchange Hours]]; [[Exchange Microstructure]] covers how the venues themselves work.

## Commodities

The note's one-line principle: commodities are "Boom & bust cycles, cyclical, depend on supply & demand". See [[Market Cycles]] and [[Commodities]].

### Oil futures

- OPEC decisions and production levels.
- Geopolitical events and supply disruptions.
- GDP growth and economic activity.
- Policy changes, such as renewable energy adoption.

More on [[Oil]].

### Natural gas

- How much is produced from the major gas fields.
- How much is in storage.
- Decisions by big producers.
- Weather: colder months mean more demand.
- Liquefied natural gas (LNG) exports.
- Energy policies.
- Competition from renewables.

More on [[Natural Gas]].

### Gold

- Investor sentiment and risk appetite.
- Safe-haven demand in economic uncertainty.
- Interest rates: higher rates make gold less attractive.
- [[DXY]] (US Dollar Index): the note says "rates low or dollar weaker = gold more attractive".
- Central bank buying.
- Jewellery demand.
- Mining supply.

More on [[Gold]].

### Silver

- Industrial demand: electronics and solar panels.
- Investor and ETF flows, both safe-haven and speculative.
- Interest rates and [[Real Yields]], plus [[DXY]].
- Relative value against gold, read through the gold–silver ratio.
- Mining and recycling supply; silver is often a by-product of base metals mining.
- Futures positioning and volatility.

More on [[Silver]].

### Orange juice (OJ) futures

- Weather conditions and forecasts.
- Crop diseases and other supply shocks.
- Supply and demand shifts.
- Trade policies and tariffs.
