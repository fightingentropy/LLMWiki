# Crypto Takes

"CT" is Erlin's archive of crypto-twitter posts: trader threads, replies and one-liners, kept word for word with the handle that posted them. The voice throughout is the quoted trader's, not Erlin's; the jokes, the copium and the rants are part of the record, and a few lines in the note carry no handle at all. Read it as source material for [[Crypto Trading]], [[Trading Psychology]], [[Trading Philosophy]], [[Liquidations]], [[Leverage]], [[Exchange Microstructure]], [[Macro Trading]] and [[Sentiment]]. Where the note saved the post itself, the link sits beside the quote; every saved post is also listed under Links.

## Price discovery and market mechanics

**Production cost pulls price toward it.** [[Satoshi Nakamoto]] in 2010, quoted by [[0xaporia]]:

> "The price of any commodity tends to gravitate toward the production cost. If the price is below cost, then production slows down. If the price is above cost, profit can be made by generating and selling more. At the same time, the increased production would increase the difficulty, pushing the cost of generating towards the price."

**Proxies refine into precise expressions.** [[thiccythot]]:

> "its interesting how price discovery moves from blunt proxies to more precise expressions with each wave. first wave → buy any oil → wti outperforms brent bc its the default proxy. second wave → better read on mechanics of the supply shock → brent cleaner expression than wti. you see it with crypto too with each cycle becoming more selective with asset quality … it's so cool to witness adaptiveness of the system, feels like modern art"

**Reflexivity.** An unattributed line in the note:

> "Reflexivity Creates Virtuous and Vicious Cycles. Price→Perception→Fundamentals→Price→Perception→Fundamentals"

**Cascades are a secondary effect.** [[fewseethis]]:

> "the simple truth is that cascades occur as a RESULT of prices going down within a levered environment. in other words a cascade is a secondary effect. then — outside of a mechanical bounce — prices can resume wherever they were going in the first place: up or down."

**Rising volume on a rally is bearish.** Unattributed in the note:

> "If volume starts to increase during rally, not bullish. Means market is running into sellers, much ammo from buyers being spent. Wouldn't you rather see price floating up on no volume? Nobody willing to sell, buyers less quick to run out of cash."

**Range builds the open interest that fuels the expansion.** [[jimtalbot]], passing on a line attributed to ImperatOr:

> "you build range to generate enough open interest to cause expansion out of range when it hits the stop losses of the OI that has been built up causing forced selling. The longer the range the bigger the expansion so yes 20k is on the table"

**Range versus trend.** [[jimtalbot]]:

> "sideways, you interact as far away from average price as possible, in trend you interact with as close to average price as possible"

**Orderflow before newsflow.** [[HsakaTrades]], with the attribution as the joke:

> "'The orderflow frontruns the newsflow' — Marcus Aurelius"

**Where bottoms form.** @Husslin_ ([post](https://twitter.com/Husslin_/status/1781147687103893882)):

> "Why these spots are ideal for bottoms: Algos mindlessly closing longs and reducing leverage in mkt; Liqs reset positioning; Funds forced to open shorts to manage risk; Market makers pulling bids/asks so books are piss thin rn; Ppl afraid to catch knives here"

The follow-up ([post](https://twitter.com/Husslin_/status/1781349288401846364)):

**Crashes carry no signal.** Alex Krüger:

## Exchange infrastructure and microstructure

**HYPE-margined collateral risk.** Unattributed in the note:

> "The risk is in possible over collateralization of positions using HYPE. USDC has a 1:1 value and can be infinitely redeemed in a liquidation for its full value. On the other hand, a HYPE margined position to maintain a precise notional value is more complicated. When liquidated, HYPE is a coin with an FDV that is not backed 1:1 by USDC, and that position may need to be sold into USDC. You create a risk of collateralizing too many positions in HYPE and a cascade could send HYPE low enough where slippage and spread leave positions UNDER collateralized and the protocol insolvent."

## Trading psychology: bag-holding, cashing out and sitting still

**The awkward bag-holding test.** [[CryptoCred]]:

> "The price action of the last week leads to awkward bag-holding. You don't want to sell because it's already down a lot and/or you're tempted to wait for a bounce to reassess. Simple test: 'If I didn't have a position, would I want the exposure I have now?' Yes → Cool, you're fine. No → Do something about it. Active positions cost mental capital, which is a scarce resource. You're less likely to spot and execute the next good trade if the current shit trade is eating away at you."

**The poker analogue.** [[0xaporia]] ([post](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/1894332130093629551)):

> "Poker players grasp that they're dealt a fixed hand and operate in a controlled environment, so they accept variance as part of the game. **Strategy hopping and losing streaks.** [[0xaporia]] ([post](https://x.com/0xaporia/status/1894297482500141122)):

> "Strategy hopping is one of the most under discussed issues on CT imo. It's mostly a flight from the grueling, nonlinear phase where most progress happens. All strategies, even brilliant ones, operate in a landscape of randomness and friction. When people encounter a losing streak, they interpret it as proof that their strategy is flawed, rather than recognizing that noise is part of the system … Losing streaks are not rare events. Yet, humans are terrible at internalizing this. We intuitively expect outcomes to balance out quickly, not grasping that streaks are inherent to randomness. A surgeon with a 95% success rate will eventually lose multiple patients in a row. A poker player should focus on whether they made mathematically optimal decisions, not whether they won the hand. **Decouple Process from Outcome.**"

## Contradictions (flagged, not resolved)

- **Crypto ingenuity vs degradation** — [[DegenSpartan]] explicitly contrasts the "ingenuity/self-sovereignty" framing against his own "degradation/greed" framing. Same asset class, opposite theses.
- **Narrative reading** — [[CL207]] says narratives are hallucinated *ex post* from price; @lightcrypto says narratives are needed as schelling points *ex ante*. Both partially true.
- **Tools vs instinct** — [[CryptoCred]]'s microstructure maximalism vs @MatriXBT's pure-instinct claim.
- **Short-term activity** — Lord Ashdrake / [[cobie]] on "sit on your ass" vs [[GiganticRebirth]]'s "sacrifice your weekends to harvest yield." Depends on life stage and regime.
- **Crypto's decorrelation** — [[RJ Killmex]] treats crypto decorrelation as a forward-looking signal; [[OxHamz]] treats the same phenomenon as a flow artifact (rebalancing inflows vs new entrants); [[GCRClassic]] calls most of it a laggard effect. Each can be right in a different regime.
