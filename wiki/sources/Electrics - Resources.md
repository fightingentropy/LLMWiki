# Electrics - Resources

A quick-reference note for electrical study, kept as written, with a correction under each rule that was wrong or incomplete. The worked versions are on [[Ohm's Law and Electrical Calculations]], [[Electrical Safety]] and [[Building Regulations]].

## Core formulas

- Ohm's law triangle: V = I x R
- Power triangle: P = I x V
- Rule of thumb at 230 V: 1 kW is about 4.35 A

P = I x V gives watts only for DC or unity power factor, and the rule of thumb carries the same limit.

## Key definitions

- **Exposed-conductive-part:** a conductive part of equipment that can be touched and is not normally live, but can become live under fault conditions.
- **Extraneous-conductive-part:** a conductive part that can introduce a potential (generally earth potential) and is not part of the electrical installation.

## Protection devices and earthing

- **RCD:** protects against earth faults and electric shock.
- **Circuit breaker:** protects against overcurrent and short circuit.
- **Earth cable:** provides a low-resistance escape route for fault current if live touches metal.

An RCD cannot protect against every shock, and its test button does not prove the installation safe ([HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/electricity/faq.htm)).

## Regulations and scope

- **Part P:** applies where people permanently reside (dwellings).
- **Reg 522.6.202:** cables less than 50 mm deep in safe zones must be 30 mA RCD protected.
- **Reg 522.5.204:** if cables are in earthed metallic covering, conduit, or trunking, an RCD can be avoided.
- **Reg 522.6.203:** if a circuit is in a stud wall made mainly of metal, 30 mA RCD is required unless Reg 522.6.204 is followed.

Correction, 22 September 2026: "522.5.204" is a misnumbering, and the metallic-covering exception has conditions; plastic conduit alone does not qualify.

Correction, 16 October 2026: Part P also covers outbuildings, common areas of flats and business premises on a dwelling's meter ([Approved Document P](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-approved-document-p) para 2.2), and 522.6.203 applies to any partition with metallic parts.

## Cable routes and safe zones

- **Safe zones:** directly above/below a switch or socket (vertical), horizontal runs to accessories, within 150 mm of the ceiling, and within 150 mm of corners.

Correction, 22 September 2026: a 30 mA RCD alone does not permit a concealed route outside the zones ([IET, Cables in walls](https://electrical.theiet.org/media/1445/cables-in-walls.pdf)).

## Structural limits for notches and chases

- **Joist notches:** maximum depth is 0.125 x joist depth.
- **Wall chases:** vertical up to 1/3 of wall thickness; horizontal up to 1/6 of wall thickness.
- If wall thickness is unknown, approximate with one brick (about 100 mm of brick depth).
- Exclude plaster thickness because it is not structural.

Correction, 16 October 2026: Approved Document A limits horizontal chases to 1/6 of the leaf, not the wall, and gives no joist-notch rule; guessing thickness from one brick is not a design basis.
