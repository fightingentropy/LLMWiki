# Culture & Observations

Erlin's "Culture" note is a commonplace book: pasted X posts and threads, a few longer passages, one-line takes and saved clips. It covers how place shapes people, Chinese business strategy, dating, technology, gender, slow decline, Curtis Yarvin's entropy frame, and provocative posts about Islam. Most long passages are someone else's words, attributed by handle where the note gives one. Read them as takes, not established claims.

## Place and identity

> "Every country has an energy. And that energy rewires you whether you notice it or not. People move to Japan and become minimal. People move to Mexico and their entire relationship with time softens. People move to New York and suddenly they can't sit still."

From a [post by @tishray](https://x.com/tishray/status/2031783721397809397). The rest of the post argues:

- Personality is far more malleable than people assume. New surroundings give you new defaults, pace, habits and values, absorbed "through proximity instead of effort".
- You are the average of the places, routines and inputs you are exposed to most, not only the five people closest to you. "Your commute shapes you. The weather shapes you. Every space you occupy is voting on who you become."
- **Choosing where you live is one of the most important decisions you'll ever make**, more important than job title and maybe the five-year plan, "because the place shapes the plan". The place shapes your energy, habits, relationships and default state.
- "Get the place right and half of the other decisions start making themselves. Get it wrong and you'll fight yourself every day."

The thesis has its own page: [[Culture_and_Environment|Culture and Environment]].

## The catfish effect

A long post by @ruima (no link in the note) on a metaphor common in Chinese business strategy and rare in Western business schools.

- **The story.** Norwegian fishermen transporting sardines lost many because the fish stopped moving and ran out of oxygen. One live catfish in the tank kept the sardines swimming, and more survived. "The catfish made the environment slightly hostile, which weirdly made it more survivable."
- **Where it stuck.** The idea floated around Western motivational books but never entered business theory the way disruptive innovation or lean operations did. In China, 鲶鱼效应 became popular in corporate training and state-aligned business commentary. The post puts that down to culture: more acceptance of discomfort as a developmental tool, and less hesitation to design pressure in from the top down.
- **Managed agitation.** "It's not 'disruption' as a natural market phenomenon, it's more like managed agitation." Stress is introduced on purpose because stagnation is the bigger risk.
- **Tesla in China.** The post says Tesla's arrival is considered in China a clear-cut case of the catfish effect in policy, and expects some to disagree. The government was frustrated with a domestic EV scene where subsidies had drawn abuse, fraud and half-baked companies. It gave Tesla rare approvals: full ownership of its China operations, fast-tracked factory deals, land, logistics support. The message to domestic firms: "Swim!" The post says the pace of innovation has picked up since, in operations, design and user experience, though not all because of Tesla.
- **West versus China.** The US leans on disruptive innovation. In China, competition can be curated and introduced, not to destroy domestic players but to get them moving again.
- **Limits.** It does not always work. Sometimes the catfish is too strong, or the sardines were already too weak. "And sometimes you get eaten, LOL."
- **The closing question:** "is that tank you're in the best environment for you? Are you swimming because you want to, or because you finally have to?"

See [[Catfish_Effect|Catfish Effect]] and [[Business_Philosophy|Business Philosophy]].

**Why young women dislike technology.** The note records a question and a string of answers, apparently from a thread. It does not say who wrote which line.

- The question: "why do so many young women just hate technology for no reason where does this impulse come from"
- "nearly all of my cool friends have either slightly negative or largely negative views on AI." Then: "Why do we think that is"
- "If you tell people that you are building a technology to replace them and that also progressively makes the internet worse, many people with cultural cache will not like it."
- Three tipping points for the internet: Zuckerberg's introduction of the feed in 2011, the "tiktokfication of everything (google, meta, Tiktok)", and now "AI slop making the entire internet much less enjoyable".
- "People can mend and shape the world around them with tools, but so can tools mend and shape the wielder. In my own anecdotal experience, women are somewhat likelier to investigate this dynamic than men. There's a wisdom in that question that we shouldn't overlook." Another line adds: "It hasn't been accounted for in the agreed consensus yet."
- "hatred of capitalism -> hatred of billionaires -> hatred of tech billionaires -> hatred of tech"
- "male dominated tech bro culture is also a contributing factor"

Most of the answers give the resistance reasons rather than treating it as irrational. See [[Technology_Critique|Technology Critique]].

## Slow decline

An unattributed passage argues there will be no sudden "collapse" like the movie *Dawn of the Dead*, with riots and soldiers in the street: "There will be no 'happening.' It's far more insidious than that." It points to T. S. Eliot's poem "The Hollow Men" and lists the slow version:

- Simple things get a little more expensive every day; homes and apartments shrink.
- Work hours get longer while pay falls; job security stops existing as a concept.
- People see family and friends less, care less about them, and lower their standards for work, food and relationships.
- People hang on to clothing longer. Fewer marry; fewer still have children.
- People sink into technological distraction and fantasy, "while never truly experiencing the real world".
- The dreams people had for their lives become a distant memory; what is left is "the reality of their debt and their poverty".
- Every minute of every day they are told: "You are stupid, ugly, and weak, but together we are free, prosperous, and safe."

Its conclusion: "That is the collapse. The reduction of the American man into a feudal serf", incapable of feeling love or hate, of seeing his situation for what it is, or of recognising his own worth.

## Entropy versus extropy

> "the left is entropic the right is extropic"

The note attributes this line to [[Curtis_Yarvin|Curtis Yarvin (Mencius Moldbug)]], from his February 2026 interview on Peter McCormack's podcast, with a fuller version: "Fundamentally it's entropy versus extropy. The left is entropic, so things can just decay. But if you're building something, you need to build."

An explainer pasted under the quote unpacks the thermodynamic metaphor:

- **Entropy (the left).** In physics, closed systems drift toward disorder unless energy is added. Politically, the left deconstructs, levels and dissolves existing structures: hierarchies, traditions, standards, borders, merit-based systems. That creates equality on paper but leads to breakdown, inefficiency and decay. Modern progressivism, democracy and bureaucratic drift are the examples, with Yarvin's line "Cthulhu always swims left".
- **Extropy (the right).** The coined opposite of entropy, from futurism and transhumanism: increasing order, complexity, intelligence and capability, as life does. The "true right" builds and maintains higher-order systems: strong hierarchies, sovereign decision-making by one person or a small group who can act, innovation that creates new capability, and patchwork or corporate-sovereign governance. Energetic construction, not passive conservatism.
- **Why the frame.** Most "conservatives" are slower versions of the entropic trend that "conserve yesterday's leftism". High-openness people who used to flock left are shifting right as the entropy becomes obvious.
- **In short:** "Left = lets the system rot (entropy wins). Right = fights rot by actively building stronger, smarter structures (extropy wins)." Politics becomes a contest between decay and deliberate creation, not policy detail. The explainer adds that this is why a poster called it "the best line... that no one is mentioning".

See [[Entropy_vs_Extropy|Entropy vs Extropy]], [[Political_Philosophy|Political Philosophy]] and [[Political_Commentary|Political Commentary]].
