# Psychology & Behavior

A scrapbook of short takes on how people decide, think, cope and relate, mostly saved from X threads and podcasts. Several are deliberately one-sided and some contradict each other; read them as provocations, not findings.

[Read the original note](/source/b2e59e703f3389fa7afd).

## Tiny decisions compound

Life rarely blows up in one move. It rises or erodes through small daily choices, the days that should be +1s and are allowed to be -3s.

- "the compounding effects of replacing tiny bad decisions (negative deltas) with tiny good ones (positive deltas)". Skip the gym and order pizza: "It’s only one day you say." _(@kelxyz_)_
- "our life is just an accumulation of these tiny deltas. Most of the time we don’t blow up with one catastrophic -100%."
- Miss a wide-open layup, get frustrated, give up a wide-open 3: the real swing is -5, and a few of those "and all of the sudden you’re down -40. Looking like the Suns in a Luka game 7."

## Overthinking and acting

Imperfect action beats perfect planning. Wanting something too hard also backfires: "unless something is a simple material trade, grinding is prolly the worst thing you can do. better move is to completely detach yourself from the outcomes."

- "One pattern I’ve noticed in all miserable people: They overthink and underact." _(@TaylinSimmonds)_
- "you are getting lapped by people 50% dumber than you because they don’t overthink shit."
- "aim. fire. correct later"

## Obsession and genius

Asked what the hyper-productive know (Asimov's 500 books, Edison's 1,093 patents), the answers point to obsession and stamina rather than balance.

- "i'll say it again: a brain properly maintaining homeostasis is incapable of this."
- "Sheer, unfazed obsession and volume. That's what they know."
- Robin Hanson: "the most successful have a lot more energy and stamina than do others".
- "intuitive decision makers thrive when they're plugged in". Cut off from the info flow, they "try to make confident inferences but will go awry". "imo this is why genius and madness ride so closely". _(roon, @tszzl)_

## Stillness, words and self-talk

The mind avoids stillness because stillness surfaces what is unresolved. Words shape what you become.

- "Do people hate being still/unstimulated/without distractions because the body will automatically and wisely serve you whichever thing is higher on the stack of things you need to resolve?" _(@nosilverv)_
- "put ALL devices away for 24 hours, exist in solitude, and simply let ur brain do its thing." "the harsh truths will come." _(@divya_venn)_
- "Also words are like spells, be very careful while labelling yourself as "traumatized" or "depressed", they start to become real."
- "In high-trust environments, what you say often matters less than what you're trying to say. In low-trust environments it's usually the opposite" _(@TheGrandBlooms)_

## Shame, regret and hope

Self-loathing poses as an edge; hope is what makes people endure. In Curt Richter's 1957 study, wild rats drowned within 15 minutes, but rats pulled out a few times first swam as long as tame ones, 40–60 hours. Once escape seemed possible, they kept going.

- Shame feels like "your secret weapon, your edge!" In truth "it’s nothing more than a parasite". _(@JasonKoon)_
- "Never entertain the thought that you might have ruined your life." "You don't know, and to reject that is simply mental illness."

## Psychedelics and false insight

One account of ayahuasca: the feeling of epiphany is a brain circuit, not proof of insight.

- "as if I was leaning on the epiphany key in my brain." "If psychedelics have given me any durable insight, it's that our consciousness is extremely fallible and often misleading". _(@hamandcheese)_
- "Kinda like LLM hallucinations: whether the insight is "false" or not isn't intrinsic but depends on whether it attaches to some ground truth."
- "Taking psychedelics is the smartest you’ll ever think you are, combined with the dumbest you’ll ever be." _(@Liu_eroteme)_
- "psychedelics are harmful and counterproductive to the vast majority of people." Yet rarely someone has "crossed the river styx and returned with skulls." _(@thecaptain_nemo)_

## Love, closure and people

- "My therapist once said "People don't abandon the people they love, they abandon the people they're using" and that was all the closure I needed"
- "You fall in love with the people who make you love the person you are when you're around them"
- "“Radical honesty” is lazy — the ability to discern between necessary disclosure and tactful omission is an important life skill" _(@muchnerve)_
- "If you don't like small talk it means you're not good at listening." "Chances are, the people you find boring or stupid probably find you awkward, charmless, and maybe self-absorbed." _(@SchrodingrsBrat)_

## Why people trade, and why they stay

Asked "Why do you trade?", traders answered with games, autonomy and self-deception. Asked why people stay in jobs they hate, the answers were fear and comfort.

- "Best computer game ever. Pure PvP where the leaderboard is your P&L."
- "cuz i lie to myself and cognitive dissonance tells me i have an edge. sometimes my bank account even reflects it"
- "People choose familiar pain over unfamiliar progress/uncrertainty." _(@milesw)_
- "A consistent paycheque is as addicting as heroin"
