# Psychology & Behavior

Erlin's psychology note is a scrapbook of short takes on how people decide, think, cope and relate: saved X posts and their replies, podcast timestamps, a blog post on a rat study, and a few lines in the note's own voice. Read it as a collection of provocations, not findings. Several takes are deliberately one-sided and some contradict each other; quotes keep their original spelling. Handles in italics come from the note or from the saved post's URL; most lines are pasted from X threads, so a handle is the likely author, not a certain one.

## Tiny decisions compound

A post saved with a link to [@kelxyz_](https://x.com/kelxyz_) calls "the compounding effects of replacing tiny bad decisions (negative deltas) with tiny good ones (positive deltas)" an obvious thing that still blows the writer's mind. Each slip feels trivial in the moment. Skip the gym and order pizza: "It’s only one day you say." Fomo into something from Twitter against the trading plan: "It’s only one trade you say."

- "our life is just an accumulation of these tiny deltas. Most of the time we don’t blow up with one catastrophic -100%." _(@kelxyz\_)_
- Basketball version: miss a wide-open layup, get frustrated, give up a wide-open 3. The score moves only -3, but the layup was worth about +2 in expected value, so the real swing is -5. Give up a few of those "and all of the sudden you’re down -40. Looking like the Suns in a Luka game 7."
- "Just reflecting on the power of tiny deltas during daily inflection points."

There is no catastrophic blowup in this model. Life goes up or down by slow accumulation or slow erosion through daily inflection points, the days that should be +1s or +2s and are allowed to be -1s or -3s. Read with [[Repeated Games]] and [[Decision-Making]].

### Shame and regret

- "Note to myself today, maybe some of you need to [read](https://x.com/JasonKoon/status/1870104809107230763) it too." The post argues that self-loathing passes itself off as a superpower: shame and anger burn each mistake into memory, so it feels like part of who you are, "your secret weapon, your edge!" In truth "it’s nothing more than a parasite", stealing presence and energy. It ends: "Fight the darkness, be kind to yourself, and keep moving forward." _(@JasonKoon)_
- "Never entertain the thought that you might have ruined your life." Regret festers only when you believe the alternative would have been perfect; you cannot know how things would have turned out. "The seemingly detrimental L may be a necessary bridge to long term success. The seemingly massive W may be setting the stage for another harsh lesson. You don't know, and to reject that is simply mental illness." It closes on taking it one day at a time, meeting experience with "objective curiosity", and "Trust in divine guidance with all you've got."

## Psychedelics and false insight

Personal accounts and opinions, not clinical guidance; see [[Mental_Health|Mental Health]] and [[Health Evidence]].

- "I did 4 cups of ayahuasca once and tunneled through my consciousness in a state of euphoria having one epiphany after another." Later the sense of epiphany ran on with no referent, "as if I was leaning on the epiphany key in my brain." _(@hamandcheese)_
- The meta-epiphany: psychedelics only make you feel you are learning something new or deep, like déjà vu as the brain's familiarity circuit misfiring in an unfamiliar context. "If psychedelics have given me any durable insight, it's that our consciousness is extremely fallible and often misleading, particularly with respect to valence."
- The writer's follow-up notes: psychedelics are "definitely not for everyone", but the writer enjoyed it and would do it again; ayahuasca is not a first trip, and prior experience probably guards against being "one-shotted".
- The therapeutic twist: the same mechanism behind "false insight" gives psychedelics their therapeutic potential, so go in with intention and aim the epiphanies at a real psychological blocker or trauma. One man in the group said aya helped him forgive his father, something he already wanted; the "false insight" made it stick emotionally. "Kinda like LLM hallucinations: whether the insight is "false" or not isn't intrinsic but depends on whether it attaches to some ground [truth](https://x.com/hamandcheese/status/1866744014432514340)."
- A reply: "Your “meta-epiphany” is the typical takeaway if you have a firm sense of self. Taking psychedelics is the smartest you’ll ever think you are, combined with the dumbest you’ll ever be. A great recipe for a specific type of person to lose touch with [reality](https://x.com/Liu_eroteme/status/1866885071887651093)." _(@Liu\_eroteme)_
- "unpopular opinion. psychedelics are harmful and counterproductive to the vast majority of [people](https://twitter.com/thecaptain_nemo/status/1764387877381894520)." Then: "But *very* rarely you meet someone who has clearly gone to the other side, seen the void, crossed the river styx and returned with skulls. you can sense it all over them." _(@thecaptain\_nemo)_

Contradiction: the ayahuasca writer enjoyed the experience and would repeat it; this post calls psychedelics harmful for the vast majority.

### Drug history and suspicion

A quoted passage in the note runs through drug history. Bayer launched heroin in 1898, made by a simple chemical change to morphine, and promoted it for coughs, gastrointestinal distress and morphine addiction. Ritalin is widely used for ADHD although, the passage says, numerous studies find it "close to a pharmacological twin of cocaine". LSD saw extensive, promising and controversial research from its discovery by Albert Hofman in 1938 until the US banned it in 1967, with a slow revival of studies on alcoholism and post-traumatic stress fifty years later.

The note's reaction: "This history gives you the impression that potentially good things have been made to look bad by an evil group of ppl demonizing things in order to outlaw them and keep them away from humanity...geez that sounds awfully familiar." And: "Wasn't Bayer part of IG [Farben](https://x.com/ColonelTowner/status/1892907481715610077)? Yes, yes it was." _(@ColonelTowner)_

## Hope and endurance

The note saves [Swimming Rats and the Power of Hope](https://www.shawnmaust.com/2016/12/swimming-rats-power-hope/#:~:text=A%20few%20weeks%20ago%2C%20I40%2D60%20hours%20on%20average.), a blog post on what it calls "a(n infamous) study" by Dr. Curt Richter in 1957.

- In water neither too hot nor too cold, domesticated Norwegian rats swam 40–60 hours on average. Wild Norwegian rats of the same breed died within 15 minutes.
- When wild rats were put in the water and pulled out after a few minutes, a few times before the final test, they then lasted about as long as the domesticated rats.
- The team postulated the deaths were more psychological than physiological. The wild rats, not used to confinement, gave up in a place that seemed impossible to escape. Once they had been pulled out before, escape seemed possible, and they lasted far longer.
- The post's lesson, with the study's ethics set aside: hopelessness strips energy and motivation, while hope gives the strength to endure far longer than expected. Giving someone hope may be one of the most valuable gifts there is.

Hopelessness kills; hope sustains. If escape seems possible, endurance multiplies.

## Friendship, small talk and solitude

- "one [thing](https://x.com/ADHDForReal/status/1871828303029342363) i really love about ADHD people is how y'all simply don't have friendship degradation mechanics. i can text an ADHD person after months or even years of radio silence and just pick right back up where we left off" A social observation, not a diagnostic trait; see [[ADHD]] and [[Neurodiversity]]. _(@ADHDForReal)_
- "If you don't like small talk it means you're not good at listening." Small talk tests boundaries, slowly builds trust and picks up details to broaden the conversation; strangers have no reason to offer valuable information before you show you can handle simple social tasks. "Chances are, the people you find boring or stupid probably find you awkward, charmless, and maybe self-absorbed." _(@SchrodingrsBrat)_
- "Why do we feel its necessary to yak about bullshit in order to be comfortable?" A reply from +SJMJ91: there is anxiety and tension in the air, particularly with infatuation, and it is released through human interaction, "kissing, touching, laughing and of course talking about "bullshit" in order to be comfortable. I study human interaction, particularly relationships."
- A longer post on preferring solitude: the writer cannot sustain the performances socialising demands, and "There is a mechanical theater to social groups that bows to imaginary hierarchies". Refusing it guarantees agency. Extrinsic motivation such as social validation ends in mimicry that reaches your moral compass, and social bias puts belonging over truth, which critical thinking cannot afford. [Source post](https://x.com/XivTroy/status/2012829353743184114m). _(@XivTroy)_

## Evidence boundary

The note's single-cause account of depression is a viewpoint, not a complete explanation or treatment model. [[Mental_Health|Mental Health]] holds the corrected synthesis, in which depression has genetic, biological, environmental and psychological contributors and treatment may include psychotherapy, medication or both; [[Health Evidence]] sets the evidence standards. The same caution applies to the study and case claims relayed in posts (thought suppression, blindness and schizophrenia, Ritalin and cocaine): they are claims as posted, with no source in the note.
