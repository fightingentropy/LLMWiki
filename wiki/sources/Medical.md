# Medical & Health

Erlin's medical note is short and opinionated: a research lead on ADHD and the body clock, a toothpaste-and-thyroid claim, a one-line claim about dopamine and sunlight, and a few blunt takes on testosterone, drugs and culture. Read it as a personal scrapbook, not a clinical reference. Two of its claims were checked against primary sources and do not hold up.

## ADHD as a circadian rhythm disorder

The note opens with a line from Nicholas Fabiano's post: "We recently reconceptualized ADHD as a circadian rhythm disorder." ([post](https://x.com/NTFabiano/status/2020162108919357483)). A graphic (`adhd.png`) is attached under it; the note does not say what it shows.

What the idea actually says, from the related material in [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]]:

- Brandon Luu and Fabiano's 2025 perspective in Frontiers in Psychiatry ([paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1697900/full); [Luu's thread](https://x.com/BrandonLuuMD/status/2102338491942347175)) cites delayed sleep/wake timing in up to 78% of people with ADHD, sleep disturbances in up to 80% of adults and 82% of children, and an evening melatonin rise about 45 minutes later in children and 90 minutes later in adults.
- Their proposed additions to standard ADHD care: screen for sleep and circadian problems, keep a consistent wake-up time, get morning bright light, cut evening light and screens, keep regular routines, and use selective low-dose melatonin where delayed timing is found. They describe the treatment evidence as still developing.
- The perspective concerns a possible subgroup. It does not redefine all ADHD as a sleep disorder; [[ADHD]] and [[Circadian Rhythm]] keep that distinction.

Two further leads sit alongside it:

- [NIH Research Matters on how ADHD medications work](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/adhd-medications-stimulate-alertness-motivation) — an NIH research summary on how ADHD medications raise alertness and motivation. A separate lead from the circadian question.
- Medication anecdotes disagree. One author attributes relationship problems, sensory irritation and personality changes to 70 mg Vyvanse ([post](https://x.com/LawrenceElliots/status/2102046696142238206)); another says Adderall helped her feel more consistently herself ([post](https://x.com/Gena_I_Gorlin/status/2101802695325528433)). They concern different people, and neither settles whether medication suits anyone else; that question belongs with the prescriber. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

## Fluoride toothpaste and the thyroid

The claim as the note records it, in the first person; the author is not named and the text may be a pasted post:

> I used Colgate, a fluoride-based toothpaste, twice a day, every day since I was a child. Little did I realize that Fluoride competes with iodine at thyroid receptor sites. Same receptors and direct competition. Your thyroid needs iodine to produce T3 and T4. Fluoride blocks the door.
>
> You're delivering a thyroid-suppressing chemical through the most absorbent tissue in your body. Twice daily. And your dentist told you it was essential.
>
> Hydroxyapatite toothpaste remineralizes teeth without touching your endocrine system. Been around for decades. Japan uses it as standard.

What the evidence says:

- The note supplies no clinical evidence that ordinary fluoride toothpaste suppresses thyroid function at normal use.
- [NHS guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/how-to-keep-your-teeth-clean/) recommends brushing with fluoride toothpaste twice daily, with at least 1,350 ppm fluoride for adults.
- Hydroxyapatite toothpaste is a separate dental-product question. That it exists is not proof of the alleged thyroid mechanism.

## Dopamine, tyrosine and sunlight

The note's claim: "Dopamine is synthesized from the amino acid Tyrosine, The first and crucial step is made by the enzyme Tyrosine Hydroxylase, Sunlight is needed for this step."

The first two parts are standard biochemistry. The third is wrong as stated. The tyrosine-hydroxylase reaction uses tyrosine, oxygen and tetrahydrobiopterin ([NCBI PubChem pathway](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pathway/BioCyc%3AHUMAN_PWY66-301); [enzyme kinetics study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6117320/)). Sunlight is not a reagent. Light does matter for circadian timing, which is a different mechanism and should not be folded into this reaction. See [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]].

## Testosterone, drugs and culture takes

Opinions saved in the note. They are attributed viewpoints: they establish no causal mechanism and recommend no treatment, and the wiki records them without adopting them.

- On testosterone: "Higher testosterone makes men do what they know to be right instead of cave to social pressures of doing what others want them to do. Surprise. It's almost like the decline in testosterone is engineered." See [[Masculinity & Men]].
- On cocaine and looks: "not a fan of cocaine but why do cokeheads almost always have a better physiognomy than users of any other drug". @ananyabhatiaa's answer: "Popping bone structure, lean chiseled looking face with hyperpigmentation acting as shadows and depth".
- The one-liner on therapy, SSRIs and masculinity: "Porn is mostly bad, therapy is mostly gay. premarital is good, age gaps are fine. ssris are overprescribed. masculinity is good, your dad was probably right". The phrasing is derogatory; it stays for context, not as a position of the wiki. The one medical claim in it, that SSRIs are overprescribed, comes with no evidence in the note. See [[Mental Health]].
- A post by @photobiogenesis ([link](https://x.com/photobiogenesis/status/2055817587779395996)) closes the note. Saved without a note.

## Corrections

- Correction, 22 September 2026: the wiki had repeated the note's fluoride-iodine thyroid mechanism as fact. That was unwarranted. The note gives no clinical evidence for it, and NHS guidance recommends fluoride toothpaste at 1,350 ppm or more for adults.
- Correction, 22 September 2026: the claim that sunlight is needed for the tyrosine-hydroxylase step was corrected. The reaction uses oxygen and tetrahydrobiopterin; light's role is in circadian timing, not in this reaction.
