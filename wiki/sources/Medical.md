# Medical & Health

This source contains a circadian-ADHD lead, dental and dopamine claims, drug anecdotes and cultural opinions. The original note remains available below; assertions in that note are not clinical findings.

## ADHD and sleep

The circadian perspective concerns a possible subgroup and does not redefine all ADHD as a sleep disorder. The separately linked NIH medication account is another research lead. [[ADHD]] and [[Circadian_Rhythm]] preserve the distinction. September medication anecdotes disagree and concern different people; neither settles treatment suitability. (from [[Bookmarks - Health & Psychology]])

## Fluoride claim corrected

The source alleges that ordinary fluoride toothpaste suppresses thyroid function through iodine competition. It supplies no clinical evidence establishing this for normal toothpaste use. [NHS guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/how-to-keep-your-teeth-clean/) recommends fluoride toothpaste, with at least 1,350 ppm for adults. The earlier wiki repeated the source's mechanism as fact; that was unwarranted. Hydroxyapatite is a separate dental-product question, not proof of the alleged thyroid mechanism.

## Dopamine claim corrected

The tyrosine-hydroxylase reaction uses oxygen and tetrahydrobiopterin; it does not require sunlight as a reagent. Light's role in circadian timing should not be conflated with that reaction. See the [NCBI biochemical pathway](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pathway/BioCyc%3AHUMAN_PWY66-301) and [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]].

## Opinions and anecdotes

The testosterone-engineering claim, cocaine/appearance comparison, and statements about therapy, SSRIs and masculinity are attributed viewpoints in the original note. They do not establish causal mechanisms or treatment recommendations. The source's derogatory phrasing is preserved in the captured original for context, not adopted by the wiki.
