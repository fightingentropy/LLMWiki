# Electrical Evidence Review - September 2026

A review of the electrical study notes against official IET, GOV.UK and HSE references, made on 22 September 2026. It covers edition status, England's Part P distinctions, concealed cable routes, safe working and PAT frequency. Its corrections apply to [[Building Regulations]], [[Part P]], [[Electrical Safety]], [[RCD Protection]], [[BS 7671]] and [[GN3 Guidance Note 3]]. Use [[Electrical Study Map]] to move from the corrected concepts to the right reference material.

This is a study aid, not design guidance: for any real installation, work from the current BS 7671 text and the rules of the jurisdiction you are in.

## Edition status

- IET states BS 7671:2018+A4:2026 is published.
- The Brown Book with Amendment 3 remains valid until 15 October 2026. Either edition may be used during the transition.
- IET lists Guidance Note 3: Inspection & Testing, tenth edition, and the On-Site Guide, ninth edition, both aligned with A4.

Sources: [IET edition checker](https://electrical.theiet.org/bs-7671-18th-edition-wiring-regulations/ensure-you-are-up-to-date-with-bs-7671/); [IET guidance publications](https://electrical.theiet.org/guidance-and-codes-of-practice/publications-by-category/amendment-42026-to-bs-76712018-iet-wiring-regulations/bs-7671-and-guidance/).

## Part P and notification in England

- The statutory Part P requirement and Approved Document P are different things. The Approved Document provides guidance.
- England's 2013 guidance covers dwellings and specified associated installations, including some business premises that share the dwelling's electricity meter. The [[Electrics - Resources]] note says Part P "applies where people permanently reside (dwellings)"; the guidance scope is wider than that line.
- Notifiable categories include new circuits, consumer-unit replacement, and additions or alterations in defined special locations.
- A kitchen location alone is not a blanket notification trigger.
- Non-notifiable work still has to comply.
- Do not apply England's notification categories to other UK jurisdictions without checking their rules.

Source: [GOV.UK Approved Document P](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-approved-document-p), sections 2–3 of the linked 2013 document.

## Concealed cables

- A 30 mA RCD alone does not permit arbitrary concealed cable routes outside prescribed zones.
- IET's 2015 article explains how zones, additional protection and permitted alternative construction methods combine. It is historical guidance under the seventeenth edition and cannot establish every current A4 clause detail.
- Plastic conduit alone is not equivalent to an earthed metallic or other specifically permitted protective method.

For design decisions, use the applicable current BS 7671 text and its conditions. Source: [IET — Cables in walls (2015)](https://electrical.theiet.org/media/1445/cables-in-walls.pdf).

## Safe work and RCD limits

- HSE says work should normally be done with equipment dead, secured against re-energisation and checked dead (see [[Safe Isolation]]).
- An RCD cannot protect against every shock.
- Maintenance and testing need appropriate competence and suitable equipment (see [[Test Equipment Standards]]).
- Pressing an RCD test button does not establish every aspect of installation safety.

Sources: [HSE electrical FAQs](https://www.hse.gov.uk/electricity/faq.htm); [HSE GS38](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/books/gs38.htm).

## Portable equipment

- HSE does not prescribe blanket annual PAT testing.
- Maintenance arrangements and inspection and testing intervals depend on risk, use and environment.
- Visual checks matter; some faults need testing.
- A test label is not a guarantee that equipment remains safe.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: the source for this point is HSE's [INDG236](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/indg236.pdf) leaflet, not the [PAT FAQ](https://www.hse.gov.uk/electricity/faq-portable-appliance-testing.htm). INDG236 says a test indicates an appliance's safety only at the time of the test and does not imply it will stay safe, so user checks must continue; the FAQ says labels and records are not legally required but are a useful management tool.

See [[PAT Testing]] and [[In-Service Testing]]. Source: [HSE portable appliance testing FAQs](https://www.hse.gov.uk/electricity/faq-portable-appliance-testing.htm).

## Corrections

- Correction, 22 September 2026: GN3 is tenth edition, not fifth. The earlier wiki description of GN3 as fifth edition was wrong and conflated it with in-service equipment material.
- Correction, 22 September 2026: the [[Electrics - Resources]] note cites "Reg 522.5.204: if cables are in earthed metallic covering, conduit, or trunking, an RCD can be avoided." That number is a source error, not a verified current clause. Plastic conduit alone does not qualify, and a 30 mA RCD alone does not make an arbitrary concealed route acceptable.
- Correction, 22 September 2026: Part P is the statutory requirement; Approved Document P is guidance. England's notification rules are not uniform UK rules.
- Correction, 22 September 2026: HSE does not require blanket annual PAT testing.
