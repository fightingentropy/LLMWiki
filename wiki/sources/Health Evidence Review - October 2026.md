# Health Evidence Review - October 2026

Research notes dated 16 October 2026 that test the claims on the wiki's health pages, from [[ADHD]] and [[Water Filtration]] to [[Retatrutide]] and [[Bacteriostatic Water]], against NHS, FDA, DailyMed, CDC, NIH and Drinking Water Inspectorate guidance, manufacturer releases and the PubMed records of the studies themselves. They follow [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]]; the NHS and FDA guidance that review relied on is unchanged. Each section gives what the sources say and the pages it applies to, and every correction is listed at the end. All sources were accessed on 16 October 2026. For the running picture of what is and is not supported, see [[Health Evidence]].

None of this is medical advice, and nothing here is a dose or a treatment plan.

## ADHD and circadian timing

- [Luu and Fabiano's paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1697900/full) is a perspective article published on 10 December 2025, with no new data. Its figures match [[ADHD]] and [[Circadian Rhythm]]: delayed sleep timing in up to 78% of people with ADHD, sleep problems in up to 80% of adults and 82% of children, and evening melatonin rising about 45 minutes late in children and 90 in adults.
- It links an earlier body clock to fewer symptoms only by correlation, places the pattern in a subgroup, says evidence of remission is lacking, and does not propose reclassifying ADHD as solely circadian.
- [NIH Research Matters](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/adhd-medications-stimulate-alertness-motivation) and a [Washington University release](https://source.washu.edu/2025/12/stimulant-adhd-medications-work-differently-than-thought/) describe Kay et al. (Cell, 2025): in 5,795 children's brain scans, stimulants acted on arousal and reward networks rather than attention networks.
- The [Vyvanse label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=704e4378-ca83-445c-8b45-3cfa51c1ecad) confirms lisdexamfetamine is a dextroamphetamine prodrug whose exact action in ADHD is unknown.
- [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/limerence) says limerence is not an official diagnosis and links it to insecure attachment and ADHD hyperfocus. It also compares limerence with addiction.
- [NIGMS](https://www.nigms.nih.gov/education/fact-sheets/Pages/circadian-rhythms) names light as the strongest cue for the roughly 24-hour body clock. [NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/how-much-sleep) recommends 7–9 hours of sleep for adults, and [NICHD](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/sleep/conditioninfo/how-much) says need varies with age and the individual.

## Stress, breathing and the swimming-rat study

- In [Richter's 1957 paper](https://doi.org/10.1097/00006842-195705000-00004), the retold figures are for rats with trimmed whiskers. All 34 trimmed wild rats died within 1–15 minutes; 9 of 12 trimmed domesticated rats swam 40–60 hours; intact domesticated rats swam 60–80 hours in 95 °F water. See [[Stress Management]].
- Wild rats conditioned by brief holding and immersion swam as long as domestic rats or longer. Richter read the sudden deaths as hopelessness.
- The breathing and cold-shower lines on [[Stress Management]] are attributed takes, not physiological claims.

## Water filtration and the DWI view of reverse osmosis

- The [Drinking Water Inspectorate](https://www.dwi.gov.uk/consumers/learn-more-about-your-water/domestic-water-filters-and-softeners/) advises checking the water company's free quality report before buying a filter, and changing cartridges on time.
- It says reverse osmosis wastes 20–30% of incoming water, that RO water is not generally considered suitable for drinking and cooking, that RO suits specialist domestic uses, and that by law the water company must be notified before an RO unit is installed. See [[Water Filtration]].
- The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/drinking-water/prevention/about-choosing-home-water-filters.html) matches filters to NSF/ANSI standards: 42 for taste and odour, 53 for cysts, 58 for reverse osmosis and 62 for distillation.
- [SharkNinja](https://newsroom.sharkninja.com/ninja-raises-the-standard-for-home-water-with-the-launch-of-ninja-hydrasense/) says HydraSense is certified to NSF/ANSI 42, 53 and 401. Its [data sheet](https://qcg-media.scanova.io/media/uploads/118513/2026/06/20260630_300252_Final120GSharkNinjaHydraSensePDS6.pdf) lists lead, PFAS and microplastics but not fluoride, and makes no NSF/ANSI 58 claim. NSF's own listings show no SharkNinja products.
- The Hommix [SanitRO](https://www.hommix.co.uk/products/hommix-sanitro-3-in-1-luxury-countertop-reverse-osmosis-filtration-system-space-grey?variant=46684755329256&country=GB&currency=GBP), [Sparo](https://www.hommix.co.uk/products/hommix-sparo-black-5-in-1-countertop-reverse-osmosis-filtration-system-uv?variant=56307986301307&country=GB&currency=GBP) and [EspressRO](https://www.hommix.co.uk/products/hommix-espressro-5-in-1-countertop-reverse-osmosis-filtration-system?variant=44065246609640&country=GB&currency=GBP) are mains-connected countertop RO systems that claim fluoride removal; none shows a product-specific NSF/ANSI 58 certification.
- [Klense](https://tryklense.com/en-gb/products/klense-filter-showerhead) cites no independent certification for its showerhead claims. The [Oasis Health post](https://x.com/oasishealthapp/status/2093501078465904775) scores about 80 products for fluoride removal with no methodology.
- The [itsactuallytad post](https://x.com/itsactuallytad/status/2092637354150510953) prices an RO system with its own countertop faucet, not a fully installed setup.

## Supplements: creatine, magnesium, vitamin C, tongkat ali

These apply to [[Supplements|Supplements & Biohacking]] and [[Body_Optimization|Body Optimization]].

- **Creatine.** The [Australian Institute of Sport](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements/group_a) puts creatine monohydrate in Group A, strong evidence for specific sporting uses. The [ISSN position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28615996/) reports consistent high-intensity performance gains and good tolerance in healthy people.
- **Magnesium and migraine.** [NIH's NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/providers/digest/headaches-and-complementary-health-approaches-science) calls the evidence limited and says the doses studied exceed the safe upper intake level, so need supervision. The [2012 AAN/AHS guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22529203/) rated magnesium probably effective (Level B) for preventing episodic migraine. The [Cochrane review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41216917/) is still a protocol.
- **Magnesium and sleep.** A [2021 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33865376/) of three small trials and a [2026 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42661485/) of 12 trials rate the evidence low to very low certainty.
- **Magnesium and hearing.** A [1994 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8135325/) in 300 noise-exposed military recruits found fewer permanent hearing-threshold shifts with magnesium.
- **Vitamin C.** The 25% and fourfold fat-burning figures match a [preliminary 2006 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16945143/) of 15 young adults with marginal vitamin C status against 7 with adequate status. The fourfold rise came from 8 people in a depletion-repletion trial and measured fat oxidation on a treadmill, not body-fat loss.
- **Tongkat ali.** A [2022 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36013514/) of five trials found it raised total testosterone, most clearly in men with low levels, and called for more research. No human study shows pomegranate offsetting an oestrogen effect of tongkat ali.
- The NHS vitamin D and fluoride guidance cited in September is unchanged.

## Blueberries and cold exposure

These apply to [[Body_Optimization|Body Optimization]]; [[Fitness]] and [[Supplements|Supplements & Biohacking]] already treat cold-shower mood reports as anecdote.

- **Blueberries.** A [2026 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41974068/) of 14 trials in healthy people found better blood-vessel function but no significant effect on executive function or mood. A [2020 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30999017/) of 12 trials found some memory gains across varied products. Neither reports brain-fog or brain-inflammation outcomes.
- **Cold and dopamine.** In a [controlled study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10751106/), one hour of head-out immersion in 14 °C water raised plasma dopamine 250%: far more exposure than a five-minute shower, and a blood level, not a brain measure.
- **Cold and mood.** A [2025 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39879231/) of 11 immersion trials found lower stress at 12 hours and better sleep quality, but no effect on mood. The [largest cold-shower trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27631616/), of 3,018 adults, found 29% less self-reported sickness absence and no fewer illness days. None shows medication equivalence.

## Exercise, reps and testosterone

These apply to [[Fitness]].

- The [NHS activity guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/) is unchanged. The [push-up study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2724778)'s scope holds; its top group did more than 40 push-ups, so "40 or more" slightly misstates it.
- **High reps.** A [meta-analysis of 21 trials](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/) found light and heavy loads built similar muscle when sets went to failure, with more maximal strength from heavy loads.
- **Testosterone.** Most daily testosterone release in men happens during sleep, and [a week of five-hour nights](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21632481/) cut young men's daytime levels 10–15%. A [high-volume leg session](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19910330/) raised testosterone briefly but added no arm muscle or strength over 15 weeks. The [human sun–testosterone link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34433056/) is observational.
- No study was found on neck bridges, or on neck blood flow and intelligence.

## Retatrutide status

These apply to [[Retatrutide]] and [[Peptide Reconstitution]].

- The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss), in a page current as of 1 October 2026, still says retatrutide is not a component of any FDA-approved drug and has not been found safe and effective. It now says retatrutide cannot be used in compounding under federal law, and that research-labelled GLP-1 products are of unknown quality.
- [Lilly's FAQ](https://lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide) says no regulator has approved it. The [phase 2 NEJM paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37366315/) confirms the code LY3437943 and once-weekly subcutaneous use.
- [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05929066) lists the four TRIUMPH phase 3 trials as completed. In [TRIUMPH-1](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-powerful-weight-loss-in-pivotal-phase-3-obesity-trial), 2,339 adults over 80 weeks, mean weight change reached −28.3% at the highest dose against −2.2% on placebo; [TRIUMPH-2 and -3](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-successful-in-two-additional-phase-3-obesity-trials-delivering-significant-improvements-in-weight-and-a1c) met their primary endpoints.
- Lilly plans to file with the FDA in the first quarter of 2027 and, in its [29 September 2026 release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-substantial-weight-loss-and-a1c-reduction-underscoring-its-potential-promise-for-people-with-obesity-and-type-2-diabetes-302891798.html), says the drug may not legally be sold for human use. No filing or approval had been reported by 16 October 2026.

## Bacteriostatic water, reconstitution and storage

These apply to [[Bacteriostatic Water]] and [[Peptide Reconstitution]].

- The [Bacteriostatic Water label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display) describes sterile, nonpyrogenic water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol, used under the drug manufacturer's instructions and not for neonates. It says to use a reconstituted drug promptly and not to store it unless that drug's manufacturer directs.
- The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/injection-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/index.html) 28-day discard rule, from USP <797>, dates an opened manufactured multiple-dose vial unless its maker says otherwise. It is not a shelf life for a peptide mixed in it.
- [Agitation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35290820/) can aggregate insulin at air–liquid interfaces, with no fixed potency-loss figure. [Freezing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17963151/) can cost protein drugs activity through cold, freeze-concentration and ice formation; the review does not describe ice breaking peptide bonds.
- The [Zepbound label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=487cd7e7-434c-4925-99fa-aa80b1cc776b) forbids freezing but allows its single-dose pens and vials 21 days unrefrigerated. Each limit belongs to its own formulation, not to a research vial.
- A [U-100 insulin label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b519bd83-038c-4ec5-a231-a51ec5cc291f) confirms 100 units equals 1 mL, so the arithmetic on [[Peptide Reconstitution]] holds.

## Studies in Health Evidence

These apply to [[Health Evidence]].

- In [Killingsworth and Gilbert's 2010 paper](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1192439), wandering to pleasant topics made no significant difference to happiness compared with staying on task; neutral and especially unpleasant topics were linked to lower happiness.
- The [UK National Screening Committee](https://view-health-screening-recommendations.service.gov.uk/prostate-cancer/)'s March 2026 review rejects population PSA screening but recommends targeted screening for men with BRCA2 variants.
- The CDC still says anyone infected can develop [long COVID](https://www.cdc.gov/long-covid/about/index.html) and lists smoking as a [conclusive risk factor](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/hcp/clinical-care/underlying-conditions.html) for severe COVID-19.
- [Macmillan](https://www.macmillan.org.uk/about-us/latest-news/news-and-stories/prostate-cancer-diagnosis-every-eight-minutes)'s own estimate is about 600,000 UK men living with prostate cancer; the 68,000 diagnoses a year come from official statistics.
- The [paternal-investment abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2020.109879) confirms 158 father–adolescent pairs and moderation by psychopathy, but not a Romanian sample or perceived resemblance.
- The page's other study summaries matched their records.

## Corrections

- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Water Filtration]], the itsactuallytad post says an RO system with its own countertop faucet costs "almost the same price"; it says nothing about installation costs.

- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Supplements|Supplements & Biohacking]] and [[Body_Optimization|Body Optimization]], the magnesium evidence differs by claim and none of it is solid: migraine prevention is rated probably effective on limited evidence, sleep evidence is low to very low certainty, and hearing rests on a few trials in narrow groups.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Supplements|Supplements & Biohacking]], the 25% and fourfold vitamin C figures come from a small preliminary study of fat oxidation on a treadmill, with the fourfold rise from 8 people against depleted controls, not body-fat loss.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Body_Optimization|Body Optimization]], blueberry trials show better blood-vessel function but no significant effect on executive function or mood, and none reports brain-fog or brain-inflammation outcomes.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Body_Optimization|Body Optimization]], the cold-exposure dopamine figure comes from an hour in 14 °C water and measures blood, not brain; immersion trials show no effect on mood, and the largest cold-shower trial found less sickness absence but no fewer illness days.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Body_Optimization|Body Optimization]], tongkat ali raised total testosterone in a meta-analysis of five trials, mostly in men with low levels; no primary source supports pomegranate offsetting an oestrogen effect.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Fitness]], light-load, high-rep training builds similar muscle to heavy loads when sets go to failure, so high reps alone do not prevent growth.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Fitness]], most daily testosterone release happens during sleep and short sleep lowers it; a leg session raises it only briefly with no added arm growth, and the sun link in humans is observational.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Health Evidence]], mind-wandering to pleasant topics made no significant difference to happiness; only neutral and unpleasant topics were linked to lower happiness.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Bacteriostatic Water]], Bacteriostatic Water for Injection is itself sterile and nonpyrogenic; the guide's "NOT sterile water" only separates it from preservative-free Sterile Water for Injection.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Bacteriostatic Water]] and [[Peptide Reconstitution]], the label says to use a reconstituted drug promptly and not store it unless its manufacturer directs; the 28-day USP <797> limit dates an opened manufactured multiple-dose vial, not a peptide mixed from it.
- Correction, 16 October 2026: on [[Bacteriostatic Water]] and [[Peptide Reconstitution]], agitation and freezing can harm peptide drugs, but no source gives the guide's fixed 30–50% shaking or 10–20%-a-day room-temperature losses or describes ice crystals destroying peptide bonds; storage limits belong to each formulation.
