# Bookmarks - Health & Psychology

Health and psychology links saved from X and the web, each with a note on what the underlying source shows and where the post attached to it overstates it. The strongest material is the primary research and guideline pages behind the posts: PubMed, NEJM, Nature Neuroscience, PNAS Nexus, NIMH, the Endocrine Society, the American Academy of Dermatology, CDC, WHO and the UK National Screening Committee. Nicholas Fabiano (@NTFabiano) is the most frequent source of study links; alexei (@alexeixbt), Minor Dissent (@minordissent), Jimmy Edgar (@JIMMYEDGAR), @apaji_x and vittorio (@IterIntellectus) recur. [[Health Evidence]] sets out the difference between saving a claim and adopting it.

How to read the collection: the Vyvanse and Adderall accounts are contrasting personal experiences and neither generalises; depression-as-purpose, three-day cures, antihistamines for ADHD and craving "rewiring" are claims, not treatment protocols; negative thinking and Alzheimer's biomarkers, imagined contractions and strength, and wearable-guided physical therapy need the underlying study or validation before practical use; dental devices, peptides, skin-treatment anecdotes and vendor links do not establish effectiveness or suitability; the reposts attached to the follicular-fluid and depression/cognition research overstate what those studies show.

## ADHD, sleep timing and stimulant medication

Personal accounts of medication here are one person's experience; medication changes belong with the prescriber or a pharmacist.

- [ADHD, delayed body clocks, and circadian interventions](https://x.com/BrandonLuuMD/status/2102338491942347175) — Brandon Luu describes a link between ADHD and later biological sleep/wake timing, with early evidence that shifting the body clock earlier may ease symptoms for some people. The [quoted explanation](https://x.com/anishmoonka/status/2102284259553890448) and his 2025 perspective with Nicholas Fabiano, [ADHD as a circadian rhythm disorder](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1697900/full), cite delayed sleep/wake timing in up to 78%, sleep disturbances in up to 80% of adults and 82% of children, and an evening melatonin rise about 45 minutes later in children and 90 minutes later in adults. The attached graphic depicts later melatonin timing and a later, flatter morning cortisol rise. Their proposed approach: screen for sleep/circadian problems, keep a consistent wake-up time, get morning bright light, cut evening light and screen exposure, keep regular routines, and use selective low-dose melatonin when delayed timing is identified. They describe these as additions to standard ADHD care, with treatment evidence still developing. Correction, 22 September 2026: the paper is a perspective article, not a new diagnostic definition; it describes a circadian subgroup within ADHD (see [[ADHD]] and [[Circadian_Rhythm]]). Related note: [[Medical|Medical & Health]]. _(@BrandonLuuMD)_
- [Negative personal experience with prescribed Vyvanse](https://x.com/LawrenceElliots/status/2102046696142238206) — The author attributes relationship problems, sensory irritation, isolation and personality changes to 70 mg lisdexamfetamine and quotes a broader anti-medication claim. Not evidence that prescribed stimulants cause autism or affect everyone the same way. _(@LawrenceElliots quoting @AntiDoc)_
- [Positive personal account of Adderall treatment](https://x.com/Gena_I_Gorlin/status/2101802695325528433) — The author says medication helped her feel more consistently herself and links a longer essay about moving from ADHD scepticism to treatment. The contrasting experience to the Vyvanse post; not a guarantee of benefit or a dosing recommendation. _(@Gena_I_Gorlin)_
- [ADHD procrastination tips](https://x.com/apaji_x/status/2096141656789598398) — Introductory post promising ADHD-friendly ways to start tasks. The saved record does not contain the list itself; read the complete thread before using it as guidance. _(@apaji_x)_
- [Limerence, attachment, and ADHD-related hyperfocus](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/limerence) — Cleveland Clinic's explainer: limerence is an intense, involuntary fixation on another person and not an official medical diagnosis. It discusses insecure attachment and a possible vulnerability related to ADHD hyperfocus, not an inevitable outcome of ADHD or a universal biochemical explanation. The [original post](https://x.com/apaji_x/status/2091560421555614093) overstates this as a severe dopamine addiction. A useful distinction: reciprocal knowledge of a real person versus an imagined relationship reinforced by checking and rumination. Persistent distress or impairment is a reason to seek professional support. _(@apaji_x)_
- [Antihistamines framed around "ADHD brains"](https://x.com/be_like_legend1/status/2096567715519434845) — Unsupported comparison of Allegra, Zyrtec and Pepcid for people with ADHD. A flagged claim, not a reason to change medication. _(@be_like_legend1)_
- [ADHD, histamine, circadian rhythm, and sleep claim](https://x.com/ArsAlendi/status/2096617680815980866) — Anecdotal thread treating ADHD as partly a histamine and sleep issue after one reported response to an antihistamine. It does not establish a general mechanism or a safe treatment. _(@ArsAlendi)_
- [Three-day depression and ADHD cure claim](https://x.com/oxidativestate/status/2097515710172025053) — Unsupported claim that a dopamine reset rapidly cured depression and ADHD. A misinformation example: not evidence, diagnosis or treatment guidance, and no substitute for qualified care. _(@oxidativestate)_

## Depression, mood and cognition

Persistent low mood or cognitive symptoms deserve clinical assessment rather than self-diagnosis from a post.

- [Depression can affect concentration, memory, and practiced skills](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression) — NIMH's depression overview: difficulty concentrating, remembering or making decisions is a recognized symptom, and severe cases can involve prominent memory and thinking problems. It supports one [bookmark author's lived experience](https://x.com/Laiiyylaa/status/2085925048335126831) and a later [anhedonia-to-skill-loss model](https://x.com/thehowlchemist/status/2094249098312384915): reduced pleasure and practice can plausibly erode fluency. Neither post establishes inevitable permanent "brain damage", neurotoxicity or irreversible pruning. _(@Laiiyylaa; @thehowlchemist)_
- [Depression, anxiety, and memory-loss claim](https://x.com/DEUILSTEARS444/status/2096602733654552989) — General observation that depression and anxiety can go with substantial memory problems. A prompt for clinical reading, not a causal conclusion. _(@DEUILSTEARS444)_
- [Purpose and life-alignment as one dimension of depression](https://x.com/minordissent/status/2094327547672805434) — A motivational post frames depression as the psyche refusing an unaligned life, with purpose as the cure. Keep the invitation to examine meaning, relationships, work and environment, but not the absolute: depression has heterogeneous biological, psychological and social contributors, and purpose does not replace assessment, therapy, medication, safety planning or other appropriate care. _(@minordissent)_
- [Isolation, rumination, and small re-entry steps](https://x.com/sunnkssdseraph/status/2086173566710743236) — Describes a loop in which staying home, repetitive thought, worry and declining motivation make ordinary life feel increasingly distant, then recommends starting with a small action. Useful as a recognition prompt, but "nervous system stuck in survival mode" is not a diagnosis; similar changes can arise from depression, anxiety, physical illness, disability, exhaustion, grief, unsafe circumstances or ordinary preference. See [NIMH's depression guidance](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression). Persistent impairment warrants professional assessment rather than forced exposure. _(@sunnkssdseraph)_
- [Legitimate suffering and neurotic avoidance](https://x.com/aristomarinetti/status/2018034523141804336) — Useful Jungian frame: attempts to evade unavoidable human pain can manufacture larger, unnecessary suffering. _(@aristomarinetti)_
- [Mind-wandering and momentary happiness](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1192439) — Killingsworth and Gilbert's 2010 experience-sampling study found that participants reported lower momentary happiness while their minds wandered, including toward neutral or pleasant topics, across many daily activities. An observational self-report study about attention and concurrent affect, not evidence that consuming instead of creating causes clinical depression or that finding a "creative quest" is a diagnosis or cure. Saved from the [original bookmark](https://x.com/NTFabiano/status/2086444739805532641). _(@NTFabiano)_
- [Repetitive negative thinking and Alzheimer's biomarkers](https://x.com/dhh/status/2101644926094373128) — Humorous reaction to a quoted claim that repetitive negative thinking is associated with Alzheimer's-related protein accumulation. Association does not establish causation or show that optimism prevents disease; the underlying study, population, measures and effect size still need locating. _(@dhh quoting @NTFabiano)_
- [Depression-associated gut microbiota transfer in rats](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2016.07.019) — A 2016 study transferred pooled fecal microbiota from three severely depressed male donors or three matched controls into microbiota-depleted rats and reported anhedonia- and anxiety-like behavior plus altered tryptophan metabolism. Small preclinical animal evidence, not proof that human depression is transmissible or a basis for self-treatment. _(via @NTFabiano)_

## Attention, habits and sleep

- [Neuroplasticity and deliberate repetition](https://x.com/alexeixbt/status/2086051211271196886) — Alexei's motivational essay centers the continuing capacity of adult brains to change through experience and the importance of repeated practice, attention, recovery and sleep. An accessible behavioral frame, not a neuroscience protocol: acetylcholine is one of several neuromodulators rather than a universal "save switch", and the essay does not establish that brief intense movement guarantees learning, that willpower is a single trainable muscle, or that wealth, confidence and personality reduce to repeated neural wiring. Background reading: a [human acetylcholine and motor-cortex plasticity study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6673455/). _(@alexeixbt)_
- [Cravings, emotional intensity, and neuroplasticity](https://x.com/alexeixbt/status/2100716907108151736) — Claims that resisting a craving at peak intensity creates a larger "rewiring" effect. Motivational framing, not an established dose-response rule; exposure and addiction interventions can require professional support and should not be improvised from a post. _(@alexeixbt)_
- [Two weeks without mobile internet](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/2/pgaf017/8016017) — A preregistered month-long randomized trial assigned participants to block internet access on their smartphones for two weeks while keeping calls, texts and internet on other devices. Intention-to-treat analyses found improvements in subjective well-being, mental health and objectively measured sustained attention, but compliance was difficult: only 119 of 467 committed participants met the preregistered compliance threshold. This was not total smartphone or internet abstinence, and the "ten years younger" comparison is an effect-size analogy, not literal rejuvenation. Posts: the [original bookmark](https://x.com/NTFabiano/status/2084270984022905286) and [additional discussion](https://x.com/sentientist/status/2054591492162040312). _(@NTFabiano)_
- [The two-hour sleep thought experiment](https://x.com/waitbutwhy/status/1906866707534147681) — Tim Urban asks why people sacrifice two hours of sleep when they would eagerly take a drug promising the benefits of two extra hours. A behavioral prompt, not a quantified drug comparison; [NIH guidance](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/how-much-sleep) says adults generally need seven to nine hours and individual needs vary. _(@waitbutwhy)_
- [Cold-shower "unbracing" exercise](https://x.com/jonnym1ller/status/1958345432410845515) — Anecdotal somatic exercise: notice anticipatory tension before cold water and practise releasing it. A mindfulness prompt, not treatment advice. _(@jonnym1ller)_

## Movement, pain and training

Suitability depends on the person, load, balance and injury history; pain or a rehabilitation goal warrants qualified assessment rather than copying a routine.

- [Foot, ankle, rotation, and bouncing movement reference](https://x.com/wirallop/status/2092319105508356490) — A short Guy Macchia clip demonstrates lower-leg work, standing rotation, supported movement and bouncing. An exercise-video reference, not a validated treatment plan: claims such as doing 75% of training below the knee, rotation being the master of decompression, and broad fascia explanations are not substantiated by the clip. _(@wirallop)_
- [Wearable-guided back-pain PT demo](https://x.com/thefountaindead/status/2097089544357327268) — Prototype connecting a wearable to a biomechanical model that suggests personalized exercises. It does not validate the model, identify the cause of pain, or show that the suggested physical therapy is safe. _(@thefountaindead)_
- [Imagined muscle contractions and strength](https://x.com/JIMMYEDGAR/status/2101074736600011033) — Reaction to a quoted claim that mentally rehearsing contractions increased strength without exercise. Before treating it as training guidance, identify the study, muscle group, protocol, comparison, effect size and limitations. _(@JIMMYEDGAR quoting @NTFabiano)_

## Skin, hair, face and teeth

Prescription retinoids, ivermectin and hair treatments are clinician-directed; the studies here are evidence to discuss, not DIY protocols.

- [Tazarotene versus tretinoin](https://x.com/Hoostethics/status/2093649112352821464) — The post argues that tazarotene is categorically stronger and more effective, using an uncontrolled before-and-after image and a routine that also included azelaic acid and moisturizers. Randomized photoaging trials, a [24-week comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15203997/) and a [multicentre concentration trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11735710/), found that some tazarotene concentrations and formulations produced faster or greater improvement on selected measures than tretinoin, with more frequent early irritation; they do not establish a universal ranking across acne, pigmentation, wrinkles, every concentration or every patient. Both are prescription retinoids, so product choice, titration, contraindications and pregnancy-related precautions belong with a clinician rather than the post's suggestion to skip straight to tazarotene. _(@Hoostethics)_
- [Demodex mites, rosacea, and topical ivermectin](https://x.com/SMB_Attorney/status/2093856369203888444) — An anecdotal post says prescription ivermectin transformed persistent skin problems after the author learned about Demodex. Demodex density can be elevated in rosacea and topical ivermectin is an established treatment for papulopustular rosacea ([National Rosacea Society](https://www.rosacea.org/rosacea-review/2025/spring/topical-ivermectin-may-improve-rosacea-indirectly)), but the mites normally inhabit healthy skin and are not a universal explanation for acne, redness or irritation. _(@SMB_Attorney)_
- [Red-light therapy: indication-specific evidence, not a cure-all](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/safety/red-light-therapy) — The American Academy of Dermatology describes evidence for some skin-aging concerns and hereditary hair loss, with results varying by device, treatment and individual. That does not support the [bookmark's claims](https://x.com/dantefofante/status/2091994204365009297) of universal improvements in recovery, mood, energy and health. Distinguish the intended indication, wavelength, dose and device; discuss suitability, photosensitizing medication and skin sensitivity with a clinician and follow the specified eye-protection instructions. The AAD overview is the useful part, not the unspecified Amazon device. _(@dantefofante)_
- [Microneedling plus minoxidil for androgenetic alopecia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23960389/) — A 2013 evaluator-blinded randomized pilot in 100 men reported mean 12-week hair-count increases of 91.4 versus 22.2 in a fixed 1 cm² area. A later [RCT meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40056230/) supports better hair-count outcomes than minoxidil alone, but protocols and study quality vary; evidence to discuss with a dermatologist. _(via @drmikehart)_
- [Proclaim custom whole-mouth water flosser](https://x.com/paulbohm/status/2095259998297133215) — A personalized mouthpiece, produced from an intraoral scan, is advertised to clean the whole mouth with multiple jets in about seven seconds ([product page](https://shop.proclaimhealth.com/products/additional-personalized-mouthpiece)). A dental-product and distribution lead, not a private-equity thesis or a replacement for professional advice; independent effectiveness, adherence, consumables, cleaning, contraindications and price still matter. _(@paulbohm)_
- [Wide-palate appearance as an online health signifier](https://x.com/MagnusVigso/status/2083961118381256999) — The post treats a naturally wide palate as evidence of a well-nourished childhood and a predominantly parasympathetic state. An aesthetic and developmental-health hypothesis, not a diagnostic fact: the post supplies no measurements or clinical source, and visible craniofacial structure reflects genetics, development, dental factors, environment and camera presentation rather than a readable autonomic-nervous-system state. _(@MagnusVigso)_
- [Bioengineered chewing gum for oral HPV and cancer-associated bacteria](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41656354/) — An ex-vivo study exposed collected saliva and oral-rinse samples to bean-lectin and antimicrobial-peptide gum preparations, reporting aggregation of up to 93% of detected HPV and reductions in selected bacteria under the experimental conditions. It does not show that ordinary chewing clears an infection, prevents cancer or improves clinical outcomes in people; an early delivery-platform result that needs in-vivo safety, dosing, efficacy and replication. Saved from the [original bookmark](https://x.com/Polymarket/status/2088726104424976834). _(@Polymarket)_

## Screening, hormones, allergy and sinus

Testing and treatment decisions here are clinical ones; the anecdotes are leads, not procedures.

- [Testosterone deficiency can overlap with depressive symptoms](https://x.com/wanted4mogging/status/2094567596490162625) — The post argues that prescribing antidepressants without testosterone screening proves systemic failure. The narrower point is useful: low mood, energy, libido, concentration and sexual symptoms can overlap with hypogonadism and may justify clinical evaluation. Per the [Endocrine Society guideline](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/testosterone-therapy), diagnosis requires compatible symptoms plus consistently low, accurately measured morning testosterone, not universal screening or assuming testosterone is the cause. _(@wanted4mogging)_
- [H. pylori and refractory levothyroxine absorption](https://x.com/FarvingCo/status/2093783673623740918) — A [2011 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21435090/) found that treating H. pylori in hypothyroid patients who were not responding to high-dose levothyroxine substantially lowered TSH, plausibly by improving absorption; 21% developed iatrogenic thyrotoxicosis afterward. This does not validate the tweet's broad symptom list, belly-fat mechanism, low-T3 certainty or self-directed supplement "kill stack". H. pylori needs proper testing and clinician-directed eradication therapy. _(@FarvingCo)_
- [Intralymphatic immunotherapy for allergic rhinitis](https://x.com/ctbeiser/status/2085089503908413470) — Chris Beiser reports substantial and lasting relief after intralymphatic immunotherapy (ILIT) in a discussion about likely dust-mite allergy, nighttime congestion and impaired sleep. His later clarification estimates a 65–70% reduction in allergy symptoms rather than a complete "permanent fix", and another participant reports no benefit or possible worsening. Controlled evidence is mixed: a [randomized double-blind trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34088322/) using dust-mite, cat and dog extracts found little overall clinical advantage over placebo and recorded pain plus moderate-to-severe systemic reactions, while a tiny [11-person pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28293934/) reported symptom improvement but included two cases of anaphylaxis. A treatment lead to discuss with an allergy specialist, not a recommendation, an established cure or a self-treatment procedure. _(@ctbeiser)_
- [DIY household-bleach sinus-rinse anecdote](https://x.com/EdaG889915/status/2052346899600998694) — The author reports feeling better after adding diluted household sodium hypochlorite to a sinus rinse and supplies a [follow-up mixing instruction](https://x.com/EdaG889915/status/2052350686268887512). An anecdote, **not a recipe or self-treatment protocol**. A small [randomized study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27853946/) of device-generated low-concentration hypochlorous acid in selected chronic-rhinosinusitis patients does not validate improvised household-bleach mixtures; hypochlorous acid and sodium hypochlorite are not interchangeable products. [FDA safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/rinsing-your-sinuses-neti-pots-safe) describes saline made with distilled, sterile or previously boiled water, and CDC bleach instructions concern disinfecting otherwise unsafe water, not adding bleach as a therapeutic ingredient. Persistent symptoms and any nonstandard rinse belong with an ENT clinician. _(@EdaG889915)_
- [UK prostate-cancer incidence and screening context](https://news.sky.com/story/one-man-diagnosed-with-prostate-cancer-in-the-uk-every-eight-minutes-13575557) — Macmillan estimates more than 68,000 UK diagnoses a year, around one every eight minutes, and roughly 600,000 men living with prostate cancer; population ageing and increased detection affect the trend. Early disease can be symptomless, but testing is a shared clinical decision rather than a universal instruction: the [UK National Screening Committee](https://view-health-screening-recommendations.service.gov.uk/prostate-cancer/) does not recommend population-wide PSA screening and currently recommends targeted screening for a defined high-risk BRCA2 group, because overdiagnosis and unnecessary treatment can cause harm. Saved from the [original bookmark](https://x.com/bertdernernert/status/2091055197405557210). _(@bertdernernert)_

## Nutrition, weight and peptides

- [Thirty years of nutrition knowledge simplified](https://gilcarvalhomdphd.substack.com/p/30-years-of-nutrition-knowledge-simplified) — Gil Carvalho summarizes practical nutrition fundamentals, dietary patterns associated with lower chronic-disease risk, and ways to recognize fad claims without eliminating whole food groups or relying on expensive supplements. A broad educational overview, not individualized medical advice. Saved from the [original bookmark](https://x.com/NutritionMadeS3/status/2081319985096384566). _(@NutritionMadeS3)_
- [Water preloading before meals in a weight-loss diet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859815/) — In a 12-week randomized trial of 48 adults aged 55–75 with overweight or obesity, both groups followed a hypocaloric diet and one group also drank 500 mL of water before each main meal. The water group lost about 2 kg more and had a 44% greater rate of weight loss. This does not support the [bookmark's claims](https://x.com/SmartNotHard1/status/2083606184594919582) of "no other dietary changes", ghrelin dilution, a 24–30% metabolism boost, faster liver-fat metabolism, zero side effects, or equivalence to a meal-delivery program; a modest adjunct, not a standalone protocol. _(@SmartNotHard1)_
- [Peptides versus supplements as an experiential claim](https://x.com/minordissent/status/2094564810554052961) — The author reports that several peptides felt noticeable while most supplements felt like placebo, while conceding that peptides are overhyped. One person's experience and a prompt for compound-specific research, not comparative evidence of efficacy, safety, purity, dosing or legality. See [[Peptides]] and [[Supplements & Biohacking]]. _(@minordissent)_
- [Peptides Lab UK](https://peptideslabuk.com/) — Peptide vendor site. A vendor reference only, not medical evidence or a safety endorsement; current legality, independent testing, contraindications and clinician review come before any purchase.

## Alcohol, tobacco and COVID claims

- [Do not trade alcohol harm for cigarette harm](https://x.com/algekalipso/status/2086859052181786862) — The post uses a convex-versus-concave marginal-risk analogy to propose that some people should "drink less, smoke more." A counterexample in multi-risk reasoning, not health advice: population risk curves do not establish a safe personal substitution, tobacco use is harmful at every level ([WHO tobacco guidance](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/tobacco)), alcohol is also carcinogenic and not risk-free ([WHO alcohol guidance](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/alcohol)), and using one addictive exposure to regulate another can compound rather than optimize harm. Reducing either should rely on evidence-based support rather than a social-media Pareto calculation. _(@algekalipso)_
- [Smoking does not prevent long COVID](https://x.com/Bitcoin_Teddy/status/2086392291862995011) — The clip claims smokers get only mild COVID, cannot develop long COVID, nicotine blocks spike protein, and nicotine patches are harmless. A medical-misinformation example: CDC says anyone infected can develop [long COVID](https://www.cdc.gov/long-covid/about/index.html), smoking is associated with [severe COVID outcomes](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/hcp/clinical-care/underlying-conditions.html), tobacco damages nearly every organ ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/tobacco)), and nicotine is addictive. Nicotine-replacement products have defined cessation uses and contraindications; the clip does not establish routine patches as a safe or effective long-COVID treatment. _(@Bitcoin_Teddy)_

## Reproduction, sex differences and social psychology

Survey and in-vitro associations do not establish causation, and the memes attached to several of these results do not follow from the research.

- [Perceived resemblance, psychopathic traits, and paternal investment](https://x.com/houseofhereaftr/status/2103150600753950923) — A [2020 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2020.109879) of 158 Romanian father–teen pairs linked perceived resemblance to reported paternal investment, with a stronger facial-resemblance association at higher psychopathy scores. Personality similarity also contributed. Survey associations do not establish causation, changes over time, clinical diagnoses or a motive of self-love. Sources: the [quoted post](https://x.com/Psikobilim_/status/2102824105024332196), the [university abstract](https://www.research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3359563) and the publisher's [indexed methods excerpt](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886920300684). The main attachment is an illustrative [photograph](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HS_kUviWsAAdr6Z.jpg); the quotation supplies a [facial-resemblance model](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HS66opaWUAAT8-F.jpg), a [personality-similarity model](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HS660e4XkAA9fPR.jpg) and the [paper's first-page excerpt](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HS67LxgWsAAhV9K.jpg). The summary rests on the abstract, the methods excerpt and the screenshots; the full paper, measurement details, moderation estimates and replication evidence were not checked. _(@houseofhereaftr)_
- [Follicular fluid differentially attracts human sperm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32517615/) — A 2020 in-vitro study using samples from couples undergoing assisted reproduction found that follicular fluid from different women consistently attracted sperm from particular men differently, and the stronger response did not systematically favor the woman's partner. This supports a possible gamete-mediated selection mechanism; it did not place eggs beside competing sperm, prove conscious choice, or establish a clinical test for unexplained infertility. A later [crude repost](https://x.com/mak_aura/status/2094854871212253473) turns the result into a joke about women making wrong decisions "molecularly"; the source trail is what is worth keeping, not that inference. _(via @anishmoonka)_
- [Sex differences in mental-rotation performance](https://x.com/antarte_/status/2093756333010952414) — The post extrapolates from a water-bottle rotation question to broad claims about women, systemizing and IQ-test construction. A [2026 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41566416/) does report an average male advantage on mental-rotation tests, with substantial heterogeneity; it does not substantiate the post's "40%" college-women statistic, its general-intelligence conclusion, or the claim that psychometricians deliberately removed systemizing to equalize scores. _(@antarte_)_
- [Body-odour disgust sensitivity and xenophobic attitudes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10090875/) — A preregistered study of 6,836 participants across nine countries found that greater self-reported sensitivity to body odours was associated with more negative attitudes toward fictitious refugee groups, partly through perceived differences in hygiene and food-preparation norms. An association in survey measures, not proof that smelling immigrants causes xenophobia, that disgust determines anyone's politics, or that the [bookmark's crude punchline](https://x.com/Basedgoymemes/status/2093361464119566353) follows from the result. _(@Basedgoymemes)_
- [Birth-control harms clip with an inflammatory repost](https://x.com/benwehrman/status/2093113877688906147) — The source video is a lead for later review, not medical evidence. The repost supplies no transcript, study, named clinical claim or verifiable context, and adds threatening rhetoric about people who promoted contraception. Different contraceptive methods have different benefits, risks, contraindications and evidence; do not infer a general medical conclusion or endorse the caption without identifying and checking the underlying claims. _(@benwehrman, crediting @florawholehealth)_

## Neuroscience, genetics and medical research

- [Complete male fruit-fly central nervous-system connectome](https://x.com/JIMMYEDGAR/status/2095579582879383756) — [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/a-connectomics-milestone-mapping-the-complete-male-fruit-fly-brain/) describes a real map containing more than 166,000 neurons, extending connectomics work across the male fly's central nervous system. The scientific milestone is the thing worth keeping; the repost's anthropomorphic claim that an AI "talked about rotting food" is a playful interpretation, not a reported experimental conclusion. _(@JIMMYEDGAR)_
- [Patient-specific in-vivo gene editing for CPS1 deficiency](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2504747) — A customized lipid-nanoparticle base editor was given to one infant with severe CPS1 deficiency; over seven weeks the patient tolerated more dietary protein and needed less nitrogen-scavenger medication without a serious treatment-related adverse event. An encouraging N-of-1 case with short follow-up, not proof of a cure or of general scalability. _(via @IterIntellectus)_
- [Streaming brain-to-voice neuroprosthesis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-01905-6) — A 2025 Nature Neuroscience study used high-density cortical recordings from one 47-year-old woman with severe paralysis and anarthria to stream synthesized speech using her pre-injury voice. A promising invasive proof of concept, but single-participant clinical-trial evidence rather than a generally available treatment. _(via @IterIntellectus)_
- [Cannabis smoking status and blood-DNA methylation associations](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12890-025-03634-9) — A cross-sectional CanCOLD subset of 93 older adults, including 10 current and 32 former cannabis smokers, found thousands of blood-DNA methylation associations versus never-smokers, with overlap in aging- and cancer-related pathways. It cannot establish causation, persistence within individuals, downstream gene expression or clinical cancer risk; the authors also note self-report, tobacco confounding, small group sizes and the absence of longitudinal repeated measures. _(via @chamath)_
- [AI-assisted longitudinal symptom investigation](https://x.com/amydeng_/status/2066928566961639716) — Amy Deng describes tracking symptoms and metrics, testing hypotheses and iterating with models while keeping medical decisions under physician guidance. A case study, not medical advice. _(@amydeng_)_
