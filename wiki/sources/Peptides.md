# Peptides

The note is a saved guide titled "Retatrutide Reconstitution Guide: From Powder to Injection". It walks from a vial of freeze-dried [[Retatrutide]] powder to a weekly injection: supplies, dilution arithmetic, mixing, storage, injection technique and a dose-escalation schedule. The guide's figures are reproduced below as the note states them. Read it as a source to scrutinise, not as validated treatment instructions: several of its precise claims have no evidence behind them, and the compound is unapproved.

## Regulatory status

- The guide opens with its own disclaimer: retatrutide (LY3437943) is an investigational compound still in Phase 3 clinical trials, is not FDA-approved, and the guide is for "educational and research reference purposes only". It tells the reader to consult a qualified healthcare provider.
- The [FDA's current statement](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss) says retatrutide is not a component of any FDA-approved drug and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. That statement covers US status only, not every country's approvals. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])
- A vendor's powder, an online schedule and correct dilution arithmetic do not establish product identity, purity, safety or clinical suitability.

## Dilution and dose arithmetic

The guide calls 10 mg vial + 2 mL BAC water = 5 mg/mL its "Most Popular Setup". Doses are given in units on a U-100 insulin syringe, where 100 units = 1 mL.

| Vial size | BAC water added | Concentration | 1 mg dose | 2 mg dose | 4 mg dose | 8 mg dose | 12 mg dose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mg | 1 mL | 5 mg/mL | 20 units | 40 units | 80 units | N/A | N/A |
| 5 mg | 2 mL | 2.5 mg/mL | 40 units | 80 units | 160 units* | N/A | N/A |
| 10 mg | 1 mL | 10 mg/mL | 10 units | 20 units | 40 units | 80 units | 120 units* |
| **10 mg** | **2 mL** | **5 mg/mL** | **20 units** | **40 units** | **80 units** | **160 units*** | **240 units*** |
| 30 mg | 3 mL | 10 mg/mL | 10 units | 20 units | 40 units | 80 units | 120 units* |
| 30 mg | 6 mL | 5 mg/mL | 20 units | 40 units | 80 units | 160 units* | 240 units* |

\*Doses over 100 units need splitting into several injections or a larger syringe.

Correction, 16 October 2026: the 12 mg column lists a dose from a 10 mg vial (120 units at 10 mg/mL, 240 units at 5 mg/mL), but a 10 mg vial holds only 100 units of solution at 10 mg/mL, or 200 units at 5 mg/mL. One 10 mg vial cannot supply 12 mg; the guide marks the equivalent cells for the 5 mg vial "N/A".

The formula, as the guide gives it:

```text
Concentration (mcg/mL) = Peptide amount (mcg) / Water added (mL)
Units to inject        = Desired dose (mcg) / Concentration (mcg/mL) × 100
```

Worked example from the guide: a 10,000 mcg vial + 2 mL water = 5,000 mcg/mL. For a 4 mg (4,000 mcg) dose, 4,000 ÷ 5,000 × 100 = 80 units. The arithmetic is sound; it says nothing about whether the vial contains what its label claims.

## Reconstitution steps

The guide's seven steps:

1. **Bring to room temperature.** Leave the powder vial and BAC water out for 15–20 minutes; cold powder is harder to dissolve.
2. **Clean everything.** Wash hands with soap and water for 20+ seconds. Wipe both rubber stoppers with alcohol swabs and let them air-dry 10–15 seconds; do not blow on them.
3. **Draw the BAC water.** Pull back air equal to the target volume, inject it into the BAC water vial to equalise pressure, invert, and draw the calculated amount slowly. Tap out bubbles and push until a drop appears at the tip.
4. **Add water to the peptide vial (the guide's "critical step").** Angle the needle at the glass wall, not the powder, and depress the plunger over 10–20 seconds so the water trickles down the inside. "Never blast water directly onto the powder — this can denature/damage the peptide."
5. **Mix gently; do not shake.** Roll the vial between the palms or swirl slowly. The powder should dissolve in 1–3 minutes into a clear, colourless solution; if not, the guide says to leave it in the fridge for 10–30 minutes and check again. The guide claims shaking creates foam that destroys peptide bonds and "can reduce potency 30–50%".
6. **Inspect.** Hold to the light. It should be completely clear with no particles, and colourless (a slight yellow tint is acceptable). Cloudy or with floating particles: discard, as it may be contaminated or degraded.
7. **Label and refrigerate immediately.** Write the compound and vial size (e.g. "Retatrutide 10mg"), the concentration (e.g. "5 mg/mL"), the date reconstituted, and a use-by date 28 days later.

## Storage

How the guide says to store it:

| State | Temperature | Shelf life |
|---|---|---|
| Lyophilized (powder) | Room temp | 6–12 months |
| Lyophilized (powder) | Refrigerated (2–8°C) | 2+ years |
| Lyophilized (powder) | Frozen (-20°C) | 3+ years |
| **Reconstituted (BAC water)** | **Refrigerated (2–8°C)** | **Up to 28 days** |
| Reconstituted (sterile water only) | Refrigerated | Use within 24 hours |

- "Never freeze reconstituted peptide — ice crystals shear peptide bonds apart. Only freeze lyophilized powder."
- "Never leave reconstituted solution at room temperature — loses potency rapidly."

### Why the guide insists on bacteriostatic water

The guide's reasoning: [[Bacteriostatic Water]] contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol, a preservative that inhibits bacterial growth, which it says lets a reconstituted vial be stored for up to 28 days and drawn from repeatedly. Sterile water for injection (SWI) has no preservative, so once punctured bacteria can grow; the guide says SWI-reconstituted peptide must be used within 24 hours and discarded. Because retatrutide is dosed weekly and one vial spans several weeks, it calls BAC water "essential". It notes that a small percentage of people are sensitive to benzyl alcohol; they must use sterile water and reconstitute fresh for each dose.

What the evidence says: the [Bacteriostatic Water for Injection label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display) describes 0.9% benzyl alcohol and directs use according to the drug manufacturer's instructions, and compatibility varies by drug. A preservative does not validate a universal 28-day life for every reconstituted drug, and the 24-hour sterile-water rule is not backed by a formulation-specific label or stability study. Storage instructions have to come from the actual product; see [[Peptide Reconstitution]]. (from [[Health Evidence Review - September 2026]])

## Injection

The guide's quick reference:

- **Route:** subcutaneous (SubQ) only.
- **Frequency:** once weekly, same day each week.
- **Sites:** abdomen (most common), front of thigh, or back of upper arm. Rotate each time, at least 1 inch from the last site.
- **Needle angle:** 45°–90° depending on body composition and needle length.
- **Technique:** pinch a fold of skin, insert the needle, push the plunger slowly over 5–10 seconds.
- Let the vial sit at room temperature for 15–30 minutes before drawing a dose; the guide says cold solution causes more discomfort.

## Titration schedule

The guide labels this a "Clinical Titration Schedule". It is not an approved prescribing schedule.

| Phase | Weeks | Weekly dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 — Initiation | Weeks 1–4 | 1 mg | Starting dose, assess tolerance |
| Phase 2 — Early Escalation | Weeks 5–8 | 2 mg | GI side effects may begin |
| Phase 3 — Mid Escalation | Weeks 9–12 | 4 mg | Appetite suppression becomes noticeable |
| Phase 4 — High Escalation | Weeks 13–16 | 6 mg | Intermediate step (Phase 3 protocol) |
| Phase 5 — Therapeutic | Weeks 17–20 | 9 mg | Significant weight loss |
| Phase 6 — Maximum | Weeks 21+ | 12 mg | Max dose studied in trials |

The guide's warning: "Do NOT skip titration." It says starting high causes severe GI side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea), and that if side effects are significant at any step, stay at the current dose for an extra 2–4 weeks.

Correction, 16 October 2026: Lilly's phase 3 obesity trials (TRIUMPH-1, -2 and -3) started participants on 2 mg once weekly, not 1 mg ([Lilly, 21 May 2026](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-powerful-weight-loss-in-pivotal-phase-3-obesity-trial); [Lilly, 23 July 2026](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-successful-in-two-additional-phase-3-obesity-trials-delivering-significant-improvements-in-weight-and-a1c)).

## Common mistakes

The guide's list of mistakes "That Ruin Your Peptide". The percentage losses have no supporting study attached.

| Mistake | Consequence (as the guide states it) |
|---|---|
| Shaking the vial | Denatures peptide, reduces potency 30–50% |
| Spraying water onto powder | Damages peptide molecular structure |
| Using sterile water instead of BAC water | No preservative = must use within 24 hours |
| Leaving reconstituted peptide at room temp | Loses 10–20% potency per day |
| Freezing reconstituted solution | Ice crystals destroy peptide bonds |
| Reusing needles | Contamination risk + dulls needle |
| Adding too little water | Ultra-concentrated = impossible to measure small doses accurately |

## What the evidence does not support

- The universal 28-day refrigerated life and the 24-hour sterile-water rule are not backed by a formulation-specific label or stability study.
- The stated percentage losses from shaking (30–50%) and room-temperature storage (10–20% per day) have no supporting study attached, and nor do the freezing claims.
- A clear-looking solution does not establish sterility, potency or identity.
- The dose-escalation table is not an approved prescribing schedule.

[[Peptide Reconstitution]] and [[Bacteriostatic Water]] explain why vehicle compatibility and storage instructions must be specific to the actual medicinal product. [[Health Evidence]] covers how the wiki separates evidence from personal claims.

## Corrections

- Correction, 22 September 2026: the guide does not generalise to other research peptides. No formulation evidence supports that; it applies only to the retatrutide product it describes, and only as the guide's own claims.
