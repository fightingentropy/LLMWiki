# Health & Psychology

Curated from the March 2026 X bookmark archive. Entries are kept here because they remain useful beyond the original timeline context.

- [Tazarotene versus tretinoin](https://x.com/Hoostethics/status/2093649112352821464) — The bookmark argues that tazarotene is categorically stronger and more effective, using an uncontrolled before-and-after image and a routine that also included azelaic acid and moisturizers. Randomized photoaging trials found that some tazarotene concentrations and formulations produced faster or greater improvement on selected measures than tretinoin, while early irritation was more frequent; they do not establish a universal ranking across acne, pigmentation, wrinkles, every concentration, or every patient. Both are prescription retinoids, so product choice, titration, contraindications, and pregnancy-related precautions belong with a qualified clinician rather than the post's suggestion to skip directly to tazarotene. _([24-week comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15203997/); [multicentre concentration trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11735710/); @Hoostethics; manually retained 2026-08-29)_
- [Body-odour disgust sensitivity and xenophobic attitudes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10090875/) — A preregistered study of 6,836 participants across nine countries found that greater self-reported sensitivity to body odours was associated with more negative attitudes toward fictitious refugee groups, partly through perceived differences in hygiene and food-preparation norms. This is an association in survey measures, not proof that smelling immigrants causes xenophobia, that disgust determines anyone's politics, or that the [bookmark's crude punchline](https://x.com/Basedgoymemes/status/2093361464119566353) follows from the result. Preserve the underlying psychology research separately from the meme. _(manually retained 2026-08-29)_
- [Birth-control harms clip with an inflammatory repost](https://x.com/benwehrman/status/2093113877688906147) — Retain the source video as a lead for later review, not as medical evidence. The repost supplies no transcript, study, named clinical claim, or verifiable context and adds threatening rhetoric about people who promoted contraception. Different contraceptive methods have different benefits, risks, contraindications, and evidence; do not infer a general medical conclusion or endorse the caption without identifying and checking the underlying claims. _(@benwehrman, crediting @florawholehealth; manually retained 2026-08-29)_
- [Foot, ankle, rotation, and bouncing movement reference](https://x.com/wirallop/status/2092319105508356490) — A short Guy Macchia clip demonstrates lower-leg work, standing rotation, supported movement, and bouncing. Save as an exercise-video reference, not a validated treatment plan: claims such as doing 75% of training below the knee, rotation being the master of decompression, and broad fascia explanations are not substantiated by the clip. Suitability depends on the person, load, balance, and injury history; pain or a rehabilitation goal warrants qualified assessment rather than copying the routine uncritically. _(manually retained 2026-08-28)_
- [Red-light therapy: indication-specific evidence, not a cure-all](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/safety/red-light-therapy) — The American Academy of Dermatology describes evidence for some skin-aging concerns and hereditary hair loss, with results varying by device, treatment, and individual. That does not establish the [bookmark's claims](https://x.com/dantefofante/status/2091994204365009297) of universal improvements in recovery, mood, energy, and health. Distinguish the intended indication, wavelength, dose, and device; discuss suitability, photosensitizing medication, and skin sensitivity with a clinician and follow the specified eye-protection instructions. Retain the source overview, not an endorsement of an unspecified Amazon device. _(manually retained 2026-08-28)_
- [Limerence, attachment, and ADHD-related hyperfocus](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/limerence) — Limerence describes intense, involuntary fixation on another person; it is not an official medical diagnosis. Cleveland Clinic discusses insecure attachment and a possible vulnerability related to ADHD hyperfocus, not an inevitable outcome of ADHD or a universal biochemical explanation. The [original post](https://x.com/apaji_x/status/2091560421555614093) overstates this as a severe dopamine addiction. A useful distinction is reciprocal knowledge of a real person versus an imagined relationship reinforced by checking and rumination; persistent distress or impairment is a reason to seek professional support. _(manually retained 2026-08-28)_

- [Legitimate suffering and neurotic avoidance](https://x.com/aristomarinetti/status/2018034523141804336) — Useful Jungian frame: attempts to evade unavoidable human pain can manufacture larger, unnecessary suffering. _(@aristomarinetti; archive #894)_
- [AI-assisted longitudinal symptom investigation](https://x.com/amydeng_/status/2066928566961639716) — Amy Deng describes tracking symptoms and metrics, testing hypotheses, and iterating with models while keeping medical decisions under physician guidance; a case study, not medical advice. _(@amydeng_; live review 2026-07-25)_
- [Microneedling plus minoxidil for androgenetic alopecia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23960389/) — A 2013 evaluator-blinded randomized pilot in 100 men reported mean 12-week hair-count increases of 91.4 versus 22.2 in a fixed 1 cm² area. Later [RCT meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40056230/) supports better hair-count outcomes than minoxidil alone, but protocols and study quality vary; this is evidence to discuss with a dermatologist, not a DIY treatment protocol. _(Discovered via Mike Hart (@drmikehart); live review 2026-07-25)_

- [Follicular fluid differentially attracts human sperm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32517615/) — A 2020 in-vitro study using samples from couples undergoing assisted reproduction found that follicular fluid from different women consistently attracted sperm from particular men differently, and the stronger response did not systematically favor the woman's partner. This supports a possible gamete-mediated selection mechanism; it did not place eggs beside competing sperm, prove conscious choice, or establish a clinical test for unexplained infertility. _(Discovered via Anish Moonka (@anishmoonka); live review 2026-07-25)_
- [Depression-associated gut microbiota transfer in rats](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2016.07.019) — A 2016 study transferred pooled fecal microbiota from three severely depressed male donors or three matched controls into microbiota-depleted rats and reported anhedonia- and anxiety-like behavior plus altered tryptophan metabolism. This is small preclinical animal evidence, not proof that human depression is transmissible or a basis for self-treatment. _(Discovered via Nicholas Fabiano, MD (@NTFabiano); live review 2026-07-25)_
- [Patient-specific in-vivo gene editing for CPS1 deficiency](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2504747) — A customized lipid-nanoparticle base editor was given to one infant with severe CPS1 deficiency; over seven weeks the patient tolerated more dietary protein and needed less nitrogen-scavenger medication without a serious treatment-related adverse event. This is an encouraging N-of-1 case with short follow-up, not proof of a cure or general scalability. _(Discovered via vittorio (@IterIntellectus); live review 2026-07-25)_
- [Cannabis smoking status and blood-DNA methylation associations](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12890-025-03634-9) — A cross-sectional CanCOLD subset of 93 older adults, including 10 current and 32 former cannabis smokers, found thousands of blood-DNA methylation associations versus never-smokers, with overlap in aging- and cancer-related pathways. It cannot establish causation, persistence within individuals, downstream gene expression, or clinical cancer risk; the authors also note self-report, tobacco confounding, small-group sizes, and the absence of longitudinal repeated measures. _(Discovered via Chamath Palihapitiya (@chamath); live review 2026-07-25)_
- [Streaming brain-to-voice neuroprosthesis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-01905-6) — A 2025 Nature Neuroscience study used high-density cortical recordings from one 47-year-old woman with severe paralysis and anarthria to stream synthesized speech using her pre-injury voice. It is a promising invasive proof of concept, but single-participant clinical-trial evidence rather than a generally available treatment. _(Discovered via vittorio (@IterIntellectus); live review 2026-07-25)_
- [The two-hour sleep thought experiment](https://x.com/waitbutwhy/status/1906866707534147681) — Tim Urban asks why people sacrifice two hours of sleep when they would eagerly take a drug promising the benefits of two extra hours. It is a behavioral prompt, not a quantified drug comparison; [NIH guidance](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/how-much-sleep) says adults generally need seven to nine hours and individual needs vary. _(Tim Urban (@waitbutwhy); live review 2026-07-25)_
- [Thirty years of nutrition knowledge simplified](https://gilcarvalhomdphd.substack.com/p/30-years-of-nutrition-knowledge-simplified) — Gil Carvalho summarizes practical nutrition fundamentals, dietary patterns associated with lower chronic-disease risk, and ways to recognize fad claims without eliminating whole food groups or relying on expensive supplements. This is a broad educational overview rather than individualized medical advice. _(Discovered via Nutrition Made Simple (@NutritionMadeS3); [original bookmark](https://x.com/NutritionMadeS3/status/2081319985096384566); live review 2026-07-27)_
- [Two weeks without mobile internet](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/2/pgaf017/8016017) — A preregistered month-long randomized trial assigned participants to block internet access on their smartphones for two weeks while preserving calls, texts, and internet access on other devices. Intention-to-treat analyses found improvements in subjective well-being, mental health, and objectively measured sustained attention, but compliance was difficult and only 119 of 467 committed participants met the preregistered compliance threshold. This was not total smartphone or internet abstinence, and the “ten years younger” comparison is an effect-size analogy rather than literal rejuvenation. _([original bookmark](https://x.com/NTFabiano/status/2084270984022905286); [additional discussion](https://x.com/sentientist/status/2054591492162040312); selected 2026-08-08; duplicate merged 2026-08-23)_
- [Water preloading before meals in a weight-loss diet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859815/) — In a 12-week randomized trial of 48 adults aged 55–75 with overweight or obesity, both groups followed a hypocaloric diet and one group also drank 500 mL of water before each main meal. The water group lost about 2 kg more and had a 44% greater rate of weight loss. This does not support the bookmark's claims of “no other dietary changes,” ghrelin dilution, a 24–30% metabolism boost, faster liver fat metabolism, zero side effects, or equivalence to a meal-delivery program; treat it as a modest adjunct, not a standalone protocol. _([original bookmark](https://x.com/SmartNotHard1/status/2083606184594919582); selected live bookmark 2026-08-08)_
- [Wide-palate appearance as an online health signifier](https://x.com/MagnusVigso/status/2083961118381256999) — The post treats a naturally wide palate as evidence of a well-nourished childhood and a predominantly parasympathetic state. Preserve it as an aesthetic and developmental-health hypothesis, not a diagnostic fact: the post supplies no measurements or clinical source, and visible craniofacial structure reflects genetics, development, dental factors, environment, and camera presentation rather than a readable autonomic-nervous-system state. _(Magnus Vigsø (@MagnusVigso); manually retained 2026-08-08)_
- [DIY household-bleach sinus-rinse anecdote](https://x.com/EdaG889915/status/2052346899600998694) — The author reports feeling better after adding diluted household sodium hypochlorite to a sinus rinse and supplies a [follow-up mixing instruction](https://x.com/EdaG889915/status/2052350686268887512). Preserve this as an anecdote, **not a recipe or self-treatment protocol**. A small randomized study of device-generated low-concentration hypochlorous acid in selected chronic-rhinosinusitis patients does not validate improvised household-bleach mixtures; hypochlorous acid and sodium hypochlorite are not interchangeable products. FDA guidance describes saline made with distilled, sterile, or previously boiled water, and CDC bleach instructions concern disinfecting otherwise unsafe water—not adding bleach as a therapeutic ingredient. Discuss persistent symptoms and any nonstandard rinse with an ENT clinician. _([clinical study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27853946/); [FDA safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/rinsing-your-sinuses-neti-pots-safe); manually retained 2026-08-08)_
- [Depression can affect concentration and memory](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression) — Difficulty concentrating, remembering, or making decisions is a recognized symptom of depression, and severe cases can involve prominent memory and thinking problems. This supports the [bookmark author's lived experience](https://x.com/Laiiyylaa/status/2085925048335126831), but not the stronger conclusion that depression necessarily causes permanent “brain damage”; persistent cognitive symptoms deserve clinical assessment rather than self-diagnosis from a post. _(Laiiyylaa (@Laiiyylaa); selected live bookmark 2026-08-09)_
- [Neuroplasticity and deliberate repetition](https://x.com/alexeixbt/status/2086051211271196886) — Alexei's motivational essay correctly centers the continuing capacity of adult brains to change through experience and the importance of repeated practice, attention, recovery, and sleep. Preserve it as an accessible behavioral frame, not a neuroscience protocol: acetylcholine is one of several neuromodulators rather than a universal “save switch,” and the essay does not establish that brief intense movement guarantees learning, that willpower is a single trainable muscle, or that wealth, confidence, and personality reduce to repeated neural wiring. _([human acetylcholine and motor-cortex plasticity study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6673455/); alexei (@alexeixbt); manually added 2026-08-09)_
- [Intralymphatic immunotherapy for allergic rhinitis](https://x.com/ctbeiser/status/2085089503908413470) — Chris Beiser reports substantial and lasting relief after intralymphatic immunotherapy (ILIT) in a discussion about likely dust-mite allergy, nighttime congestion, and impaired sleep. His later clarification estimates a 65–70% reduction in allergy symptoms rather than a complete “permanent fix,” and another participant reports no benefit or possible worsening. Controlled evidence is mixed: a [randomized double-blind trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34088322/) using dust-mite, cat, and dog extracts found little overall clinical advantage over placebo and recorded pain plus moderate-to-severe systemic reactions, while a tiny [11-person pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28293934/) reported symptom improvement but included two cases of anaphylaxis. Preserve this as a treatment lead to discuss with an allergy specialist—not a recommendation, established cure, or self-treatment procedure. _(Chris Beiser (@ctbeiser); manually added 2026-08-09)_
- [Do not trade alcohol harm for cigarette harm](https://x.com/algekalipso/status/2086859052181786862) — The post uses a convex-versus-concave marginal-risk analogy to propose that some people should “drink less, smoke more.” Preserve it as a counterexample in multi-risk reasoning, not health advice: population risk curves do not establish a safe personal substitution, tobacco use is harmful at every level, alcohol is also carcinogenic and not risk-free, and using one addictive exposure to regulate another can compound rather than optimize harm. Reducing either should rely on evidence-based support rather than a social-media Pareto calculation. _([WHO tobacco guidance](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/tobacco); [WHO alcohol guidance](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/alcohol); Andrés Gómez Emilsson (@algekalipso); manually retained 2026-08-11)_
- [Smoking does not prevent long COVID](https://x.com/Bitcoin_Teddy/status/2086392291862995011) — The clip claims smokers get only mild COVID, cannot develop long COVID, nicotine blocks spike protein, and nicotine patches are harmless. Preserve it as a medical-misinformation example: CDC says anyone infected can develop long COVID, smoking is associated with severe COVID outcomes, tobacco damages nearly every organ, and nicotine is addictive. Nicotine-replacement products have defined cessation uses and contraindications; the clip does not establish routine patches as a safe or effective long-COVID treatment. _([CDC Long COVID](https://www.cdc.gov/long-covid/about/index.html); [CDC risk evidence](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/hcp/clinical-care/underlying-conditions.html); [WHO tobacco guidance](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/tobacco); manually retained 2026-08-11)_
- [Mind-wandering and momentary happiness](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1192439) — Killingsworth and Gilbert's 2010 experience-sampling study found that participants reported lower momentary happiness while their minds wandered, including toward neutral or pleasant topics, across many daily activities. It is an observational self-report study about attention and concurrent affect—not evidence that consuming instead of creating causes clinical depression or that finding a “creative quest” is a diagnosis or cure. _([original bookmark](https://x.com/NTFabiano/status/2086444739805532641); manually retained 2026-08-11)_
- [Isolation, rumination, and small re-entry steps](https://x.com/sunnkssdseraph/status/2086173566710743236) — The post describes a loop in which staying home, repetitive thought, worry, and declining motivation make ordinary life feel increasingly distant, then recommends starting with a small action. That can be a useful recognition prompt, but “nervous system stuck in survival mode” is not a diagnosis and similar changes can arise from depression, anxiety, physical illness, disability, exhaustion, grief, unsafe circumstances, or ordinary preference. Persistent impairment warrants professional assessment rather than forced exposure or self-diagnosis from the post. _([NIMH depression guidance](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression); lyra (@sunnkssdseraph); manually retained 2026-08-11)_
- [Bioengineered chewing gum for oral HPV and cancer-associated bacteria](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41656354/) — An ex-vivo study exposed collected saliva and oral-rinse samples to bean lectin and antimicrobial-peptide gum preparations, reporting aggregation of up to 93% of detected HPV and reductions in selected bacteria under the experimental conditions. This does not show that ordinary chewing clears an infection, prevents cancer, or improves clinical outcomes in people; preserve it as an early delivery-platform result requiring in-vivo safety, dosing, efficacy, and replication. _([original bookmark](https://x.com/Polymarket/status/2088726104424976834); selected live bookmark 2026-08-23)_
- [UK prostate-cancer incidence and screening context](https://news.sky.com/story/one-man-diagnosed-with-prostate-cancer-in-the-uk-every-eight-minutes-13575557) — Macmillan estimates more than 68,000 UK diagnoses annually, around one every eight minutes, and roughly 600,000 men living with prostate cancer; population ageing and increased detection affect the trend. Early disease can be symptomless, but testing is a shared clinical decision rather than a universal instruction: the [UK National Screening Committee](https://view-health-screening-recommendations.service.gov.uk/prostate-cancer/) does not recommend population-wide PSA screening and currently recommends targeted screening for a defined high-risk BRCA2 group because overdiagnosis and unnecessary treatment can cause harm. _([original bookmark](https://x.com/bertdernernert/status/2091055197405557210); selected live bookmark 2026-08-23)_
