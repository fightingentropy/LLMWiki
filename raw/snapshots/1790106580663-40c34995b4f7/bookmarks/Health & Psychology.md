- [ADHD, delayed body clocks, and circadian interventions](https://x.com/BrandonLuuMD/status/2102338491942347175) — Brandon Luu describes a link between ADHD and later biological sleep/wake timing, with early evidence that shifting the body clock earlier may ease symptoms for some people. The [quoted explanation](https://x.com/anishmoonka/status/2102284259553890448) and his 2025 perspective with Nicholas Fabiano cite delayed sleep/wake timing in up to 78%, sleep disturbances in up to 80% of adults and 82% of children, and an evening melatonin rise about 45 minutes later in children and 90 minutes later in adults. The attached graphic depicts later melatonin timing and a later, flatter morning cortisol rise. Their proposed approach includes screening for sleep/circadian problems, a consistent wake-up time, morning bright light, less evening light and screen exposure, regular routines, and selective low-dose melatonin when delayed timing is identified. They describe these measures as additions to standard ADHD care, with treatment evidence still developing. _([paper: ADHD as a circadian rhythm disorder](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1697900/full); [related existing Brain note](../misc/Medical.md); Brandon Luu, MD (@BrandonLuuMD); manually added 2026-09-22)_

- [Tazarotene versus tretinoin](https://x.com/Hoostethics/status/2093649112352821464) — The bookmark argues that tazarotene is categorically stronger and more effective, using an uncontrolled before-and-after image and a routine that also included azelaic acid and moisturizers.
