# Electrical evidence review — 22 September 2026

Capture type: dated research notes from the official sources below. Scope: edition status, England Part P distinctions, cable-route correction, safe working and PAT frequency. Original webinar transcripts are unavailable, so detailed historical test procedures have not been authenticated.

## Edition status

IET states BS 7671:2018+A4:2026 is published. The Brown Book with Amendment 3 remains valid until 15 October 2026; either edition may be used during the transition. IET lists Guidance Note 3: Inspection & Testing, tenth edition, and On-Site Guide, ninth edition, aligned with A4. The earlier wiki's description of GN3 as fifth edition was incorrect and conflated it with in-service equipment material.

Sources: [IET edition checker](https://electrical.theiet.org/bs-7671-18th-edition-wiring-regulations/ensure-you-are-up-to-date-with-bs-7671/); [IET guidance publications](https://electrical.theiet.org/guidance-and-codes-of-practice/publications-by-category/amendment-42026-to-bs-76712018-iet-wiring-regulations/bs-7671-and-guidance/), accessed 2026-09-22.

## England: Part P and notification

The statutory Part P requirement and Approved Document P are different: the Approved Document provides guidance. England's 2013 guidance covers dwellings and specified associated installations, including some business premises sharing the dwelling's electricity meter. Notifiable categories include new circuits, consumer-unit replacement and additions/alterations in defined special locations. A kitchen location alone is not a blanket notification trigger. Non-notifiable work still has to comply. Do not apply England's notification categories to other UK jurisdictions without checking their rules.

Source: [GOV.UK Approved Document P](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-approved-document-p), sections 2–3 of the linked 2013 document, accessed 2026-09-22.

## Concealed cables

A 30 mA RCD alone does not permit arbitrary concealed cable routes outside prescribed zones. IET's 2015 article explains the combination of zones, additional protection and permitted alternative construction methods. It is historical guidance under the seventeenth edition and cannot establish all current A4 clause details. Plastic conduit alone is not equivalent to an earthed metallic or other specifically permitted protective method. The raw Resources note's number 522.5.204 is retained as a source error, not copied as a verified current clause.

Source: [IET — Cables in walls (2015)](https://electrical.theiet.org/media/1445/cables-in-walls.pdf), accessed 2026-09-22. For design decisions use the applicable current BS 7671 text and its conditions.

## Safe work and RCD limitations

HSE says work should normally be done with equipment dead, secured against re-energisation and checked dead. An RCD cannot protect against every shock. Maintenance and testing require appropriate competence and suitable equipment; pressing an RCD test button does not establish every aspect of installation safety.

Sources: [HSE electrical FAQs](https://www.hse.gov.uk/electricity/faq.htm); [HSE GS38](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/books/gs38.htm), accessed 2026-09-22.

## Portable equipment

HSE does not prescribe blanket annual PAT testing. Maintenance arrangements and inspection/testing intervals depend on risk, use and environment. Visual checks matter; some faults require testing. A test label is not a guarantee that equipment remains safe.

Source: [HSE portable appliance testing FAQs](https://www.hse.gov.uk/electricity/faq-portable-appliance-testing.htm), accessed 2026-09-22.
