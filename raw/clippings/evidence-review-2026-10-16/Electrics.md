# Electrical evidence review — 16 October 2026

Capture type: dated research notes from the sources below; manufacturer and industry sources are marked and cannot prove what BS 7671 says. Scope: the electrical study pages, from edition status to portable equipment. BS 7671, GN3 and the IET in-service Code of Practice are paywalled, so their clause text was not read.

## Edition status

BSI set 15 October 2026 as the withdrawal date for BS 7671:2018+A2:2022+A3:2024, so from 16 October 2026 BS 7671:2018+A4:2026 is the only current edition. The IET edition checker still carries the transition wording, and its errata section names A2:2022 rather than A3. The IET still lists GN3 tenth edition and the On-Site Guide ninth edition as its A4 books. The A3 introduction says an installation built to an earlier edition is not necessarily unsafe or in need of upgrading.

## GS38, probes and test equipment standards

GS38 (fourth edition, 2015) is HSE guidance under the Electricity at Work Regulations 1989. Paragraph 9 caps the exposed probe tip at 4 mm and strongly recommends 2 mm or less, or retractable screened probes. GS38 has no 19 mm figure; Hioki (manufacturer) says 19 mm was the IEC 61010-031 allowance before its 2011 revision. Paragraph 11 asks for shrouded or firmly attached connectors and names no plug size. GS38 sets no before-every-use check of leads: it asks for maintenance by a competent person with inspection records, and for clamp meters to be examined before use (paras 26, 30); INDG354 leaves pre-use checks to written safe systems of work. It defines no "GS38 compliant" marking: leads should meet BS EN 61010-031 and carry their CAT rating and maker's mark. It sets no record-retention period.

GS38's footnote to paragraph 8, citing BS EN 61010, puts CAT IV at the source of the installation, CAT III in the building installation, and CAT II on circuits directly connected to it, such as appliances and portable tools. It gives no distances; the 10 m and 20 m examples come from Fluke (manufacturer). HBM (manufacturer) says IEC 61010-2-030 replaced CAT I with an "O" marking. HSE's rough shock-hazard threshold is 50 V ac or 120 V ripple-free dc in dry conditions, lower in wet, confined or conductive places (INDG354; GS38 para 5).

IEC 61010-031 covers hand-held probe assemblies, so the 61010 series covers leads as well as instruments. IEC 61557-1 states it does not cover functional safety, and Part 12 is titled Power metering and monitoring devices (PMD).

## Safe isolation and the DNO cut-out

HSG85 (paras 46–55) confirms the sequence: identify, disconnect, lock off, keep removed fuses, post notices, and prove dead at the point of work with a detector proved before and after use, not a multimeter or non-contact stick. Neither HSG85 nor GS38 sets an order of conductor pairs; HSG85 para 53 asks that all supply conductors be proved dead, and Electrical Safety First's Best Practice Guide 2 (industry) asks the same of line, neutral and protective conductors.

The IET (July 2025) says the cut-out fuse may be withdrawn by the DNO, the supplier's meter operator, Safe Isolation Providers under the Retail Energy Code, and anyone the DNO expressly permits; some DNOs, such as SSEN, allow trade-association electricians.

Sources: [HSE HSG85 (PDF)](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg85.pdf); [Electrical Safety First — Best Practice Guide 2 (PDF)](https://select.org.uk/common/Uploaded%20files/Technical/FreeDownloads/Best_Practice_Guide_2.pdf) (industry); [IET Wiring Matters — Removing the distributor's cut-out fuse](https://electrical.theiet.org/wiring-matters/years/2025/106-july-2025/removing-the-distributors-cut-out-fuse/), accessed 2026-10-16.

## Continuity, calculations and test limits

There is no general 0.1 Ω limit for circuit protective conductors: Zs is worked out as Ze + (R1 + R2) and compared with the Chapter 41 maximum or the maker's data, corrected for temperature (×0.8 in the IET's May 2024 example). A 1.5 mm² CPC is about 0.12 Ω over 10 m (Prysmian, manufacturer). The IET's May 2025 neutral current diversion update warns that removing an earthing or bonding conductor can put hazardous touch voltages on conductive parts where an open PEN fault exists, and describes clamp-meter checks before and after isolation. Prysmian confirms the twin-and-earth CPC sizes and 1.83 mΩ/m for 10 mm²; every worked sum checked is correct.

In AC circuits V × I is apparent power, equal to watts only at unity power factor. Voltage drop limits come from BS 7671 Regulation 525 and Appendix 4, not the Building Regulations: 3% for lighting and 5% for other uses on a public supply, as informative values.

## Certificates and the EICR

The IET's A4 model forms (15 April 2026) call for a separate MEIWC for each existing circuit worked on, or one EIC in place of several MEIWCs; an EIC is needed once a new circuit is introduced, not one per circuit. The EIC has no qualifications field, adds a second designer signature where design is shared, and allows a single-signature version.

The A4 model EICR words FI as "Further investigation is advised" and treats C3 and FI as advisory, so only C1 or C2 makes a report Unsatisfactory. The superseded A2:2022 form made FI unsatisfactory; the IET's July 2026 Wiring Matters article explains the change. The A4 Schedule of Inspection records a tick, C1/C2, C3, FI, N/V, LIM or N/A, with "X" only for inadequate intake equipment, and the declaration has no qualifications field.

In England, regulation 3 of SI 2020/312 confirms the five-year interval and 28-day deadlines, and requires written confirmation from a qualified person that the standards are met or further work is needed. S.I. 2025/1043 extended the regulations to social landlords. GOV.UK guidance (1 November 2025) places HMOs within them and still treats FI as needing work within 28 days, so it now differs from the A4 form. Scotland's Repairing Standard guidance and Wales' 2022 fitness regulations each require inspection at least every five years.

## Part P, Building Regulations and concealed cables

Approved Document P (2013) is still the current England edition. Paragraph 2.2 extends Part P beyond homes to outbuildings, common areas of flats and business premises on a dwelling's meter. Regulation 12(6A) of the Building Regulations 2010 makes every new circuit and consumer unit replacement notifiable wherever it is; location matters only for work on existing circuits, notifiable inside a special location. Paragraph 3.13 says non-notifiable work, including replacements, should still be inspected, tested and certificated to BS 7671, and the A4 MEIWC says inspection and testing should always be carried out.

The IET's 2015 Wiring Matters article (seventeenth edition) applies the metallic-partition rule, irrespective of depth, to any partition with metallic parts other than fixings such as nails or screws, not only one mainly of metal. It places the concealed-cable rules in Regulation Group 522.6, consistent with "522.5.204" being a misnumbering. Approved Document A, paragraph 2C30, limits vertical chases to 1/3 of the wall thickness, or of the leaf in a cavity wall, and horizontal chases to 1/6 of the leaf; it gives no joist-notch rule.

## Portable equipment and in-service testing

HSE's PAT FAQ says labelling and records are not legally required but are a useful management tool, and that testing alone cannot find every defect; it does not say a test label is no guarantee of safety. HSG107 (2013) confirms the three levels of check, that a label should carry no next-test date (para 54), and that Class II equipment has no earth (para 14), so its insulation test is made to accessible conductive parts, as Seaward (manufacturer) describes. INDG236 says a test shows safety only at the time it is done. The IET's published forms give V.3 only green PASS and red FAIL labels and title V.6 "Test instrument record". The in-service Code of Practice is in its fifth edition (2020), separate from GN3. The "fuse follows the flex" rule conflicts with Greater Manchester Fire and Rescue Service guidance, which sizes the plug fuse by appliance wattage; HSG107 asks only for the correct fuse.
