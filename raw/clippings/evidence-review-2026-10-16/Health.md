# Health evidence review — 16 October 2026

Capture type: dated research notes from the primary sources below, covering claims on the wiki's health pages; scoped paraphrases, not copies of the sources.

## ADHD and circadian timing

Luu and Fabiano's paper is a perspective article published on 10 December 2025, with no new data. Its figures match the wiki: delayed sleep timing in up to 78% of people with ADHD, sleep problems in up to 80% of adults and 82% of children, and evening melatonin rising about 45 minutes late in children and 90 in adults. It links an earlier clock to fewer symptoms only by correlation, places the pattern in a subgroup, says remission evidence is lacking, and does not propose reclassifying ADHD as solely circadian.

NIH Research Matters and Washington University describe Kay et al. (Cell, 2025): in 5,795 children's brain scans, stimulants acted on arousal and reward networks rather than attention networks. The Vyvanse label confirms lisdexamfetamine is a dextroamphetamine prodrug whose exact action in ADHD is unknown. Cleveland Clinic says limerence is not an official diagnosis and links it to insecure attachment and ADHD hyperfocus; it also compares limerence with addiction.

NIGMS calls light the strongest cue for the roughly 24-hour body clock. NHLBI recommends 7–9 hours of sleep for adults; NICHD says need varies with age and the individual.

Sources: [Luu and Fabiano, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1697900/full); [NIH Research Matters](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/adhd-medications-stimulate-alertness-motivation); [WashU release](https://source.washu.edu/2025/12/stimulant-adhd-medications-work-differently-than-thought/); [Vyvanse label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=704e4378-ca83-445c-8b45-3cfa51c1ecad); [Cleveland Clinic — Limerence](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/limerence); [NIGMS — Circadian rhythms](https://www.nigms.nih.gov/education/fact-sheets/Pages/circadian-rhythms); [NHLBI — sleep](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/how-much-sleep); [NICHD — sleep](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/sleep/conditioninfo/how-much), accessed 2026-10-16.

## Stress, breathing and the swimming-rat study

Correction: in Richter's 1957 paper, the retold figures are for rats with trimmed whiskers. All 34 trimmed wild rats died within 1–15 minutes; 9 of 12 trimmed domesticated rats swam 40–60 hours; intact domesticated rats swam 60–80 hours in 95 °F water. Wild rats conditioned by brief holding and immersion swam as long as domestic rats or longer. Richter read the deaths as hopelessness. The wiki's breathing and cold-shower lines are attributed takes, not physiological claims.

Sources: [Richter, 1957](https://doi.org/10.1097/00006842-195705000-00004), accessed 2026-10-16.

## Water filtration and the DWI view of reverse osmosis

The Drinking Water Inspectorate advises checking the water company's free quality report before buying a filter, and changing cartridges on time. It says reverse osmosis wastes 20–30% of incoming water; RO water is not generally considered suitable for drinking and cooking; RO suits specialist domestic uses; and by law the water company must be notified before an RO unit is installed. The CDC matches filters to NSF/ANSI 42 (taste and odour), 53 (cysts), 58 (reverse osmosis) and 62 (distillation).

The Oasis Health post scores about 80 products with no methodology.

## Supplements: creatine, magnesium, vitamin C, tongkat ali

Correction (creatine): the Australian Institute of Sport puts creatine monohydrate in Group A, strong evidence for specific sporting uses. The ISSN position stand reports consistent high-intensity performance gains and good tolerance in healthy people.

Correction (magnesium): the evidence differs by claim. NIH's NCCIH calls the migraine evidence limited and says the doses studied exceed the safe upper intake level, so need supervision; the 2012 AAN/AHS guideline rated magnesium probably effective (Level B) for preventing episodic migraine. The Cochrane migraine review is still a protocol. For sleep, a 2021 meta-analysis of three small trials and a 2026 review of 12 trials rate the evidence low to very low certainty. For hearing, a 1994 trial in 300 noise-exposed military recruits found fewer permanent threshold shifts with magnesium.

Correction (vitamin C): the 25% and fourfold figures match a preliminary 2006 study of 15 marginal-status against 7 adequate-status young adults. The fourfold rise came from 8 people in a depletion-repletion trial and measured fat oxidation on a treadmill, not body-fat loss.

Correction (tongkat ali): a 2022 meta-analysis of five trials found it raised total testosterone, most clearly in men with low levels, and called for more research. No human study shows pomegranate offsetting an oestrogen effect of tongkat ali.

The NHS vitamin D and fluoride guidance cited in September is unchanged.

Sources: [AIS Group A](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements/group_a); [ISSN creatine stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28615996/); [NCCIH — headaches](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/providers/digest/headaches-and-complementary-health-approaches-science); [Holland et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22529203/); [Cochrane protocol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41216917/); [Mah and Pitre, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33865376/); [Lopresti et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42661485/); [Attias et al., 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8135325/); [Johnston et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16945143/); [Leisegang et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36013514/), accessed 2026-10-16.

## Blueberries and cold exposure

Correction (blueberries): a 2026 meta-analysis of 14 trials in healthy people found better blood-vessel function but no significant effect on executive function or mood; a 2020 review of 12 trials found some memory gains across varied products. Neither reports brain-fog or brain-inflammation outcomes.

Correction (cold showers): one hour of head-out immersion in 14 °C water raised plasma dopamine 250%, far more exposure than a five-minute shower and a blood level, not a brain measure. A 2025 meta-analysis of 11 immersion trials found lower stress at 12 hours and better sleep quality, but no effect on mood. The largest cold-shower trial, 3,018 adults, found 29% less self-reported sickness absence and no fewer illness days. None shows medication equivalence.

Sources: [He et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41974068/); [Travica et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30999017/); [Šrámek et al., 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10751106/); [Cain et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39879231/); [Buijze et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27631616/), accessed 2026-10-16.

## Exercise, reps and testosterone

NHS activity guidance is unchanged. The push-up study's scope holds; its top group did more than 40 push-ups, so "40 or more" slightly misstates it.

Correction (high reps): a meta-analysis of 21 trials found light and heavy loads built similar muscle when sets went to failure, with more maximal strength from heavy loads. High reps alone do not prevent growth.

Correction (testosterone): most daily testosterone release in men happens during sleep, and a week of five-hour nights cut young men's daytime levels 10–15%. A high-volume leg session raised testosterone briefly but added no arm muscle or strength over 15 weeks. The human sun–testosterone link is observational. No study was found on neck bridges or neck blood flow and intelligence.

Sources: [NHS activity guidelines](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/); [Yang et al., 2019](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2724778); [Schoenfeld et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/); [Leproult and Van Cauter, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21632481/); [West et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19910330/); [Parikh et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34433056/), accessed 2026-10-16.

## Retatrutide status

The FDA page, current as of 1 October 2026, still says retatrutide is not a component of any FDA-approved drug and has not been found safe and effective. It now says retatrutide cannot be used in compounding under federal law, and that research-labelled GLP-1 products are of unknown quality. Lilly's FAQ says no regulator has approved it. The phase 2 NEJM paper confirms the code LY3437943 and once-weekly subcutaneous use.

ClinicalTrials.gov lists the four TRIUMPH phase 3 trials as completed. Lilly reported results in 2026: in TRIUMPH-1, 2,339 adults over 80 weeks, mean weight change reached −28.3% at the highest dose against −2.2% on placebo; TRIUMPH-2 and -3 met their primary endpoints. Lilly plans to file with the FDA in the first quarter of 2027 and says the drug may not legally be sold for human use. No filing or approval had been reported by 16 October 2026.

Sources: [FDA — unapproved GLP-1 drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss); [Jastreboff et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37366315/); [Lilly FAQ](https://lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide); [TRIUMPH-1 record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05929066); [TRIUMPH-1 release](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-powerful-weight-loss-in-pivotal-phase-3-obesity-trial); [TRIUMPH-2 and -3 release](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-successful-in-two-additional-phase-3-obesity-trials-delivering-significant-improvements-in-weight-and-a1c); [TRIUMPH-2 full results](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-substantial-weight-loss-and-a1c-reduction-underscoring-its-potential-promise-for-people-with-obesity-and-type-2-diabetes-302891798.html), accessed 2026-10-16.

## Bacteriostatic water, reconstitution and storage

Correction (sterility): the label describes Bacteriostatic Water for Injection as sterile, nonpyrogenic water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol, used under the drug manufacturer's instructions and not for neonates. The guide's "NOT sterile water" only separates it from preservative-free Sterile Water for Injection.

Correction (28 days and 24 hours): the label says to use a reconstituted drug promptly and not store it unless its manufacturer directs. The CDC's 28-day discard rule, from USP <797>, dates an opened manufactured multiple-dose vial, such as the BAC water itself, unless its maker says otherwise; it is not a shelf life for a peptide mixed in it.

Correction (shaking, freezing, room temperature): agitation can aggregate insulin at air–liquid interfaces, with no fixed potency-loss figure; freezing can cost protein drugs activity through cold, freeze-concentration and ice formation, and the review does not describe ice breaking peptide bonds; the Zepbound label forbids freezing but allows its single-dose pens and vials 21 days unrefrigerated. None gives the guide's 30–50% or 10–20%-a-day losses, and each limit belongs to its own formulation.

A U-100 insulin label confirms 100 units equals 1 mL; the reconstitution page's arithmetic holds.

Sources: [Bacteriostatic Water label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ccadcf46-6a6f-436b-9bbc-17e2983a335f&type=display); [CDC — injection practices](https://www.cdc.gov/injection-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/index.html); [Chouchane et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35290820/); [Bhatnagar et al., 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17963151/); [Zepbound label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=487cd7e7-434c-4925-99fa-aa80b1cc776b); [Humulin R label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b519bd83-038c-4ec5-a231-a51ec5cc291f), accessed 2026-10-16.

## Studies in Health Evidence

Correction (mind-wandering): in Killingsworth and Gilbert's 2010 paper, wandering to pleasant topics made no significant difference to happiness compared with staying on task; neutral and especially unpleasant topics were linked to lower happiness. The wiki's summary had said happiness fell even for pleasant topics.

Time-sensitive guidance holds. The UK National Screening Committee's March 2026 review rejects population PSA screening but recommends targeted screening for men with BRCA2 variants. The CDC still says anyone infected can develop long COVID and lists smoking as a conclusive risk factor for severe COVID-19. Macmillan's own estimate is about 600,000 UK men living with prostate cancer; the 68,000 diagnoses a year come from official statistics.

The other study summaries matched their records. The paternal-investment abstract confirms 158 father–adolescent pairs and moderation by psychopathy, but not a Romanian sample or perceived resemblance.

Sources: [Killingsworth and Gilbert, 2010](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1192439); [UK NSC — prostate](https://view-health-screening-recommendations.service.gov.uk/prostate-cancer/); [CDC — long COVID](https://www.cdc.gov/long-covid/about/index.html); [CDC — severe COVID risk](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/hcp/clinical-care/underlying-conditions.html); [Macmillan release](https://www.macmillan.org.uk/about-us/latest-news/news-and-stories/prostate-cancer-diagnosis-every-eight-minutes); [Ene et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2020.109879), accessed 2026-10-16.
